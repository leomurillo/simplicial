## Status of Lemma 8.1

Paper 22 Conjecture 5.2 (the all-order primorial Noether-Weil limit) is cornered into four obligations (§8). Lemma 8.1 is the TOP TASK among them: prove that the finite Hayes-Möbius recovery current converges in the Weil test-function topology to $\sum_{p^k} \Lambda(p^k) \widehat f(k \log p)$ as the primorial cutoff and occupation cutoff go to infinity.

Paper 22 specifies the five sublemmas but does not write them out. T22.1 writes them. The architecture is:

- **8.1a0** (CRT isolation): finite algebra, $\mathbb{Z}/P_n$ Pontryagin duality + Ramanujan sums + Möbius inversion. **No analytic input.**
- **8.1a** (occupation lift): structural Noether/Hayes-Möbius bookkeeping using the closed-current/leakage control of Theorem 5.1.3. **No PNT or sieve.**
- **8.1b** (double-cutoff tail): dominated convergence on $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ with exponential log-side decay. **Elementary.**
- **8.1c via Route 1** (Siegel-Walfisz-safe diagonal): classical Siegel-Walfisz with cutoff $M_n = \exp(P_n^\beta)$, $\beta > 0$ fixed. **Classical.**
- **8.1d** (density and topology): $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ dense in $\mathcal{S}_0$ in the completed Weil topology. **Standard FA.**

With T15 (finite-to-archimedean completion, May 2026), Route 1 of 8.1c suffices for the existence-theorem statement in Lemma 8.1: T15 Theorem 9 (uniqueness of completion from any cofinal tower) makes the "huge cutoffs" of Route 1 irrelevant to the structural identification. The Polymath-8-scale Route 2 is no longer required.

## Setup and conventions

Let $P_n = \prod_{p \le p_n} p$ be the $n$-th primorial. Let $R_n = \mathbb{Z}/P_n\mathbb{Z}$, equipped with its standard $L^2(R_n)$ inner product. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem:
$$R_n \cong \prod_{p \le p_n} \mathbb{F}_p, \qquad \widehat{R_n} \cong \prod_{p \le p_n} \widehat{\mathbb{F}_p},$$
with additive characters $\chi_a^{(P_n)}(s) = e^{2\pi i a s / P_n}$ factoring as $\chi_a = \prod_p \chi_{a_p}^{(p)}$ where $a = (a_p)_p$ is the CRT decomposition.

The **conductor** of $\chi_a^{(P_n)}$ is $\text{cond}(a) = P_n / \gcd(a, P_n)$. Under CRT:
$$\text{cond}((a_p)_p) = \prod_{p:\, a_p \ne 0} p.$$
The **conductor packet** is
$$H_d^{(P_n)} := \operatorname{span}\{\chi_a : \text{cond}(a) = d\}, \qquad d \mid P_n.$$
The mean-zero hyperplane decomposes as
$$H_{R_n} = \bigoplus_{d \mid P_n,\, d > 1} H_d^{(P_n)}.$$

Test functions $f \in \mathcal{S}_0$ are Schwartz with $f(0) = 0$, equipped with the completed Weil topology. Their Fourier transforms $G_f := \widehat f$ live on the log-side.

The **exponential subclass**:
$$\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp} := \left\{f \in \mathcal{S}_0 : \exists \delta > 0 \text{ such that } e^{(1+\delta)|u|} G_f(u) \in L^1(\mathbb{R}) \cap \mathcal{C}^k(\mathbb{R}) \text{ for some } k \ge 1\right\}.$$
This is the natural domain of dominated convergence (sublemma 8.1b).

The **finite Hayes-Möbius recovery current** at level $n$ with occupation cutoff $K$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{n,K}(f) := \sum_{\eta \in \widehat{R_n}} \widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \cdot \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f),$$
where $\widehat{h_n^f}$ are the Hayes-Möbius coefficients (Paper 11) and $\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f)$ contains the convolution factors and closed-sector projection.

The **doubly-cutoff finite Mangoldt distribution**:
$$\text{VM}_{n, K, M}(G) := \sum_{\substack{p \le p_n,\, r \le K\\ p^r \le M}} (\log p) \, G(r \log p).$$

The target:
$$\text{VM}(G) := \sum_p \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) G(r \log p) = \sum_{p^r} \Lambda(p^r) G(\log p^r).$$

## Sublemma 8.1a0: CRT isolation of the finite observable

**Statement.** *Let $R_n = \mathbb{Z}/P_n\mathbb{Z}$. Additive character orthogonality, Ramanujan sums, and Möbius inversion on the divisor lattice identify the primitive support projector used by $\text{VM}_{n,K}$ with the CRT conductor packet projector*
$$P_{H_{R_n}} = \bigoplus_{d \mid P_n,\, d > 1} P_{H_d^{(P_n)}}.$$
*After this identification, the character observables $\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}$ admit an expansion into Dirichlet characters and imprimitive character factors modulo divisors $q \mid P_n$, with local Euler factors at primes $p \mid q$ separated from coprime residue classes.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Pontryagin duality on $R_n$).* Since $R_n$ is finite abelian, $\widehat{R_n}$ is canonically isomorphic to $R_n$ via $a \leftrightarrow \chi_a$. The orthogonality relation:
$$\frac{1}{P_n} \sum_{s \in R_n} \chi_a(s) \overline{\chi_b(s)} = \mathbb{1}_{a = b}.$$
The "primitive support" of a vector $v \in \mathbb{C}^{R_n}$ is the subset of additive characters on which $v$ has nonzero Fourier coefficient. The projector $P_{H_{R_n}}$ keeps only $a \ne 0$ (excluding the gauge mode).

*Step 2 (Ramanujan sum identification).* The Ramanujan sum:
$$c_q(n) := \sum_{\substack{a \bmod q\\ \gcd(a, q) = 1}} e^{2\pi i a n / q} = \sum_{d \mid (q, n)} d \cdot \mu(q/d).$$
This identity factors the additive character indicator on conductor-$q$ characters into a Möbius-weighted sum over divisors.

For the finite cumulant observable $\text{VM}_{n,K}(G)$, we have
$$\text{VM}_{n,K}(G) = \sum_{p \le p_n} \sum_{r \le K} (\log p) G(r \log p),$$
which is expressed in terms of additive characters at prime powers $p^r$ modulo $P_n$. The decomposition by CRT:
$$\sum_{p^r \in \mathbb{Z}/P_n} (\log p) G(r \log p) = \sum_d \sum_{\substack{p^r\\ \text{cond}(p^r \bmod P_n) = d}} (\log p) G(r \log p).$$

Applying Möbius inversion on the divisor lattice $\mathcal D_{P_n} := \{d : d \mid P_n, d > 1\}$, the conductor classification separates the prime-power $p^r$ into conductor packets. For $p \le p_n$, the conductor of $p^r$ (as a residue mod $P_n$) is determined by $\gcd(p^r, P_n) = p^{\min(r, 1)}$ (since $P_n$ is squarefree). Hence the conductor of $p^r \bmod P_n$ is $P_n/p$ (a divisor of $P_n$, hence in $\mathcal D_{P_n}$).

*Step 3 (Local-global Euler factor separation).* By CRT, for each prime $p \le p_n$:
$$R_n / p R_n \cong \mathbb{F}_p, \qquad R_n[p] := \{s \in R_n : p \cdot s = 0\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.$$
The local Euler factor at $p$ in the character observable $\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}$ is the restriction to $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ (coprime to $p$) of the $\mathbb{F}_p$-component of $\eta = (\eta_p)_p$. Specifically, if $\eta_p \ne 0$, then the local factor at $p$ contributes via the Gauss-sum normalization for $\eta_p$.

The character expansion into Dirichlet characters: any character of $(\mathbb{Z}/q)^\times$ for $q \mid P_n$ pulls back via CRT to a product of local characters at each prime $p \mid q$. Each local character is a character of $(\mathbb{F}_p)^\times$ (cyclic of order $p-1$), with the principal character separated from non-principal.

After the CRT identification:
$$\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f) = \prod_{p \mid q} \phi_{\eta_p, p}^{(K)}(f),$$
with each local factor $\phi_{\eta_p, p}^{(K)}$ depending only on $f$'s behavior at the $p$-component.

This is finite algebra (CRT + Ramanujan + Möbius). No analytic input. $\square$

## Sublemma 8.1a: Occupation lift from squarefree axes

**Statement.** *Let $G_f = \widehat f$. For fixed $n$ and occupation cutoff $K$, the finite scalar observable $\mathcal{L}_{n,K}(f)$ admits the decomposition*
$$\mathcal{L}_{n,K}(f) = \sum_{\substack{p \le p_n\\ 1 \le r \le K}} (\log p) G_f(r \log p) + \mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f) + \mathcal{R}^{\text{leak}}_{n,K}(f),$$
*where:*

1. *$\mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f)$ is the mixed-axis contribution indexed by occupations involving at least two distinct prime axes;*
2. *$\mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f) = 0$ on $\mathrm{Crit}_n$, or more generally $|\mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f)| \le C_f \Lambda_n(J_n(f))$;*
3. *$\mathcal{R}^{\text{leak}}_{n,K}(f)$ is the finite leakage/projection error, vanishing in the hard packet-preserving stratum and $O_f(\mu_n^\star(\mathcal{H}_n) \mathrm{polylog}(P_n))$ in the first-order priced stratum.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Occupation classification of Hayes-Möbius recovery).* The Hayes-Möbius coefficients $\widehat{h_n^f}(\eta)$ are constructed (Paper 11) to invert the finite-modulus character expansion of the von Mangoldt distribution. For each $\eta \in \widehat{R_n}$:
$$\widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) = \sum_d \mu(P_n / d) \cdot \widehat{f}_d(\eta \bmod d),$$
the standard Möbius-inverted lift.

Each Hayes-Möbius contribution $\widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \cdot \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f)$ is classified by its **occupation pattern**: the multiset of primes $\{p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_j\}$ with $\eta_{p_i} \ne 0$.

A *single-axis* occupation has $|\{p : \eta_p \ne 0\}| = 1$ — i.e., $\eta$ is supported at exactly one prime.

A *mixed-axis* occupation has $|\{p : \eta_p \ne 0\}| \ge 2$.

*Step 2 (Single-axis occupation reduction).* For $\eta = (0, \ldots, 0, \eta_p, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with single nonzero component at prime $p$, the Hayes-Möbius coefficient and character observable collapse to:
$$\widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \cdot \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f) = (\log p) \cdot \sum_{r = 1}^K G_f(r \log p) \cdot (\text{local-factor normalization}).$$

By Paper 10's all-order conductor-convolution support theorem (the squarefree case), the local-factor normalization is unity for primitive characters of conductor $p$ (with $\eta_p$ non-principal at $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$); for non-primitive it absorbs as Möbius coefficient.

Aggregating over all single-axis occupations:
$$\sum_{\eta:\, \text{single-axis}} \widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f) = \sum_{p \le p_n} \sum_{r = 1}^K (\log p) G_f(r \log p).$$
This is the **target von Mangoldt prime-power sum**.

*Step 3 (Mixed-axis contributions on $\mathrm{Crit}_n$).* For mixed-axis $\eta$ with at least two nonzero components $\eta_{p_1}, \eta_{p_2}, \ldots$, the contribution $\widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f)$ is a **cross-prime carry term**. By Paper 32 §6.1 Lemma 6.1a (the cubic conductor selection rule at $\mathbb{Z}/6$, generalized to all primorials by §6.2A's four-thread root): cross-prime carry terms vanish on the closed sector $\mathrm{Crit}_n$ in the diagonal/closed-current normalization (Paper 22 Theorem 5.1.3).

Specifically: $\mathrm{Crit}_n$ is the kernel of the leakage form $\Lambda_n$. On this kernel, the closed-current restriction sends every mixed-axis $\eta$ to zero (via the closed-Hayes-Möbius restriction operator of Paper 11).

Therefore:
$$\mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f) := \sum_{\eta:\, \text{mixed-axis}} \widehat{h_n^f}(\eta) \Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}(f) = 0 \quad \text{on } \mathrm{Crit}_n.$$

In the *first-order priced* stratum (outside $\mathrm{Crit}_n$ but with bounded leakage), Paper 22 Theorem 5.1.3 gives $|\mathcal{M}_{n,K}(f)| \le C_f \Lambda_n(J_n(f))$ where $\Lambda_n$ is the leakage form and $J_n(f)$ is the Hayes-Möbius vector. The bound $C_f$ depends only on the test class.

*Step 4 (Leakage term).* The leakage term $\mathcal{R}^{\text{leak}}_{n,K}(f)$ is the residual after closed-sector projection. By Paper 22 Theorem 5.1.3, it satisfies:

- **Packet-preserving stratum**: $\mathcal{R}^{\text{leak}}_{n,K}(f) = 0$ identically.
- **First-order priced stratum**: $\mathcal{R}^{\text{leak}}_{n,K}(f) = O_f(\mu_n^\star(\mathcal{H}_n) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n))$, where $\mu_n^\star$ is the leakage measure (Paper 25 §3).

Combining steps 2-4 gives the decomposition. $\square$

## Sublemma 8.1b: Double-cutoff tail

**Statement.** *Work on $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp} \subset \mathcal{S}_0$: test functions $f$ with $e^{(1+\delta)|u|} G_f(u) \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$ for some $\delta > 0$. Then for every cofinal diagonal $(p_n, K_n, M_n) \to \infty$:*
$$T_n(f) := \sum_{\substack{p > p_n\\ \text{or } r > K_n\\ \text{or } p^r > M_n}} (\log p) |G_f(r \log p)| \longrightarrow 0$$
*and hence*
$$\sum_{\substack{p \le p_n\\ r \le K_n\\ p^r \le M_n}} (\log p) G_f(r \log p) \longrightarrow \sum_p \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) G_f(r \log p).$$
*Convergence is locally uniform on bounded subsets of $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Majorant by exponential decay).* For $f \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ with $|G_f(u)| \le C_f e^{-(1+\delta)|u|}$:
$$(\log p) |G_f(r \log p)| \le C_f (\log p) e^{-(1+\delta) r \log p} = C_f (\log p) p^{-r(1+\delta)}.$$

The full absolute sum:
$$\sum_p \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) |G_f(r \log p)| \le C_f \sum_p \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) p^{-r(1+\delta)} = C_f \sum_p \frac{(\log p) p^{-(1+\delta)}}{1 - p^{-(1+\delta)}}.$$

Both factors converge (the prime sum by comparison with $\sum_m (\log m) m^{-(1+\delta)}$). The total is absolutely summable.

*Step 2 (Three tail estimates).* For cutoffs $(P, K, M) \to \infty$:

**Axis tail** (primes $> P$):
$$\sum_{p > P} \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) p^{-r(1+\delta)} \le C(\delta) P^{-\delta} (\log P + 1)$$
by integral comparison.

**Occupation tail** (depth $> K$):
$$\sum_p \sum_{r > K} (\log p) p^{-r(1+\delta)} \le C(\delta) 2^{-(1+\delta)K}.$$

**Dirichlet tail** ($p^r > M$):
$$\sum_{p^r > M} (\log p) p^{-r(1+\delta)} \le C(\delta) M^{-\delta} (\log M + 1).$$

*Step 3 (Joint tail $\to 0$).* For any cofinal diagonal:
$$T_n(f) \le C_f \cdot \left[P_n^{-\delta}(\log P_n + 1) + 2^{-(1+\delta)K_n} + M_n^{-\delta}(\log M_n + 1)\right].$$
Each term $\to 0$ as $(P_n, K_n, M_n) \to \infty$.

*Step 4 (Locally uniform on bounded sets).* The constant $C_f$ depends on $f$ only through $\|G_f\|_{1, \delta} := \int e^{(1+\delta)|u|} |G_f(u)| du < \infty$. On bounded sets $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$, $C_{\mathcal{B}} := \sup_{f \in \mathcal{B}} \|G_f\|_{1, \delta} < \infty$, and the same tail bound applies uniformly.

Dominated convergence: the cutoff indicator $\mathbb{1}\{p \le P, r \le K, p^r \le M\} \to 1$ pointwise; the integrand is dominated by the summable majorant; hence the cutoff sum converges to the full sum. $\square$

## Sublemma 8.1c (Route 1): Primorial character uniformity via Siegel-Walfisz

**Statement.** *Fix $\beta > 0$. Choose the cutoff diagonal $M_n = \exp(P_n^\beta)$. Then for every primitive Dirichlet character $\chi$ of conductor $q$ with $q \mid P_n$, and every $X \le M_n$:*
$$\psi(X, \chi) := \sum_{m \le X} \Lambda(m) \chi(m) = \delta_{\chi = \chi_0} \cdot X + O\left(X \exp(-c \sqrt{\log X})\right)$$
*for some constant $c > 0$ (ineffective, dependent on $\beta$), uniformly in all such $\chi$.*

*Consequently, the finite character observable $\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K_n)}(f)$ satisfies*
$$\max_{\chi \in \mathcal X_n} \left| \sum_{m \le X} \Lambda(m) \chi(m) G_f(\log m) - \delta_{\chi=\chi_0} \int_1^X G_f(\log t) dt \right| \le X \cdot E_n(f)$$
*with $E_n(f) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, locally uniformly on $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Diagonal choice and scale relation).* With $M_n = \exp(P_n^\beta)$ and $\beta > 0$ fixed:
$$\log M_n = P_n^\beta, \qquad P_n = (\log M_n)^{1/\beta}.$$
For any $A > 1/\beta$ and any $X \in [\sqrt{M_n}, M_n]$:
$$P_n \le (\log X)^A \cdot (1 + o(1)).$$

*Step 2 (Classical Siegel-Walfisz).* The Siegel-Walfisz theorem states: for any fixed $A > 0$, there exists $c = c(A) > 0$ (ineffective) such that for every primitive Dirichlet character $\chi$ of conductor $q \le (\log X)^A$:
$$\psi(X, \chi) - \delta_{\chi = \chi_0} X = O\left(X \exp(-c \sqrt{\log X})\right),$$
uniformly in $\chi$.

By the scale relation of Step 1, this applies to every primitive character mod $q \mid P_n$ for $X \in [\sqrt{M_n}, M_n]$.

*Step 3 (Dyadic decomposition).* Decompose $[1, M_n]$ into dyadic blocks $[X/2, X]$ for $X \in \{2, 4, 8, \ldots, M_n\}$. On each block, apply Siegel-Walfisz:
$$\sum_{X/2 \le m \le X} \Lambda(m) \chi(m) = \delta_{\chi=\chi_0} \cdot (X - X/2) + O(X \exp(-c\sqrt{\log X})).$$

Summing over dyadic blocks: the total is $\delta_{\chi=\chi_0} \cdot X + O(X \exp(-c'\sqrt{\log X}))$ where $c' = c/2$ or similar geometric factor.

*Step 4 (Smooth weight $G_f$).* By partial summation on the dyadic decomposition:
$$\sum_{m \le X} \Lambda(m) \chi(m) G_f(\log m) = \int_1^X G_f(\log t) \, d\psi(t, \chi).$$
Substituting the Siegel-Walfisz expansion:
$$= \delta_{\chi=\chi_0} \int_1^X G_f(\log t) dt + O\left(\int_1^X |G_f(\log t)| \cdot t \exp(-c\sqrt{\log t}) \cdot t^{-1} dt\right) + \text{boundary},$$
where the error term is the partial-summation tail.

By the exponential decay $|G_f(u)| \le C_f e^{-(1+\delta)|u|}$:
$$\int_1^X |G_f(\log t)| \exp(-c \sqrt{\log t}) dt \le C_f \int_0^{\log X} e^{-(1+\delta)u} \exp(-c\sqrt{u}) e^u du = C_f \int_0^{\log X} e^{-\delta u} \exp(-c\sqrt{u}) du.$$
The integrand is bounded by $e^{-\delta u}$ for $u$ large; the integral converges as $X \to \infty$, giving $E_n(f) \to 0$.

*Step 5 (Uniformity on bounded subsets).* The error $E_n(f)$ depends on $f$ via $C_f = \|G_f\|_{1, \delta}$ and the Siegel-Walfisz constant $c(\beta)$. On bounded $\mathcal{B} \subset \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$, $\sup_{\mathcal{B}} C_f < \infty$, so $E_n$ is uniform on $\mathcal{B}$.

*Step 6 (Ineffectivity, and the T15 escape).* The Siegel-Walfisz constant $c$ is ineffective (due to potential Landau-Siegel zeros). For the **existence** statement of Lemma 8.1 — that the limit of the finite Mangoldt distribution equals the full $\sum \Lambda(n) G_f(\log n)$ — ineffective constants suffice. We need only $E_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$; we do not need rate control.

The cost is **huge cutoffs** $M_n = \exp(P_n^\beta)$, growing super-exponentially in $n$. **T15 Theorem 9 (Finite-to-Archimedean Completion)** asserts the completed object is uniquely determined by ANY cofinal tower, so the unnatural scale of Route 1 is irrelevant to the existence theorem. The completed limit is the same whether we use natural-scale or Siegel-Walfisz-safe cutoffs. $\square$

**Remark.** Route 2 (natural-scale diagonal, $M_n$ polynomial in $P_n$) would require a Bombieri-Vinogradov-type bound at primorial moduli or a structural primorial-moduli argument — the Polymath-8-scale obligation. With T15 in the structural toolkit, Route 1 fully suffices.

## Sublemma 8.1d: Density and topology

**Statement.** *The exponential subclass $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ is dense in $\mathcal{S}_0$ in the completed Weil test-function topology. Therefore, if Sublemmas 8.1a + 8.1b + 8.1c establish the limit identity on $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ locally uniformly on bounded subsets, the identity extends to all of $\mathcal{S}_0$ by continuity.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Definition of the Weil topology).* The completed Weil test-function topology on $\mathcal{S}_0 \subset \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by the seminorms
$$\|f\|_{(k, N)} := \sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} (1 + |x|)^N |f^{(k)}(x)| + \sup_{u \in \mathbb{R}} (1 + |u|)^N |G_f^{(k)}(u)|, \qquad k, N \ge 0,$$
modulo the constraint $f(0) = 0$ (Weil normalization). $\mathcal{S}_0$ is Frechet under this topology.

*Step 2 (Schwartz × exponential cutoff).* Given $f \in \mathcal{S}_0$, define the Gaussian cutoff:
$$f_\epsilon(x) := f(x) \cdot \exp(-\epsilon \cosh x).$$
The factor $\exp(-\epsilon \cosh x)$ is even, $\mathcal{C}^\infty$, $\le 1$, and decays super-exponentially as $|x| \to \infty$.

*Step 3 ($f_\epsilon \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$).* Compute:
$$G_{f_\epsilon}(u) = \int f_\epsilon(x) e^{-2\pi i u x} dx.$$
Since $\exp(-\epsilon \cosh x)$ has exponential decay in $|x|$ at rate $\epsilon$, the product $f \cdot \exp(-\epsilon \cosh x)$ has at least exponential decay. By Paley-Wiener applied to the Fourier transform: $G_{f_\epsilon}$ extends analytically to a strip $|\Im u| < \epsilon$, hence $|G_{f_\epsilon}(u)| \le C(\epsilon, f) e^{-\epsilon |u|}$ for large $|u|$.

For $\delta < \epsilon$: $e^{(1+\delta)|u|} G_{f_\epsilon}(u)$ has exponential decay $e^{-(\epsilon - 1 - \delta)|u|}$ (for $\epsilon > 1 + \delta$), hence is $L^1$. So $f_\epsilon \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ for $\epsilon > 1 + \delta$.

For $\epsilon \in (0, 1+\delta]$: a different construction is needed. Take instead $f_\epsilon(x) := f(x) \cdot \chi_\epsilon(x)$ where $\chi_\epsilon$ is a $\mathcal{C}^\infty$ cutoff to $[-1/\epsilon, 1/\epsilon]$. Then $f_\epsilon$ has compact support, hence $G_{f_\epsilon}$ is entire of exponential type, and $f_\epsilon \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ trivially (compact log-side support is a stronger condition than exponential decay).

*Step 4 (Convergence $f_\epsilon \to f$ in the Weil topology).* As $\epsilon \to 0$ (for the compact cutoff version):
$$\|f_\epsilon - f\|_{(k, N)} \to 0$$
because $f$ is Schwartz, so the difference $f - f_\epsilon$ is supported on $|x| > 1/\epsilon$ where $f$ has Schwartz decay. The same holds for the Fourier-transformed seminorms (by Plancherel and the rapid decay of $f$).

For the Gaussian-cutoff version: as $\epsilon \to 0$, $\exp(-\epsilon \cosh x) \to 1$ pointwise and in $\mathcal{C}^k$-norm on every compact set, with rate $\epsilon$. The Schwartz seminorms of $f - f_\epsilon$ vanish accordingly.

*Step 5 (Extension by continuity).* If the limit identity 
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathcal{L}_{n, K_n}(f) = \sum_{p^r} \Lambda(p^r) G_f(\log p^r)$$
holds for all $f \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ locally uniformly on bounded subsets, and both sides are continuous in $f$ in the Weil topology, then by density of $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ in $\mathcal{S}_0$:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathcal{L}_{n, K_n}(f) = \sum_{p^r} \Lambda(p^r) G_f(\log p^r)$$
for all $f \in \mathcal{S}_0$.

Continuity of the LHS: each $\mathcal{L}_{n, K_n}$ is a finite linear functional, continuous in $f$ via the Weil seminorms. The limit (if it exists locally uniformly) is continuous by the uniform limit theorem.

Continuity of the RHS: the absolutely convergent prime-power sum $\sum_{p^r} \Lambda(p^r) G_f(\log p^r)$ is continuous in $f$ via the $(k, N)$-seminorm bounds on $G_f$. $\square$

## Proposition 8.2: Conditional proof of Lemma 8.1 from the sublemmas

**Statement.** *Assume Sublemmas 8.1a0, 8.1a, 8.1b, 8.1c (Route 1), 8.1d. Choose the diagonal $(n, K_n, M_n) = (n, K_n, \exp(P_n^\beta))$ for some fixed $\beta > 0$ and $K_n \to \infty$. Then for every $f \in \mathcal{S}_0$:*
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathcal{L}_{n, K_n}(f) = \sum_p \sum_{r \ge 1} (\log p) G_f(r \log p) = \sum_{p^r} \Lambda(p^r) G_f(\log p^r).$$

**Proof.**

*Step 1.* Sublemma 8.1a0 makes the finite observable precise: the CRT decomposition + Ramanujan + Möbius reduces $\Phi_{\eta, n}^{(K)}$ to local-factor products.

*Step 2.* Sublemma 8.1a converts the closed Hayes-Möbius recovery current into the single-axis occupation sum, modulo mixed-axis and leakage terms. Mixed-axis vanishes on $\mathrm{Crit}_n$; leakage vanishes in the packet-preserving stratum or is $o(1)$ in the priced stratum.
