## Status

This note steps back after five adversarial probes:

- B10: current versus continuous temporal flow;
- E11: noise/structure versus continuous uncertainty;
- E12: ambiguity/sampling versus expected information gain;
- E13: capacity/tool-use versus resource-rational control;
- E14: governance/refusal versus risk/preference control.

The purpose is not to celebrate a sequence of wins. The purpose is to state
what the results force us to change.

## What Changed

The original B2 phrasing was:

\[
\text{prediction errors route into buckets.}
\]

After B10/E11/E12/E13/E14, the better phrasing is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{brains route continuous control fields through governed admissions.}
}
\]

This is a real correction. The bucket is usually not the primitive neural
variable. The primitive variable is more likely continuous:

- temporal flow;
- uncertainty;
- expected information gain;
- resource cost;
- risk/preference gradient.

The bucket is the policy decision that says whether that continuous field
should become an operation:

- admit current;
- close structure;
- preserve ambiguity;
- externalize cognition;
- refuse a competent completion.

---

## Main Interpretation

The probes repeatedly split two jobs that the early bucket table blended:

1. **Intensity/drive.** How much should the organism update, attend, sample,
   inhibit, externalize, or expend effort?
2. **Admission/route.** Should this evidence be allowed to become a durable
   operation?

Continuous rivals usually win the first job. Gates usually win the second.

That matters for the brain hypothesis. It suggests animal brains should not be
reverse-engineered as classifiers over prediction-error types. They should be
reverse-engineered as layered systems:

\[
\text{continuous evidence fields} \rightarrow \text{admission gates} \rightarrow \text{operations}.
\]

The early bucket taxonomy remains useful, but as the operational surface, not
the substrate.

---

## Why This Is Not Just a Victory

The results are partly generated by the design of the falsifiers. Each probe
creates:

- a continuous variable that should explain graded effort;
- a bait or null condition that punishes naive admission;
- a held-out persistence signal that protects admission.

So the consistency is not biological evidence by itself. It is evidence that
the revised architecture is coherent and more falsifiable than the original
bucket story.

The strongest honest statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{the synthetic program falsifies primitive buckets more than it confirms them.}
}
\]

What survives is not "nine buckets in the brain." What survives is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{admission-governed use of continuous evidence.}
}
\]

---

## What This Implies About Human Brains

If this program is pointing in the right direction, the human brain is not a
single prediction-error minimizer and not a discrete bucket router. It is a
multi-layer admission system.

The plausible division of labor is:

| Layer | Brain-like role |
|---|---|
| continuous field | prediction error, uncertainty, flow, value, cost, risk |
| persistence test | whether the signal generalizes beyond local fluctuation |
| admission gate | whether the signal may enter action, learning, attention, report, or refusal |
| operation | update, sample, close, externalize, inhibit, recalibrate |
| conscious access | terminal governed admission under capacity and report constraints |

This fits the T17/T18/T19 stack better than the original bucket language:

- T17 supplies packet-resolved active inference and route variables.
- T18 supplies lawful admission/calibration.
- T19 supplies governed terminal access.
- The B-series now supplies a reverse-engineering hypothesis: find continuous
  neural fields and the gates that admit them into operations.

---

## The New Core Claim

The revised B-series core claim should be:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{animal brains implement governed active boundary inference}\\
\text{by converting continuous control fields into admitted operations.}
\end{array}
}
\]

This is stronger than a metaphor and weaker than a biological proof. It gives
us a concrete experimental target.

For each candidate "bucket", ask:

1. What continuous field explains graded response?
2. What held-out or persistent evidence controls false admission?
3. What neural or behavioral gate mediates the transition from field to
   operation?
4. What perturbation breaks admission without destroying the underlying field?

---

## Remaining Risks

1. **Synthetic-feature leakage.** Held-out persistence is explicitly provided
   to the gate models. Real organisms may not have such clean variables.
2. **Model class weakness.** The continuous rivals are strong but still simple.
   A better continuous active-inference model might absorb the gate.
3. **No neural anchoring yet.** These are behavioral/computational probes, not
   recordings or perturbations.
4. **Too-consistent generator grammar.** B10-E14 share the same falsifier
   grammar, so the pattern may reflect our experimental design more than the
   brain.
5. **Consciousness not yet tested.** T19 predicts terminal governed admission,
   but these probes mostly test operation routing, not conscious report.

These risks do not kill the program. They specify the next phase.

---

## Next Phase

The next phase should stop asking whether individual buckets can be rescued.
It should test the architecture itself:

\[
\text{field} \rightarrow \text{persistence} \rightarrow \text{gate} \rightarrow \text{operation}.
\]

A good next experiment would combine multiple domains in one agent:

- uncertainty;
- EIG;
- resource cost;
- risk;
- tool persistence;
- governance persistence;
- terminal report capacity.

The target is not higher synthetic accuracy. The target is a failure mode:

\[
\text{can one learned continuous policy absorb all gates without loss?}
\]

If yes, the B-series becomes a convenient engineering decomposition. If no,
the governed-admission architecture earns independent bite.

---

## Current Bottom Line

The B-series should now retire the naive slogan:

\[
\text{a brain is a prediction-error bucket router.}
\]

and replace it with:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a brain is a governed admission system for continuous control fields.}
}
\]

That is the cleaner reverse-engineering hypothesis for animal brains, including
human brains.
