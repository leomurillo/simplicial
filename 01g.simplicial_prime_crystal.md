## Abstract

Give the $N$ primes $2,3,\dots,p_N$ the $N$ unit vectors of the simplicial frame, and place each integer $n$ at its exponent vector $e(n)=(v_2(n),v_3(n),\dots)$. Distances use the frame metric of Paper 01, $\langle a,b\rangle_N=\tfrac{1}{N-1}\big(N\,a\cdot b-\textstyle\sum a\sum b\big)$. The frame size grows with $n$: a new frame vector is born at every prime. We call the resulting object the **prime crystal**, and the polygon $1\to2\to3\to\cdots$ the **$+1$ walk**.

**The space.** The frame is the *valuation space* $\mathcal V_N=\mathbb Z^N/\mathbb Z\mathbf 1$. The valuation map turns multiplication into translation. Its kernel is generated by the primorial $P_N=p_1\cdots p_N$, so $1$ and $P_N$ are the same point (the **primorial gauge**). $\mathcal V_N$ is the lattice $A_{N-1}^*$: triangular for $N=3$ (the genesis grid) and body-centred cubic for $N=4$. Primes are the weights of the defining representation of $SU(N)$, the co-primes $P_N/p$ are the weights of its conjugate, and $P_N$ is the determinant. Inside the window $n<p_{N+1}$ every integer is in standard form, and **divisibility is a side of a wall**: $p\mid n$ exactly when $\langle e(n),e_p\rangle>0$. The chamber of $n$ is therefore labelled by its radical.

**The $+1$ steps.** A step joins two disjoint faces of the prime simplex. Its vector is the superparticular ratio $(n+1)/n$, and its length and its angle at $1$ are exact functions of $\Omega$ and $Q=\sum v_p^2$. Each step lives in the sublattice of the primes dividing $n(n+1)$. By Størmer's theorem every sublattice is used finitely often: the last steps of the $\{2,3\}$, $\{2,3,5\}$, $\{2,\dots,7\}$ sublattices are $8\to9$, $80\to81$, $4374\to4375$. In the era of each prime exactly two steps involve it, the step into it and the step out of it. The remaining steps of the era live in older sublattices.

**Rigidity theorems.**
- From the plane on, no two steps are ever parallel.
- Every turn of the walk is strictly obtuse.
- Every point of the window has positive cubic chirality once $N\ge5$, so the crystal lies in the "numbers" half of the frame and the reciprocals fill the other half.
- The walk folds on the line and tangles three times in the plane (the step $5\to6$ is a diameter through $1$). From the birth of $7$ on it is **self-avoiding forever**. The proof turns on the logarithm: $\ln n$ is a strictly monotone height on the un-gauged walk, and Bonse's inequality $P_N>p_{N+1}^2$ makes the gauge quotient injective on the walk.

**Prime birth as un-gauging.** When $p_{N+1}$ is born, the old gauge direction becomes the new axis. The old primorial moves from the origin to the exact antipode of the newborn prime, and every old integer drops to height $-\Omega(m)/N$ along the new axis. The mass $\Omega$, invisible in frame $N$, becomes geometry. The metric changes by the exact rule $\langle a,b\rangle_{N+1}-\langle a,b\rangle_N=(\Omega_a\Omega_b-a\cdot b)/(N(N-1))$. In $SU$ language, the old singlet $\Lambda^N$ becomes the antifundamental of $SU(N+1)$. Each rigidity property is carried from $N$ to $N+1$ by one classical prime inequality: Bertrand, Bonse, or a Bonse-type bound.

**Shape and statistics.**
- The integers up to $x$ are exactly the lattice points of a log-simplex whose spike along $p$ has length $\log_px$.
- The farthest node from $1$ is $2^{\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor}$, and the diameter joins the $2$- and $3$-spike tips.
- The centroid is the point of $(n!)^{1/n}$, and it converges to distance $\big(\sum_p(p-1)^{-2}\big)^{1/2}=1.172632$.
- The mean position correlation of $n$ and $n+j$ is an explicit additive function $c(j)$ of the gap. Consecutive integers therefore form, on average, a *regular simplex* (the frame's own shape) distorted only by $c$. The mean turn tends to $120^\circ$, as $-\tfrac12-\tfrac34/\overline Q$.

**The rational world and continuity.**
- Every $p_N$-smooth rational is a lattice point: an integer divided by a power of the primorial. The crystal never densifies.
- The continuum appears only in the archimedean shadow $\ln q$, and in frame $N$ it densifies at the Weyl rate $R^{N-1}$. The finest resolutions of frames $2,3,4$ are the commas of just intonation.
- Farey neighbours differ by exactly one $+1$ step, so continued fractions are journeys of $+1$ steps.
- By Størmer's theorem, every irrational's journey leaves every frame: its convergents involve infinitely many primes.
- Continuity is therefore reached by going *up* in dimension (prime births, exponential growth), not *out* in distance (polynomial growth within a frame).

**Collatz.** The shortcut Collatz map is a $\{2,3\}$ dynamics on the crystal: halving and tripling are translations, and the "+1" is a superparticular step.
- Its inverse tree is exactly binary on $\mathbb Z[1/3]$. The integer tree branches exactly where the $-1$ step enters the $3$-chamber, and $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ drains into $\mathbb Z$ forward.
- A given cycle closes for free at every finite place: it is a chain of superparticular ratios multiplying to a $3$-limit comma. Whether a parity word has an integer cycle at all is a divisibility at the primes of $2^K-3^m$.
- The four cycles with denominator $1$ are the four Størmer pairs of the line frame. The trivial cycle is the step $3\to4$, whose comma $4/3$ also sets the mean descent.
- The cycle conjecture is equivalent to one finite check per prime birth, and it holds through the frame of $23$.
- On the rationals with odd denominator, the $3$-coordinate is attracted to $0$, and the remaining denominator is a conserved charge whose fibre is the cousin world $3n+d$. Every primitive parity word has exactly one rational cycle, and the integers are the charge-$1$ fibre.
- The Krasikov–Lagarias difference inequalities are a worst-case-lift Perron problem (as found independently by Allikvere and by Nikpour–Rabbani, who certify $x^{0.9149}$). Their averaged identity is the coverage equation, with roots $1$ and $2$. The exponents increase strictly with the modulus, a question left open in 2003. An exact formula gives the gain from one level to the next, and any level-uniform lower bound on that gain in terms of the lift loss would give $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$. Through modulus $3^{14}$ the gain is at least quadratic in the lift loss.
- A positive orbit that is not eventually periodic involves infinitely many primes. Repeating an expanding block consumes $2$-adic precision at an exactly computable rate.
- **The fair game.** One Syracuse step multiplies size by $3\cdot2^{-a}$ with probability $2^{-a}$. Its geometric mean is $\tfrac34$ (descent), and its arithmetic mean is exactly $1$ (fairness), because $\tfrac14+\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{64}+\cdots=\tfrac13$. Through the product formula these are the two thresholds of the $3$-adic Syracuse law: descent is its entropy excess, which (by Tao's mixing theorem) gives it a density, and fairness is its collision entropy, which we show denies that density square-integrability. The proof is an exact identity: two backward histories agree modulo $3^n$ only as *twins* (equal first steps) or as *siblings*, whose tails differ by the sibling map $m\mapsto4m+1$. For an odd multiplier $q\ge5$ the analogous law is singular, so $3$ is the only one with a density, and there the density sits exactly on the $L^2$ threshold. Both thresholds are the two roots of the coverage equation, which come from the Størmer pair $(3,4)$. The averaged Krasikov–Lagarias family crosses the same $L^2$ threshold exactly at its target exponent $1$. Through Tao's theorem, the bound $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ reduces to one comparison at each level. The Krasikov–Lagarias vector must lose at its top digit no more than the Syracuse law does. The data hold this with a factor of $2.4$ to spare, and the exponent deficit $1-\gamma_k$ tracks the Syracuse lift loss through modulus $3^{14}$.

All statements are theorems about factorization, re-encoded geometrically; none uses or implies anything about the distribution of primes beyond the classical inequalities it cites. A certificate checks every computational claim (Appendix A).

### 1.1 The construction {#sec:1-1}

The construction is the genesis author's. Start with $1$ at the origin. When $2$ appears it becomes the first ray, and $2$ sits on it at unit distance. The **$+1$ walk** is the polygon through $e(1),e(2),e(3),\dots$, with each step drawn in the frame current at its endpoint.

### 1.4 Notation {#sec:1-4}

- **Primes and factorizations.** $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ are the primes, $\pi(x)$ counts them, and $P_N=p_1\cdots p_N$. For $n\ge1$: $v_p(n)$ is the $p$-adic valuation, $e(n)=(v_{p_1}(n),\dots,v_{p_N}(n))$, $\Omega(n)=\sum_pv_p(n)$, $\omega(n)$ is the number of distinct prime factors, and $Q(n)=\sum_pv_p(n)^2$. $\operatorname{rad}(n)$ is the radical and $P^+(n)$ the largest prime factor.
- **The frame.** $\langle a,b\rangle_N=\tfrac1{N-1}(N\,a\cdot b-\sum_ia_i\sum_jb_j)=a^\top G_Nb$ with $G_N=\tfrac{1}{N-1}(NI-J)$ is the metric of Paper 01, Prop. 2.1, valid in every gauge (01e, Thm. 2.1). $e_p$ denotes the frame vector of $p$, and $|x|_N^2=\langle x,x\rangle_N$.
- **Window and home frame.** The *window* of frame $N$ is $W_N=\{1,\dots,p_{N+1}-1\}$. The *home frame* of $n\ge3$ is $N=\pi(n)$, and the walk step $n\to n+1$ is drawn in frame $\max(2,\pi(n+1))$.
- **Steps and turns.** The step is $\Delta_n=e(n+1)-e(n)$. The *turn* at $n$ is the angle between $\Delta_{n-1}$ and $\Delta_n$.

## 2. The valuation space {#sec:2}

**Definition 2.1.** The *valuation space* of frame $N$ is $\mathcal V_N=\mathbb Z^N/\mathbb Z\mathbf 1$ with the metric $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_N$. The *valuation map* sends a positive $p_N$-smooth rational $r$ to the class of $e(r)\in\mathbb Z^N$.

The valuation map is a group homomorphism from multiplication to addition, $e(rs)=e(r)+e(s)$. Its kernel is $P_N^{\mathbb Z}$. **The displacement from $m$ to $k$ is $e(k/m)$**, the valuation vector of the ratio, so distances are invariant under multiplication: $|e(km)-e(lm)|=|e(k)-e(l)|$. In this sense the geometry is logarithmic. It takes one logarithm per prime (the valuation $v_p$, base-free) and keeps all of them. The ordinary logarithm is one linear functional of these (Proposition 2.4).

**Proposition 2.2 (the lattice; classical, 01e Prop. 4.5).**
- $\mathcal V_N$ is the weight lattice of $SU(N)$, isometric to $A_{N-1}^*$ scaled so that the frame vectors have unit length. It is the triangular lattice for $N=3$ (the genesis grid) and the body-centred cubic lattice for $N=4$.
- The primes are the weights of the defining representation. The co-primes $P_N/p$ sit at $-e_p$, the weights of the conjugate representation.
- The primorial is the determinant, $e(P_N)=\mathbf 1\equiv0$.
- The genesis massaction of $e(n)$ is the largest $j$ with $P_N^j\mid n$, and the standard form of $n$ is $n/P_N^{j}$.
- Sorted standard forms are Young diagrams, which here are the *prime signatures*, and $\Omega(n)\bmod N$ is the $N$-ality.
- The unit shell of the lattice is the $2N$ points $\{p\}\cup\{P_N/p\}$: the hexagon $2,3,5,15,10,6$ for $N=3$, and for $N=4$ the cube $2,3,5,7,105,70,42,30$, two interpenetrating tetrahedra.

**Proposition 2.3 (window faithfulness).** For $N\ge2$, every $n\in W_N$ has massaction $0$. Hence $n\mapsto e(n)$ is injective on $W_N$, and the standard form of the point is the exponent vector itself.

*Proof.* By Bertrand's postulate $p_{N+1}<2p_N\le P_N$ for $N\ge2$, so $P_N\nmid n$ for $n<p_{N+1}$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.4 (size is a hidden linear field).** Put $\bar\ell_N=\tfrac1N\ln P_N$ and $f_N=\tfrac{N-1}{N}(\ln p-\bar\ell_N)_{p\le p_N}$, a zero-sum vector. For every $p_N$-smooth $n$ in standard form,
$$
\ln n=\bar\ell_N\,\Omega(n)+\langle e(n),f_N\rangle_N .
$$
The second term is gauge-invariant; the first is the mass $\Omega$, which the frame does not see.

*Proof.* $\langle e(n),f_N\rangle_N=\tfrac N{N-1}e(n)\cdot f_N$ because $\sum f_N=0$, and this equals $\sum_pv_p(n)(\ln p-\bar\ell_N)=\ln n-\bar\ell_N\Omega(n)$. $\square$

So the ordinary logarithm is the *archimedean shadow* of the valuation vector: the product formula $\ln n=\sum_pv_p(n)\ln p$ read as a linear functional. The frame keeps every finite place with unit weight and forgets the archimedean weights $\ln p$.

**Proposition 2.5 (symmetry and its invariants).**
- For $N\ge3$ the isometry group of $\mathcal V_N$ fixing $0$ is $S_N\times\{\pm1\}$ [Conway–Sloane]. $S_N$ relabels the primes, and $-1$ sends $n$ to $1/n\equiv P_N^{k}/n$.
- The $S_N$-orbit of a point is its Young diagram, i.e. its prime signature. Under $-1$ a diagram goes to its complement, the conjugate representation.
- All distances and angles among points are functions of joint signature data. In particular $|e(n)|_N$ depends only on the signature of $n$:
$$
|e(n)|_N^2=Q(n)-\frac{2\,\varpi(n)}{N-1},\qquad \varpi(n)=\sum_{p<q}v_p(n)v_q(n).
$$
- The size field of Proposition 2.4 breaks $S_N$. A transposition $\tau=(p\,q)$ changes the logarithm by $\ln(\tau n)-\ln n=(v_q(n)-v_p(n))\ln(p/q)$.

Thus swapping two primes is a symmetry of the geometry, and its conserved quantities are the orbit labels (the prime signatures). The radius is the first of these, of degree two, and the cubic chirality of Section 10 is the next, of degree three. The arithmetic (which point is which number, and in what order the walk visits them) lives entirely in the symmetry-breaking field $\ln p$.

## 3. Divisibility is a side of a wall {#sec:3}

**Theorem 3.1.** Let $N\ge2$ and $2\le n\in W_N$. For every $p\le p_N$,
$$
\langle e(n),e_p\rangle_N=\frac{N\,v_p(n)-\Omega(n)}{N-1},
$$
which is positive when $p\mid n$ and equals $-\Omega(n)/(N-1)<0$ when $p\nmid n$. Consequently the $N$ hyperplanes $e_p^\perp$ cut $\mathcal V_N\otimes\mathbb R$ into $2^N-2$ open chambers, labelled by the nonempty proper subsets of the primes. Every $n\in W_N$, $n\ge2$, lies in the chamber labelled $\operatorname{rad}(n)$.

*Proof.* The formula is the definition of the metric. If $p\mid n$ then $Nv_p(n)-\Omega(n)\ge N-\Omega(n)$. For $N\ge3$, $p_{N+1}\le2^N$ (by induction from $p_4=7<8$, using Bertrand's postulate), so $\Omega(n)\le\log_2n<N$. The cases $N=2$, $n\le4$, are checked directly. The chambers are the sign vectors of $(\langle x,e_p\rangle)_p$. All of them except all-positive and all-negative occur, because $\sum_pe_p=0$, and the point $\mathbf 1_S$ realizes the sign vector of $S$. $\square$

The genesis notes label a region by its absent coordinate. Theorem 3.1 says that, inside the window, the absent coordinates of $n$ are exactly the primes not dividing it, and they are visible as the negative sides of the walls.

Outside the window the statement can fail. $162=2\cdot3^4$ is even, but in frame $3$ it lies on the odd side of the $2$-wall, because $3\cdot1-5<0$.

## 4. The +1 steps {#sec:4}

**Theorem 4.1 (step geometry).** For $n\ge1$ and every frame $N\ge\max(2,\pi(n+1))$:
1. $e(n)$ and $e(n+1)$ have disjoint supports, and
$$
|\Delta_n|_N^2=\frac{N\big(Q(n)+Q(n+1)\big)-\big(\Omega(n+1)-\Omega(n)\big)^2}{N-1}.
$$
2. $\langle e(n),e(n+1)\rangle_N=-\Omega(n)\Omega(n+1)/(N-1)$. For $n\ge2$ the triangle $1,n,n+1$ is therefore obtuse at $1$. Its angle there tends to $90^\circ$ as $N\to\infty$.
3. $\Delta_n=e\big((n+1)/n\big)$ is the valuation vector of a superparticular ratio.
4. Inside the window, the step crosses exactly $\omega(n)+\omega(n+1)$ walls, always including the $2$-wall. It joins the chambers of two disjoint radicals. The $2$-coordinate of the walk is the ruler sequence $v_2(n)$, so the $2$-component of the step alternates in sign.

*Proof.* $\gcd(n,n+1)=1$ gives disjoint supports. Then (1) and (2) are the metric applied to vectors with disjoint supports, (3) is the homomorphism property, and (4) follows from Theorem 3.1. $\square$

**Definition 4.2.** The *height* of the step $n\to n+1$ is $h(n)=\pi\big(P^+(n(n+1))\big)$. It is the smallest frame containing both endpoints, the sublattice the step lives in. The step is *native* if $h(n)$ equals the walk frame $\pi(n+1)$.

**Theorem 4.3 (sublattice lifetimes and native steps).**
- (a) **Finite lifetimes.** For each $N$ only finitely many steps have height at most $N$. By Størmer's theorem there are finitely many pairs of consecutive $p_N$-smooth integers. Their numbers for $N=1,\dots,9$ are $1,4,10,23,40,68,108,167,241$, and the last such steps are at $n=1,8,80,4374,9800,123200,336140,11859210,11859210$ (certified, G7, in agreement with Lehmer's lists). After its last step a sublattice is never used again.
- (b) **Native steps.** In the era of $p_N$, the steps $n\to n+1$ with $p_N\le n+1<p_{N+1}$ for $N\ge2$, exactly two steps are native: the *birth step* $p_N-1\to p_N$ and the *exit step* $p_N\to p_N+1$. Every other step of the era lives in a strictly older sublattice.

*Proof of (b).* A native step has $p_N\mid n(n+1)$. Since $2p_N>p_{N+1}$ (Bertrand), the only multiple of $p_N$ in the era is $p_N$ itself. Every other integer of the era is composite, with all prime factors at most half of it, hence below $p_N$. $\square$

Hence the native fraction near $x$ is about $2/\ln x$; it is $0.1212$ on $[10^7,2\cdot10^7)$. Between two primes the walk lives entirely in older sublattices, and it touches the newest prime only at the two ends of its era.

**Observation 4.4 (records).** Every record-length step below $2\cdot10^6$ has $n$ or $n+1$ a power of $2$ (certified, G8). The records include the Catalan step $8\to9$, the steps into $2^k$ from the Mersenne primes $31,127,8191,131071,524287$, and $65535\to65536$ with $65535=3\cdot5\cdot17\cdot257$, the product of the four Fermat primes above $3$. The record length is about $\log_2n$.

## 5. Alignment {#sec:5}

**Lemma 5.1a.** Let $a,b\in\mathbb Z^N$ and suppose some coordinate vanishes in both. If $a$ and $b$ are parallel in $\mathcal V_N$, then $a=\lambda b$ as vectors of $\mathbb Z^N$.

*Proof.* Parallel in $\mathcal V_N$ means $a-\lambda b=k\mathbf 1$ for some real $k$. The common zero coordinate forces $k=0$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.1 (no parallel steps).** For $N\ge3$, no two distinct steps of the window $W_N$ are parallel or antiparallel in frame $N$. The same holds in every later frame.

*Proof.* Suppose $\Delta_n=\lambda\Delta_m$ as integer vectors, with $n\ne m$, $\lambda=a/b$ in lowest terms.
- **$\lambda>0$.** Comparing positive parts gives $(n+1)^b=(m+1)^a$ and $n^b=m^a$. So $n=t^a$, $m=t^b$, $n+1=s^a$, $m+1=s^b$ with $s>t\ge1$. Then $s^a-t^a=1=s^b-t^b$ forces $a=b=1$, so $n=m$.
- **$\lambda<0$.** Comparing parts gives $(n+1)^b=m^a$ and $n^b=(m+1)^a$, which is impossible since $n<n+1$ forces $m+1<m$.

By Lemma 5.1a this settles every pair with a common zero coordinate. A pair without one needs every prime up to $p_N$ to divide $n(n+1)m(m+1)<p_{N+1}^4$. That is impossible for $N\ge7$: $P_7=510510>19^4$, and the inductive step $p_{N+2}^4<16p_{N+1}^4\le p_{N+1}^5$ holds since $p_{N+1}\ge16$. The frames $3\le N\le6$ are checked exhaustively (G3, extended to $N\le11$). Later frames add a coordinate on which every old step vanishes. $\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (own-frame ray alignments).** Seen in its own frame $N=h(n)$, a step is parallel to the ray of $p$ exactly when one of its endpoints is $p^j$ and the other is $(P_N/p)^k$, that is, when $p^j\pm1=(P_N/p)^k$.
- For $N=3$ the solutions are exactly $16=15+1$ (ray $2$), $9=10-1$ (ray $3$) and $5=6-1$ (ray $5$). That gives one aligned step per prime, $15\to16$, $9\to10$, $5\to6$, using $30=2\cdot15=3\cdot10=5\cdot6$; also $1\to2$ lies on the $2$-ray trivially.
- For $4\le N\le2000$ there are none.
- Among all Størmer steps, the only other own-frame parallel classes for $N\le5$ are $\{1\to2,\ 15\to16\}$.

*Proof.* If any coordinate other than $p$ vanishes on the step, Lemma 5.1a forces the step to be a multiple of $e_p$, so $n=1$. Otherwise all coordinates other than $p$ are equal, which gives the stated form. For $j,k\ge2$, Mihăilescu's theorem leaves only $3^2-2^3$, which occurs only for $N=2$. For $j=1$, $p<P_N/p-1$ once $N\ge4$. The case $k=1$ is certified for $N\le2000$ (G3). $\square$

In the walk itself, only $5\to6$ keeps its alignment. The steps $9\to10$ and $15\to16$ occur in frames $4$ and $6$, after the births of $7$ and $13$ have removed the gauge freedom that aligned them (Lemma 5.1a). *(Rhyme, fenced: the three ratios $16/15$, $10/9$, $6/5$ are the just semitone, the minor whole tone and the minor third of five-limit tuning.)*

## 6. Turns {#sec:6}

**Theorem 6.1 (every turn is obtuse).** For $n\ge2$ put $A_n=v_2(n-1)\,v_2(n+1)$ if $n$ is odd and $A_n=0$ if $n$ is even. Then, in every frame $N\ge\max(2,\pi(n+1))$,
$$
(N-1)\,\langle\Delta_{n-1},\Delta_n\rangle_N=-N\big(Q(n)+A_n\big)+\big(\Omega(n)-\Omega(n-1)\big)\big(\Omega(n)-\Omega(n+1)\big)<0 .
$$
As $N\to\infty$ the inner product tends to $-(Q(n)+A_n)$.

*Proof.* We have $e(n-1)\cdot e(n)=e(n)\cdot e(n+1)=0$, and $e(n-1)\cdot e(n+1)=A_n$ because $\gcd(n-1,n+1)\mid2$; expanding gives the identity.
- **Negativity for $N\ge100$.** The second term is at most $(\log_2p_{N+1})^2$ and the first is at most $-N$. Since $N>(\log_2p_{N+1})^2$ for all $N\ge100$ (Rosser's bound for $p_{N+1}$; G4 confirms it up to $10^5$), the sum is negative. The remaining home frames contain only $n<p_{100}=541$, which the certificate covers within its range $n<10^7$.
- **Later frames.** The inner product moves monotonically, since $f(N+1)-f(N)=(X-Y)/(N(N-1))$ with $X=Q(n)+A_n$ and $Y$ the second term. So it stays between its home value and its negative limit. $\square$

The closest approach to $90^\circ$ below $10^7$ is $92.55^\circ$, at $n=4109374=2\cdot2054687$, between $3^7\cdot1879$ and $5^6\cdot263$: a vertex of small $Q$ flanked by powerful neighbours. At an odd prime vertex the limiting inner product is $-(1+k)$, where $k$ is the $2$-adic valuation of whichever of $p\pm1$ is divisible by $4$.

**Theorem 6.3 (position kernel).** For $j\ge1$, the mean of $e(n)\cdot e(n+j)$ over $n\le x$ tends to
$$
c(j)=\sum_{p\mid j}\big(1-p^{-v_p(j)}\big)\frac{p+1}{(p-1)^2},
$$
an additive function of the gap $j$. For example $c(1)=0$, $c(2)=\tfrac32$, $c(3)=\tfrac23$, $c(4)=\tfrac94$, $c(6)=\tfrac{13}6$.

*Proof.* Only primes dividing $j$ can divide both $n$ and $n+j$. Fix such a $p$. The integers with $p^i\mid n$ and $p^k\mid n+j$ have density $p^{-\max(i,k)}$ when $p^{\min(i,k)}\mid j$, and none otherwise. So the mean of $v_p(n)v_p(n+j)$ is $\sum_{\min(i,k)\le v_p(j)}p^{-\max(i,k)}$. This is the full sum $(p+1)/(p-1)^2$ minus its shifted tail, $p^{-v_p(j)}(p+1)/(p-1)^2$. $\square$

**Corollary 6.4 (consecutive integers form a regular simplex; mean turn).** Let $\overline Q(x)$ be the mean of $Q$ near $x$. Then $\overline Q(x)=\ln\ln x+B+o(1)$, where the routine normal-order computation gives $B=M+\sum_p\frac{3p-1}{p(p-1)^2}=3.785$, with $M$ the Mertens constant. The measured value is $\overline Q=6.524$ on $[2\cdot10^6,4\cdot10^6)$, against $\ln\ln(3\cdot10^6)+B=6.487$; the difference is of the order of $1/\ln x$. In the limit $N\to\infty$ the mean squared distance between $n$ and $n+j$ is $2(\overline Q-c(j))$. So the points $n,n+1,\dots,n+k$ form, on average, a regular $k$-simplex with edge $\sqrt{2\overline Q}$, distorted only by the bounded kernel $c$: **the integers rebuild the frame's own shape.** For $k=2$ the triangle is equilateral up to $c(2)=\tfrac32$, and the ratio of mean turn inner product to mean squared step is
$$
\frac{C_1}{C_0}=-\frac12-\frac{c(2)}{2\overline Q}=-\frac12-\frac{3}{4\,\overline Q}\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac12,
$$
so the mean turn tends to $120^\circ$, slowly, like $1/\ln\ln x$. The measured mean angle is $129.2^\circ$ on $[10^7,2\cdot10^7)$.

## 7. Prime birth {#sec:7}

**Theorem 7.1 (birth is un-gauging).** Let $p=p_{N+1}$ be born, and work in frame $N+1$.
- (a) **The old gauge becomes the new axis.** $e(P_N)=-e_p$, at distance $1$ from the origin. The old gauge fibre unfolds along the new axis: $e(kP_N^j)=e(k)-j\,e_p$. In frame $N$, $1$ and $P_N$ were one point; at the birth of $p$, $P_N$ moves to the antipode of $p$.
- (b) **Mass becomes visible.** For every $m$ coprime to $p$, in particular every $m\in W_N$, $\langle e(m),e_p\rangle_{N+1}=-\Omega(m)/N$. The old window lies in layers by $\Omega$ below the newborn, which sits at height $+1$.
- (c) **The metric update.** For old vectors $a,b$,
$$
\langle a,b\rangle_{N+1}-\langle a,b\rangle_N=\frac{\Omega_a\Omega_b-a\cdot b}{N(N-1)} .
$$
- (d) **In $SU$ language.** $\Lambda^N\mathbb C^{N+1}\cong(\mathbb C^{N+1})^*\otimes\det$: the old determinant (singlet) is the anti-weight of the new prime.

*Proof.* (a) is the balancing identity $\sum_{i\le N+1}e_{p_i}=0$ of Paper 01. (b) and (c) follow from the metric, the latter checked symbolically (G6). (d) is classical. $\square$

**Corollary 7.2 (what a birth does to the crystal).**
1. **Radii inflate** by $2\varpi(m)/(N(N-1))$. Primes and prime powers do not move.
2. **The prime simplex contracts:** its edge$^2$ goes from $2N/(N-1)$ to $2(N+1)/N$, while its circumradius stays $1$.
3. **A step stretches exactly when** $(\Omega(n+1)-\Omega(n))^2>Q(n)+Q(n+1)$ and contracts otherwise. The first stretching step is $59\to60$; about $22\%$ of steps stretch near $10^6$ (G14).
4. **Turn inner products** move monotonically toward their negative limits (proof of Theorem 6.1).
5. **Gauge coincidences end.** Every gauge-dependent coincidence of frame $N$ (alignments, crossings) is destroyed, because the old points share the new zero coordinate (Lemma 5.1a).

**Theorem 7.3 (the induction step).** Each rigidity property of the crystal passes from frame $N$ to frame $N+1$ by one classical inequality:

| Property (window $W_N$) | Holds for | Carried by |
|---|---|---|
| Faithful standard form (Prop. 2.3) | $N\ge2$ | $P_N\ge2p_N>p_{N+1}$ (Bertrand) |
| Walls $=$ divisibility (Thm. 3.1) | $N\ge2$ | $p_{N+1}\le2^N$ (Bertrand) |
| Exactly two native steps per era (Thm. 4.3) | $N\ge2$ | $2p_N>p_{N+1}$ (Bertrand) |
| No parallel steps (Thm. 5.1) | $N\ge3$ | $P_N>p_{N+1}^4$ for $N\ge7$ (Bonse-type) |
| Every turn obtuse (Thm. 6.1) | all $N$ | monotone update; $N>(\log_2p_{N+1})^2$ for $N\ge100$ |
| Self-avoidance (Thm. 9.1) | $N\ge4$ | $P_N>p_{N+1}^2$ (Bonse) |
| Positive chirality (Thm. 10.2) | $N\ge5$ | $N>3\log_2p_{N+1}$ for $N\ge20$ |

*Proof.* Each row is proved in the cited theorem. In each case the frame-$N$ statement is a statement about a window of integers, and the inequality is what keeps the window inside the regime of the proof when the next prime is born. $\square$

**Remark 7.4 (the birth ladder is the Helmert ladder; the metric trick).**
- *The ladder.* [T4] descends an $N$-slot ledger by recording its mean and dropping the last slot. It climbs back by appending the balancing coordinate. The coordinates this produces are the unnormalized Helmert balances $h_k=(1,\dots,1,-k,0,\dots,0)$, with $k$ leading ones.
- *Helmert balances are primorial-to-newborn ratios.* In the crystal, with the primes in their natural order, $h_k$ is the exponent vector of the rational $P_k/p_{k+1}^{\,k}$:
$$
h_1=e(\tfrac23),\quad h_2=e(\tfrac6{25}),\quad h_3=e(\tfrac{30}{343}),\quad h_4=e(\tfrac{210}{14641}),\ \dots
$$
- *Climbing the ladder is the sequence of births.* By Theorem 7.1(a), in frame $k+1$ this point is $-(k+1)\,e_{p_{k+1}}$. So the Helmert vector of rung $k$ is the axis of the prime born at that rung, reversed and scaled by $k+1$.
- *Ladder coordinates are visible mass.* The ladder coordinates of $n$ are $m_k(n)=\big(\Omega_{\le k}(n)-k\,v_{p_{k+1}}(n)\big)/(k(k+1))$, where $\Omega_{\le k}$ counts the prime factors among $p_1,\dots,p_k$. This is the mass made visible at each birth (Theorem 7.1(b)). The last coordinate, $m_N=\Omega(n)/N$, is the gauge.
- *Orthogonality.* The $h_k$ are mutually orthogonal in every frame $N>k$, with $\langle h_j,h_k\rangle_N=N\,k(k+1)\,\delta_{jk}/(N-1)$.
- *The metric trick.* Use the exact-arithmetic metric $M_N=NI-J=(N-1)G_N$ of [Paper 01, §7.5] and [T4, §2], the convention $M=4I-J$ of Quadray implementations [Thomson]. Then every inner product in the crystal is an integer, $\langle e(m),e(n)\rangle_{M_N}=N\,e(m)\cdot e(n)-\Omega(m)\Omega(n)$. The birth update of Theorem 7.1(c) becomes
$$
\langle a,b\rangle_{M_{N+1}}-\langle a,b\rangle_{M_N}=a\cdot b:
$$
each birth adds exactly the Euclidean dot product of the exponent vectors. In particular $|e(n)|^2_{M_N}=N\,Q(n)-\Omega(n)^2$, which grows by $Q(n)$ at every birth. The metric energy is not a height. In frame $9$ the step $15\to23$ of the Syracuse map lowers it from $14$ to $8$, while $33\to25$ raises it from $14$ to $32$ (Astro). Size lives only in the archimedean shadow of Proposition 2.4.
- *The field clock.* Unit-normalizing the ladder costs $\sqrt{k(k+1)}$ at rung $k$. By [T4, Thm. 3.1] this adjoins $\sqrt q$ exactly when $k+1=q$ is prime. In the crystal the rung index counts primes, so the radical field of the orthonormalized birth ladder grows at the births of $p_q$ with $q$ prime: $3,5,11,17,31,41,\dots$ *(A curiosity; nothing below depends on it.)*

Section 12.12 uses the same ladder on the $3$-adic tree of the Collatz problem, with three slots at every node.

## 8. The shape of the crystal {#sec:8}

**Theorem 8.1 (the log-simplex).** For $x\ge1$, let $K_x\subset\mathcal V_N\otimes\mathbb R$ be the simplex with vertices $(\ln x/\ln p)\,e_p$, $p\le p_N$. Then $n\mapsto e(n)$ is a bijection from $\{n\le x:\ n\ p_N\text{-smooth},\ P_N\nmid n\}$ onto $\mathcal V_N\cap K_x$. In particular the window $W_N$ is the set of lattice points of $K_{p_{N+1}-1}$.

*Proof.* Write a point of $K_x$ as $\sum_pt_p(\ln x/\ln p)e_p$ with $t$ in the closed probability simplex, and take its standard form $c$ (subtract the minimum $\mu\ge0$). Then $\sum_pc_p\ln p=\ln x\sum_pt_p-\mu\ln P_N\le\ln x$. Conversely, if $n\le x$ then $e(n)=\sum_p\big(v_p(n)\ln p/\ln x\big)(\ln x/\ln p)e_p$ with weights summing to $\ln n/\ln x\le1$. The rest goes to the origin, which is the interior point of $K_x$ with barycentric weights $\ln p/\ln P_N$, by $\sum_pe_p=0$. Standard forms are unique (Prop. 2.2). $\square$

The crystal is a "bulb": the spike along $p$ has length $\log_px$, longest along $2$. The convex hull of its lattice points has as vertices the prime-power tips $p^{\lfloor\log_px\rfloor}$ together with "efficient" mixed numbers close to $x$, such as $78732=2^2\cdot3^9$ and $93312=2^7\cdot3^6$ for $N=3$, $x=10^5$.

**Theorem 8.2 (farthest node and diameter).** Let $x\ge4$ and $N\ge\pi(x)$.
1. The node farthest from $1$ among $n\le x$ is $2^{\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor}$, at distance $\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor$, and it is unique.
2. With $a=\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor$ and $b=\lfloor\log_3x\rfloor$, the diameter of $\{e(n):n\le x\}$ is $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+2ab/(N-1)}$, attained by the pair $\{2^a,3^b\}$. The pair is unique for $x\ge9$ except at $x\in\{25,26\}$, where $5^2$ ties with $3^2$.

*Proof.*
1. By Proposition 2.5, $|e(n)|_N^2\le Q(n)\le\Omega(n)^2\le(\log_2n)^2$. Equality holds throughout only for a power of $2$.
2. Divide two points by their gcd; this changes neither their difference nor its length, and makes them coprime, so at most one of them is even. For disjoint supports,
$$
|e(m)-e(k)|^2=\frac{N(Q_m+Q_k)-(\Omega_m-\Omega_k)^2}{N-1}\le\Omega_m^2+\Omega_k^2+\frac{2\Omega_m\Omega_k}{N-1}.
$$
   The right side increases in both $\Omega$'s. We have $\Omega\le a$ for an even number and $\Omega\le b$ for an odd one, with equality and $Q=\Omega^2$ only for $2^a$ and an odd prime power $q^b\le x$. That forces $q=3$, except when $5^b\le x<3^{b+1}$, which happens only for $b=2$, $x\in\{25,26\}$. Certified for $x\le1500$ (G9). $\square$

**Theorem 8.3 (centroid).** The centroid of $e(1),\dots,e(n)$ is $e(n!)/n$ (Legendre), the point of the geometric mean $(n!)^{1/n}\approx n/e$. As $n\to\infty$ in its home frame, its distance from $1$ tends to $\big(\sum_p(p-1)^{-2}\big)^{1/2}=1.172632$. The value at $n=10^6$ is $1.17256$ (G13). The centroid leans toward the $2$-ray, with weights $1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac16,\dots=\tfrac1{p-1}$.

*Proof.* The centroid equals $e(n!)/n$ by Legendre's formula, and $v_p(n!)/n\to1/(p-1)$ with $v_p(n!)/n\le 1/(p-1)$. The frame correction $(\sum_px_p)^2/(N(N-1))$ is $O((\ln\ln n)^2/N)\to0$. Dominated convergence gives the limit. $\square$

Meanwhile the mean squared radius is $\overline Q-O((\ln\ln n)^2/N)$, which grows like $\ln\ln n$. The cloud spreads without bound while its centre of mass settles.

## 9. Untangling {#sec:9}

**Theorem 9.1 (self-avoidance from the birth of 7).**
1. **Line ($N=2$, $n\le4$).** The walk folds back on itself: $2\to3$ retraces $1\to2$ and passes through the node $1$, and $1\to2$ overlaps $3\to4$.
2. **Plane ($N=3$, $n\le6$).** There are exactly three contacts between non-adjacent steps. The step $5\to6$ is a diameter of the unit circle through the node $1$, and it crosses $2\to3$ at parameters $(\tfrac12,\tfrac34)$ and $3\to4$ at $(\tfrac13,\tfrac56)$.
3. **From $N=4$ on.** For every $N\ge4$ the polygon $1\to2\to\cdots\to p_{N+1}-1$ is a simple path in frame $N$, and it stays simple in every later frame. Non-adjacent steps never meet, no step passes through a node other than its endpoints, and adjacent steps meet only at their common node.

*Proof of (3).* Lift the walk to $\mathbb R^N_{\ge0}$ with the actual exponent vectors. A point of the step $n\to n+1$ has support $\operatorname{rad}(n(n+1))$. By Bonse's inequality $P_N>p_{N+1}^2>n(n+1)$ for $N\ge4$, so every point of the walk has a zero coordinate. Two nonnegative vectors with a common zero coordinate that differ by $k\mathbf 1$ have $k=0$, so the quotient map is injective on the lifted walk.

On the lift, the linear functional $\ell(x)=\sum_px_p\ln p$ takes the value $(1-t)\ln n+t\ln(n+1)$ at parameter $t$ of step $n$. It is strictly increasing along the whole walk, and its ranges on steps $n$ and $m$ are disjoint for $|n-m|\ge2$. Later frames only add coordinates on which the walk vanishes. Parts (1) and (2) are exact computations (G11). $\square$

So the ordinary logarithm, invisible in the frame (Proposition 2.4), is a hidden *height function* that makes the walk monotone. The gauge can fold the walk only while the primorial is too small to keep the walk off the all-positive orthant. **Vertices are faithful from the line on (Euclid–Bertrand); segments are faithful from the body-centred cubic frame on (Bonse).** The tangle of the plane is untied by the birth of $7$.

## 10. Chirality: the numbers half and the reciprocals half {#sec:10}

**Definition 10.1.** The *chirality* of $x\in\mathcal V_N$ is $\kappa_3(x)=\sum_p(x_p-\bar x)^3$, the third central power sum. It is gauge-invariant and $S_N$-invariant, and it is odd under inversion $x\mapsto-x$, that is, under $n\mapsto1/n$. Up to normalization it is the uniform Amari–Chentsov cubic of Paper 01, Section 3.5, and Paper 01c.

For $n$ in standard form,
$$
N^2\kappa_3(e(n))=N^2\textstyle\sum_pv_p(n)^3-3N\,\Omega(n)Q(n)+2\,\Omega(n)^3 .
$$
For a prime $\kappa_3=(N-1)(N-2)/N^2$; for a co-prime $P_N/p$ it is the negative of that. For a squarefree number with $k$ prime factors it is $k(N-k)(N-2k)/N^2$.

**Theorem 10.2.** For $N\ge5$, every $n\in W_N$ with $n\ge2$ has $\kappa_3(e(n))>0$. For $N\le4$ the exceptions are exactly these:
- the line is achiral ($\kappa_3\equiv0$);
- in the plane, $6=P_3/5$ is negative;
- in the body-centred cubic frame, $6$ and $10$ are achiral.

*Proof.* Since $\sum v^3\ge Q$, we get $N^2\kappa_3\ge Q\,(N^2-3N\Omega)+2\Omega^3>0$ whenever $N>3\Omega$. That holds for $N\ge20$, because $\Omega(n)\le\log_2n<\log_2p_{N+1}<N/3$ by Rosser's bound. Frames $N<20$, and in fact all $N\le6000$, are certified (G10). $\square$

The unit shell of frame $N$ carries $2N$ lattice points: the $N$ primes and the $N$ co-primes. Chirality separates them, $+$ for primes and $-$ for co-primes. As $N$ grows the co-primes leave every window, since $P_N/p\ge P_{N-1}$. The whole crystal then lies in the positive half of the frame, and the reciprocals $1/n$ fill the negative half. The radius cannot tell a number from its reciprocal; the chirality can.

## 11. The rational world and the limit of continuity {#sec:11}

Everything so far concerned integers. The valuation space contains every $p_N$-smooth positive rational, since $e(a/b)=e(a)-e(b)\in\mathbb Z^N$. Three facts govern the passage to the continuum:
- In the crystal, which records the finite places, the rationals form a lattice, and nothing densifies.
- The continuum appears only in the archimedean shadow, the size $\ln q$. How fast that shadow fills depends on the number of primes.
- An irrational is not a point of the crystal. It is an infinite journey of $+1$ steps that never stays inside any frame.

**Proposition 11.1 (rationals are lattice points).** For $N\ge2$ the valuation map sends the $p_N$-smooth positive rationals onto $\mathcal V_N$. The class of $q$ contains exactly one integer $n$ not divisible by $P_N$, and $q=n/P_N^{\,j}$ for a unique $j\in\mathbb Z$. For example $1/2\equiv15$ in frame $3$, and $1/p\equiv P_N/p$ in general. Frame $N$ cannot tell $q$ from $n$; only the size $\ln q=\ln n-j\ln P_N$ does.

*Proof.* The class of $e(q)$ is $e(q)+\mathbb Z\mathbf 1$, and its standard form (Proposition 2.2) is $e(n)$ with $n=qP_N^{\,j}$ for $j=-\min_pv_p(q)$. $\square$

**Theorem 11.2 (the archimedean shadow).** Fix $N\ge2$, and let $\mathcal C_N(R)$ be the set of classes of $\mathcal V_N$ with $|x|_N\le R$.
- (a) **Weyl law.** $\#\mathcal C_N(R)=c_N R^{N-1}(1+o(1))$, with $c_N=\omega_{N-1}\sqrt N\big(\tfrac{N-1}{N}\big)^{(N-1)/2}$ and $\omega_d$ the volume of the unit $d$-ball. Concretely $c_2=2$, $c_3=2\pi/\sqrt3$, $c_4=\pi\sqrt3$.
- (b) **Equidistribution.** The log-sizes of the rationals in these classes form a periodic set with period $\ln P_N$ and $\#\mathcal C_N(R)$ points per period. As $R\to\infty$ these points equidistribute modulo $\ln P_N$.
- (c) **Cost of resolution.** Uniform resolution $\varepsilon$ in log-size within radius $R$ therefore requires
$$
R\ \ge\ \Big(\frac{\ln P_N}{c_N\,\varepsilon}\Big)^{1/(N-1)}(1+o(1)).
$$

*Proof.*
- (a) $A_{N-1}^*$ has covolume $1/\sqrt N$ in the zero-sum metric [Conway–Sloane], and the frame metric scales every length by $\sqrt{N/(N-1)}$. Count lattice points in a ball.
- (b) Each class contributes one coset of $\mathbb Z\ln P_N$. Take representatives with last coordinate $0$; their sizes modulo $\ln P_N$ are $\sum_{i<N}v_i\ln p_i$. The ratios $\ln p_i/\ln P_N$ are irrational, and together with $1$ they are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$, by unique factorization. Weyl's criterion over growing balls gives equidistribution.
- (c) is pigeonhole: the largest gap is at least the mean gap, $\ln P_N/\#\mathcal C_N(R)$. $\square$

Certified (R4): the count ratio to $c_NR^{N-1}$ is $1.016$, $0.995$, $1.001$ for $N=2$ ($R=32$), $N=3$ ($R=32$), $N=4$ ($R=12$), and half the sizes fall in the first half-period to within $0.008$. The measured slopes of the mean gap against $R$ are $-0.95$, $-1.98$, $-2.94$. For $N=2$ the motif is $\{d\ln2\bmod\ln6:\ |d|\le R\}$, a Sturmian set to which the three-distance theorem applies [Sós]: its gaps take at most three values.

The continuum is a shadow. The crystal never densifies, but its projection to the archimedean place does, at the polynomial rate $R^{N-1}$ set by the number of primes.

**Remark 11.3 (commas).** Frames $2,3,4$ are the $3$-, $5$- and $7$-limit lattices of just intonation (Euler's *Tonnetz*). Their finest resolutions are the commas of tuning theory (certified, R5):

| Frame | Radius | Finest comma | Name |

| $N=3$ | $R=24$ | $32805/32768$ | schisma |
| $N=4$ | $R=10$ | $4375/4374$ | ragisma |
| $N=4$ | $R=12$ | $250047/250000$ | landscape comma |

The superparticular commas, such as $81/80$, $2401/2400$ and $4375/4374$, are Størmer steps. So the finest $+1$ resolution of frame $N$ is $\ln(1+1/n_{\max}(N))$, with $n_{\max}=1,8,80,4374,9800,123200,336140$ for $N\le7$ (R3). Below that floor a frame refines only through non-superparticular ratios, at the cost in radius of Theorem 11.2(c). *(Musical names are a rhyme; the lattice statement is exact.)*

**Theorem 11.4 (Farey edges are $+1$ steps).** If $a/b<c/d$ are Farey neighbours, $bc-ad=1$, then
$$
e(c/d)-e(a/b)=\Delta_{ad},\qquad \ln\frac{c/d}{a/b}=\ln\Big(1+\frac1{ad}\Big).
$$
Hence the Farey graph and the Stern–Brocot tree embed in every frame containing their primes, and every edge is a $+1$ step. Mediant insertion, the densification of $\mathbb Q$, uses only $+1$ steps.

*Proof.* $e(c/d)-e(a/b)=e(bc)-e(ad)$, and $bc=ad+1$. $\square$ Certified for all $13565$ Farey edges with $b,d\le150$ (R1).

**Corollary 11.5 (continued fractions are $+1$ journeys).** Let $x>0$ have convergents $p_k/q_k$. Consecutive convergents are Farey neighbours, so
$$
e(p_k/q_k)-e(p_{k-1}/q_{k-1})=\pm\Delta_{n_k},\qquad n_k=\min(p_kq_{k-1},\,p_{k-1}q_k),
$$
and the archimedean jump is $\ln(1+1/n_k)\approx1/n_k\to0$. In the crystal the same steps do not shrink: $|\Delta_{n_k}|^2\to Q(n_k)+Q(n_k+1)\ge2$ as $N\to\infty$.
- For the golden ratio, Cassini's identity gives the steps $3\to4$, $9\to10$, $24\to25$, $64\to65$, $168\to169$, …, between squares and products of Fibonacci numbers.
- For $\sqrt2$, Pell's equation gives $2\to3$, $14\to15$, $84\to85$, $492\to493$, ….

Certified for $\varphi,\sqrt2,e,\pi,\sqrt[3]2$ over $40$ convergents each, with the log-jump matching $\ln(1+1/n_k)$ to $10^{-40}$ (R2).

**Theorem 11.6 (every irrational births infinitely many primes).** Let $x>0$ be irrational. For every $N$, all but finitely many steps of its continued-fraction journey have height greater than $N$: the journey leaves every frame. In particular the numerators and denominators of the convergents involve infinitely many primes, and $P^+(p_kq_kp_{k-1}q_{k-1})\to\infty$. A positive real is rational exactly when its journey is finite.

*Proof.* The integers $n_k$ increase without bound. If infinitely many steps had height at most $N$, there would be infinitely many pairs of consecutive $p_N$-smooth integers, contradicting Størmer's theorem. The last sentence is the finiteness of the continued fraction of a rational. $\square$

Effective forms of Størmer's theorem, obtained by Baker's method [Shorey–Tijdeman], make the rate explicit; we do not use them. For the golden ratio more is true. By Carmichael's theorem on primitive divisors of Fibonacci numbers, the numerator $F_{k+2}$ brings a prime never seen before in the journey at every step except $k=4$ ($F_6=8$) and $k=10$ ($F_{12}=144$) (R2).

**Theorem 11.7 (going up versus going out).** Two routes reach archimedean resolution $\varepsilon$.
1. **Out.** Inside a fixed frame $N$, uniform resolution requires radius at least $(\ln P_N/(c_N\varepsilon))^{1/(N-1)}$ (Theorem 11.2(c)). For $N=3$ and $\varepsilon=10^{-16}$ that is $R\approx10^8$.
2. **Up.** With prime births allowed, the continued-fraction journey reaches $|x-p_k/q_k|<1/q_k^2$, at log-height about $\tfrac12\ln(1/\varepsilon)$. Its crystal radius stays small. In the limit metric, $\sqrt{Q(p_k)+Q(q_k)}$ stays below $6.8$ over the first $40$ convergents of $\varphi$, $\pi$ and $e$, while their denominators reach $9$, $23$ and $19$ digits.

The same contrast appears in the counting. The $p_N$-smooth rationals of log-height at most $L$ number $\asymp L^N$: each frame is a flat crystal with polynomial growth. All positive rationals in $(0,1]$ of log-height at most $L$ number $\sim(3/\pi^2)e^{2L}$, which is exponential. **Continuity is reached by going up in dimension, not out in distance.** The exponential growth of the union of frames is the growth of a hyperbolic space.

**Remark 11.8 (the hyperbolic boundary; classical).** The Farey graph is the edge set of the Farey tessellation of the hyperbolic plane. Continued fractions are the cutting sequences of geodesics through it [Series], and the irrationals are its ideal boundary points. By Theorem 11.4, every Farey edge crossed is a $+1$ step of the crystal. The reading "an irrational is an infinite journey into finer resolution" is therefore exact: it is a geodesic to the boundary at infinity, which the crystal sees as an unbounded sequence of $+1$ steps of growing height.

**Proposition 11.9 (the continuum limit of one frame: the tent law).** Fix $N$, and let $x\to\infty$. In the frame, the points $e(n)/\ln x$ for $p_N$-smooth $n\le x$ converge in distribution to the push-forward of the uniform measure on the simplex $\Sigma=\{u\ge0:\ \langle\ell,u\rangle\le1\}$, where $\ell=(\ln p)_p$. Its density at a frame point $y$ is proportional to
$$
\big(1-\langle\ell,s(y)\rangle\big)_+ ,
$$
where $s(y)$ is the standard-form representative. It is supported on the log-simplex $K_1=\operatorname{conv}\{e_p/\ln p\}$ of Theorem 8.1, and it peaks at the origin.

The peak is the primorial gauge stacking the powers $P_N^{\,j}$ onto $1$. Almost all smooth numbers in a fixed frame have positive massaction, since $\Psi(x/P_N,p_N)/\Psi(x,p_N)\to1$.

*Proof.* The lattice points of $T\Sigma$ become uniformly distributed after scaling by $1/T$, by the usual Riemann-sum argument. The quotient by $\mathbb R\mathbf 1$ pushes Lebesgue measure to the fibre length, and the fibre over $y$ is $\{s(y)+t\mathbf 1:\ 0\le t\le(1-\langle\ell,s(y)\rangle)/\ln P_N\}$. $\square$ Certified for $N=3$, $T=120$ ($245104$ points): the distribution of the size statistic agrees with the Monte Carlo law to Kolmogorov distance $0.013$ (R6).

So a fixed frame has two continuum limits, and neither is resolution. Zoomed out, it is a tent on the log-simplex. Projected to the archimedean place, it is a polynomially densifying shadow. Genuine continuity, with exponentially cheap resolution, needs the frames to keep growing.

## 12. Collatz as a $\{2,3\}$ phenomenon of the crystal {#sec:12}

Write $T$ for the shortcut map, $T(n)=n/2$ for even $n$ and $T(n)=(3n+1)/2$ for odd $n$. Its multiplicative parts are translations of the crystal: halving is $-e_2$ and tripling is $e_3$. Its additive part is a $+1$ step. This section reads the map, its inverse tree and its cycles in that language. The cycle theory of the cousin family $3n+b$ is developed in a separate monograph [Cousins]. We reuse its results where they apply, and we claim here only the crystal readings and three small theorems.

### 12.1 The step and the tree {#sec:12-1}

An odd step $n\mapsto(3n+1)/2$ is three moves in the crystal:
1. the translation $e_3$, to the point $3n$ in the $3$-chamber;
2. the $+1$ step $\Delta_{3n}$, the superparticular vector of $(3n+1)/(3n)$, which leaves the $3$-chamber and enters the $2$-chamber;
3. the drain $-e_2$.

Even steps are drains. Since $3n+1\equiv1\pmod3$, after its first odd step an orbit stays forever on the negative side of the $3$-wall (Theorem 3.1). Every later odd step is a round trip into the $3$-chamber and back through a $+1$ step. The coordinates of the primes $p\ge5$ change only through these $+1$ steps.

**Proposition 12.1 (the tree lives in $\mathbb Z[1/3]$).** Extend $T$ to $\mathbb Z[1/3]$, giving $a/3^k$ the parity of $a$.
- (a) **Exactly two-to-one.** On $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ the map $T$ is exactly two-to-one, and the preimages of $x$ are $2x$ and $(2x-1)/3$.
- (b) **The integer tree.** For $x\in\mathbb Z$, the odd preimage $(2x-1)/3$ is an integer exactly when $x\equiv2\pmod3$; otherwise it has $3$-adic valuation $-1$. If $v_3(x)=-k<0$, then $v_3((2x-1)/3)=-k-1$ and $v_3(2x)=-k$. So a backward branch that leaves $\mathbb Z$ never returns. Multiples of $3$ have no integral odd preimage anywhere in their tower $2^jm$, and integers $\equiv1\pmod3$ branch at every other level. The integral inverse tree therefore grows by the factor $4/3$ per level.
- (c) **Forward drain.** Forward, $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ drains into $\mathbb Z$: $a/3^k$ with $3\nmid a$ becomes an integer after exactly $k$ odd steps. Hence the Collatz conjecture on $\mathbb Z_{>0}$ is equivalent to: every positive element of $\mathbb Z[1/3]$ reaches $1$.
- (d) **In the crystal.** The odd preimage of $m$ exists exactly when the $-1$ step from $2m$ enters the $3$-chamber.

*Proof.* (a) $T(2x)=x$, and $(2x-1)/3$ has odd numerator with $T((2x-1)/3)=x$; conversely, solving $T(y)=x$ on each parity class gives exactly these two. (b) and (c): if $x=a/3^k$ is odd with $k\ge1$, then $T(x)=(a+3^{k-1})/(2\cdot3^{k-1})$, which has denominator $3^{k-1}$; halving does not change the denominator. (d) is Theorem 3.1 applied to $2m-1$. $\square$

Certified (C2, C3): the two-to-one property, the drain and the leak on $20000$ random cases each; the branching rule for $m\le10^6$; the level ratio $1.3335$ at depth $40$. After the first odd step, every odd iterate is a unit of $\mathbb Z/6$: the plane frame's units carry the whole odd dynamics. The genesis-style description, "every branch splits in two, and when it does not, it is going to $\mathbb Z[1/3^n]$", is Proposition 12.1(a)–(b) exactly.

### 12.2 Three places, one budget {#sec:12-2}

**Proposition 12.2 (pathwise three-place balance).** Let $x\neq y$ share their first $K$ parity bits, $m$ of them odd. Then $T^Kx-T^Ky=(3^m/2^K)(x-y)$, so
$$
v_2(T^Kx-T^Ky)=v_2(x-y)-K,\qquad v_3(T^Kx-T^Ky)=v_3(x-y)+m .
$$
Along any orbit with odd density $f$, the Lyapunov exponents at the three places are
- $\lambda_2=+\ln2$ (the parity shift),
- $\lambda_3=-f\ln3$ (each odd step contracts $3$-adic distances by exactly $3$),
- $\lambda_\infty=f\ln3-\ln2+\tfrac1K\sum_i\ln(1+\tfrac1{3n_i})$,

and $\lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_\infty$ equals the $+1$ corrections alone: the product formula, step by step.

*Proof.* Along a shared word, $T^K$ is the same affine map $n\mapsto(3^mn+S_w)/2^K$ on both points. The archimedean exponent is the logarithm of the size ratio. $\square$ Certified on $2000$ random pairs (C4).

Consequences:
- **Slope versus descent.** The affine *slope* of an orbit segment contracts exactly when its $3$-adic contraction is weaker than its $2$-adic expansion, $f<\log_32$. Actual descent also needs the $+1$ corrections $\sum\ln(1+\tfrac1{3n_i})$ to be paid. These matter for small values: the trivial cycle has $f=\tfrac12<\log_32$ and yet zero net change, because its correction $\ln\tfrac43$ exactly cancels the slope. For large $n$ the corrections are small, and slope contraction is then the leading effect. Segments where the slope contracts but the value does not descend are the "paradoxical" sequences of [Rozier–Terracol]. They study them in connection with Terras's coefficient stopping-time conjecture.
- **The typical word.** For the typical density $f=\tfrac12$, the exponent is $\lambda_\infty=-\tfrac12\ln\tfrac43$: minus half the logarithm of the trivial cycle's ratio $4/3$ (Theorem 12.4).
- **A correction.** An earlier internal note described the $3$-adic side of Collatz as non-contracting. Proposition 12.2 corrects it: the odd branch contracts $3$-adically by exactly $3$, and this contraction is what the archimedean descent balances.

### 12.3 Cycles in the crystal {#sec:12-3}

**Lemma 12.3 (cycles close for free at every finite place).** Let a cycle have $K$ steps and odd elements $n_1,\dots,n_m$. Then
$$
\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{3n_i+1}{3n_i}=\frac{2^K}{3^m},\qquad \sum_i\Delta_{3n_i}=K\,e_2-m\,e_3 .
$$
The components of the $m$ superparticular steps on every prime $p\ge5$ cancel by telescoping, because $3n_i+1=2^{a_i}n_{i+1}$.

So, *given* a cycle, its polygon in the crystal closes automatically at every finite place, and its sizes satisfy the archimedean comma condition: **$m$ superparticular ratios multiply to a $3$-limit comma $2^K/3^m$.** For a positive cycle the comma lies in $(1,e^{m/(3n_{\min})}]$. This is the crystal form of the multiplicative cycle equation of [Cousins].

The *existence* of an integer cycle with a given parity word, however, is not archimedean. It is the divisibility $2^K-3^m\mid S_w$, a condition at the primes dividing $2^K-3^m$ (Theorem 12.8). So the comma condition is necessary, not sufficient. Proving that the divisibility fails for every unwanted positive word is the arithmetic content of the cycle problem.

**Theorem 12.4 (the unimodular cycles are the Størmer pairs of the line).** The cycles whose periodic points have denominator $|2^K-3^m|=1$ are exactly $\{0\}$, $\{-1\}$, $\{1,2\}$ and $\{-5,-7,-10\}$. They correspond, in this order, to the four consecutive $\{2,3\}$-smooth pairs $(1,2)$, $(2,3)$, $(3,4)$, $(8,9)$: the Størmer pairs of the line frame $N=2$ (Theorem 4.3), whose last pair, $8\to9$, is the Catalan step.

In particular **the trivial positive cycle is the Størmer step $3\to4$**. Its comma is the perfect fourth $4/3$, which is the same $4/3$ that sets the mean descent in Proposition 12.2.

*Proof.* $|2^K-3^m|=1$ has only the four solutions (Gersonides; Mihăilescu in general). With denominator $1$ every word is integral, which gives the listed cycles. The pairs $\{2^K,3^m\}$ are consecutive by definition. $\square$ Certified (C5).

**How the cycle displaces and why it breaks.** A parity word $w$ ($K$ steps, $m$ odd) acts on the integers that follow it as an affine map
$$
A_w(n)=\frac{3^mn+S_w}{2^K},\qquad n^*_w=\frac{S_w}{2^K-3^m},
$$
with slope $3^m/2^K$; the fixed point $n^*_w$ is the rational cycle of $w$ [Lagarias].
- **Contracting words** ($f<\log_32$) pull the integers that follow them toward a positive fixed point. **Expanding words** have negative fixed points, and the sign of $n^*_w$ is the sign of $2^K-3^m$ (C6; [Cousins]).
- **The anchor.** Every alternating word $(10)^j$ has $n^*=1$. The trivial cycle is the common attracting fixed point of the typical words.
- **Displacement.** Pushing a word's odd density toward $\log_32$ pushes $n^*_w$ outward and gives it a denominator $\delta=|D|/\gcd(D,S_w)$. The cycle *displaces* into $\mathbb Q$.
- **The break.** The displaced cycle *breaks* off the integers unless $\delta=1$. Its integer shadow is a cycle of the cousin map $3n+\delta$, which in the crystal is the translation by $e(\delta)$ [Cousins, rational-cycle bijection].

**Theorem 12.5 (frame-confined cycles: one finite check per prime birth).** Call a cycle *confined to frame $N$* if all its elements are $p_N$-smooth.
- (a) **Finitely many per frame.** Every odd step of a confined cycle is a Størmer pair of frame $N$: $(3n,3n+1)$ for $n>0$, and $(3|n|-1,3|n|)$ for $n<0$. Hence each frame confines only finitely many cycles, and they can be computed from the finite list of consecutive $p_N$-smooth pairs [Lehmer].
- (b) **Frames up to $23$.** For $2\le N\le9$ (primes up to $23$), the nonzero confined cycles are exactly $\{1,2\}$ and $\{-1\}$, together with $\{-5,-7,-10\}$ from the birth of $7$ on. The eleven-step cycle $\{-17,\dots\}$ is confined to frame $18$, since its largest prime is $61$.
- (c) **The conjecture as checks.** The Collatz cycle conjecture, for both signs, is equivalent to: for every $N$, the cycles confined to frame $N$ are the known ones. That is one finite, effective computation per prime birth.

*Proof.* (a) Both members of each pair are $p_N$-smooth, and Størmer's theorem gives finiteness. (b) is a computation from the complete Størmer lists (certified census G7; C1). (c) Every cycle is finite, hence confined to the frame of its largest prime. $\square$ Each check is effective but grows quickly with $N$; this is a reformulation, not a proof.

**Remark (positive frames up to $97$).** For positive cycles, the checks of Theorem 12.5(c) are settled through the frame of $97$ ($N\le25$) without any further computation. The complete list of [Luca–Najman] shows that the largest $n$ with $n$ and $n+1$ both $97$-smooth is $n=9591468737351909375<2^{68}$ [OEIS A002072]. Every element of a nontrivial positive cycle exceeds $2^{68}$ [Barina]. So every odd step $3n\to3n+1$ of such a cycle lies beyond the Størmer horizon of the frame of $97$: it involves a prime larger than $97$. Certified (L8). Negative cycles would need the complete lists themselves, and Theorem 12.5(b) covers them only up to $23$.

### 12.4 Why other cycles are hard to make: three locks {#sec:12-4}

1. **Entropy lock.** A cycle needs odd density $m/K\approx\log_32$. That is a large deviation from the fair parity coin, costing $D(\log_32\,\|\,\tfrac12)=1-H(\log_32)=0.0500$ bits per step (C8). The $2^{0.95K}$ admissible words fall short of the $\sim2^K$ size of the denominators [Cousins, clustering law].
2. **Comma lock.** A positive cycle all of whose elements exceed $A$ needs $0<K\ln2-m\ln3<m/(3A)$. That is a $3$-limit comma finer than $m/(3A)$, and the line frame densifies only at the Weyl rate of Theorem 11.2. Scanning the upper convergents and intermediate fractions of $\log_23$, the first admissible pair is:
   - $(K,m)=(301994,190537)$ for $A=1.765\times10^{10}$, the positive-side certificate of [Cousins];
   - $(K,m)=(114208327604,\ 72057431991)$ for $A=2^{68}$, using Barina's verification [Barina]. This reproduces the known lower bound on cycle length [Eliahou method; Hercher] (C7).
3. **Prime-birth lock.** The denominator $2^K-3^m$ of a would-be cycle carries primes outside $\{2,3\}$, which the numerator $S_w$, a sum of $\{2,3\}$-smooth terms, must absorb. Moreover, every odd step of a nontrivial positive cycle has height beyond the Størmer horizon of every frame $N\le25$ (primes up to $97$), since its elements exceed $2^{68}$, beyond the horizon $9.6\times10^{18}$ of that frame (the remark after Theorem 12.5).

### 12.5 The rational world of Collatz: charges and fibers {#sec:12-5}

The map extends to the ring $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$ of rationals with odd denominator, with parity read off the numerator [Lagarias, Lagarias 1990]. In the crystal, $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$ is the set of rationals with nonnegative $2$-coordinate (Section 11). Rationals with $2$ in the denominator have no parity, and the map does not reach them. Proposition 12.1 was the $3$-part of this picture; the full picture is as follows.

**Proposition 12.6 (charges are conserved; the $3$-coordinate is attracted to $0$).** Let $x=a/(3^kd)$ in lowest terms, with $3\nmid d$.
- (a) **The denominator law.** While $k>0$, each odd step divides the denominator by exactly $3$. Once $k=0$, the denominator $d$ never changes again. Even steps never change it.
- (b) **The $3$-attractor.** In the crystal, a negative $3$-coordinate rises by one at each odd step. After at most $\max(-v_3(x),0)+1$ odd steps the $3$-coordinate is $0$ and stays $0$.
- (c) **Charges.** The negative coordinates on the primes $p\ge5$, i.e. the **charge** $d$, are conserved. They label invariant fibres.

*Proof.* For odd $x=a/D$ with $\gcd(a,D)=1$, $T(x)=(3a+D)/(2D)$, and $\gcd(3a+D,D)=\gcd(3,D)$. If $3\mid D$, exactly one factor $3$ cancels, since $\gcd(a+D/3,D/3)=1$. If $3\nmid D$, nothing cancels, and the new numerator $3a+D$ is prime to $3$. $\square$ Certified on $5000$ random orbits of $300$ steps each (Q2, Q7).

**Proposition 12.7 (fibres are cousin worlds).** On the fibre of charge $d$, multiplication by $d$ (the crystal translation by $e(d)$) conjugates $T$ to the cousin map $T_d(a)=a/2$ or $(3a+d)/2$, acting on integers prime to $d$. The integer Collatz problem is the charge-$1$ fibre. In the fibre of charge $d$, the $+1$ step of the odd move becomes a $+d$ step, from $3a$ to $3a+d$. Certified (Q5).

**Theorem 12.8 (rational cycles and their charges).** Every primitive periodic parity word $w$ of length $K$ with $m$ odd steps is realized by exactly one rational cycle, through $n^*_w=S_w/(2^K-3^m)$ [Lagarias 1990]. Its charge is
$$
\delta(w)=\frac{|2^K-3^m|}{\gcd(2^K-3^m,\,S_w)} ,
$$
whose primes are exactly the primes of $2^K-3^m$ that the $\{2,3\}$-smooth sum $S_w$ fails to absorb.

So the rationals carry exactly one cycle per primitive binary necklace: $\tfrac1K\sum_{e\mid K}\mu(e)2^{K/e}$ of them with $K$ steps, and $31042$ with $K\le18$ (Q3).
- **No absorption is the norm.** In $89.6\%$ of these cycles nothing is absorbed: the charge is the full $|2^K-3^m|$. The charge is prime in $13.9\%$ of them.
- **The integer fibre.** Charge $1$, i.e. the integers, keeps exactly the five known cycles (Q4).
- **The Wikipedia example.** The word $(1011001)$ gives $151/47$ and the seven-step cycle $151/47\to250/47\to125/47\to211/47\to340/47\to170/47\to85/47$, in the fibre of charge $47$ (Q1).
- **Small charges.** Every charge $d\le60$ prime to $6$ carries a primitive cycle. Most appear with $K\le18$; the shortest found for $d=41$ has $20$ steps and for $d=53$ it has $29$ (Q6).

The displacement picture of Section 12.3 is now literal. Varying the word moves its cycle from fibre to fibre. The integers are one fibre among infinitely many, and they keep only the cycles whose charge cancels completely: the four unimodular cycles of Theorem 12.4, and the eleven-step cycle, where $S_w=2363=17\cdot139$ absorbs $D=-139$.

**Proposition 12.9 (frame-confined cycles in a fixed charge).** Fix a charge $d$ and a frame $N$. In a cycle of the charge-$d$ fibre whose numerators are $p_N$-smooth, each odd step is a pair $(3a,3a+d)$ of $p_N$-smooth integers at distance $d$, or its mirror for negative $a$. The two-term $S$-unit equation has only finitely many solutions [Mahler], so each pair (charge, frame) confines only finitely many cycles, effectively computable. This generalizes Theorem 12.5 from $d=1$.

**The Periodicity Conjecture in the crystal.** Lagarias's Periodicity Conjecture says every $x\in\mathbb Z_{(2)}$ has an eventually periodic parity sequence. By Propositions 12.6–12.7 it is equivalent to: no orbit of any cousin world $T_d$ diverges. The odd-denominator world first drains, in finitely many odd steps, onto the $3$-free fibres. It then stays in its fibre forever, and the conjecture asks that every fibre end in cycles. Collatz is its charge-$1$ case.

### 12.6 The binary tree and the Collatz tree {#sec:12-6}

Put Collatz in its family $T_a(x)=x/2$ for even $x$ and $(ax+1)/2$ for odd $x$, with $a$ odd; Collatz is $a=3$.

**Proposition 12.10 (the binary tree is the $1x+1$ tree).** The inverse tree of $T_1$ rooted at $1$ is the binary tree.
- **Structure.** Every $n\ge2$ has exactly one parent, $n/2$ or $(n+1)/2$. Its first-arrival depth is $\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, so the new level $k$ is $(2^{k-1},2^k]$. Counting the fixed point $1$ (since $T_1(1)=1$), the full $k$-step preimage set is $T_1^{-k}(1)=\{1,\dots,2^k\}$.
- **Two places.** At the three places of Proposition 12.2, $T_1$ lives on two: $\lambda_2=\ln2$ and $\lambda_\infty=-\ln2$ on every orbit, since $1$ is a $3$-adic unit. Every orbit descends deterministically.
- **No drain.** The odd preimage $2x-1$ is always an integer.

Certified for $n\le2^{20}$ (B1).

**Theorem 12.11 (two perfect $2$-adic transversals: two gauges).** For every $k$, the $k$-step preimage sets $T_1^{-k}(1)$ and $T^{-k}(1)\subset\mathbb Z_2$ each contain exactly one point of every residue class modulo $2^k$.
- **The binary gauge.** Its representatives are the integers $\{1,\dots,2^k\}$: the archimedean-minimal choice. (An earlier draft wrote the new level $(2^{k-1},2^k]$ here. That set has only $2^{k-1}$ elements, so it cannot represent all $2^k$ classes.)
- **The Collatz gauge.** Its representatives are $x_w=(2^k-S_w)/3^m\in\mathbb Z[1/3]$, the dynamical choice. About $1.6\,(4/3)^k$ of the $2^k$ are positive integers.
- **In the language of 01e.** Both are gauges, i.e. sections of $\mathbb Z_2\to\mathbb Z/2^k$, in the sense of 01e. They agree at the $2$-adic place and differ only at $3$ and at $\infty$.

The positive half of the Collatz conjecture, cycles and divergence together, says: every positive integer is eventually chosen by the dynamical gauge.

*Proof.* The parity-vector bijection with $\mathbb Z/2^k$ extends to $\mathbb Z_2$ [Bernstein–Lagarias]. On the class of points following a word $w$, $T^k$ is the affine bijection $A_w$ onto $\mathbb Z_2$, and the class's preimage of $1$ is $A_w^{-1}(1)$. $\square$ Certified for $k=8,12,16$ (B2).

**Proposition 12.12 (parity charge: the dual gauge).** Let $Q(x)=\sum_i\varepsilon_i(x)2^i$, where $\varepsilon_i(x)$ is the parity of $T^ix$. Then $Q$ is a homeomorphism of $\mathbb Z_2$ that conjugates $T$ to the shift [Bernstein–Lagarias], and $Q(1)=-1/3$.
- **The criterion.** A positive integer $n$ reaches $1$ exactly when $Q(n)\in\tfrac13\mathbb Z\setminus\mathbb Z$, i.e. when its *parity charge*, the denominator of $Q(n)$, is exactly $3$.
- **Denominators traded.** Over a cycle with primitive word $w$, $Q$ is a rational whose denominator divides $2^K-1$. So $Q$ trades the Collatz denominator $2^K-3^m$ for the binary denominator $2^K-1^m$.
- **Divergence.** Divergent orbits are exactly the $n$ with $Q(n)$ irrational.

*Proof.* If $T^kn=1$, the parity sequence is $r$ followed by $(10)^\infty$, so $Q(n)=r-2^k/3$. Conversely, $a/3$ has $2$-adic digits eventually periodic of period $2$. The tail $(10)$ or $(01)$ is the trivial cycle, because each periodic word has a unique periodic point (Theorem 12.8); the tails $0^\infty$ and $1^\infty$ are $0$ and $-1$. $\square$ Certified: $Q(n)=a/3$ for $n\le20000$; $Q(-1)=-1$, $Q(0)=0$; the denominator of $Q(-5)$ is $7$ and that of $Q(-17)$ is $2047=2^{11}-1$ (B3).

So the gauge statement "every other positive cycle requires a denominator larger than $1$" has two equivalent forms. In the *point charge* form, every primitive positive word other than $(10)$ has $\delta(w)>1$. In the *parity charge* form, every positive integer has parity charge exactly $3$. The second covers divergence as well.

**Theorem 12.13 (the coverage equation; mean-field).** Consider the mean-field branching model of the $T_a$ inverse tree, in which each integer node has an odd child with probability $1/a$. The expected number of nodes with $\log_2$-size in $[L,L+1)$ grows like $2^{\theta L}$, governed by
$$
g_a(\theta)=2^{-\theta}\big(1+a^{\theta-1}\big)=1 .
$$
The root $\theta=1$ occurs for every $a$, because preimage counting conserves density. Beyond it:
1. **$a=1$:** there is no other root. The binary tree covers perfectly.
2. **$a=3$:** the roots are exactly $\theta=1$ and $\theta=2$, the solutions of $2^\theta-3^{\theta-1}=1$. They are the line frame's Størmer pairs $(1,2)$ and $(3,4)$, the second of which is the trivial cycle. The slope $g_3'(1)=\tfrac12\ln\tfrac34$ is the mean archimedean drift.
3. **$a=4$:** a double root at $\theta=1$. This is the stability boundary, where the mean drift $\tfrac12\ln(a/4)$ vanishes.
4. **$a\ge5$:** the second root falls below $1$: $0.651$ for $a=5$ and $0.374$ for $a=7$. The tree of $1$ is predicted to contain only about $X^{\theta_2}$ integers up to $X$. Up to $X=10^6$ the measured exponents are $0.58$ and $0.29$, while for $a=3$ every $n\le10^6$ is covered (B4, B5).

So $3$ is the largest odd multiplier whose tree keeps full coverage exponent. Its distance from the boundary $4$ is the comma $4/3$, and its second root is the trivial cycle itself ($3+1=4$).
- **Resolution, binary tree.** The binary tree resolves the integers at unit resolution, level by level: depth $=\log_2n$.
- **Resolution, Collatz tree.** The Collatz tree resolves them only at the mean depth of the classical total stopping time, about $6.95\ln n\approx4.8\log_2n$. Its exponent-$1$ coverage is protected only by the gap between the roots $1$ and $2$.

*Fence.* Theorem 12.13 is a statement about a mean-field model. The real tree's odd-child rule is the deterministic residue condition $x\equiv2\pmod3$, not a coin flip.

### 12.7 What a proof would need {#sec:12-7}

The crystal does not prove the conjecture. It does separate the two halves cleanly, and it quantifies each reduction.
- **The cycle half: the point-charge gauge.** It requires $D(w)\nmid S_w$ for every primitive positive word other than $(10)$. What is established:
  - every frame up to the prime $23$ is clean for both signs (Theorem 12.5), and every frame up to $97$ for positive cycles (the remark after it);
  - any other positive cycle needs at least $114208327604$ steps (the comma lock);
  - any other positive cycle has more than $91$ local minima, i.e. more than $91$ returns to $x\equiv1\pmod4$ [Hercher] (Section 12.9);
  - the entropy deficit is $0.05$ bits per step.

  What remains is a *quantitative non-vanishing* statement: residue $0$ of $S_w$ modulo $D$ must be avoided for every such word, prime or composite $D$ alike [Cousins]. Equidistribution of $S_w$ would only describe frequencies; it would not exclude the residue $0$ unless it came with error bounds strong enough to rule out every exception. A uniform-in-$N$ version of Theorem 12.5 would suffice, but the Størmer bounds outgrow every verified range.
- **The divergence half: rationality of the parity charge.** It requires $Q(n)$ to be rational for every positive $n$. That gives eventual periodicity, i.e. no divergence, but not by itself coverage by the tree of $1$: which cycle is reached is the cycle half. Coverage is the stronger statement that the denominator is exactly $3$ (Proposition 12.12). Rigorously:
  - the tree of $1$ contains at least $X^{0.914947}$ integers up to $X$ [Nikpour–Rabbani; Allikvere], from the difference inequalities of Krasikov (Section 12.8), which are the rigorous counterpart of the coverage equation;
  - almost all orbits, in logarithmic density, attain almost bounded values [Tao]. This leaves individual exceptional orbits unresolved.

  A proof along this line needs two separate steps: the rigorous coverage exponent must be raised to $1$, through Conjecture 12.18 or the two-level Conjecture 12.24 (Theorem 12.22). Then exponent $1$ must be upgraded to every integer. That second step is genuinely further: exponent $1$ implies neither positive density nor full coverage.

### 12.8 The Krasikov–Lagarias inequalities in the crystal {#sec:12-8}

The best lower bounds for $\pi_a(x)=\#\{n\le x:\ a\text{ lies on the orbit of }n\}$, for $a\not\equiv0\pmod3$, come from Krasikov's difference inequalities [Krasikov 1989] and their programming treatment:
- $x^{0.81}$ at modulus $3^9$ [Applegate–Lagarias];
- $x^{0.84}$ at modulus $3^{11}$ [Krasikov–Lagarias, 2003];
- and, in 2026, $x^{0.9145645}$ [Allikvere] and $x^{0.914947}$ [Nikpour–Rabbani], both at modulus $3^{20}$.

The 2026 works recast the program as a nonlinear Perron–Frobenius power iteration and certify it in exact integer arithmetic. Nikpour–Rabbani also show that the same operator, advanced one $3$-adic digit at a time, is the Frobenius–Perron operator of Tao's Syracuse random variable. In the language of Section 12.6 these inequalities are the coverage equation made rigorous, with one information loss: each odd backward step forgets one $3$-adic digit.

**Proposition 12.14 (the inequalities are a worst-case-lift Perron problem).** On residues $r$ modulo $3^k$, define
$$
\Phi_\gamma(c)_r=2^{-\gamma}c_{2r}+[r\equiv2\ (3)]\Big(\tfrac32\Big)^\gamma\min_{t\in L(r)}c_t ,
$$
with $c=0$ on the multiples of $3$. Here $L(r)$ is the set of the three lifts modulo $3^k$ of $(2r-1)/3$ modulo $3^{k-1}$. The odd backward step loses one $3$-adic digit, and the minimum takes the worst value of the lost digit (the $\mathbb Z/3$ lift torsor).

The largest $\gamma$ for which $\Phi_\gamma$ has a positive vector with $\Phi_\gamma(c)\ge c$ equals the exponent $\log_2\lambda_k$ of the linear-program family $L_k^{NT}(\lambda)$ of [Krasikov–Lagarias]. Eliminating the classes $r\equiv1\pmod3$ by $r\mapsto2r$ turns $\Phi_\gamma$ into their constraints (L1)–(L3), with $\lambda=2^\gamma$. Power iteration reproduces their Table 2 for $2\le k\le11$ to all seven printed digits ($0.4365880,\ \dots,\ 0.8168300,\ 0.8295450,\ 0.8417560$), in seconds per modulus.

*Priority.* This reformulation was found independently here, but it is not new. It appears, with exact-integer certificates up to $k=20$, in [Allikvere] and [Nikpour–Rabbani] (their Lemma 2.4).

**Theorem 12.15 (independent reproduction, $k\le14$).** For $k=12,13,14$ the linear programs $L_k^{NT}(\lambda)$ are feasible at
$$
\gamma=\log_2\lambda=0.8531,\quad 0.8629,\quad 0.8724 ,
$$
with critical values $0.853135$, $0.863005$ and $0.872451$. These certify $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{0.872}$ by [Krasikov–Lagarias, Thm. 2.2]. They are consistent with, and superseded by, the certified $x^{0.914947}$ at $k=20$ [Nikpour–Rabbani].

*Certificate.* The explicit feasible vectors ($177147$, $531441$ and $1594323$ principal variables) are stored in `empirical_01g/kl/`. An independent checker verifies (L0)–(L4) as follows:
- every constraint holds in float64 with minimum relative slack at least $9.7\times10^{-6}$, while the rounding error of each three-term evaluation is below $10^{-14}$ relative;
- the $6000$ tightest constraints, rechecked in $60$-digit arithmetic, agree with the float values.

The maximum ratio $C^{\max}_k$ and the averages $\bar c_{k-1,k}$ agree with the published columns to about four digits for $k\le11$.

**Proposition 12.16 (the exponent is one number away from the coverage equation).** Let $\rho_k=\bar c_{k-1,k}/\bar c_{k,k}$ be the *lift retention*: the mean over classes of the worst lift, divided by the mean. At the critical $\lambda_k$ every constraint is tight. Summing them, using that $r\mapsto4r$ permutes the classes and that $m\mapsto(4m-2)/3$ and $m\mapsto(2m-1)/3$ are bijections onto the auxiliary classes, gives exactly
$$
4^{-\gamma_k}+\frac{\rho_k}{3}\Big(\big(\tfrac32\big)^{\gamma_k}+\big(\tfrac34\big)^{\gamma_k}\Big)=1 .
$$
At $\rho=1$ this is the coverage equation of Theorem 12.13 in two-step form, with roots exactly $\gamma=1$ and $\gamma=2$.

The summed inequality is in [Krasikov–Lagarias, §6], and it appears as a trace identity in [Nikpour–Rabbani, Lemma 4.1]. Their criticality of $\lambda=2$ (a Markov adjoint) is the root $\gamma=1$. What is added here is the second root $\gamma=2$ ($\lambda=4$), its reading as the trivial cycle's Størmer pair $(3,4)$, and the conversion factor below.

So the exponent depends on a single number. Without lift loss the method would give exponent $1$. Near $\rho=1$,
$$
1-\gamma\approx3.476\,(1-\rho),\qquad 3.476=\frac{\partial h/\partial\rho}{|\partial h/\partial\gamma|} .
$$
The denominator $|\partial h/\partial\gamma|=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac12\ln\tfrac43$ is the descent margin. The gap between the roots $1$ and $2$ is exactly the factor that converts lost lifts into lost exponent. Krasikov and Lagarias observed that $\lambda_k\to2$ if and only if $\rho_k\to1$; the identity makes this quantitative.

**Observation 12.17 (lift loss decays at least like $1/k$).** From our critical vectors for $k=9,\dots,14$, and from the certified exponents of [Nikpour–Rabbani] at $k=19,20$ via the identity of Proposition 12.16:

| $k$ | $9$ | $10$ | $11$ | $12$ | $13$ | $14$ | $19$ | $20$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $k(1-\rho_k)$ | $0.618$ | $0.628$ | $0.631$ | $0.629$ | $0.626$ | $0.620$ | $\le0.568$ | $\le0.555$ |

The last two are upper bounds: a certified $\gamma$ lies below the critical one, and the $\rho$ solving the identity increases with $\gamma$.
- **Shape.** $k(1-\rho_k)$ peaks near $k=11$ and then decreases, so the loss decays slightly faster than $1/k$.
- **The deficit at $\lambda=2$.** [Nikpour–Rabbani, §7] tabulate a related quantity, the deficit $\delta_k=1-\rho_k(2)$ of the Collatz–Wielandt value at $\lambda=2$. It behaves the same way: $k\delta_k=0.72,\,0.73,\,0.72,\,0.70,\,0.66,\,0.63,\,0.59$ for $k=8,10,\dots,20$. They also report a spectral gap $\approx0.32/k$ for the linear pieces of the operator [Nikpour–Rabbani, Rem. 6.3].
- **Spread.** The loss is spread over all classes: the median is close to the mean, and even the $99.9\%$ quantile is only about $3\times$ the median. There is a mild excess near the $3$-adic attractor $-1$.
- **Prediction.** The law predicted $\gamma_{14}=0.8714$ before the computation; the computed value is $0.8725$.

**Conjecture 12.18 (lift retention).** $\sup_kk(1-\rho_k)<\infty$.

The conjecture would give $\gamma_k\to1$, and hence $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, the goal stated by Krasikov and Lagarias. By [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 4.2] every single level has $\gamma_k<1$, so exponent $1$ can only be a limit. Their Proposition 6.1 also shows that, for eigenvector certificates, the implied constants degrade geometrically in $k$. Proving the conjecture is a statement about the dependence of the worst-case Perron vector on its top $3$-adic digit.

**Fence.** Theorem 12.15 is a computer-certified application of [Krasikov–Lagarias, Thm. 2.2], superseded by [Nikpour–Rabbani]. Observation 12.17 is empirical, and Conjecture 12.18 is open.

### 12.9 Expanding blocks, renewal, and the return map {#sec:12-9}

This subsection records results obtained in a parallel session of this program (Astro, 2026-10-17). The affine-word and valuation arguments are standard, and no originality is claimed for them. Write $U(n)=(3n+1)/2^{a(n)}$ with $a(n)=v_2(3n+1)$ for the odd-to-odd map.

**The height budget.** For an odd orbit $n_{i+1}=U(n_i)$ with $A_r=\sum_{i<r}a(n_i)$,
$$
\log\frac{n_r}{n_0}=r\log3-A_r\log2+\sum_{i<r}\log\Big(1+\frac1{3n_i}\Big).
$$
The Collatz conjecture is equivalent to: every odd $n_0>1$ has some $r$ with $A_r\log2$ exceeding the rest. That is first descent, and strong induction finishes. This is a reformulation, but it names what any geometric lemma must supply. The frame metric cannot do it alone, because it identifies $n$ with $nP_N$. The height $\log n$ of Proposition 2.4 has to be restored, and so does residue information modulo powers of $2$: for example, $7$ and $11$ are both unit frame vectors, yet $U(7)=11$ and $U(11)=17$.

**Corollary 12.19 (finite prime support forces periodicity).** If every member of a positive integer orbit has its prime factors in a fixed finite set, the orbit is eventually periodic. Hence a non-eventually-periodic positive orbit must involve infinitely many primes.

*Proof.* At every odd iterate $n$ the integers $3n$ and $3n+1$ are smooth over the set, enlarged by $\{2,3\}$, and they are consecutive. Størmer's theorem leaves finitely many odd values, which must repeat. $\square$

This sharpens Theorem 12.5. In the frame filtration, another positive cycle is a closed orbit inside one frame, and divergence is an orbit escaping every frame. Prime births alone are not a contradiction, though: a proof would have to show that repeated frame escape forces height loss.

**Proposition 12.20 (repetition of an expanding block is finite and exactly counted).** Let a word $w=(a_1,\dots,a_m)$ act on its admissible integers by $F_w(n)=(3^mn+C)/2^S$, with $S=\sum a_i$, and suppose it is expanding, $D=3^m-2^S>0$. Then the maximal number of consecutive repetitions of $w$ from a positive odd $n$ is
$$
k_{\max}=\Big\lfloor\frac{v_2(Dn+C)-1}{S}\Big\rfloor ,
$$
and for $w=(1)$ this is $v_2(n+1)-1$.

*Proof.* Let $\xi_w=-C/D\in\mathbb Z_2$ be the fixed point of $F_w$, the block's negative rational cycle point (Theorem 12.8). Since $D$ is odd, $v_2(Dn+C)=v_2(n-\xi_w)$.
- *One application.* Following $w$ exactly prescribes $S+1$ parity bits of the shortcut map: the bits of the steps inside the block, plus the oddness of the output. By the parity-vector bijection [Lagarias], the integers that follow $w$ form one class modulo $2^{S+1}$. The point $\xi_w$ follows $w$ forever, so it lies in that class. Hence $n$ follows $w$ exactly when $v_2(n-\xi_w)\ge S+1$.
- *Each repetition.* $F_w(n)-\xi_w=(3^m/2^S)(n-\xi_w)$, so each application removes exactly $S$ from the valuation.

So $k$ repetitions are possible exactly when $v_2(n-\xi_w)\ge kS+1$. $\square$

Repetition therefore consumes the $2$-adic precision with which $n$ approximates $\xi_w$, and a positive integer cannot repeat an expanding block forever. The classical runs $2^M-1\mapsto3^j2^{M-j}-1$ show that the count is unbounded.

**Renewal.** For odd $M\ge3$, put $x_M=(2^{M+2}-5)/3$. Then $v_2(x_M+1)=1$ and $U(x_M)=2^M-1$. A single contracting step thus renews the expansion capacity from $0$ to $M-1$, and $U^M(x_M)=2\cdot3^{M-1}-1>x_M$. For example $41\to31\to47\to71\to107\to161$. A proof must therefore control successive renewals along one orbit, not single blocks.

**The return map.** On $E=\{x\equiv1\pmod4\}$ put $a=v_2(3x+1)$, $y=(3x+1)/2^a$ and $b=v_2(y+1)$. The first return to $E$ is
$$
R(x)+1=\frac{3^{b-1}(3x+1+2^a)}{2^{a+b-1}},\qquad
\log\frac{R(x)+1}{x+1}=b\log3-(a+b-1)\log2+\log\Big(1+\frac{2^a-2}{3(x+1)}\Big).
$$
In the shifted height $\log(n+1)$, which belongs to the crystal point of $n+1$ (the $+1$ walk's partner), every expanding step is exact: $U(n)+1=\tfrac32(n+1)$. The open problem is now concrete. Control the coupled valuations $a=v_2(3x+1)$ and $b=v_2(3x+1+2^a)-a$ along the returns of a single orbit, and show that the accumulated height change becomes negative. There is no bound on $b$ in terms of $a$ alone, as the renewal family shows.

**Odd cores and the next return (Argon, 2026-10-17).** For $x\in E$ write $y+1=2^bu$ with $u$ odd, the *odd core* of the return. Then
$$
R(x)+1=2\cdot3^{b-1}u,\qquad 3R(x)+1=2\,(3^bu-1),
$$
so the next return has $a'=1+v_2(3^bu-1)$, and its valuation $b'$ and odd core $u'$ satisfy
$$
2^{a'-1+b'}u'=3^bu+2^{a'-1}-1 .
$$
*Proof.* $3x+1+2^a=2^a(y+1)=2^{a+b}u$; substitute this into the formula for $R$. Then $3R(x)+1=3(R(x)+1)-2$, and $y'+1=(3^bu-1)/2^{a'-1}+1$. $\square$
- *The block leaves a power of $3$ on the partner.* The expanding block multiplies the $+1$ partner by $3^{b-1}$, so $v_3(R(x)+1)\ge b-1$.
- *The renewal family does not chain.* It has $u=1$ and odd $b=M$. Then $3^M-1\equiv2\pmod4$ and $3^M+1\equiv4\pmod8$, so its next return always has $(a',b')=(2,1)$. This is not a bound on successive renewals: by the parity-vector bijection [Lagarias], every finite sequence of return shapes is followed by exactly one residue class of starting values modulo a power of $2$.
- *Worst-case returns are $+1$ steps.* When $a'=2$, the recurrence is the single $+1$ step $3^bu\to3^bu+1=2^{b'+1}u'$, from a $3$-heavy to a $2$-heavy point of the crystal. Any prime dividing $u$ and $u'$ divides $2^{a'-1}-1$, so the cores on either side of such a return are coprime.
- *Cycles counted by returns.* A cycle with $r$ returns to $E$ has $r$ local minima, so it is an $r$-cycle in the sense of [Simons–de Weger]. For $r=1$ the recurrence reads $(2^{a-1+b}-3^b)\,u=2^{a-1}-1$, and Steiner's theorem says its only positive solution is the trivial cycle $a=2$, $b=1$, $u=1$ [Steiner]. Nontrivial $r$-cycles are excluded for $r=2$ [Simons], for $r\le68$ [Simons–de Weger] and for $r\le91$ [Hercher]. All of these proofs use linear forms in logarithms.

**The bridge identity (Argon, 2026-10-17).** For consecutive odd iterates $n_{i-1}\to n_i\to n_{i+1}$ of $U$, adding $3n_{i-1}+1=2^{a_{i-1}}n_i$ and $3n_i+1=2^{a_i}n_{i+1}$ with opposite signs removes the $+1$:
$$
3n_{i-1}+2^{a_i}n_{i+1}=\big(2^{a_{i-1}}+3\big)\,n_i .
$$
A prime $p\ge5$ dividing $n_i$ divides neither neighbour, since $3n_{i-1}\equiv-1$ and $2^{a_i}n_{i+1}\equiv1\pmod p$. Taking valuations, $v_p(n_i)=v_p(3n_{i-1}+2^{a_i}n_{i+1})-v_p(2^{a_{i-1}}+3)$. Moreover $\gcd(n_{i-1},n_{i+1})$ divides $2^{a_{i-1}}+3$: a prime can come back after one odd step only through the bridge factor. For example, $35\to53\to5$ returns to the $5$-chamber because $2+3=5$. In the crystal, a prime $p\ge5$ occupies the odd iterates one vertex at a time, and the telescoping of Lemma 12.3 is this two-step cancellation at each vertex.

**First crossing.** Suppose the slope of a word first contracts at its end: $S_j\log2<j\log3$ for $1\le j<m$, but $S\log2>m\log3$. Then every term of $C/3^m=\sum_{j<m}2^{S_j}/3^{j+1}$ is at most $\tfrac13$, so $C/3^m\le m/3$. Hence $F_w(n)<n$ as soon as
$$
n\Big(\frac{2^S}{3^m}-1\Big)>\frac m3 .
$$
The unresolved cases are words very close to the line $S\log2=m\log3$, at small $n$. Removing $n$ from this bound would be a form of Terras's coefficient stopping-time conjecture [Rozier–Terracol].

Exact checks are in `empirical_01g/attack_expanding_blocks.py`: $44000$ block/start pairs, $100$ renewal cases, $10000$ return-map inputs, $12000$ odd-core and $100000$ bridge cases, all passing.

### 12.10 Climbing the levels {#sec:12-10}

The exponents of Section 12.8 come one $3$-adic digit at a time. This subsection measures what one more digit buys. Write $\Phi^{(k)}_\gamma$ for the operator of Proposition 12.14 at modulus $3^k$, acting on the live residues ($3\nmid r$), and $\rho_k(\gamma)$ for its Perron value. Let $\gamma_k$ be the critical exponent, so $\rho_k(\gamma_k)=1$, and $c_k>0$ a critical vector, $\Phi^{(k)}_{\gamma_k}(c_k)=c_k$. Its *lift* to modulus $3^{k+1}$ is $c_k'(r)=c_k(r\bmod3^k)$. A *policy* $\iota$ fixes, in every row, one of the three lifts over which $\Phi$ takes its minimum; $M_\iota$ is the resulting nonnegative matrix and $u_\iota$ its left Perron vector.

**Theorem 12.21 (the lift-gain formula; the exponents increase strictly).** For every $k\ge3$:
- (a) **The lift is a supersolution.** $\Phi^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_k}(c_k')=c_k'+g_k$ with $g_k\ge0$ and $g_k\ne0$.
- (b) **The exact gain.**
$$
\rho_{k+1}(\gamma_k)=1+G_k,\qquad G_k=\min_\iota\frac{\langle u_\iota,g_k\rangle}{\langle u_\iota,c_k'\rangle}>0 .
$$
- (c) **Strict increase.** $\gamma_{k+1}\ge\gamma_k+\log_2(1+G_k)>\gamma_k$. So $\lambda_k=2^{\gamma_k}$ increases strictly with $k$. So does the lift retention $\rho_k$, because by Proposition 12.16 it is an increasing function of $\gamma_k$ on $[0.6,1]$ (the function $R$ of Theorem 12.22).

Krasikov and Lagarias proved $\lambda_k\le\lambda_{k+1}$ by the lift of part (a), and wrote that it "remains an open problem to show that the values $\lambda_k$ are strictly increasing in $k$" [Krasikov–Lagarias, §6; Nikpour–Rabbani, Rem. 4.3]. They also asked whether the supremum $\lambda_k$ is attained with all principal inequalities tight, which they called likely but possibly difficult to prove. The Perron eigenvector settles this [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 2.3].

*Proof.* (a) Let $r$ be a row modulo $3^{k+1}$ and $r_0=r\bmod3^k$.
- *The doubling term* of $c_k'$ at $r$ equals that of $c_k$ at $r_0$.
- *The odd term.* The lifted vector is constant on every lift triple modulo $3^{k+1}$. So the minimum in $\Phi^{(k+1)}$ is the value of $c_k$ at $\tau(r)=(2r-1)/3\bmod3^k$. That residue is one of the three lifts over which $\Phi^{(k)}$ minimizes at $r_0$.

Hence $\Phi^{(k+1)}(c_k')(r)\ge\Phi^{(k)}(c_k)(r_0)=c_k(r_0)$. As $r$ runs over the three lifts of $r_0$, $\tau(r)$ runs over all three lifts of $(2r_0-1)/3\bmod3^{k-1}$, since $\tau(r+3^k)=\tau(r)+2\cdot3^{k-1}$.

So $g_k\equiv0$ would force $c_k$ to be constant on every lift triple, that is, to depend only on $r\bmod3^{k-1}$. The descent of [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 4.2] then applies:
- if $c_k$ depends only on $r\bmod3^\ell$ with $\ell\ge2$, comparing the eigen-equations at $r$ and $r+3^\ell$ shows that it depends only on $r\bmod3^{\ell-1}$;
- at $\ell=1$, $c_k$ takes one value $A$ on $r\equiv1$ and one value $B$ on $r\equiv2\pmod3$. The rows $r\equiv1\pmod3$ and $r\equiv5\pmod9$ have no odd term, and they give $A=2^{-\gamma_k}B$ and $B=2^{-\gamma_k}A$. So $\gamma_k=0$, a contradiction.

(b) For each policy, $\Phi^{(k+1)}\le M_\iota$ pointwise, with equality at the Perron vector of $\Phi^{(k+1)}$ for the policy that attains the minima there. Hence $\rho_{k+1}=\min_\iota\rho(M_\iota)$. Each $M_\iota$ is irreducible, because doubling is a single cycle on the units modulo $3^{k+1}$, so $u_\iota>0$. Since $c_k'$ is constant on lift triples, $M_\iota c_k'=c_k'+g_k$ for *every* $\iota$. Pairing with $u_\iota$ gives $\rho(M_\iota)=1+\langle u_\iota,g_k\rangle/\langle u_\iota,c_k'\rangle$, and this exceeds $1$ by (a).

(c) For $h\ge0$, every weight of $\Phi_{\gamma+h}$ is at least $2^{-h}$ times the corresponding weight of $\Phi_\gamma$. So $\rho_{k+1}(\gamma_k+h)\ge2^{-h}(1+G_k)\ge1$ whenever $2^h\le1+G_k$. $\square$

Certified for $3\le k\le12$ (L1–L4): the formula of (b) holds to $10^{-6}$ relative, with $\rho_{k+1}(\gamma_k)$ computed independently by power iteration.

**Theorem 12.22 (what one level must buy).** Let $H_k=\sum g_k/\sum c_k'$ be the Haar average of the gain, and $\nu_k=G_k/H_k$ its *visibility* to the worst policy.
- (a) **Exact Haar gain.** With $\rho_k$ the lift retention of Proposition 12.16,
$$
H_k=\frac{(3/2)^{\gamma_k}}3\,(1-\rho_k)=2^{-\gamma_k}+\frac{(3/2)^{\gamma_k}}3-1 .
$$
- (b) $\sum_{k\ge3}\log_2(1+G_k)\le1-\gamma_3<0.39$. Hence $\sum_k\nu_k(1-\rho_k)<0.92$.
- (c) Suppose $G_k\ge f(1-\rho_k)$ for all large $k$, for some increasing $f$ with $f(x)>0$ for $x>0$. Then $\gamma_k\to1$, so $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $a\not\equiv0\pmod3$. If $f(x)=cx^2$, then moreover $1-\gamma_k\le1/(c\,\ln^2\!\tfrac43\,(k-3))$.
- (d) The same conclusion holds if $\sum_k\nu_k=\infty$. For example, it holds if $\sum_{K\le k<2K}\nu_k\ge c>0$ for all large $K$.

Part (a) and the analytic bound on $R'$ below are due to Astro (parallel session, 2026-10-17). They sharpen an earlier lower bound $H_k\ge\tfrac14(1-\rho_k)$.

*Proof.* (a) At modulus $3^{k+1}$ the residue $\tau(r)$ runs once through every class modulo $3^k$. So the odd terms of $\Phi^{(k+1)}(c_k')$ sum to $(3/2)^{\gamma_k}\sum c_k$, the doubling terms sum to $3\cdot2^{-\gamma_k}\sum c_k$, and $\sum c_k'=3\sum c_k$. This gives the second expression.

For the first, sum the critical equation at level $k$: $\sum c_k=2^{-\gamma}\sum c_k+(3/2)^\gamma\sum_j\min_{L(j)}c_k$. The retention over all units, $3\sum_j\min/\sum c_k$, equals $\rho_k$. Indeed, the classes $r\equiv1\pmod3$ carry $2^{-\gamma}$ times the values of their doubles, and doubling maps their triples onto the principal ones, so numerator and denominator acquire the same factor $1+2^{-\gamma}$. Hence
$$
\rho_k=R(\gamma_k),\qquad R(\gamma)=\frac{3(1-2^{-\gamma})}{(3/2)^\gamma}=\frac{3(1-4^{-\gamma})}{(3/2)^\gamma+(3/4)^\gamma},
$$
which is the identity of Proposition 12.16. Rearranged, it is (a).

(b) The increments telescope, and $\gamma_k<1$. [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 4.2] prove this; directly, at $\gamma=1$ the summed equation reads $\sum\Phi_1(c)=\tfrac12\sum c+\tfrac32\sum_j\min\le\sum c$. Equality would require every lift triple to be constant, which the descent in the proof of Theorem 12.21(a) excludes. So the sum of increments is at most $1-\gamma_3$, with $\gamma_3=0.6113$. Each $G_k\le2^{0.39}-1<1$, and $\log_2(1+x)\ge x$ on $[0,1]$. By (a), $H_k\ge\tfrac13(3/2)^{\gamma_3}(1-\rho_k)>0.427(1-\rho_k)$.

(c) By (b), $f(1-\rho_k)\to0$, so $\rho_k\to1$. Since $R$ is continuous, $R(1)=1$ and $R<1$ on $[0,1)$, we get $\gamma_k\to1$, and [Krasikov–Lagarias, Thm. 2.2] gives the bound. For the rate,
$$
R'(\gamma)=3\,(2/3)^\gamma\big(2^{-\gamma}\ln3-\ln\tfrac32\big)\ge2\big(\tfrac12\ln3-\ln\tfrac32\big)=\ln\tfrac43\quad\text{on }[0,1].
$$
So $1-\rho_k\ge e_k\ln\tfrac43$ with $e_k=1-\gamma_k$. Then $e_{k+1}\le e_k-c\ln^2(\tfrac43)\,e_k^2$, hence $1/e_{k+1}\ge1/e_k+c\ln^2\tfrac43$.

(d) If $1-\rho_k\ge\eta>0$ for all large $k$, then (b) gives $\sum\nu_k<\infty$. So $\liminf(1-\rho_k)=0$, $\gamma_k\to1$ along a subsequence, and therefore everywhere by Theorem 12.21. Disjoint dyadic blocks give the example. $\square$

So the exponent problem of Krasikov and Lagarias reduces to a single two-level inequality: the worst choice of lifts at modulus $3^{k+1}$ must not hide *all* of what the level-$k$ vector loses at its top digit. Any rate will do. The same exponent $x^{1-o(1)}$ would also follow from Tao's question on the smallest atom of the Syracuse random variable, $\beta=1$ [Tao 2020; Nikpour–Rabbani].

**Remark (the drain is the lift).** A backward odd step from an integer $x$ lands on the fraction $(2x-1)/3$, with $3$-coordinate $-1$, unless $x\equiv2\pmod3$. Fractions never return backward (Proposition 12.1(b)).
- Along a backward word with $L$ steps, $m$ of them odd, the descendant $(2^Lx-S)/3^m$ is integral for exactly one class of $x$ modulo $3^m$.
- Integrality of that descendant implies integrality of every node between it and $x$, since $T$ maps $\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z$.

So the residue of $x$ modulo $3^k$ decides which descendants with at most $k$ odd backward steps stay integral, and no more. Level $k$ of the Krasikov–Lagarias program is the drain into the fraction lattice of Section 11, predicted $k$ odd steps ahead. The minimum over lifts is the worst case for the next wall crossing, and the lift loss measures how much the drain depends on the digit not yet seen.

**Remark (the shape of the place completion).** The reduction has the shape of the place-completion keystone [P0].
- *There*, positivity at each finite level is free, because the finite Fourier transform is unitary. The Riemann hypothesis is positivity surviving the projective limit once the archimedean factor is adjoined.
- *Here*, criticality at each finite level is free for the *averaged* operator: at $\gamma=1$ Haar measure is exactly invariant [Nikpour–Rabbani, Lemma 4.1]. The minimum over lifts is the truncation, and $x^{1-\varepsilon}$ is criticality surviving the limit $k\to\infty$.
- The weights $2^{-\gamma}$ and $(3/2)^\gamma$ are archimedean sizes. So $\gamma$ plays the role of the archimedean coordinate, as $s$ does in [P0].

The parallel is structural, and no transfer of results between the two problems is claimed.

**Conjecture 12.24 (two-level gain).** There is $c>0$ with $G_k\ge c\,(1-\rho_k)^2$ for every $k\ge3$.

By Theorem 12.22(c), Conjecture 12.24 implies $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ with $1-\gamma_k=O(1/k)$. Its retention form, $\rho_{k+1}-\rho_k\ge A(1-\rho_k)^2$ (observed with $A\ge1.15$), is a logistic recursion. It gives $1/(1-\rho_{k+1})\ge1/(1-\rho_k)+A$, which implies Conjecture 12.18 directly. So the $1/k$ law of Observation 12.17 has a dynamical source: each level recovers a fixed multiple of the square of what the previous level lost.

Conjecture 12.24 is a statement about one level at a time. It asks that no choice of lifts can steer its left Perron vector into the region where the level-$k$ vector is already flat at its top digit, beyond a second-order remainder. The data support it with a margin that grows with $k$. Certified in L1–L6.

**Remark (the point $-1$ at three places).** On the odd branch $T(x)+1=\tfrac32(x+1)$, so $-1$ is the fixed point whose multiplier $\tfrac32$ has absolute values $2$, $\tfrac13$ and $\tfrac32$ at the places $2$, $3$ and $\infty$; their product is $1$. It is the cycle $\{-1\}$, the Størmer pair $(2,3)$ of Theorem 12.4. The three sizes are three phenomena already met in this section:
- **At $2$** it repels. The forward expansion reserve of $n$ is its $2$-adic closeness to $-1$, $v_2(n+1)-1$ steps (Proposition 12.20 for $w=(1)$).
- **At $3$** it attracts forward, so the backward odd branch $(2x-1)/3$ repels from it. The critical vectors grow toward $-1$ by the archimedean weight $(3/2)^{\gamma}$ per $3$-adic digit [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 6.1]. At $k=11$ the minimum over classes of depth $v=v_3(r+1)$ grows by factors between $1.42$ and $1.58$ for $v\ge2$, against $(3/2)^{\gamma_{11}}=1.41$. This is the excess of lift loss near $-1$ noted in Observation 12.17.
- **At $\infty$** the weight $(3/2)^\gamma$ is the archimedean size of the multiplier, raised to the coverage exponent.

The law of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ shows the same point. Its largest atom modulo $3^n$ sits at $-1$ and grows like $(3/2)^n$ relative to the mean, for $n\le13$.

**Remark (the Syracuse law as a self-similar measure).** $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ is the stationary law of the maps $y\mapsto2^{-a}(1+3y)$, chosen with probability $2^{-a}$ [Tao, Lemma 1.12]. Every map contracts $\mathbb Z_3$ by exactly $3$, and all their images are the same ball $2+3\mathbb Z_3$: the overlaps are complete, as in a Bernoulli convolution.
- **Entropy excess.** The entropy of the choice is $2\ln2$ and the contraction is $\ln3$, so the excess is $\ln\tfrac43$, the trivial cycle's comma again. For $5x+1$ it would be $\ln\tfrac45<0$, which matches the failure of coverage for $a=5$ (Theorem 12.13).
- **Computed exactly modulo $3^n$ for $n\le13$.**
  - The Shannon entropy is $n-1.02$ trits, so the dimension is full.
  - The collision probability times the number of units grows exactly linearly, by $0.31$ per digit. That is the borderline case for an $L^2$ density, with a logarithmic divergence.
  - The smallest atom times the number of units decreases slowly, from $0.050$ at $n=8$ to $0.033$ at $n=13$; the value at $n=5$ reproduces $c_5$ of [Nikpour–Rabbani].
  - The largest atom is pinned at $-1$, as above. The smallest atoms all lie in the class $x\equiv-2\pmod{81}$ ($3$-adic digits $1,2,2,2$) for $5\le n\le12$, and spread out below that scale.

  The mechanism of the minimum is the drain. For $x\equiv-2\pmod9$ the heaviest branch available to the class $x\equiv1\pmod3$, namely $a=2$ with weight $\tfrac14$, sends $x$ to $(4x-1)/3\equiv0\pmod3$, where $\mathrm{Syrac}$ has no mass. So the largest atom is where the $a=1$ branch fixes $-1$, and the smallest is where the $3$-wall kills the $a=2$ branch.

Whether the excess $\ln\tfrac43$ forces the smallest atom to stay within $3^{-n(1+o(1))}$ is Tao's question $\beta=1$. In this language it is the $3$-adic analogue of absolute continuity for Bernoulli convolutions with entropy above the Lyapunov exponent.

### 12.11 The linear model, and what Conjecture 12.24 needs {#sec:12-11}

An attempt to prove Conjecture 12.24 leads to the following exact facts, and to a precise account of what is still missing.

**Proposition 12.25 (the Syracuse law is the averaged Perron vector).** Let $\mu_n$ be the law of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ modulo $3^n$. Replace the minimum over lifts in the operator of Proposition 12.14, at $\gamma=1$, by the average. Then $\mu_n$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $1$:
$$
\mu_n(r)=\tfrac12\,\mu_n(2r)+[r\equiv2\ (3)]\,\tfrac32\operatorname*{avg}_{t\in L(r)}\mu_n(t).
$$

*Proof.* The three lifts carry $\mu_{n-1}((2r-1)/3)$ in total, so the right side is $\tfrac12\mu_n(2r)+[r\equiv2]\tfrac12\mu_{n-1}((2r-1)/3)$. In the stationarity $Y=2^{-a}(1+3Y')$ [Tao, Lemma 1.12], separate $a=1$ from $a\ge2$:
$$
P(Y\equiv r)=\tfrac12P(1+3Y'\equiv2r)+\tfrac12\sum_{a\ge1}2^{-a}P\big(2^{-a}(1+3Y')\equiv2r\big).
$$
The first term is $[r\equiv2]\tfrac12\mu_{n-1}((2r-1)/3)$, and by stationarity at $2r$ the second is $\tfrac12\mu_n(2r)$. $\square$

So at the critical exponent, the Krasikov–Lagarias operator is the Syracuse law's own equation with *average over the unseen digit* replaced by *minimum over it*. Nikpour and Rabbani reach the same identification through their digit-clock operator [Nikpour–Rabbani, Lemma 5.2, Thm. 1.3]. Two consequences:
- **Deficit equals half the lift loss.** Write $\ell(v)=3\sum_j(\operatorname{avg}-\min)/\sum v$ for the lift loss over all live classes. For the Perron vector $v_n$ of $\Phi^{(n)}_1$, the trace identity gives exactly $\rho_n(1)=1-\tfrac12\ell(v_n)$. For the Syracuse law itself, $\sum\Phi^{(n)}_1(\mu_n)=1-\tfrac12\ell(\mu_n)$.
- **A $1/k$ law in the computed range, which is pre-asymptotic.** Computed exactly for $n\le13$, $n\,\ell(\mu_n)$ rises to $2.04$, $2.08$, $2.11$, $2.14$, $2.15$, $2.16$ for $n=8,\dots,13$, and the total-variation distance of the top digit from uniform, averaged over the classes below, behaves like $0.9/n$. Asymptotically the decay is faster than every power of $n$, by Tao's fine-scale mixing (Corollary 12.37). So the $1/k$ behaviour of the minimum operator (Observation 12.17) is not inherited, asymptotically, from the Syracuse law.

**Proposition 12.26 (absolute continuity forces vanishing lift loss).** If the law of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Haar measure, then $\ell(\mu_n)\to0$.

*Proof.* Let $f\in L^1(\mathbb Z_3)$ be the density, and $E_n$ the conditional expectation onto residues modulo $3^n$. By $L^1$ martingale convergence, $E_nf\to f$ in $L^1$, so $\|E_nf-E_{n-1}f\|_1\to0$. On every lift triple, the excess of the average over the minimum is at most the sum of the absolute deviations from the average. Hence $\ell(\mu_n)\le3\|E_nf-E_{n-1}f\|_1$. $\square$ The hypothesis holds, by Tao's theorem (Corollary 12.37).

**Lemma 12.27 (monodromy: perfect alignment is impossible).**
- Along doubling twice, the backward images satisfy $\tau(4r)=4\tau(r)+1$ exactly.
- The map $A(x)=4x+1$ is an odometer on $\mathbb Z_3$: $v_3(A^Nx-x)=v_3(N)$ for every $x$.
- So $A^{3^{k-1}}$ fixes every class modulo $3^{k-1}$ and moves every class modulo $3^k$.
- Hence no choice of one lift per class modulo $3^{k-1}$ is carried to itself by $A$ all the way around its cycle.

*Proof.* $A^Nx-x=(4^N-1)(x+\tfrac13)=\tfrac{4^N-1}3(3x+1)$. Here $3x+1$ is a $3$-adic unit, and $v_3(4^N-1)=1+v_3(N)$ by the lifting-the-exponent lemma. $\square$

In the language of Theorem 12.21: a row's successor under doubling twice is a zero-gain row exactly when the minimizing lift is transported by $A$. So the zero-gain lanes of the worst policy must break somewhere on every cycle. This gives a second proof that $g_k\ne0$, but no rate.

**Proposition 12.28 (the top digit of the Syracuse law is twisted by archimedean wrap-around).** For a context $j\in\mathbb Z/3^{n-1}$ with representative $J\in[0,3^{n-1})$, let
$$
Z_n(j)=\sum_{i=0}^2\mu_n(J+i\,3^{n-1})\,\omega^i,\qquad\omega=e^{2\pi i/3},
$$
be the Fourier coefficient of its top digit. Let $c(j)=[\,2J\ge3^{n-1}\,]$ be the bit that records whether doubling $J$ wraps around the modulus. Then
$$
Z_n(j)=\tfrac12\,\omega^{c(j)}\,\overline{Z_n(2j)}+\tfrac12\,[j\equiv2\ (3)]\,\omega^{c(j)}\,\overline{Z_{n-1}(\tau j)},\qquad\tau j=\tfrac{2J-1}3\bmod3^{n-2}.
$$
Consequently $\|Z_n\|_1\le\|Z_{n-1}\|_1$.

*Proof.* Substitute Proposition 12.25 into the definition.
- *Doubling* sends the lift of index $i$ over $j$ to the lift of index $c(j)+2i$ over $2j$.
- *The odd branch* sends it to the lift of index $c(j)+2i$ over $\tau j$, since $(2J-1)/3\ge3^{n-2}$ exactly when $2J\ge3^{n-1}$.

Since $2\equiv-1\pmod3$, reindexing turns $\omega^i$ into $\omega^{c}\,\overline{\omega^{i'}}$. For the norm inequality: doubling permutes $\mathbb Z/3^{n-1}$, and $j\mapsto\tau j$ is a bijection from the classes $j\equiv2$ onto $\mathbb Z/3^{n-2}$. Then apply the triangle inequality and sum. $\square$

The three places meet in this one recursion.
- The context is a $3$-adic residue, and its lowest digit decides whether the odd branch exists: this is the drain.
- Doubling and the odd branch are the backward Collatz moves on residues.
- The twist counts archimedean wrap-arounds, the binary digit of the real number $J/3^{n-1}$.

On mean-zero vectors over $\mathbb Z/3$, $|Z|$, the total variation and the excess of mean over minimum are equivalent norms. So the lift loss of the Syracuse law tends to $0$ exactly when $\sum_n\kappa_n=\infty$, where
$$
\kappa_n=1-\frac{\|Z_n\|_1}{\|Z_{n-1}\|_1}
$$
is the *alignment defect*: the relative failure of the two terms of the recursion to point in the same direction. Computed exactly:
- $n\kappa_n=0.46,\ 0.60,\ 0.68,\ 0.69,\ 0.74,\ 0.76,\ 0.79,\ 0.83,\ 0.89,\ 0.93,\ 0.99$ for $n=3,\dots,13$, rising, so the series diverges in the computed range.
- The defect sits entirely on the contexts $j\equiv2\pmod3$, where both terms exist, and it is spread evenly over the archimedean position $J/3^{n-1}$.
- The relative top-digit coefficient is uniformly small: its mass-weighted median is $0.11$ and its $99.9\%$ quantile $0.33$ at $n=12$.
- **The defect is small because of imbalance, not alignment.** The angle between the two terms is spread almost uniformly; its mass-weighted mean square is $3.25$, against $\pi^2/3=3.29$ for uniform angles. The defect stays small because the two terms are rarely comparable in size. Between $64\%$ and $70\%$ of their combined mass lies in pairs where one term is less than $\tfrac1{20}$ of the other. The $5\%$ of mass in pairs within a factor $2$ of each other carries about $30\%$ of the defect.
- **Faster than harmonic.** The local exponent of $\kappa_n\propto n^{-p}$ falls from $p\approx0.46$ ($n=8$ to $13$) to $p\approx0.35$ ($n=11$ to $13$). So $n\kappa_n$ is still rising, and the series diverges with room to spare in the computed range. Divergence itself is a theorem (Corollary 12.37), and the falling local exponent fits the super-polynomial decay of $\|Z_n\|_1$ given there.
- **A twist between the two terms.** Unrolling the first term gives $\tfrac14\omega^{c(j)-c(2j)}Z_n(4j)$. So the second wrap-around bit $c(2j)$ twists one term against the other, and the partial phase coherence between $Z_n(4j)$ and $Z_{n-1}(\tau j)$ differs sharply with that bit. Its mean resultant is $0.60$, $0.46$, $0.40$ at $n=9,11,13$ when $c(2j)=0$, against $0.09$ to $0.16$ when $c(2j)=1$.

**Remark (limits of operator-norm arguments; Astro).** Write the recursion as $Z_n=T_nZ_{n-1}$ with $T_n=(2I-U_n)^{-1}V_n$, where $U_n$ is the twisted doubling and $V_n$ the twisted odd branch. On complex vectors with the sum of moduli, $\|T_n\|=(1-q)/\sqrt{1+q+q^2}$ with $q=2^{-2\cdot3^{n-2}}$, and single-context inputs attain it. So every level contracts, but only by a summable amount, and no input-independent bound $1-c/n$ holds. For blocks of fixed length $m$, $\|T_{n+m}\cdots T_{n+1}\|\to1$ as $n\to\infty$, because distinct short backward words separate modulo $3^{n+m-1}$. Regularity therefore has to come from the actual Syracuse field, as it does in Tao's proof (Corollary 12.37). (Exact witnesses: `syracuse_block_separation_exact.py`; norm checks: `syracuse_phase_energy_probe.py`.)

**Remark (the top digit is the primitive conductor packet; Argon).** The units modulo $3^n$ form a cyclic group generated by $2$, so doubling is diagonal in its character basis: for $f$ on $(\mathbb Z/3^n)^\times$ and a character $\psi$, the coefficient of $f(2\,\cdot)$ at $\psi$ is $\psi(2)\hat f(\psi)$. For a unit $J$, the lift triple $\{J+i\,3^{n-1}\}$ is the coset $J\cdot\{1+i\,3^{n-1}\}$ of the kernel of reduction modulo $3^{n-1}$. A function is therefore constant on every lift triple exactly when its coefficients vanish on the $4\cdot3^{n-2}$ primitive characters of conductor $3^n$, those with $\psi(1+3^{n-1})=\omega^{\pm1}$ [T8, Sect. 1.3].
- So the top-digit field $Z_n$ of Proposition 12.28 is the primitive conductor-$3^n$ packet of $\mu_n$.
- The wrap-around twist $c(j)$ belongs to the additive coordinates: in the character basis it is absorbed into $\psi(2)$.
- The odd branch $j\mapsto(2j-1)/3$ is affine, so it mixes packets through Gauss sums [T8, Thm. 4.1]. The minimum of Proposition 12.14 is not linear at all.

The change of basis relocates the difficulty of Conjecture 12.45; by itself it gives no estimate.

**Proposition 12.30 (uniform cap on the top digit).** For every $n\ge2$ and every context $j$ with $\mu_{n-1}(j)>0$, the relative top-digit coefficient $z_n(j)=Z_n(j)/\mu_{n-1}(j)$ satisfies
$$
|z_n(j)|\le\frac1{\sqrt7},
$$
with equality at $n=2$. More precisely (Astro), the conditional law of the top digit given the context lies in the hexagon spanned by the six permutations of $(2,8,11)/21$, and $1/\sqrt7$ is the radius of its circumscribed circle. So every conditional top-digit probability lies in $[\tfrac2{21},\tfrac{11}{21}]$, and sibling atoms of the Syracuse law differ by a factor of at most $\tfrac{11}2$; both bounds are attained at $n=2$, where the conditional laws are $(8,11,2)/21$ and $(8,2,11)/21$ [Tao, after Lemma 1.12].

*Proof.* Write $Y=\sum_{m\ge1}3^{m-1}2^{-A_m}$ with $A_m=a_1+\dots+a_m$. Split $a_{n-1}=p+2k$ with $p\in\{1,2\}$. Given $p$, $k$ is geometric with $P(k)=\tfrac34\,4^{-k}$, independent of everything else. Let $\mathcal G$ be generated by $a_1,\dots,a_{n-2}$ and $p$.
- *The context is $\mathcal G$-measurable.* The terms $m\le n-2$ are, and the term $m=n-1$ enters $Y\bmod3^{n-1}$ only through $2^{-A_{n-1}}\bmod3=(-1)^{A_{n-2}+p}$.
- *Shifting $k$.* Since $4^{-k}\equiv1-3k\pmod9$, shifting $k$ multiplies $2^{-A_{n-1}}$ by a unit that is $1$ modulo $3$. It therefore moves only the top digit, by $-uk\pmod3$, where $u=2^{-(A_{n-2}+p)}\bmod3$.
- *The parity of $a_n$.* Since $4^{-k}\equiv1\pmod3$, it moves the top digit by $u\varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon=(-1)^{a_n}$, through $3^{n-1}2^{-A_n}$.

So the top digit is $D=X_0+u(\varepsilon-k)$, with $X_0$ and $u=\pm1$ $\mathcal G$-measurable and $k,\varepsilon$ independent of $\mathcal G$. The residue $k\bmod3$ has law $(16,4,1)/21$, and $\varepsilon=+1$ with probability $\tfrac13$, $-1$ with probability $\tfrac23$. So $\varepsilon-k\bmod3$ has law $(2,8,11)/21$, and given $\mathcal G$ the law of $D$ is a permutation of it, with
$$
\big|E[\omega^D\mid\mathcal G]\big|=\Big|\sum_k\tfrac34\,4^{-k}\omega^{-uk}\Big|\cdot\Big|\tfrac13\omega^{u}+\tfrac23\omega^{-u}\Big|=\frac3{\sqrt{21}}\cdot\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac1{\sqrt7}.
$$
Since the context is $\mathcal G$-measurable, the conditional law of $D$ given $j$ is an average of these, which proves both statements. $\square$

So the top digit is never far from uniform, and $z_n=\tfrac1{\sqrt7}E[e^{i\psi}\mid j]$ is the average, over the backward histories consistent with the context, of a unit *carry phase* $\psi$. Decay of $z_n$ is decorrelation of that phase across histories. The computed maximum of $|z_n|$ decreases from $1/\sqrt7=0.378$ to $0.329$ at $n=13$.

**Proposition 12.31 (the chi-square energy identity).** Let $V_n=\sum_j\mu_{n-1}(j)\,|z_n(j)|^2$, the $\chi^2$-energy of the top digit. For a context $j\equiv2\pmod3$, let
$$
\alpha_j=\frac{\mu_{n-1}(2j)}{2\mu_{n-1}(j)}=P(a_1\ge2\mid j),
$$
the posterior weight of the doubling branch. Then
$$
V_n=V_{n-1}-2\Delta_n,\qquad\Delta_n=\sum_{j\equiv2}\mu_{n-1}(j)\,\alpha_j(1-\alpha_j)\,\big|z_n(2j)-z_{n-1}(\tau j)\big|^2 .
$$

*Proof.* Dividing Proposition 12.28 by $\mu_{n-1}(j)$ gives $z_n(j)=\omega^c\,\overline{z_n(2j)}$ for $j\equiv1$, and $z_n(j)=\alpha_j\,\omega^c\overline{z_n(2j)}+(1-\alpha_j)\,\omega^c\overline{z_{n-1}(\tau j)}$ for $j\equiv2$. Apply $|\alpha X+(1-\alpha)Y|^2=\alpha|X|^2+(1-\alpha)|Y|^2-\alpha(1-\alpha)|X-Y|^2$. Sum with weights $\mu_{n-1}(j)$, using $\mu_{n-1}(j)\alpha_j=\tfrac12\mu_{n-1}(2j)$ and $\mu_{n-1}(j)(1-\alpha_j)=\tfrac12\mu_{n-2}(\tau j)$. Doubling permutes the contexts and $\tau$ is a bijection; solving the resulting linear relation gives the identity. $\square$

So the energy falls by exactly *posterior balance* $\alpha(1-\alpha)$, the uncertainty about whether the last backward step was odd, times the *squared difference* of what the two branches say about the top digit. The top digit tends to uniform, in $\chi^2$ and hence in total variation, exactly when
$$
\sum_n r_n=\infty,\qquad r_n=\frac{2\Delta_n}{V_{n-1}}.
$$
Computed exactly, $r_n=0.276$, $0.247$, $0.223$, $0.193$, $0.177$, $0.160$, $0.150$, $0.140$, $0.135$, $0.129$, $0.125$ for $n=3,\dots,13$. So $n\,r_n$ rises from $0.83$ to $1.63$, faster than harmonic. The divergence is a theorem (Corollary 12.37).

**Certificates for the gain, and why the new digit is essential.** The next two results are due to Astro (parallel session, 2026-10-17). A lower bound for $G_k$ can be certified without left vectors. If a vector $v$ at modulus $3^{k+1}$ satisfies
$$
0\le v\le c_k',\qquad\Phi^{(k+1)}_{\gamma_k}(v)\ge v+\epsilon\,c_k',
$$
then $G_k\ge\epsilon$. Indeed $M_\iota v\ge\Phi(v)$ for every policy; pairing with $u_\iota$ and using $u_\iota\cdot v\le u_\iota\cdot c_k'$ gives $\rho(M_\iota)-1\ge\epsilon$. Finding $v$ is a finite linear program.

**Proposition 12.29 (old-digit obstruction).** No $v$ lifted from modulus $3^k$, even a signed one, satisfies these inequalities with $\epsilon>0$.

*Proof.* Let $v$ be the lift of $w$. For a fixed row modulo $3^k$, take the minimum of the inequalities over its three lifts. Their odd targets run over the three lifts at level $k$, and the other terms descend, so $\Phi^{(k)}_{\gamma_k}(w)\ge w+\epsilon c_k$. A level-$k$ policy $M_*$ with spectral radius $1$ exists by Theorem 12.21(b) applied at level $k$. Pairing $M_*w\ge\Phi^{(k)}(w)$ with its positive left vector gives $0\ge\epsilon\,u_*\cdot c_k>0$. $\square$

So every certificate must use the new digit. A formula in a fixed number of low digits cannot work at all levels.
- **Unrestricted programs.** For $k=2,\dots,6$, $v$ unrestricted recovers $66\%$ to $77\%$ of the computed $G_k$. All $2178$ row inequalities of these certificates were verified in exact rational arithmetic, with proved rational lower bounds for $2^{-\gamma}$ and $(3/2)^\gamma$. Rerunning the checker passes.
- **Compact ansätze.** These all gave zero margin at $k\ge3$: the lift from the previous level (by Proposition 12.29), a function of the depth $v_3(r+1)$ alone, and functions of the new top digit together with one or two low digits.

A level-uniform certificate must therefore carry the new digit's variation along doubling, which is the twisted transport of Proposition 12.28.

**What Conjecture 12.24 needs.** The conjecture asks for a rate at which the top-digit variation of the critical vectors shrinks, together with the fact that the worst policy cannot hide that variation in the aligned remainder.
- Its linear model is the top-digit regularity of the Syracuse law. By Proposition 12.28 it is equivalent to $\sum_n\kappa_n=\infty$, and in the form of Proposition 12.31 to $\sum_nr_n=\infty$. **It is a theorem**: Tao's fine-scale mixing [Tao, Prop. 1.14] gives it at a super-polynomial rate (Corollary 12.37). An earlier draft of this section said the known estimates fall short of it. That was an error, caught in an audit by Astro: Tao's Proposition 1.14 is a total-variation statement at every scale, equivalent to his Fourier bound (his Remark 1.18), not only a bound on individual coefficients. The proof of Proposition 12.30 shows what local moves can and cannot do.
- *Local moves.* A move that changes only the top digit and preserves the measure, such as shifting a single $A_m$ by $2\cdot3^{n-m-1}$ and compensating in a neighbouring step, needs a step longer than $2\cdot3^{n-m-1}$. That happens only with probability about $2^{-2\cdot3^{n-m-1}}$. So such moves exist cheaply only for the last one or two steps. They give the constant cap $1/\sqrt7$ and no decay.
- *Global moves.* Decay has to come from global coincidences: different backward histories with the same residue modulo $3^{n-1}$ but different top digits. Their abundance is the entropy excess $\ln\tfrac43$ per digit, since a context leaves about $0.29n$ nats of history undetermined. Section 12.12 measures them directly. They are sibling coincidences of the map $x\mapsto4x+1$.

These coincidences do scramble the carry phase: in total variation at a super-polynomial rate (Tao), and in collision weight only at a non-summable rate, which the data put at $1/(2n)$ (Section 12.12).
- The measured entropy deficit of $\mu_n$ appears to converge. Its increments fall from $0.026$ at $n=9$ to $0.015$ at $n=13$, roughly like $n^{-1.6}$, a summable rate. Absolute continuity holds (Corollary 12.37).
- In $L^2$ the regularity is exactly borderline. The collision growth of $0.31$ per digit is the critical twin coefficient of Theorem 12.33, and Theorem 12.38 traces it to the fairness of the Collatz step.

So the averaged model is settled. What Conjecture 12.24 needs is the transfer from the average to the minimum. Section 12.13 records exact reductions of that transfer to two entropy estimates. The transfer is not proved here.

### 12.12 Collisions: twins and siblings {#sec:12-12}

The global coincidences of Section 12.11 can be measured directly, and they have a name. Let $Y,Y'$ be independent copies of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$, with
$$
K_n=\sum_x\mu_n(x)^2=P(Y\equiv Y'\bmod 3^n),\qquad f_n=\tfrac23\,3^nK_n ,
$$
so $f_n$ is the number of units modulo $3^n$ times the collision probability, the quantity of check S3 ($f_0=\tfrac23$). Let
$$
A(x)=4x+1,\qquad A^k(x)=4^kx+\frac{4^k-1}3\quad(k\in\mathbb Z).
$$
This is the **sibling map** of the Collatz tree: for odd $m$, $3(4m+1)+1=4(3m+1)$, so $4m+1$ and $m$ have the same Syracuse image. It is also the odometer of Lemma 12.27. Put
$$
C_m(k)=P\big(Y\equiv A^kY'\bmod3^m\big),\qquad c_m(k)=\tfrac23\,3^mC_m(k),\qquad F_n=\sum_{k\ge1}4^{-k}c_{n-1}(k).
$$
Finally let $E_n=\sum_j|Z_n(j)|^2$, and let $W_n=E_n/K_{n-1}=\sum_j\mu_{n-1}(j)^2|z_n(j)|^2\big/\sum_j\mu_{n-1}(j)^2$ be the *collision-weighted* top-digit energy. It is the analogue of $V_n$ in which each context is weighted by $\mu_{n-1}(j)^2$ instead of $\mu_{n-1}(j)$.

**Theorem 12.32 (twins and siblings).** Two backward histories can agree modulo $3^n$ only if their first steps differ by an even number $2k$ of halvings. If $k=0$ (*twins*), their tails must agree modulo $3^{n-1}$. If $k\ne0$ (*siblings*), the tails must satisfy $Y_2\equiv A^kY_2'\pmod{3^{n-1}}$. Consequently, for $n\ge1$,
$$
K_n=\tfrac13K_{n-1}+\tfrac23\sum_{k\ge1}4^{-k}C_{n-1}(k),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad E_n=\sum_{k\ge1}4^{-k}C_{n-1}(k),
$$
and in normalized form
$$
f_n=f_{n-1}+2F_n,\qquad W_n=\frac{F_n}{f_{n-1}} .
$$

*Proof.* Write $Y=2^{-a}(1+3Y_2)$ and $Y'=2^{-a'}(1+3Y_2')$ with all four variables independent [Tao, Lemma 1.12].
- *Parity.* Modulo $3$ the condition $Y\equiv Y'$ reads $(-1)^a\equiv(-1)^{a'}$, so $a-a'=2k$ is even.
- *The tails.* Then $Y\equiv Y'\pmod{3^n}$ if and only if $1+3Y_2\equiv4^k(1+3Y_2')$, that is, $Y_2\equiv A^k(Y_2')\pmod{3^{n-1}}$. For $k<0$, $(4^k-1)/3$ is a $3$-adic integer because $4^k\equiv1\pmod3$.
- *Weights.* $P(a-a'=2k)=\sum_{a'}2^{-a'}2^{-a'-2|k|}=4^{-|k|}/3$, and $C_m(-k)=C_m(k)$ by exchangeability.

This gives the formula for $K_n$. On each context, $|\sum_ix_i\omega^i|^2=\tfrac32\sum_ix_i^2-\tfrac12(\sum_ix_i)^2$, so $E_n=\tfrac12(3K_n-K_{n-1})$, which gives the rest. $\square$

So the top-digit energy at level $n$ is *exactly* the geometric average of sibling coincidences at level $n-1$. An odd difference of first steps never collides. A colliding pair is a twin for some number of steps, then becomes a sibling pair. The local moves in the proof of Proposition 12.30 are sibling moves at the deepest level: shifting $k$ there replaces $a_{n-1}$ by $a_{n-1}+2$.

**Theorem 12.33 (the Syracuse law has no $L^2$ density).** The law of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ has no density in $L^2(\mathbb Z_3)$ with respect to Haar measure. Equivalently, the collision factor $f_n$ is nondecreasing and unbounded, and $\sum_nW_n=\infty$.

*Proof.* By Theorem 12.32, $f_n-f_{n-1}=2F_n\ge0$.

Suppose the law were $g\,d\mathrm{Haar}$ with $g\in L^2$, and let $\mathbb E_m$ be the conditional expectation onto balls of radius $3^{-m}$.
- *Collision factors converge.* $3^mK_m=\|\mathbb E_mg\|_2^2\to\|g\|_2^2$. Since $A$ is a Haar-preserving isometry that permutes the balls of each radius, also $3^mC_m(k)=\langle \mathbb E_mg,(\mathbb E_mg)\circ A^{-k}\rangle\to\langle g,g\circ A^{-k}\rangle$.
- *Pass to the limit.* Theorem 12.32 gives $f_n/f_{n-1}=1+2\sum_k4^{-k}c_{n-1}(k)/f_{n-1}$. The left side tends to $1$. The right side tends to $1+2\sum_k4^{-k}\langle g,g\circ A^{-k}\rangle/\|g\|_2^2$, by dominated convergence, since $0\le c_m(k)\le f_m$.
- *Contradiction.* Every term of that sum is $\ge0$ because $g\ge0$. By Lemma 12.27, $A^{3^j}$ moves every point by exactly $3^{-j}$, so $A^{3^j}\to\mathrm{id}$ uniformly, and $\langle g,g\circ A^{-3^j}\rangle\to\|g\|_2^2>0$ (approximate $g$ in $L^2$ by a locally constant function). So some term is positive, a contradiction.

Conversely, a bounded $f_n$ would make the densities $3^m\mu_m$ an $L^2$-bounded martingale, whose limit is an $L^2$ density. Finally, $f_n=f_0\prod_m(1+2W_m)$, so $f_n\to\infty$ exactly when $\sum W_m=\infty$. $\square$

*Why the threshold is exact.* The twin coefficient $\tfrac13=\sum_a4^{-a}$ is the collision probability of a single backward step. The Rényi-$2$ entropy of $\mathrm{Geom}(\tfrac12)$ is therefore exactly $\ln3$, the $3$-adic scale, while its Shannon entropy is $\ln4$ (the excess $\ln\tfrac43$ of Section 12.10). Twin collisions alone would keep $f_n$ constant, and every sibling coincidence increases it. This is the "borderline strength" of the collision growth noted in Section 12.11. *(Fenced heuristic: the Rényi-$q$ entropies $\ln(2^q-1)/(q-1)$ exceed $\ln3$ for $q<2$, which suggests an $L^q$ density for every $q<2$. This is not proved.)* We did not find Theorems 12.32–12.33 in [Tao], [Tao 2020], [Nikpour–Rabbani] or [Allikvere]. Tao's analysis controls individual Fourier coefficients, and absolute continuity of the limit law is not settled there.

**Proposition 12.34 (renewal: twins inherit, siblings forget).** Among the pairs that collide modulo $3^{n-1}$:
- (a) Twins have share $f_{n-2}/f_{n-1}$, and their top-digit coherence $E[\omega^{t-t'}]$ is $W_{n-1}$. Siblings with $a_1-a_1'=\pm2k$ have share $2\cdot4^{-k}c_{n-2}(k)/f_{n-1}$, and coherence $\tfrac12\big(c_{n-1}(k)/c_{n-2}(k)-1\big)$.
- (b) Hence $W_n=\big(f_{n-2}W_{n-1}+F_n-F_{n-1}\big)/f_{n-1}=F_n/f_{n-1}$. Twins pass their coherence down unchanged, and siblings contribute only through the growth of their own factor.
- (c) Therefore $W_n\to0$ if and only if $F_n=o(f_{n-1})$. By Theorem 12.33 this holds whenever $\sup_nF_n<\infty$. If $F_n\to F_\infty\in(0,\infty)$, then $f_n\sim2F_\infty n$ and $nW_n\to\tfrac12$, whatever the value of $F_\infty$.

*Proof.* (a) Twin pairs at level $n$ are pairs of tails at level $n-1$, with top digits transported up to a sign, because the lower digits agree. Sibling pairs carry the digit $n-2$ of $Y_2-A^kY_2'$. Apply the identity $\tfrac32\sum p_iq_i-\tfrac12\sum p\sum q$ to the lift triples of the two laws. The shares are those of Theorem 12.32 at level $n-1$. (b) Sum, and use $W_{n-1}=F_{n-1}/f_{n-2}$. (c) follows from (b) and $f_n=f_{n-1}+2F_n$. $\square$

**Observation 12.35 (measured, $n\le15$; checks S14–S17).**
- *The identity.* Theorem 12.32 holds to relative error $10^{-15}$ in float64, and exactly in rational arithmetic for $n\le7$.
- *Twins.* $f_n$ grows by $2F_n=0.3095,\dots,0.3139$ per digit for $n=4,\dots,15$, reaching $f_{15}=5.476$.
- *Siblings.* The sibling factors converge. By $m=14$, $c(2)=0.31551$, $c(3)=0.82469$, $c(4)=0.5736$, $c(5)=0.5100$ and $c(9)=1.4077$. Each $c_m(k)$ with $2\le k\le12$ moves by less than $5\times10^{-4}$ per level from $m=12$ on.
- *The exception $k=1$.* $c_m(1)$ rises by about $0.011/m$ per level, to $0.4854$ at $m=14$, consistent with slow logarithmic growth. So $F_n$ drifts slowly, from $0.1547$ at $n=3$ to $0.1570$ at $n=15$.
- *The $1/(2n)$ law.* $nW_n=0.29,0.32,\dots,0.456$ for $n=2,\dots,15$, rising toward $\tfrac12$.
- *Direct count.* We sampled $4\times10^6$ histories at $n=9$, with collisions taken modulo $3^8$. No colliding pair has an odd difference of first steps. The twin share is $0.9056$ against $0.9055$ exact, with coherence $+0.0520$ against $W_8=0.0522$. Siblings with $|a_1-a_1'|=2,4,6$ have shares $0.0725$, $0.0122$, $0.0079$ (exact $0.0727$, $0.0121$, $0.0079$) and coherence $+0.0011$, $+0.0003$, $-0.0015$.

So the global coincidences of Section 12.11 are sibling coincidences, and they carry no top-digit coherence. The decay of $W_n$ is a renewal. At each level, a fraction about $2F/f\approx1/n$ of the colliding pairs are fresh siblings, which forget the carry phase, while twins pass it down.

**Remark 12.36 (the $3$-adic tower as a Helmert ladder; the metric trick).**
- *The ladder.* Over each context $j$ modulo $3^{n-1}$ sits the lift triple $x_j=(\mu_n(J+i\,3^{n-1}))_{i=0,1,2}$. Descending to level $n-1$ records its sum $\mu_{n-1}(j)$ and keeps its zero-sum part. This is the reversible ladder of [T4], with three slots at every node of the $3$-adic tree. The top-digit coefficient $Z_n(j)$ is its Helmert detail, written as the complex coordinate of the zero-sum plane.
- *The metric trick.* With the exact-arithmetic metric $M_3=3I-J$ of [Paper 01, §7.5] and [T4, §2], we have $|Z_n(j)|^2=\tfrac12x_j^\top M_3x_j$. Quadray implementations use the same convention, $M=4I-J$ [Thomson].
- *Parseval is the collision bookkeeping.* The ladder's Parseval identity reads
$$
K_n=\tfrac13K_{n-1}+\tfrac13\sum_jx_j^\top M_3x_j,\qquad f_n-f_{n-1}=\tfrac49\,3^nE_n=2F_n .
$$
- *Everything is rational.* Every atom of $\mu_n$ is rational, so Theorem 12.32 and Proposition 12.30 can be checked with no $\omega$ and no radicals. The cap becomes the rational inequality $7\,x^\top M_3x\le2\,(\mathbf 1^\top x)^2$. Its constant is $7=\Phi_3(4)/3=(4^2+4+1)/3$, the Eisenstein norm $N(4-\omega)=21$ divided by $3$, and the $4$ is the multiplier of the sibling map. In unit normalization the cap is $1/\sqrt7$, and $\sqrt7$ is the radical residue of normalizing, in the sense of [T4].
- *Exact checks.* $f_1=\tfrac{10}9$ and $f_2=\tfrac{10}7$. The identities were checked exactly for $n\le7$, where the denominator of $E_7$ has $580$ digits (S15).
- *The recursion over $\mathbb Q$ (Astro).* Write $Z=u+v\omega$ with $u=x_0-x_2$ and $v=x_1-x_2$. The two carry operations $\omega^c\,\overline{Z}$ of Proposition 12.28 become the integer matrices $R_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$ and $R_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, so the recursion reads $z_n(j)=\tfrac12R_{c(j)}\big(z_n(2j)+[j\equiv2]\,z_{n-1}(\tau j)\big)$ with rational vectors. Both matrices preserve the hexagonal norm $H(u,v)=\max(|u|,|v|,|u-v|)=\tfrac12(|u|+|v|+|u-v|)$, which is the range of the lift triple. With $R_n=\sum_jH(z_n(j))$, one has $R_n\le\ell(\mu_n)\le2R_n$, and $R_{n-1}-R_n$ is exactly the total opposite-sign overlap $\sum_{j\equiv2}\sum_{s}\mathbf 1[\lambda_s(a_j)\lambda_s(b_j)<0]\min(|\lambda_s(a_j)|,|\lambda_s(b_j)|)$ of the forms $\lambda=u,v,u-v$ on the two branches $a_j=z_n(2j)$, $b_j=z_{n-1}(\tau j)$. So the decay of Corollary 12.37 is a statement about opposite signs meeting with enough amplitude, written with no radical and no cosine. Checked exactly for $n\le7$ (`prime_crystal_metric_ladder.py`).

**Corollary 12.37 (what Tao's fine-scale mixing gives; audit by Astro).** [Tao, Prop. 1.14] states that for all $1\le m\le n$ and every $A>0$,
$$
\sum_{r\bmod3^n}\big|\mu_n(r)-3^{m-n}\mu_m(r\bmod3^m)\big|\ll_Am^{-A}.
$$
Consequently:
- (a) the law of $\mathrm{Syrac}(\mathbb Z_3)$ is absolutely continuous, with a density $f\in L^1(\mathbb Z_3)$;
- (b) the top-digit total variation, the lift loss $\ell(\mu_n)$, $\|Z_n\|_1$ and $V_n$ all decay faster than every power of $n$, so $\sum_n\kappa_n=\infty$ and $\sum_nr_n=\infty$;
- (c) the $1/n$ laws measured for $n\le15$ in Sections 12.10–12.11 are pre-asymptotic. In collision weight the picture is the opposite: $\sum_nW_n=\infty$ (Theorem 12.33).

*Proof.* (a) For $g_n=3^n\mu_n$ on $\mathbb Z_3$ the left side is $\|g_n-g_m\|_1$, so the $g_n$ converge in $L^1$, and the limit is a density. (b) Take $m=n-1$. The left side is then twice the top-digit total variation. On each lift triple, $|Z|\le\sum_ix_i-3\min_ix_i\le3\sum_i|x_i-\bar x|$, so $\|Z_n\|_1\le\ell(\mu_n)$, and $\ell(\mu_n)$ is at most $3$ times the left side. Also $V_n=\sum_j\mu_{n-1}(j)|z_n(j)|^2\le\tfrac1{\sqrt7}\|Z_n\|_1$ by Proposition 12.30. Finally $\|Z_n\|_1=\|Z_1\|_1\prod_m(1-\kappa_m)$ and $V_n=V_2\prod_m(1-r_m)$ tend to $0$, so both series diverge. $\square$

So the top digit of the Syracuse law becomes uniform quickly for almost every context and slowly for the heavy ones. The density exists; the next theorem shows why it cannot be square-integrable, and why the dividing line is drawn by the multiplier $3$.

**Theorem 12.38 (the fair game: why $3$).** Let $a\sim\mathrm{Geom}(\tfrac12)$ be the number of halvings in one backward Syracuse step, and let
$$
M(s)=E\big[(3\cdot2^{-a})^s\big]=\frac{3^s}{2^{s+1}-1}\qquad(s>-1)
$$
be the $s$-th moment of the archimedean factor by which one Syracuse step multiplies size.
- (a) **Three places.** $M(s)=3^s\sum_aP(a)^{s+1}$. Because the $2$-adic probability $P(a)=|2^a|_2$ equals the archimedean factor $|2^{-a}|_\infty$ (the product formula at $2$), the archimedean moments of the step are the Rényi sums of its $2$-adic law, measured against the $3$-adic scale. $\log M$ is strictly convex, $M(s)=1$ exactly at $s=0$ and $s=1$, and $M'(0)=\ln3-2\ln2=-\ln\tfrac43$. The coverage equation of Theorem 12.13 is the equation $M(\theta-1)=1$: $g_3(\theta)-1$ and $M(\theta-1)-1$ have the same numerator $1+3^{\theta-1}-2^\theta$. Its two roots $\theta=1,2$ are $s=0,1$.
- (b) **Descent gives a density.** $M'(0)<0$ says that the step contracts size in geometric mean, by $\tfrac34$ (the trivial cycle's comma). Equivalently, the Shannon entropy $\ln4$ of the step law exceeds the $3$-adic scale $\ln3$, the entropy excess of Section 12.10. The Syracuse law has a density (Corollary 12.37).
- (c) **Fairness denies $L^2$.** $M(1)=1$ says that one Syracuse step preserves size exactly on average. Equivalently, the collision entropy of the step law equals $\ln3$: $\tfrac14+\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{64}+\cdots=\tfrac13$. This is the twin coefficient of Theorem 12.32, so the density is not square-integrable (Theorem 12.33). Moreover $3^nP(Y\equiv Y'\bmod3^n)$ grows at least linearly in $n$.
- (d) **Why $3$.** For an odd multiplier $q\ge3$, let $Y_q=\sum_jq^{j-1}2^{-A_j}\in\mathbb Z_q$ be the offset variable of the map $n\mapsto(qn+1)/2^a$, with moments $M_q(s)=q^s/(2^{s+1}-1)$. If $q\ge5$, the law of $Y_q$ is singular, carried by a set of Hausdorff dimension at most $\log_q4$, and its collision factor $q^nP(Y_q\equiv Y_q'\bmod q^n)$ grows at least like $(q/3)^n$. So $q=3$ is the only odd multiplier whose step descends in geometric mean ($M_q'(0)=\ln\tfrac q4<0$), the only one whose step is fair ($M_q(1)=\tfrac q3=1$), and the only one whose Syracuse law has a density. That density sits exactly on the $L^2$ threshold.

*Proof.* (a) $E[(3\cdot2^{-a})^s]=\sum_a2^{-a}3^s2^{-sa}=3^s/(2^{s+1}-1)$. The second derivative of $\log M$ is $(\ln2)^2\,2^{s+1}/(2^{s+1}-1)^2>0$, so $M=1$ has at most two roots, and $s=0,1$ are roots. The common numerator is direct. (b) The Shannon entropy is $-\sum P\ln P=E[a]\ln2=2\ln2$, and $E\ln(3\cdot2^{-a})=\ln3-E[a]\ln2$. (c) $M(1)=3\sum_a4^{-a}$ is three times the twin coefficient. For the growth, by Theorem 12.32, $f_n-f_{n-1}=2F_n\ge2\cdot4^{-K}c_{n-1}(K)$ for any $K$. With $g_m=3^m\mu_m$, we have $c_m(K)=\tfrac23\int g_m\,(g_m\circ A^{-K})$, and $\int gh\ge(\int\min(g,h))^2$ for densities $g,h$ on the probability space $\mathbb Z_3$. By Corollary 12.37(a), $\int\min(g_m,g_m\circ A^{-K})\to\int\min(f,f\circ A^{-K})=1-\tfrac12\|f-f\circ A^{-K}\|_1$. For $K=3^j$ with $j$ large this is at least $\tfrac12$, because $A^{3^j}\to\mathrm{id}$ (Lemma 12.27). So $F_n\ge\delta>0$ for large $n$, and $f_n\ge2\delta n-O(1)$. (d) The descent and fairness statements are (a) with $3$ replaced by $q$. *Singularity:* $-\ln P(a_1,\dots,a_n)=(\ln2)\sum_ia_i$, so by Cramér's theorem the histories with $\sum_ia_i\le n(2+\epsilon)$ carry all but an exponentially small part of the probability. There are at most $4^{n(1+\epsilon)}$ of them, and $Y_q\bmod q^n$ is a function of the history. By Borel–Cantelli, $Y_q$ almost surely lies, for all large $n$, in a union of at most $4^{n(1+\epsilon)}$ balls of radius $q^{-n}$. Their Haar measure tends to $0$ when $q>4^{1+\epsilon}$, which gives singularity and the dimension bound. *Collision growth:* twins alone give $K_n\ge\tfrac13K_{n-1}$ in base $q$, so $q^nK_n\ge\tfrac q3\,q^{n-1}K_{n-1}$. $\square$

The fairness of the Collatz step, $E[T(n)/n]\to1$ for the shortcut map, is classical. What is new here is that, through the product formula, it is the same equation as the $L^2$ threshold of the $3$-adic Syracuse law, just as the classical descent $E\ln(T(n)/n)<0$ is the same equation as its entropy excess. Both are read off one function $M$. Its two zeros of $\log M$ are the two roots of the coverage equation, and the Størmer pair $(3,4)$ produces both: $4-1=3$.

**Proposition 12.39 (the $L^2$ transition of the averaged Krasikov–Lagarias family).** At exponent $\gamma$, the averaged operator of Proposition 12.25, $c\mapsto2^{-\gamma}c_{2r}+[r\equiv2](3/2)^\gamma\operatorname{avg}_{t\in L(r)}c_t$, has eigenvalue $\Lambda(\gamma)=2^{-\gamma}+(3/2)^\gamma/3$. Its Perron vector is the biased Syracuse law with steps $P(a)=(1-q)q^{a-1}$, where $q=q(\gamma)=1/(1+3^{\gamma-1})$ (Astro). For this law, Theorem 12.32 holds with twin weight $(1-q)/(1+q)$ and sibling weights $q^{2k}(1-q)/(1+q)$. The normalized twin coefficient is
$$
\theta(\gamma)=\frac{3(1-q)}{1+q}=\frac{3^\gamma}{2+3^{\gamma-1}} ,
$$
which is $<1$ for $\gamma<1$, $=1$ at $\gamma=1$, and $>1$ for $\gamma>1$. For $\gamma>1$ the collision factor grows at least like $\theta(\gamma)^n$, so the law is not of full $L^2$ dimension. **The exponent $\gamma=1$ that the Krasikov–Lagarias program must reach is exactly the $L^2$ threshold of its own averaged Perron family.**

*Proof.* The biased law satisfies $p(r)=q\,p(2r)+(1-q)[r\equiv2]\,p_{k-1}(\tau r)$, since $a=1$ with probability $1-q$ and $a-1$ has the same law otherwise. The three lifts carry $p_{k-1}(\tau r)$ in total, so this is the eigen-equation with $q=2^{-\gamma}/\Lambda$ and $1-q=(3/2)^\gamma/(3\Lambda)$. The collision computation of Theorem 12.32 goes through with $P(a-a'=2k)=q^{2|k|}(1-q)/(1+q)$. $\square$

Numerically (S19), the collision factors of the laws below the threshold converge: their ratios are $1.009$ and $1.000$ at $\gamma=0.63$ and $0.23$ ($n=9$). This is consistent with square-integrable densities there, but not proved.

**Status.** The averaged model is settled. Tao's theorem gives the top digit's regularity in total variation, while in collision weight it holds only at the non-summable rate of Theorem 12.33. Whether $F_n$ stays bounded now concerns only the exact order of the $L^2$ divergence: $f_n\asymp n$, or $n\log n$ if the creep of $c_m(1)$ persists. It is no longer a step of the Collatz program. What Conjecture 12.24 needs is the transfer from the average to the minimum.

### 12.13 The minimum transfer {#sec:12-13}

The reductions in this section are due to Astro (parallel session, 2026-10-17; probes `policy_concentration_parameter_probe.py` and `syracuse_tao_bridge_exact.py` in `empirical_01g/kl`). Write $r_k(\gamma)=\min_\iota\rho(M_{\iota,\gamma})$ for the eigenvalue of the minimum operator at level $k$, $\beta=(3/2)^\gamma$, and $p_\gamma$ for the averaged Perron vector of Proposition 12.39.

**Proposition 12.40 (transfer bound, and why it must use minimizing policies).**
- (a) For a policy $\iota$ with left Perron vector $u_\iota$, put $C_\iota=\max_ru_\iota(r)/(u_\iota\cdot p_\gamma)$. Then $\rho(M_{\iota,\gamma})\ge\Lambda(\gamma)-\tfrac\beta3\,C_\iota\,\ell_k(p_\gamma)$. In particular $r_k(\gamma)\ge\Lambda(\gamma)-\tfrac\beta3C_*\ell_k(p_\gamma)$, with $C_*$ taken over minimizing policies.
- (b) Over all policies no polynomial bound on $C_\iota$ is possible. At $\gamma=1$, choosing $r$ itself as the odd target at $r\equiv-1$ is admissible, since $3r\equiv2r-1$. This policy has a diagonal entry $\tfrac32$, so $\rho\ge\tfrac32$. Since $|\rho(M_{\iota,1})-1|\le\tfrac32C_\iota R_k$, where $R_k$ is the sum of the ranges of the lift triples of $\mu_k$, we get $\max_\iota C_\iota\ge1/(3R_k)$, which grows faster than every power of $k$ by Corollary 12.37.

*Proof.* (a) $M_\iota p\ge\Phi_\gamma p=\Lambda p-d$, where $d=\beta(\operatorname{avg}-\min)$ on odd rows and $\sum_rd_r=\tfrac\beta3\ell_k(p)$. Pair with $u_\iota$. (b) Pair $M_{\iota,1}\mu-\mu$, whose sum of absolute values is at most $\tfrac32R_k$, with $u_\iota$. $\square$

Since $\Lambda(\gamma)>1$ for $0\le\gamma<1$, (a) shows: *if $C_*\ell_k(p_\gamma)\to0$ for each fixed $\gamma<1$, then $\gamma_k\to1$, and $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ by Theorem 12.22.* The self-loop policy in (b) never minimizes, so the needed bound concerns minimizing policies only.

**Theorem 12.41 (the parameter identity; Astro).** Fix a policy $\iota$ with Perron root $\rho(\gamma)$ and right and left Perron vectors $x,u$. Give each labelled edge $e:r\to s$ (doubling or odd) of weight $w_e$ the probability $P_e=w_ex_s/(\rho\,x_r)$, let $\pi_r=u_rx_r/(u\cdot x)$, and let $h(\gamma)=-\sum_e\pi_{r(e)}P_e\log P_e$ be the entropy of this stationary edge process. Then
$$
\frac{d}{d\gamma}\Big(\frac{\log\rho(\gamma)}{\gamma}\Big)=-\frac{h(\gamma)}{\gamma^2}.
$$
Consequently $\log r_k(\gamma)/\gamma$ is strictly decreasing, the critical exponent $\gamma_k$ is the unique root of $r_k=1$, and
$$
-\log r_k(1)=\int_{\gamma_k}^1\frac{h_k^*(t)}{t^2}\,dt ,
$$
where $h_k^*$ is the entropy of an active minimizing policy. In particular $\gamma_k\ge\eta_k/(\eta_k-\log r_k(1))$, where $\eta_k$ is the essential infimum of $h_k^*$ on $[\gamma_k,1]$.

*Proof.* Differentiating $M x=\rho x$ and pairing with $u$ gives $d\log\rho/d\gamma=-\ln2+\vartheta\ln3$, where $\vartheta$ is the stationary frequency of odd edges. Substituting $P_e$ into $h$, stationarity cancels the $\log x$ terms and leaves $h=\log\rho+\gamma\ln2-\gamma\vartheta\ln3$. Combine the two. Every policy has a row with two positive edges, so $h>0$. The integral formula follows by integrating along the lower envelope, which is Lipschitz and switches policies finitely often. $\square$

This is the thermodynamic formalism of the Krasikov–Lagarias exponent: $\log\rho/\gamma$ is a pressure, and its slope is minus an entropy.

**What remains (Astro's targets).** By Corollary 12.37, Proposition 12.40 and Theorem 12.41, $\gamma_k\to1$ follows from two entropy estimates, neither of which is proved:
- *endpoint concentration:* for an endpoint-minimizing policy, the weights $q_r=u_r\mu_r/(u\cdot\mu)$ satisfy $D(q\Vert\mu)=O(\log k)$, or merely $o(\log(1/\ell_k))$. A Jensen argument gives $1-r_k(1)\le L(D+\ln2)/\ln(L/m_k)$, with $L=\tfrac57$ and $m_k=\ell_k/2$;
- *parameter stability:* the entropy of active minimizing policies stays above a fixed $h_0>0$ on $[\gamma_k,1]$.

Measured for $k=2,\dots,9$, the relative entropy $D(q\Vert\mu)$ grows irregularly from $0.17$ to $2.99$, and the policy entropy stays near $0.23$. These are floating-point diagnostics, not certificates. Section 12.14 replaces both estimates by a single inequality, and shows why the relative entropy was the wrong quantity to bound.

### 12.14 The comparison target {#sec:12-14}

The endpoint estimate is about how strongly the worst policy concentrates on the defect. Measuring it directly shows two things. The bound $1-r_k(1)\le E_q[e]$ of Section 12.13 is nearly sharp: the ratio is $0.89$ to $1.0$ for $4\le k\le10$. The concentration itself is the wrong measure, though. The occupation weights $q$ concentrate exponentially, with $\max q/\mu$ growing roughly geometrically, but they concentrate *away* from the defect: $E_q[e]$ is only about half of $E_{\mu}[e]$. The adversary avoids the hot regions where the Syracuse law is irregular, because those are the regions of fast growth. What has to be bounded is therefore not concentration but *correlation with the defect*. One exact identity turns that into a comparison between two explicit vectors.

**Proposition 12.42 (the deficit is the lift loss, at every exponent).** For $k\ge2$ and $\gamma>0$, let $v=v_k(\gamma)$ be a positive eigenvector of $\Phi^{(k)}_\gamma$, with eigenvalue $r_k(\gamma)$ [Nikpour–Rabbani, Prop. 2.3]. Let $\rho_k(\gamma)=3\sum_j\min_{L(j)}v/\sum v$ be its lift retention, so that its lift loss is $1-\rho_k(\gamma)$. Then
$$
\Lambda(\gamma)-r_k(\gamma)=\frac{(3/2)^\gamma}3\,\big(1-\rho_k(\gamma)\big),\qquad\Lambda(\gamma)=2^{-\gamma}+\frac{(3/2)^\gamma}3 .
$$
At $\gamma=\gamma_k$, where $r_k=1$, this is Theorem 12.22(a).

*Proof.* The averaged operator $A_\gamma$ (Proposition 12.39) has the uniform vector as a left eigenvector with eigenvalue $\Lambda(\gamma)$. Each unit $t$ receives $2^{-\gamma}$ from $t/2$ and $(3/2)^\gamma/3$ from the odd row $(3t+1)/2$. On odd rows, $A_\gamma v-\Phi_\gamma v=(3/2)^\gamma(\operatorname{avg}-\min)$ over the target triple, and the odd rows run once over the lift triples. Summing $A_\gamma v=r_kv+(A_\gamma v-\Phi_\gamma v)$ over all units gives $\Lambda\sum v=r_k\sum v+(3/2)^\gamma\big(\tfrac13\sum v-\sum_j\min\big)$. $\square$

**Theorem 12.43 (the comparison would give $x^{1-\varepsilon}$).** Suppose that for some $\gamma_0<1$, all large $k$, and all $\gamma\in[\gamma_0,1]$,
$$
1-\rho_k(\gamma)\ \le\ \ell(\mu_k), \tag{comparison}
$$
where $\ell(\mu_k)$ is the lift loss of the Syracuse law. Then $1-\gamma_k\le\ell(\mu_k)/\ln\tfrac43$ for all large $k$. Consequently, by Tao's theorem (Corollary 12.37), $\gamma_k\to1$ faster than every power of $1/k$, and $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $a\not\equiv0\pmod3$ [Krasikov–Lagarias, Thm. 2.2]. The same holds if the comparison is weakened to $1-\rho_k(\gamma)\le k^B\ell(\mu_k)$ for a fixed $B$.

*Proof.* $\Lambda$ is convex, with $\Lambda(1)=1$ and $\Lambda'(1)=-\tfrac12\ln\tfrac43$, so $\Lambda(\gamma)\ge1+\tfrac12\ln\tfrac43\,(1-\gamma)$. Since $(3/2)^\gamma/3\le\tfrac12$ for $\gamma\le1$, Proposition 12.42 and the comparison give
$$
r_k(\gamma)\ \ge\ 1+\tfrac12\Big(\ln\tfrac43\,(1-\gamma)-\ell(\mu_k)\Big),
$$
which exceeds $1$ whenever $1-\gamma>\ell(\mu_k)/\ln\tfrac43$. By Theorem 12.41, $r_k(\gamma)>1$ forces $\gamma<\gamma_k$. The weakened form only replaces $\ell(\mu_k)$ by $k^B\ell(\mu_k)$, which still decays faster than every power. $\square$

So the minimum transfer needs neither an entropy floor nor a biased form of Tao's theorem. One inequality between two explicit vectors at each level is enough: the Perron vector of the minimum operator must lose at its top digit no more than the Syracuse law does.

**Observation 12.44 (the comparison in the data; S21–S22).**
- *The ratio.* $(1-\rho_k(\gamma))/\ell(\mu_k)$ was computed for $2\le k\le11$ and $\gamma\in\{0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9,0.95,1\}$, and for $k=12,13$ at $\gamma\in\{0.9,0.95,1\}$. It is at most $0.666$ (at $k=2$, $\gamma=1$), and at most $0.530$ for $k\ge3$. It increases with $\gamma$. At $\gamma=1$ it falls from $0.530$ to $0.416$ ($k=3$ to $13$); at $\gamma=\tfrac12$ it falls from $0.305$ to $0.102$ ($k=3$ to $11$). Proposition 12.42 holds to $4\times10^{-13}$ at every computed point.
- *Lockstep.* For $3\le k\le14$ the exponent deficit tracks the Syracuse lift loss: $(1-\gamma_k)/\ell(\mu_k)=0.712,\ 0.704,\ 0.724,\ 0.748,\ 0.768,\ 0.774,\ 0.793,\ 0.806,\ 0.815,\ 0.819,\ 0.826,\ 0.829$. The comparison would give at most $1/\ln\tfrac43=3.48$.
- *The turn.* $k\,\ell(\mu_k)=2.114,\ 2.137,\ 2.152,\ 2.157,\ 2.155$ for $k=10,\dots,14$. It has peaked, as Corollary 12.37 requires it eventually to. Together with the lockstep, this is the first sign that the observed $1/k$ behaviour of the exponents is pre-asymptotic.
- *Iterating from the averaged vector.* Iterate $\Phi$ from the averaged Perron vector ($k=5,7,9$; $\gamma=0.9,1$). The lift loss never exceeds its initial value, and it settles at $0.43$ to $0.47$ of it.
- *Minimality is needed.* The tangent inequality $\rho(M_\iota)\ge\langle\mathbf 1,M_\iota p\rangle/\langle\mathbf 1,p\rangle$ holds with a clear margin for minimizing policies. It fails for some random policies, so a proof must use minimality.

**Conjecture 12.45 (comparison).** For all $k\ge2$ and $\gamma\in[\tfrac12,1]$, the Krasikov–Lagarias vector loses at its top digit at most what the Syracuse law loses:
$$
1-\rho_k(\gamma)\le\ell(\mu_k).
$$
Equivalently, $r_k(\gamma)\ge\Lambda(\gamma)-\tfrac{(3/2)^\gamma}3\,\ell(\mu_k)$. At $\gamma=1$, it says $r_k(1)\ge\sum_r\Phi_1(\mu_k)_r$: the minimum operator never grows more slowly than in its first step from the Syracuse law.

By Theorem 12.43, Conjecture 12.45 implies $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$. The data hold it with a factor of about $2.4$ to spare. Its content is that the worst choice of lifts smooths the top digit at least as much as averaging does, which is the mechanism visible in the occupation data above.

Three exact facts organize the attack on Conjecture 12.45. The third shows that minimality alone cannot close it.

**Proposition 12.46 (the level recursion at a fixed exponent).** Fix $\gamma>0$ and $k\ge2$. Let $c'$ be the lift to modulus $3^{k+1}$ of $v_k(\gamma)$, and let $g=\Phi^{(k+1)}_\gamma(c')-r_k(\gamma)c'\ge0$. Then
$$
r_{k+1}(\gamma)=r_k(\gamma)+G_k(\gamma),\qquad G_k(\gamma)=\min_\iota\frac{\langle u_\iota,g\rangle}{\langle u_\iota,c'\rangle},\qquad\frac{\langle\mathbf 1,g\rangle}{\langle\mathbf 1,c'\rangle}=\Lambda(\gamma)-r_k(\gamma).
$$
So, with the visibility $\nu_k(\gamma)=G_k(\gamma)/(\Lambda(\gamma)-r_k(\gamma))\in[0,1]$,
$$
\Lambda(\gamma)-r_k(\gamma)=\big(\Lambda(\gamma)-r_2(\gamma)\big)\prod_{j=2}^{k-1}\big(1-\nu_j(\gamma)\big).
$$
Consequently, if $\sum_j\nu_j(\gamma)=\infty$ for every $\gamma<1$, then $\gamma_k\to1$ and $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$. Conjecture 12.45 says that the product tracks the Syracuse lift loss. At $\gamma=\gamma_k$ this is Theorems 12.21–12.22.

*Proof.* The argument of Theorem 12.21 works at any fixed $\gamma$. The lift $c'$ is constant on lift triples, so $M_\iota c'=\Phi^{(k+1)}_\gamma c'=r_kc'+g$ for *every* policy $\iota$ at modulus $3^{k+1}$. Pairing with $u_\iota$ gives $\rho(M_\iota)=r_k+\langle u_\iota,g\rangle/\langle u_\iota,c'\rangle$; now minimize over $\iota$. As $r$ runs over the three lifts of a row $r_0$, the target it selects runs over $L(r_0)$. So $\sum g=3(3/2)^\gamma\sum_j(\operatorname{avg}-\min)$ while $\sum c'=3\sum v_k$, and Proposition 12.42 gives the Haar average. $\square$

**Proposition 12.47 (the interpolating family).** For $\theta\in[0,1]$, let $\Phi_\theta=(1-\theta)A_\gamma+\theta\,\Phi_\gamma$, with eigenvalue $r(\theta)$ and eigenvector $v_\theta$, so that $v_0=p_\gamma$ and $r(1)=r_k(\gamma)$. Then:
- the deficit is the lift loss along the family:
$$
\Lambda-r(\theta)=\theta\,\frac{(3/2)^\gamma}3\,\big(1-\rho(v_\theta)\big);
$$
- if $\theta\mapsto1-\rho(v_\theta)$ is non-increasing on $[0,1]$, for instance if $r$ is convex, then $1-\rho_k(\gamma)\le\ell(p_{\gamma,k})$: Conjecture 12.45 against the averaged law of the same exponent;
- at a point where the active policy has Perron pair $(u_\theta,v_\theta)$, the secant $(\Lambda-r(\theta))/\theta$ is non-increasing exactly when $\langle u_\theta,A_\gamma v_\theta\rangle\le\Lambda\,\langle u_\theta,v_\theta\rangle$.

*Proof.* Write $\Phi_\theta=A_\gamma-\theta D$ with $D=A_\gamma-\Phi_\gamma$, and pair with the uniform left vector of $A_\gamma$, as in Proposition 12.42. The function $r(\theta)$ is a minimum of finitely many smooth functions $\rho\big(A_\gamma-\theta(A_\gamma-M_\iota)\big)$, so by the envelope theorem $r'(\theta)=-\langle u_\theta,Dv_\theta\rangle/\langle u_\theta,v_\theta\rangle$ almost everywhere. Then $\theta(\Lambda-r)'\le\Lambda-r$ is the stated inequality, after using $\langle u_\theta,(A_\gamma-\theta D)v_\theta\rangle=r\langle u_\theta,v_\theta\rangle$. $\square$

**Proposition 12.48 (minimality alone is not enough).** For general minima of row choices, the comparison fails: the inequality $r(\Phi)\ge\langle a,\Phi p\rangle/\langle a,p\rangle$, with $(a,p)$ the Perron pair of the averaged operator, is false. Take
$$
\Phi(x)=\big(\min(x_0,x_1),\ x_1+\min(x_0,x_1)\big),
$$
whose averaged operator $A=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&3/2\end{pmatrix}$ has $\Lambda=1+1/\sqrt2$ and $a=p=(1,1+\sqrt2)$. Then $\langle p,\Phi p\rangle/\langle p,p\rangle=1+\sqrt2/4$, but the policy $x\mapsto(x_0,x_0+x_1)$ has spectral radius $1$. In a random search, $1526$ of $3000$ such systems violate the inequality. These had $3$ to $6$ states and three choices per row, and half of them had the Collatz-like form of one movable weight.

So Conjecture 12.45 cannot follow from the min-of-averages form, or from minimality of the policy, alone. It must use the arithmetic of the Collatz operator. In the $2\times2$ example the choice acts at once.

**Remark 12.49 (what the arithmetic must supply: delay and misalignment).** In the Collatz operator, a lift chosen at an odd step changes only the top digit. That digit reaches the bottom, where it decides whether the next odd step is possible, only after $k-1$ further odd steps. Along the way it is scrambled by the doublings in between: the cap of Proposition 12.30 bounds how much of it survives. In the level recursion this delay has a precise form.
- The worst policy at modulus $3^{k+1}$ hides the gain by placing occupation on zero-gain lifts. It can do this for the fresh odd inflow into each row, and for the inflow it sends one doubling ahead.
- Mass carried two doublings from an odd row lands on the lift dictated by $A(x)=4x+1$, since $\tau(4r)=A(\tau r)$ (Lemma 12.27). It stays hidden only where the minimizing lift of one target triple is carried by $A$ onto the minimizing lift of the next.
- By Lemma 12.27, that alignment must break somewhere on every $A$-cycle.

A quantitative form of this misalignment is what $\sum_j\nu_j(\gamma)=\infty$ needs: the misaligned rows must carry a non-summable share of the worst policy's occupation.

*Data (S23, S25, and the probes).*
- *Visibilities.* $\nu_k$ is $0.315,0.231,0.166,0.149,0.158$ at $\gamma=0.8$ and $0.276,0.190,0.136,0.114,0.143$ at $\gamma=1$, for $k=3,\dots,7$.
- *Secant.* $(\Lambda-r(\theta))/\theta$ is non-increasing on a $41$-point grid for $k=5,7,9$ and $\gamma=0.9,1$, and $r(\theta)$ is convex for $k=7,9$.
- *Per-level decay.* At every tested $\gamma<1$ ($\gamma\in\{0.5,0.8,0.9,0.95\}$, $k\le13$), the Krasikov–Lagarias lift loss decays at least as fast as the Syracuse lift loss: $\ell(v_{k+1})/\ell(v_k)\le\ell(\mu_{k+1})/\ell(\mu_k)$. At $\gamma=1$ this fails once, marginally ($0.8864$ against $0.8848$ at $k=6\to7$), while the cumulative comparison holds.

**Proposition 12.50 (the misalignment bound).** Fix $\gamma$, $k$ and a policy $\iota$ at modulus $3^{k+1}$, with Perron root $\rho$ and left vector $u$. Let $U(x_0)=\sum_{x\equiv x_0}u(x)$ be its coarse occupation at modulus $3^k$, and let $\delta(y)=v_k(y)-\min v_k$ over the lift triple of $y$. For $W\ge1$ define the *$W$-window misalignment* of an odd row $x_0$:
$$
m_W(x_0)=\big(\tfrac32\big)^\gamma\min_{t\in L(x_0)}\sum_{i<W}\delta(A^it),\qquad A(y)=4y+1 .
$$
It vanishes exactly when some target of $x_0$ stays a minimizing lift for $W$ consecutive steps of $A$. Then
$$
\frac{\langle u,g\rangle}{\langle u,c'\rangle}\ \ge\ \frac{(2^{-\gamma}/\rho)^{2(W-1)}}{W}\cdot\frac{\sum_{x_0}U(x_0)\,m_W(x_0)}{\sum_{x_0}U(x_0)\,v_k(x_0)} .
$$
For the minimizing policy this is a lower bound for $G_k(\gamma)$.

*Proof.*
- *Doubling is always available.* So $\rho\,u(2s)\ge2^{-\gamma}u(s)$, and hence $u(4^ix)\ge(2^{-\gamma}/\rho)^{2i}u(x)$.
- *Relabel.* For each $i<W$, $\langle u,g\rangle=\sum_xu(4^ix)\,g(4^ix)\ge(2^{-\gamma}/\rho)^{2i}\sum_xu(x)\,g(4^ix)$. Average over $i$.
- *The gain along a window is fixed by arithmetic.* By Lemma 12.27, $\tau(4x)=A(\tau x)$, and $A$ maps lift triples onto lift triples. So $g(4^ix)=(3/2)^\gamma\,\delta(A^it_x)$, where $t_x$ is the target of $x$.
- *Sum over lifts.* As $x$ runs over the three lifts of $x_0$, $t_x$ runs over $L(x_0)$, so these lifts contribute at least $U(x_0)\,m_W(x_0)$. Finally, $\langle u,c'\rangle=\sum U\,v_k$. $\square$

The bound uses only moves that the adversary cannot refuse: doublings, and the arithmetic of $4x+1$. The single quantity it leaves to the adversary is the coarse occupation $U$.

**Observation 12.51 (alignment breaks within a few steps; S26, S28).**
- *Persistence.* Take $k=5,\dots,8$ and $\gamma\in\{0.9,1\}$. The fraction of odd rows with a $W$-aligned target is $0.76$–$0.82$ for $W=2$, $0.28$–$0.37$ for $W=3$, $0.14$–$0.19$ for $W=4$, at most $0.04$ for $W=6$, and at most $0.013$ for $W=8$.
- *No coherence (Argon).* Independent minimizing lifts would give $\tfrac13$ at $W=2$, and an earlier draft read the excess as coherence of the minimizing lifts along $A$. The excess is arithmetic instead (Proposition 12.54). On the rows $x_0\equiv5,8\pmod9$, two thirds of the odd rows, one of the first two targets is a multiple of $3$, where $\delta=0$, so these rows are $2$-aligned automatically. On the live rows $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$ the aligned fraction is $p=0.27$–$0.44$ ($k=5,6,7$), close to the $\tfrac13$ of independent lifts, and the total at $W=2$ is exactly $\tfrac23+\tfrac p3$. Lemma 12.27 guarantees one break per cycle of length $3^{k-1}$; the measured breaks come within about six steps.
- *Strength of the bound.* Proposition 12.50 at $W=2$ recovers $0.10$–$0.17$ of $G_k$, with no decrease in $k$.
- *Two factors.* $\nu_k$ is at least an occupation factor times a misalignment ratio. The occupation factor, the $U$-weighted misalignment divided by the uniformly weighted one, is $0.4$–$0.7$, and rising. The misalignment ratio, the uniformly weighted $W=2$ misalignment divided by $H_k$, is $0.023$–$0.040$, slowly decreasing, about like $1/k$ at these levels.

**Status of the minimum transfer.** The open step has three forms: Conjecture 12.45; $\sum_j\nu_j(\gamma)=\infty$ for $\gamma<1$; and $\ell(v_\theta)$ non-increasing in $\theta$. Each yields $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ through Proposition 12.46 or Theorem 12.43. Proposition 12.48 shows that a proof must use the delayed control of Remark 12.49. Proposition 12.50 turns that delay into a rigorous bound, and Observation 12.51 shows that the misalignment it needs comes within $O(1)$ steps of $A$. By Proposition 12.50, $\sum_j\nu_j=\infty$ follows from two estimates:
- (I) *the worst occupation does not avoid misaligned rows:* its coarse marginal weights the $W$-window misalignment at least a fixed fraction of the uniform weighting (measured $0.4$–$0.7$);
- (II) *the misalignment of one explicit vector is not summable:* the uniformly weighted $W$-window misalignment of $v_k$ is at least $c/k$ times its gap $H_k$ (measured $0.023$–$0.040$).

Estimate (II) is a statement about the Perron vector $v_k$ alone, which the twisted recursion of Proposition 12.28 controls for the Syracuse law. Estimate (I) is the last place where the adversary acts. The next proposition confines the adversary to the top digit.

**Proposition 12.52 (below the top digit, the occupation is adversary-free).** Let $\iota$ be *any* policy at modulus $3^{k+1}$, with Perron root $\rho$ and left vector $u$, and let $u_j(s)=\sum_{x\equiv s\ (3^j)}u(x)$ be its marginal at modulus $3^j$. For every $j\le k$ and every unit $s$ modulo $3^j$,
$$
\rho\,u_j(s)=2^{-\gamma}u_j(s/2)+\big(\tfrac32\big)^\gamma u_{j+1}\big(T(s)\big),\qquad T(s)=\frac{3s+1}2\bmod3^{j+1}.
$$
Equivalently, $u_j(s)=\frac{(3/2)^\gamma}\rho\sum_{m\ge0}\big(\tfrac{2^{-\gamma}}\rho\big)^m\,u_{j+1}\big(T(2^{-m}s)\big)$. So the marginal at level $j$ is obtained from the top-level occupation by $k+1-j$ steps of the *forward* Syracuse map, with geometrically distributed halvings. The policy enters only through $u_{k+1}=u$.

*Proof.* Sum the left eigen-equation $\rho u(x)=2^{-\gamma}u(x/2)+(3/2)^\gamma u(T(x))[x\text{ chosen}]$ over the lifts $x$ of $s$. Doubling permutes the lifts of $s/2$ onto those of $s$. The rows $T(x)$ for $x\equiv s\pmod{3^j}$ are exactly the residues $\equiv T(s)\pmod{3^{j+1}}$. The three targets of each such row differ by multiples of $3^k$, so for $j\le k$ all three are $\equiv s\pmod{3^j}$. Exactly one of them is chosen, and it receives the row's full odd weight, whichever it is. Summing gives $(3/2)^\gamma u_{j+1}(T(s))$. $\square$

This is the delay of Remark 12.49 written as an exact law of the occupation. A policy can shape only the top digit, and its shaping reaches the low digits through the forward Syracuse transport, the dynamics whose fine-scale mixing is Tao's theorem.

**Observation 12.53 (anatomy of the gap and of the worst occupation; $\gamma=0.9$, $k=6,\dots,9$).**
- *Moderately concentrated gap.* The gap mass $H_k$ is only moderately concentrated: the top $10\%$ of odd rows carry $0.52$–$0.56$ of it, and the median relative gap is $0.38$–$0.49$ of the mean.
- *Shallow depth.* By depth $j=v_3(x_0+1)$ near the hot point $-1$, the gap shares are $0.355,\ 0.30,\ 0.165,\ 0.084,\ 0.043,\ 0.021$ for $j=1,\dots,6$. They are stable in $k$ and roughly halve at each depth, so depth $\le2$ carries about $66\%$ of the gap.
- *Suppression only deep.* The worst occupation (its $v$-weighted share against the uniform share) is not suppressed at shallow depth: the ratio is $1.09$–$1.10$ at $j=1$ and $0.96$–$0.99$ at $j=2$. It is suppressed only deep, and there it rises with $k$: at $j=3$ it is $0.70,\ 0.72,\ 0.74,\ 0.77$, and at $j=4$ it is $0.35,\ 0.40,\ 0.47,\ 0.53$, for $k=6,7,8,9$.
- *The factor.* The resulting occupation factor on the gap is $0.48$–$0.57$, stable in $k$.

The rising ratios at fixed depth are what Proposition 12.52 predicts: the policy's shaping of the top digit washes out at a fixed number of low digits as the number of forward Syracuse steps between them, $k+1-j$, grows.

### 12.15 The gain on live rows {#sec:12-15}

Propositions 12.54–12.56 are due to Argon (external contribution, 2026-10-17). The summed form in Proposition 12.56(c) and Proposition 12.57 were added while checking them. The notation is that of Proposition 12.50: $v=v_k(\gamma)$ with eigenvalue $r_k=r_k(\gamma)$, $\delta(y)=v(y)-\min v$ over the lift triple of $y$ (so $\delta=0$ on the multiples of $3$), $\beta=(3/2)^\gamma$ and $A(y)=4y+1$.

**Proposition 12.54 (dead targets; Argon).** Let $k\ge3$.
- (a) $A(y)\equiv y+1\pmod3$. So among any three consecutive points $t,A(t),A^2(t)$, each class modulo $3$ occurs exactly once.
- (b) Every target $t\in L(x_0)$ of an odd row $x_0$ satisfies $t\equiv(2x_0-1)/3\pmod3$, and this residue depends only on $x_0\bmod9$:
  - if $x_0\equiv5\pmod9$, then $t\equiv0$: the row has no live odd term, and $m_2(x_0)=0$;
  - if $x_0\equiv8\pmod9$, then $t\equiv2$ and $A(t)\equiv0$, and $m_2(x_0)=0$;
  - if $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$, then $t\equiv1$ and $A(t)\equiv2$, and both are live.
- (c) So all $W=2$ misalignment sits on the *live rows* $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$, one third of the odd rows. If $p$ is the fraction of live rows with a $2$-aligned target, the fraction of all odd rows with one is exactly $\tfrac23+\tfrac p3$.
- (d) $m_3(x_0)=m_2(x_0)$ for $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$, and $m_3(x_0)=m_2(4x_0)$ for $x_0\equiv5\pmod9$, where $4x_0\equiv2\pmod9$. So at $W=3$ the aligned fraction is exactly $p$ on two of the three classes.

*Proof.* (a) $4\equiv1\pmod3$. (b) The targets in $L(x_0)$ differ by multiples of $3^{k-1}$, and $(2x_0-1)/3$ changes by a multiple of $6$ when $x_0$ changes by a multiple of $9$. The three cases are $(2\cdot5-1)/3=3$, $(2\cdot8-1)/3=5$ and $(2\cdot2-1)/3=1$. Since $A(t+i\,3^{k-1})=A(t)+4i\,3^{k-1}$, $A$ maps the lift triple $L(x_0)$ onto a lift triple, which contains its own minimizing lift. For $x_0\equiv5$, $\delta(t)=0$ and the minimum of $\delta(A(t))$ over $t\in L(x_0)$ is $0$. For $x_0\equiv8$, $\delta(A(t))=0$ and the minimum of $\delta(t)$ is $0$. (c) follows. (d) For $x_0\equiv2$, $A^2(t)\equiv0\pmod3$, so the third term vanishes. For $x_0\equiv5$, $\delta(t)=0$, and $\tau(4x_0)=A(\tau x_0)$ (Lemma 12.27), so $A$ maps $L(x_0)$ onto $L(4x_0)$. Then $m_3(x_0)=\beta\min_{t\in L(x_0)}[\delta(A(t))+\delta(A^2(t))]=m_2(4x_0)$. $\square$

Measured (S28; $k=5,6,7$, $\gamma\in\{0.9,1\}$): the rows $x_0\equiv5,8\pmod9$ are $2$-aligned without exception. The live fraction is $p=0.27$–$0.44$, and the identities (c) and (d) hold exactly. On the rows $x_0\equiv8\pmod9$ the $W=3$ fraction differs from $p$, for example $0.26$ against $0.41$ at $k=5$, $\gamma=0.9$.

**Proposition 12.55 (opposite orientations on the live rows; Argon).** Let $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$ be a live row at modulus $3^k$, let $t_0\in[0,3^{k-1})$ represent $(2x_0-1)/3\bmod3^{k-1}$, and put
$$
z_1=2t_0\bmod3^{k-1},\quad z_2=(4t_0+1)\bmod3^{k-1},\quad c=[\,2t_0\ge3^{k-1}\,],\quad q=\lfloor4t_0/3^{k-1}\rfloor,\quad\alpha=2^{-\gamma}/r_k .
$$
- (a) $\delta(y)=\alpha\,\delta(2y)$ for every unit $y\equiv1\pmod3$.
- (b) For $i\in\mathbb Z/3$,
$$
\delta\big(t_0+i\,3^{k-1}\big)=\alpha\,\delta\big(z_1+(c-i)\,3^{k-1}\big),\qquad\delta\big(A(t_0+i\,3^{k-1})\big)=\delta\big(z_2+(q+i)\,3^{k-1}\big).
$$
- (c) Hence
$$
m_2(x_0)=\beta\min_{i\in\mathbb Z/3}\Big[\alpha\,\delta\big(z_1+(c-i)\,3^{k-1}\big)+\delta\big(z_2+(q+i)\,3^{k-1}\big)\Big],
$$
where $z_1,z_2\equiv2\pmod3$ and $z_2+1\equiv2(z_1+1)\pmod{3^{k-1}}$. So the $W=2$ misalignment compares the lift triples of two odd rows, and the index $i$ runs through them in opposite directions. If $v$ has a unique minimizing slot $i_1$ on the triple of $z_1$ and $i_2$ on that of $z_2$, then $m_2(x_0)=0$ exactly when $i_1+i_2\equiv c+q\pmod3$.

*Proof.* (a) A row $y\equiv1\pmod3$ has no odd term, so $r_kv(y)=2^{-\gamma}v(2y)$, and doubling maps the lift triple of $y$ onto that of $2y$. (b) $2(t_0+i\,3^{k-1})=z_1+(c+2i)\,3^{k-1}$ and $2\equiv-1\pmod3$; apply (a), since $t_0\equiv1\pmod3$. Next, $A(t_0+i\,3^{k-1})=z_2+(q+4i)\,3^{k-1}$ and $4\equiv1\pmod3$. Here $4t_0+1$ wraps exactly when $4t_0$ does, because $3\nmid4t_0+1$. (c) Substitute (b) into the definition of $m_2$. Modulo $3^{k-1}$, $z_2+1=4t_0+2=2(z_1+1)$. The first term vanishes exactly at $i\equiv c-i_1$, and the second exactly at $i\equiv i_2-q$. $\square$

The opposite directions are the reindexing $2\equiv-1\pmod3$ of Proposition 12.28, now applied to $v_k$. Estimate (II) at $W=2$ is therefore a statement about how often, and by how much, the slot congruence of (c) fails on the live rows.

**Proposition 12.56 (the gain is a pairing with the coarse occupation; Argon).** Let $\iota$ be any policy at modulus $3^{k+1}$, with Perron root $\rho$, left vector $u$ and coarse occupation $U$ at modulus $3^k$, and let $c'$ and $g$ be as in Proposition 12.46. For a residue $s$ modulo $3^k$ put $r(s)=(3s+1)/2\bmod3^{k+1}$.
- (a) $r(s)$ is the unique row modulo $3^{k+1}$ whose three targets are the lifts of $s$. Every row $\equiv2\pmod3$ is some $r(s)$, with $g(r(s))=\beta\,\delta(s)$, and $g=0$ on the rows $\equiv1\pmod3$.
- (b) For every unit $s$, $\beta\,u(r(s))=\rho\,U(s)-2^{-\gamma}U(s/2)$.
- (c) Consequently
$$
\langle u,g\rangle=\sum_s\big(\rho\,U(s)-2^{-\gamma}U(s/2)\big)\,\delta(s)=\sum_sU(s)\,h(s),\qquad h(s)=\rho\,\delta(s)-2^{-\gamma}\delta(2s),
$$
and $\langle u,c'\rangle=\sum_sU(s)\,v(s)$. So the policy enters $\langle u,g\rangle/\langle u,c'\rangle$ only through $U$ and $\rho$.

*Proof.* (a) A row $r\equiv2\pmod3$ has as targets the lifts of $\tau(r)=(2r-1)/3\bmod3^k$, and $\tau(r)\equiv s$ exactly when $r\equiv(3s+1)/2\pmod{3^{k+1}}$. The lifted vector $c'$ is constant on these targets, so the odd term of $\Phi^{(k+1)}_\gamma(c')$ at $r(s)$ is $\beta v(s)$. At level $k$, the same row reduced modulo $3^k$ has odd term $\beta$ times the minimum of $v$ over the lift triple of $s$. The doubling terms agree, so $g(r(s))=\beta\,\delta(s)$. Rows $\equiv1\pmod3$ have only doubling terms. (b) is Proposition 12.52 at $j=k$, since $T(s)=r(s)$. (c) Pair $g$ with $u$ using (a) and (b). The second form follows by substituting $s\mapsto2s$ in the term with $U(s/2)$, since doubling permutes the units. Finally $\langle u,c'\rangle=\sum_xu(x)\,v(x\bmod3^k)$. $\square$

Proposition 12.56 turns the window bound of Proposition 12.50 at $W=1$ into an identity, and the test function $h$ is fixed by $v$ and $\rho$. On $s\equiv1\pmod3$, Proposition 12.55(a) gives $h(s)=2^{-\gamma}\delta(2s)\,(\rho/r_k-1)\ge0$. This is of the order of the gain itself, since $\rho\ge r_{k+1}=r_k+G_k$. On $s\equiv2\pmod3$, $h(s)=\rho\,\delta(s)-(4^{-\gamma}/r_k)\,\delta(4s)$, which can be negative. Measured (S30; $k=5,6,7$, $\gamma\in\{0.9,1\}$): $h<0$ on $8.6\%$ to $10.5\%$ of the units; its negative part is $6\%$ to $13\%$ of its positive part, decreasing in $k$; and the minimizing policy puts $7\%$ to $12\%$ of its occupation there.

**Proposition 12.57 (what the coarse occupation can and cannot see).** In the setting of Proposition 12.56, put $\alpha=2^{-\gamma}/\rho$, normalize $\sum u=1$, and let $L=\varphi(3^k)$.
- (a) *Low digits are occupied for free.* For $j\le k$ and every unit $s$ modulo $3^j$,
$$
u_j(s)\ \ge\ \Big(\sum_{d=0}^{\varphi(3^j)-1}\alpha^{-d}\Big)^{-1}.
$$
In particular every unit class modulo $9$ carries at least $1/\sum_{d<6}\alpha^{-d}$ of the occupation, about $\tfrac1{63}$ at $\gamma=1$, uniformly in $k$ and in the policy. This makes precise a lower bound proposed by Argon for the live rows.
- (b) *The doubling inequality sees no gain.* By Proposition 12.56(b), every coarse occupation satisfies $U(2t)\ge\alpha\,U(t)$. For a unit $t$ modulo $3^k$ with $\delta(t)=0$, the vector $U_t=\sum_{d=0}^{L-1}\alpha^d\,\mathbf 1_{2^dt}$ satisfies these inequalities, is positive on every unit, and has $\sum_sU_t(s)\,h(s)=0$.

*Proof.* (a) $2$ is a primitive root modulo $3^j$, so doubling is a single cycle of length $\varphi(3^j)$ on the units. Summing $\rho\,u(2x)\ge2^{-\gamma}u(x)$ over $x\equiv s\pmod{3^j}$ gives $u_j(2s)\ge\alpha\,u_j(s)$, hence $u_j(2^{-d}s)\le\alpha^{-d}u_j(s)$ for $0\le d<\varphi(3^j)$. Summing over $d$ covers every unit class once, and $\sum u=1$. (b) First, $\alpha<1$: $\rho\ge r_k$, and summing the eigen-equation of $v$ gives $(r_k-2^{-\gamma})\sum v=\beta\sum_j\min>0$. Along the orbit, $U_t(2^{d+1}t)=\alpha\,U_t(2^dt)$ for $d<L-1$, and at the closure $U_t(t)=1\ge\alpha^L=\alpha\,U_t(2^{L-1}t)$. Since $2^dt$ runs over all units, $U_t>0$. The quantity $\rho\,U_t(s)-2^{-\gamma}U_t(s/2)$ vanishes at $s=2^dt$ for $d\ge1$ and equals $\rho(1-\alpha^L)$ at $s=t$. By Proposition 12.56(c), $\sum_sU_t(s)h(s)=\rho(1-\alpha^L)\,\delta(t)=0$. $\square$

So no lower bound for $G_k$ that uses $U$ only through the doubling inequality can be positive. In $U_t$ the implied odd-row occupations $\rho\,U_t(s)-2^{-\gamma}U_t(s/2)$ vanish except at $s=t$: all the odd inflow sits on one row. Estimate (I) must therefore use how the odd edges at modulus $3^{k+1}$ route the occupation, not only its doubling transport. This is the lesson of Proposition 12.48, one level down. Part (a) shows that (I) is a statement at the scale of single rows: at a fixed number $j$ of low digits the occupation is bounded below for free, but the bound decays like $\alpha^{2\cdot3^{j-1}}$. Measured (S31): for the minimizing policy the unit classes modulo $9$ carry $0.11$ to $0.19$ of the occupation each, far above the bound of (a), which is about $0.02$. On the live rows, though, the minimizing policy puts between $0.09$ and $0.61$ of their occupation on $2$-aligned rows, against Haar shares of $0.27$ to $0.44$, so at some levels it does lean toward aligned rows.

**Where the proof stands.** The deficit $\Lambda(\gamma)-r_k(\gamma)$ obeys the exact product of Proposition 12.46. Proposition 12.50 bounds each visibility below by misalignment weighted by the coarse occupation, and Proposition 12.52 shows that occupation to be the forward-Syracuse transport of the top digit. Summing the visibilities, and hence $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$, follows from two estimates, each now tied to a concrete mechanism:
- (I) *mixing of the transported occupation:* the forward-Syracuse transport of the worst top-level occupation weights the $W$-window misalignment of $v_k$ at least a fixed fraction of the uniform weighting. In the depth decomposition this is a uniform-in-policy version of fine-scale mixing at a fixed number of low digits (measured: ratios $\approx1$ at depth $\le2$, rising toward $1$ deeper). By Propositions 12.56–12.57 the adversary acts only through the pairing $\sum_sU(s)h(s)$. Fixed low digits are occupied for free, while the doubling inequality alone admits zero gain. So (I) is a statement at the scale of single rows about how the odd edges at modulus $3^{k+1}$ route the occupation;
- (II) *misalignment of the Perron vector:* the $W$-window misalignment of $v_k$ is a non-summable fraction of its gap, with its mass at bounded depth (measured: about $1/k$ of the gap; depth $\le2$ carries two thirds). By Propositions 12.54–12.55, at $W=2$ it lives only on the live rows $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$. There it compares the minimizing slots of $v_k$ on the lift triples of two odd rows $z_1$ and $z_2=2z_1+1$, traversed in opposite directions.

Neither is proved here. Estimate (I) is a Tao-type mixing statement, uniform over the top-level input, for the forward Syracuse walk tested against one explicit function. Estimate (II) concerns $v_k$ alone.

**Fence.** None of this proves the Collatz conjecture. The cycle half needs a quantitative non-vanishing of $S_w$ modulo $2^K-3^m$; the divergence half needs control of renewals. The crystal contributes:
- the geometric reading: given a cycle its valuations telescope, and integrality lives at the primes of $2^K-3^m$;
- the reduction to one finite check per prime birth (Theorem 12.5 and Corollary 12.19);
- the identification of the coverage equation inside the Krasikov–Lagarias program;
- the strict increase of its exponents, and the reduction of $x^{1-\varepsilon}$ to one two-level gain inequality (Section 12.10);
- the decomposition of collisions of backward histories into twins and siblings, and the fair-game theorem: through the product formula, the classical descent and fairness of the Collatz step are the density and $L^2$ thresholds of the Syracuse law, so its density (Tao) is not square-integrable and $3$ is the only odd multiplier with a density (Section 12.12);
- the averaged Krasikov–Lagarias family crosses the $L^2$ threshold exactly at the target exponent $\gamma=1$ (Proposition 12.39), and Astro's reductions of the minimum transfer (Section 12.13);
- the reduction of $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ to a single comparison between the Krasikov–Lagarias vector and the Syracuse law, through Tao's theorem (Theorem 12.43, Conjecture 12.45);
- Argon's localization of the windowed misalignment to the live rows and the exact pairing form of the gain, with the fence that the doubling inequality alone sees no gain (Section 12.15).

## 13. Fences and open problems {#sec:13}

**Fences.**
- Everything here is a geometric re-encoding of unique factorization. The prime inequalities used are inputs, not outputs. Nothing is claimed about gaps between primes, about the Riemann hypothesis, or about the distribution of primes beyond what the cited inequalities state.
- The $SU(N)$ dictionary is an identity of lattices and representations, not physics.
- The musical remark in Section 5 is a rhyme.

**Open problems.**
1. **Ray alignments.** Prove Theorem 5.2 for all $N\ge4$, i.e. that $p^j\pm1=P_N/p$ has no solutions. The certificate covers $N\le2000$, and a Zsigmondy-type argument looks feasible.
2. **Turn distribution.** Determine the limiting distribution of turn angles, beyond the mean of Corollary 6.4.
3. **Chirality and higher invariants.** Describe the distribution of $\kappa_3$ and of the higher central power sums along the walk, i.e. the joint law of the orbit labels of Proposition 2.5.
4. **Integer hulls.** Describe the vertices of the integer hull of $K_x$ (the "efficient" smooth numbers near $x$), together with their counts and shape.
5. **Birth shock.** Quantify the total deformation of the crystal at each birth, as a function of $N$, using Corollary 7.2.
6. **The archimedean place.** Relate the frame to the logarithmic $S$-unit embedding of Dirichlet's unit theorem, where the archimedean coordinate is restored and the product formula replaces the gauge. Proposition 2.4 is the first step. Section 11 is the second: frame plus size is a lattice embedding of the $S$-units, which is discrete in the product and dense in the archimedean factor.
7. **Gaps in higher frames.** For $N=2$ the motif of Theorem 11.2 has at most three gap lengths. How many distinct gaps can occur for $N\ge3$, and what is the largest gap relative to the mean? The measured slopes ($-1.97$ for $N=3$, $-2.43$ for $N=4$ at small $R$) leave this open.
8. **Rate of frame growth along a journey.** For a given irrational $x$, how fast must the frame grow along its journey? For the golden ratio, Carmichael's theorem gives almost one new prime per step. For algebraic $x$, the $p$-adic Roth theorem of Ridout should give a lower bound in terms of $q_k$.
9. **Collatz: the next frames.** Extend Theorem 12.5(b) beyond $p_N=23$ using complete lists of consecutive smooth pairs for larger primes, such as those obtained through Lehmer's Pell-equation method for $x^2-1$ [Luca–Najman]. Does the eleven-step cycle at frame $18$ appear alone?
10. **Collatz: the cost of each check.** Bound the cost of the frame-$N$ check of Theorem 12.5(c). Each prime birth adds a finite batch of new superparticular moves; can any batch close a new loop?
11. **Collatz: every charge?** Does every charge $d$ prime to $6$ carry a primitive rational cycle? Through $d\le60$ the answer is yes, with lengths up to $29$ ($d=53$). How does the length of the shortest cycle grow with $d$?
12. **Collatz: lift retention.** Prove Conjecture 12.24, or any level-uniform lower bound $G_k\ge f(1-\rho_k)$ with $f>0$ on $(0,1]$; by Theorem 12.22 that gives $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$. Equivalently, show that no choice of lifts can make its left Perron vector blind to the top-digit gain. Explain why the ratio $(\rho_{k+1}-\rho_k)/(1-\rho_k)^2$ grows with $k$, and why $k(1-\rho_k)$ and $k\delta_k$ decrease beyond $k\approx11$.
13. **Collatz: renewals.** Along the returns of one orbit to $x\equiv1\pmod4$, control the coupled valuations $(a,b)$ of Section 12.9 well enough to force a negative accumulated height change. Corollary 12.19 says a divergent orbit must keep escaping frames; can each escape be charged against the height budget? In the odd-core form of Section 12.9 the question is whether the recurrence $2^{a'-1+b'}u'=3^bu+2^{a'-1}-1$ can keep $\sum b\ln\tfrac32$ ahead of $\sum(a-1)\ln2$ forever along one orbit.
14. **Collatz: the order of the $L^2$ divergence.** Theorem 12.38 gives $f_n\gtrsim n$. Is $f_n\asymp n$, i.e. are the sibling factors $F_n$ of Section 12.12 bounded? The data show $c_m(k)$ converging for $k\ge2$, and $c_m(1)$ creeping like $0.011\ln m$. Does the Syracuse density lie in $L^q$ for every $q<2$, as the Rényi entropies $\ln(2^q-1)/(q-1)>\ln3$ suggest? Do the averaged Krasikov–Lagarias laws with $\gamma<1$ have square-integrable densities (Proposition 12.39)?
15. **Collatz: the comparison.** Prove Conjecture 12.45: $1-\rho_k(\gamma)\le\ell(\mu_k)$ for $\gamma\in[\tfrac12,1]$, or any bound $k^{O(1)}\ell(\mu_k)$. By Theorem 12.43 and Tao's theorem it gives $\pi_a(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$, with $1-\gamma_k$ decaying faster than every power of $1/k$. The data hold it with a factor of $2.4$ to spare. Equivalent targets are $\sum_j\nu_j(\gamma)=\infty$ for $\gamma<1$ (Proposition 12.46) and monotonicity of the interpolating lift loss (Proposition 12.47). Minimality alone cannot prove it (Proposition 12.48). Proposition 12.50 reduces it to two estimates: (I) the worst occupation does not avoid misaligned rows, and (II) the misalignment of $v_k$ along $x\mapsto4x+1$ is a non-summable fraction of its gap. By Proposition 12.52, (I) is a uniform-in-policy fine-scale mixing statement for the forward Syracuse transport of the top digit. By Section 12.15, (I) concerns the pairing $\sum_sU(s)h(s)$ and cannot follow from the doubling inequality alone. At $W=2$, (II) lives on the rows $x_0\equiv2\pmod9$, where it is the failure rate of one slot congruence between the triples of $z_1$ and $2z_1+1$.
