## Abstract

**Situation.** [\heb{ל}0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20423985) named the apex generator $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z/N}e^{\beta y_s}\overline{\chi_c(s)}$ of the structured categorical simplex — a finite Fourier–Mellin transform on $\mathbb Z/N$ of the centered log-ledger $y$ of a positive ledger $x\in\mathbb R_{>0}^N$ — and scoped the holomorphic structure of $\mathcal M_x$ in complex $\beta$ (its zeros, contour deformations, analytic continuation) as a forward door (\heb{ל}0 §6.6). [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442) lifted the generator to $\mathbb C^N$, gave the qubit zero set in closed form (\heb{ל}7 Prop. 6.2), proved Carlson rigidity on the decoherent face (\heb{ל}7 Prop. 5.7), and recorded two open doors: the **Lee–Yang zero variety** for $N\ge 3$ (\heb{ל}7 Conj. 7.4) and the **RH-shaped critical-line question** (\heb{ל}7 §7.6), there split into an *additive aspiration* (Conj. 7.6, flagged heuristic) and a *concrete provable target* relocated to the function-field / Weil setting.

**Gap.** Both doors were stated only at $N=2$ (the $\cosh$ lattice) or as aspirations. Three things were missing: (i) the zero set of $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$ for general $N$ and general ledger — is it a "structured subvariety," and of what shape; (ii) an honest verdict on the additive critical-line question — can the additive apex ever *force* zeros onto a line, and if not, exactly which ledgers land on a line and why; (iii) a worked instance in which a critical line is a *theorem*, built inside the apex frame rather than imported by analogy. This paper settles all three for the finite generator and builds the function-field instance to the point where Weil's $\tfrac12$ is read off the apex coefficients directly.

**Action.** We treat $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$ as what it is — an entire function of exponential type given by a finite exponential sum — and bring to bear the classical zero theory of such functions (Pólya, Langer, Schwengeler, Moreno; Boas, *Entire Functions*, §9). We prove a seven-part structural spine (analytic class, strip confinement, counting law, exact-line locus, functional equation, the Newton-polygon zero variety, and the imported Carlson rigidity), isolate three new theorems beyond the $N=2$ results of \heb{ל}0/\heb{ל}7, and then change the base ring $\mathbb Z/N\rightsquigarrow\mathbb F_q$ to obtain the function-field apex, whose coefficients are Gauss and Jacobi sums of modulus $q^{1/2}$. Every claim is backed by a deterministic certificate (C1–C12, `empirical/apex_zeros/`; argument-principle zero counting + `mpmath` Newton refinement + exact arithmetic; no floating-point physics, no network).

**Result.** The organizing thesis is a single formula. Write the asymptotic real part of the zeros of a one-axis apex as a **tilt**,
$$\mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*\;=\;\frac{\log|\text{coefficient ratio}|}{\log(\text{base})},$$
the slope of the Newton diagram of the pairs $(y_s,\log|a_s|)$. Three rungs follow:

1. **Additive characters** ($|a_s|=1$, flat diagram) $\Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*=0$ — a *trivial* line, forced by unitarity, with no arithmetic content (Thm. 2.1, Thm. 3.3). The exact line is attained **iff the ledger is geometric** (equal log-spacing): the maximal-symmetry locus, a codimension-$(N-2)$ subvariety, not an arithmetic one (Thm. 3.1, generalizing \heb{ל}0 Prop. 2.5 to all $N$).
2. **Units-indicator (Ramanujan) ledger** ($\varphi$ vs $N-\varphi$) $\Rightarrow$ the line **moves off zero** to $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*=\log\frac{N-\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N)}$, growing along primorials, while the zero **density stays linear** in $T$ — no $\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ (Thm. 6.1). Arithmetic tilts the line; it does not bend the density.
3. **Function field** ($\mathbb F_q$, $|a_s|=q^{1/2}$, Gauss/Jacobi sums $=$ Frobenius eigenvalues on $H^1$ of the Fermat curve) $\Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Re}\,s=\tfrac12$ **exactly**, which is **Weil's theorem** (Thm. 7.1). The $\tfrac12$ is norm-$q$ purity in the cyclotomic CM field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ of the apex character (Thm. 8.1), and it appears at **both places** — archimedean $|\alpha|=q^{1/2}$ and average $p$-adic Newton slope $\tfrac12$ (Thm. 9.1).

The keystone is the **Newton-polygon zero variety** (Thm. 4.1): for complex frequencies, the zeros of $\mathcal M$ accumulate on the rays perpendicular to the edges of the indicator polygon $\mathrm{conv}\{y_s\}\subset\mathbb C$, with density $|\text{edge}|/2\pi$ per ray; the real (decoherent) ledger is the degenerate case — a segment, one imaginary-axis family — which subsumes the counting law and makes \heb{ל}7 Conj. 7.4's "structured subvariety" precise.

**Harvest.** The additive critical-line question of \heb{ל}7 §7.6 is **settled as a no-go with mechanism**: the additive apex carries no scale that can pin $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta$ except the trivial symmetry line, exactly as \heb{ל}7 anticipated, now with an exact characterization of the symmetry locus and of the arithmetic tilt. The provable critical line lives one ring change away, over $\mathbb F_q$, where Frobenius supplies the $q^{1/2}$ tilt for free; we read Weil's $\tfrac12$ off the apex and trace it to its CM source and its second ($p$-adic) place. Finally (Thm. 10.1) the function-field Weil form is the Toeplitz matrix of normalized point counts, whose positivity $\Leftrightarrow$ Schur contractivity $\Leftrightarrow$ purity — the same Carathéodory–Fejér / Szegő mechanism that governs the archimedean window-positivity frontier of the standing simplicial-RH programme. We state precisely what this does and does **not** do for RH: it is a complete map of a new object and a bridge to the classical giants (Lee–Yang, Carlson, Weil, Mazur, Schur–Szegő), a structural reduction and a clarifying lens — **not** a theorem-moving step for the Riemann Hypothesis. The one conjectural seam — that the additive apex's *missing* $\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ is exactly the archimedean completion $\zeta$ must manufacture — is firewalled as [C] throughout.

**Keywords:** apex generator, exponential sum, entire function of exponential type, Lee–Yang zeros, Carlson's theorem, Newton polygon / indicator diagram, Ramanujan sum, critical line, Weil conjectures for curves, Gauss and Jacobi sums, Fermat curve, complex multiplication, $p$-adic Newton polygon, Mazur's inequality, Toeplitz positivity, Schur algorithm, Szegő theory, finite-categorical Fourier–Mellin.

## 1. The apex generator and its analytic class {#sec:1}

### 1.1 The object {#sec:1-1}

**Definition 1.1 (Apex generator; apex zero variety).** For a ledger $x\in\mathbb R_{>0}^N$ with centered log-ledger $y_s=\log x_s-\frac1N\sum_j\log x_j$ and a character index $c\in\widehat{\mathbb Z/N}$, the **apex generator** is
$$\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z/N}e^{\beta y_s}\,\overline{\chi_c(s)},\qquad \chi_c(s)=e^{2\pi ics/N},\ \beta\in\mathbb C .$$
Its **zero variety** is $\mathcal Z(x,c)=\{\beta\in\mathbb C:\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=0\}$.

Since $\beta\mapsto\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$ is a finite linear combination of exponentials $e^{\beta y_s}$, it is entire in $\beta$; the whole of this paper studies $\mathcal Z(x,c)$. We will repeatedly use that $\mathcal M_x(\cdot,c)$ is an **exponential polynomial** with real frequencies $\{y_s\}$ (decoherent ledger) or complex frequencies (the complex extension of [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442)).

### 1.2 Exponential type and the unit bound {#sec:1-2}

**Theorem 1.2 (Analytic class). [P]** $\beta\mapsto\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$ is entire of exponential type $\tau=\max_s|y_s|$. On the decoherent ledger ($y_s\in\mathbb R$) it satisfies the unit bound $|\mathcal M_x(it,c)|\le 1$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$, so its indicator on the imaginary axis is $0$; and $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)\to 0$ along no real direction unless $c\equiv 0$ (where $\mathcal M_x(0,0)=1$).

*Proof.* Each $e^{\beta y_s}$ is entire of type $|y_s|$, and a finite sum has type the maximum, so $\tau=\max_s|y_s|$. For $\beta=it$ with $y_s\in\mathbb R$,
$$|\mathcal M_x(it,c)|=\Big|\tfrac1N\sum_s e^{ity_s}\overline{\chi_c(s)}\Big|\le\tfrac1N\sum_s|e^{ity_s}|=\tfrac1N\sum_s 1=1 ,$$
so the imaginary-axis indicator vanishes. $\square$

The unit bound is the hypothesis of Carlson's uniqueness theorem; we use it in Prop. 1.4.

### 1.3 Strip confinement {#sec:1-3}

Order the frequencies $y_{(1)}\le y_{(2)}\le\cdots\le y_{(N)}$ and write the **top gap** $\delta_+=y_{(N)}-y_{(N-1)}$ and **bottom gap** $\delta_-=y_{(2)}-y_{(1)}$ (both $>0$ when the extreme frequencies are simple).

**Theorem 1.3 (Strip confinement). [P]** Every zero $\beta\in\mathcal Z(x,c)$ of the decoherent apex lies in the vertical strip
$$-\frac{\log(N-1)}{\delta_-}\ \le\ \mathrm{Re}\,\beta\ \le\ \frac{\log(N-1)}{\delta_+}.$$
In particular, at $N=2$ the bound collapses to $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$: all zeros are purely imaginary.

*Proof.* At a zero, the top-frequency term cannot exceed the sum of the rest in modulus. With $|\overline{\chi_c(s)}|=1$,
$$e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\,y_{(N)}}=\Big|\sum_{s\ne s_{\max}}e^{\beta y_s}\overline{\chi_c(s)}\Big|\le\sum_{s\ne s_{\max}}e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\,y_s}\le(N-1)\,e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\,y_{(N-1)}}\quad(\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\ge 0),$$
giving $e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\,\delta_+}\le N-1$, i.e. $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\le\log(N-1)/\delta_+$. The symmetric estimate at the bottom frequency for $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\le 0$ gives the lower bound. At $N=2$, $\log(N-1)=\log 1=0$, so $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$. $\square$

This recovers, and explains, the purely-imaginary $\cosh$ lattice of [\heb{ל}0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20423985) Prop. 2.5: at $N=2$ there is a single gap and the strip has zero width. (Certificate **C1**: every Newton-located zero lies in this strip for $N=3,4,6$, structured and random ledgers, all $c$; argument-principle count equals the Newton-found count, residual $|\mathcal M|\sim10^{-31}$.)

### 1.4 Carlson rigidity (imported) {#sec:1-4}

**Proposition 1.4 (Carlson rigidity; [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442) Prop. 5.7). [A]** On the decoherent ledger, the unit bound of Thm. 1.2 places $\mathcal M_x(\cdot,c)$ in the hypothesis of Carlson's theorem (Carlson 1914; Boas 1954, Thm. 9.2.1): it is the unique entire function of imaginary-axis type $<\pi$ taking the prescribed values $\{\mathcal M_x(m,c)\}_{m\ge 0}$ on the non-negative integers. Hence the closure diagonal determines $\mathcal M_x(\cdot,c)$ — and therefore $\mathcal Z(x,c)$ — entirely; the rigidity holds sheet-by-sheet and degrades across sheets exactly through the $\mu_N$ Kummer cover ([\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442) Rem. 5.8).

We use Prop. 1.4 only to certify that the zero variety is a *rigid* invariant of the ledger (no hidden degrees of freedom): the integer samples already fix it. The proof is in [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442); we do not reprove it.

## 2. The counting law {#sec:2}

**Theorem 2.1 (Counting law). [P]** For a decoherent ledger of spread $\Delta_y=\max_s y_s-\min_s y_s$, the number of zeros with $|\mathrm{Im}\,\beta|\le T$ obeys
$$n(T)=\frac{\Delta_y}{\pi}\,T+O(1)\qquad(T\to\infty),$$
with the $O(1)$ a boundary term and the leading density **independent of the character index $c$**. The density is set by the support width $\Delta_y$ alone — not by where the zeros sit in the strip.

*Proof (sketch; full derivation is the $N$-edge specialization of Thm. 4.1).* The decoherent indicator diagram $\mathrm{conv}\{y_s\}$ is the segment $[\min y,\max y]$ of length $\Delta_y$. By the indicator-diagram theorem for exponential sums (Pólya 1920; Boas 1954, §9.4), the zeros of $\sum_s a_s e^{\beta y_s}$ accumulate along the rays perpendicular to the edges of the diagram, the density per edge being (edge length)$/2\pi$. A segment has two oriented sides, each of length $\Delta_y$ and each perpendicular to the imaginary axis, so the combined imaginary-axis density is $2\cdot\Delta_y/2\pi=\Delta_y/\pi$, independent of the coefficients $a_s$ (hence of $c$). $\square$

The constancy of the density is the first half of the no-go: **arithmetic cannot change the leading count.** Whatever the coefficients do (and §6 shows they move the *line*), they cannot manufacture the $\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$ growth of $\zeta$'s zero count from a finite exponential sum. (Certificate **C2**: regression slope within $1\%$ of $\Delta_y/\pi$ for $N=4,5,6$, geometric/ramp/random ledgers; residuals bounded in $O(1)$, not growing; $c$-independence confirmed across all five characters at $N=5$. A $T\log T$ model misfits by $3.8\times$.)

## 3. The exact critical line and the additive no-go {#sec:3}

### 3.1 The equal-spacing exact-line theorem {#sec:3-1}

**Theorem 3.1 (Equal-spacing exact line). [P]** Suppose the ledger is **geometric**, $x_s=x_0\rho^{s}$ ($\rho>0$), equivalently the centered log-ledger is an arithmetic progression with common spacing $a=\log\rho$. Then for every $N$ and every $c$,
$$\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\,e^{-\beta a(N-1)/2}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\big(e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}\big)^{s}=\frac1N\,e^{-\beta a(N-1)/2}\,\frac{e^{aN\beta}-1}{e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}-1},$$
a geometric series in $w=e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}$, and the zero variety is the **exact imaginary lattice**
$$\boxed{\ \mathcal Z(x,c)=\Big\{\beta=\frac{2\pi i\,m}{aN}\ :\ m\in\mathbb Z,\ m\not\equiv c\!\!\pmod N\Big\}\ }\subset i\mathbb R .$$
All zeros have $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$ exactly; the lattice has spacing $2\pi/(aN)$ with the residue class $m\equiv c$ deleted.

*Proof.* With $y_s=a\,(s-\tfrac{N-1}{2})$ and $\overline{\chi_c(s)}=\zeta_N^{-cs}$,
$$\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N e^{-\beta a(N-1)/2}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}e^{a\beta s}\zeta_N^{-cs}=\frac1N e^{-\beta a(N-1)/2}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w^s,\qquad w=e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}.$$
The prefactor never vanishes, and $\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}w^s=\frac{w^N-1}{w-1}$ for $w\ne1$ (and $=N$ for $w=1$). Now $w^N=e^{aN\beta}\zeta_N^{-cN}=e^{aN\beta}$ since $\zeta_N^N=1$. Thus $\mathcal M_x=0\iff w^N=1$ and $w\ne1\iff e^{aN\beta}=1$ and $e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}\ne1$. The first gives $aN\beta\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$, i.e. $\beta=2\pi i m/(aN)$; then $w=e^{a\beta}\zeta_N^{-c}=\zeta_N^{m}\zeta_N^{-c}=\zeta_N^{m-c}$, and $w\ne1\iff m\not\equiv c\pmod N$. $\square$

At $N=2$, $c=0$ this is $\mathcal M_x(\beta,0)=\cosh(a\beta)$ with zeros $\beta=i\pi(k+\tfrac12)/a$ — exactly [\heb{ל}0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20423985) Prop. 2.5. **Theorem 3.1 generalizes that $N=2$ result to all $N$ and all $c$**, and identifies the exact additive "critical line" with the **maximal-symmetry (equal-spacing) locus** — a codimension-$(N-2)$ subvariety of ledger space, a *symmetry* phenomenon, not an arithmetic one. (Certificate **C3**: geometric ledgers $N=2,\dots,7$, all $c$, match the predicted set to $\sim10^{-30}$; genuinely non-geometric controls are off-line. Caution recorded in C3: $[1,2,4]$ is *secretly* geometric — equal log-spacing is judged by spacings, not by "looks like a ramp.")

### 3.2 The character index is a phase/selection index {#sec:3-2}

**Proposition 3.2 (Cyclic-shift covariance). [P]** Relabelling $x_s\mapsto x_{s+1}$ (cyclic) multiplies the generator by a unit phase, $\mathcal M_{x(+1)}(\beta,c)=\zeta_N^{c}\,\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$, so each mode's zero variety $\mathcal Z(x,c)$ is invariant under cyclic relabelling. Combined with the $c$-independence of the density (Thm. 2.1) and the residue-class deletion of Thm. 3.1, the index $c$ acts as an **imaginary-axis offset / selection rule**, not as a degree of freedom that can move the line.

*Proof.* $\mathcal M_{x(+1)}(\beta,c)=\frac1N\sum_s e^{\beta y_{s+1}}\zeta_N^{-cs}=\frac1N\sum_{s'}e^{\beta y_{s'}}\zeta_N^{-c(s'-1)}=\zeta_N^{c}\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$. A nonzero scalar leaves the zero set unchanged. $\square$

(Certificate **C4**: ratio error $\sim10^{-30}$; cross-residual confirming each mode's zeros are invariant $\sim10^{-31}$.)

### 3.3 The additive no-go {#sec:3-3}

**Theorem 3.3 (Additive no-go, with mechanism). [P]** Off the equal-spacing locus the decoherent apex has **no critical line**: for generic ledgers the empirical mean of $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta$ over the zeros tends to $0$ as the counting box grows, while the RMS spread of $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta$ stays bounded away from $0$. The $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$ accumulation seen on average is forced by **unitarity** (equal coefficient moduli $|a_s|=1/N$, the flat indicator diagram of Thm. 2.1/4.1), and is exact only on the symmetry locus of Thm. 3.1. An arithmetic critical line requires the equal-modulus condition to be **broken** by an external scale.

*Proof.* Thm. 4.1 gives the asymptotic real parts as the slopes of the Newton diagram of $(y_s,\log|a_s|)$. With $|a_s|=1/N$ constant the diagram is flat, so the only asymptotic slope is $0$ (mean $\to0$); the $O(1)$ spread is the finite-$\beta$ deviation, which does not vanish unless the diagram degenerates to a single point (equal spacing). The line is attained exactly iff Thm. 3.1 applies. $\square$

This is precisely the verdict [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442) §7.6 anticipated for Conj. 7.6 ("the additive setting supplies no mechanism that pins $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta$"), now upgraded from aspiration to theorem with the exact symmetry-locus characterization. (Certificate **C5**: generic ledgers give mean $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta\to0$ with bounded RMS spread $\sim0.4$–$0.9$; the geometric locus gives spread $2\times10^{-30}$; among arithmetic flavors, primes $[2,3,5,7,11,13]$ give the *largest* off-line spread — irregular log-spacing pushes hardest off the line.)

## 4. The zero variety: the Newton polygon {#sec:4}

This is the keystone, resolving [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442) Conj. 7.4.

**Theorem 4.1 (Newton-polygon zero variety). [P/A]** Let the frequencies $y_s\in\mathbb C$ be the centered complex log-ledger of [\heb{ל}7](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20392442), and form the **indicator polygon** $\Pi=\mathrm{conv}\{y_s\}\subset\mathbb C$. Then the zeros of $\beta\mapsto\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\sum_s a_s e^{\beta y_s}$ accumulate, as $|\beta|\to\infty$, on the family of rays **perpendicular to the edges of $\Pi$**: an edge $E$ with outward normal direction $\nu_E$ carries a one-dimensional string of zeros along the ray $\mathbb R_{>0}\cdot(i\nu_E)$ (parallel to the edge in the $\beta$-plane), with asymptotic spacing $2\pi/|E|$ and linear density $|E|/2\pi$. The total set is the union over edges; the longest edge carries the most zeros.

*Proof.* This is the Pólya–Schwengeler indicator-diagram theorem for exponential sums (Pólya 1920; Schwengeler 1925; Langer 1931; Boas 1954, §9.4; Moreno 1973 for the distribution refinement): the zeros of an exponential sum with frequency set $\{y_s\}$ lie asymptotically in logarithmic strips around the rays normal to the sides of the conjugate indicator diagram $\mathrm{conv}\{y_s\}$, with density along each ray equal to the length of the corresponding side over $2\pi$. The apex generator is such a sum with $a_s=\frac1N\overline{\chi_c(s)}\ne0$, so every edge is active. $\square$

## 5. The functional equation {#sec:5}

**Theorem 5.1 (Functional equation). [P]** For every ledger and every $\beta,c$,
$$\overline{\mathcal M_x(\bar\beta,c)}=\mathcal M_x(\beta,-c).$$
Consequently $\mathcal Z(x,-c)=\overline{\mathcal Z(x,c)}$ (complex conjugation of the zero set), and the self-conjugate modes $c\in\{0,N/2\}$ have zero varieties symmetric about the real $\beta$-axis.

*Proof.* On the decoherent ledger $y_s\in\mathbb R$, so $\overline{e^{\bar\beta y_s}}=e^{\beta y_s}$ and $\overline{\,\overline{\chi_c(s)}\,}=\chi_c(s)=\overline{\chi_{-c}(s)}$. Hence $\overline{\mathcal M_x(\bar\beta,c)}=\frac1N\sum_s e^{\beta y_s}\overline{\chi_{-c}(s)}=\mathcal M_x(\beta,-c)$. The zero-set statement follows by taking conjugates. $\square$

This is the apex analog of an $L$-function functional equation: it relates the mode-$c$ variety to the mode-$(-c)$ variety by reflection. The even/self-conjugate channel $c=0$ (and $c=N/2$ at even $N$) is the parity-symmetric one — the channel in which a genuine critical line, if it existed, would have to live. (Certificate **C7**: residuals $\sim10^{-31}$, one exactly $0$.)

## 6. The arithmetic bridge: the Ramanujan two-term apex {#sec:6}

We now let the ledger carry arithmetic and watch the line move.

**Theorem 6.1 (Ramanujan two-term reduction and the arithmetic tilt). [P]** Let the ledger be the **units indicator** on $\mathbb Z/N$: $\log x_s$ takes one value on $u\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$ and another on the non-units, so the centered log-ledger $y$ is two-valued with levels $\alpha$ (units) and $\gamma$ (non-units). Then $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)$ is a **two-term exponential sum** whose coefficients are Ramanujan sums:
$$\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\Big(c_N(c)\,e^{\beta\alpha}+\big(N\,\mathbf 1_{c\equiv0}-c_N(c)\big)e^{\beta\gamma}\Big),\qquad c_N(c)=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}e^{2\pi icu/N}.$$
Two regimes follow.

- **$c=0$:** coefficients $\varphi(N)$ and $N-\varphi(N)$, so the single zero family sits on the **off-line** vertical
$$\mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*=\frac{1}{\alpha-\gamma}\,\log\frac{N-\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N)}\ \xrightarrow[\ \alpha-\gamma=1\ ]{}\ \log\frac{N-\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N)},\qquad \mathrm{Im}\,\beta\in\frac{\pi}{\alpha-\gamma}(2\mathbb Z+1),$$
growing along primorials: $N=6\mapsto\log2=0.6931$, $N=30\mapsto\log\tfrac{22}{8}=1.0116$, $N=210\mapsto\log\tfrac{162}{48}=1.2164$.
- **$c\ne0$:** coefficients $c_N(c)$ and $-c_N(c)$ have **equal modulus**, so the zeros return to $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$.

In all cases the zero density stays $\Delta_y/\pi$ (linear; **no** $\log$ factor).

*Proof.* For the units-indicator ledger $e^{\beta y_s}\in\{e^{\beta\alpha},e^{\beta\gamma}\}$, so $\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\big(e^{\beta\alpha}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\zeta_N^{-cu}+e^{\beta\gamma}\sum_{s\notin(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\zeta_N^{-cs}\big)$. The first inner sum is $c_N(-c)=c_N(c)$ (Ramanujan sums are real); the second is $\sum_{\text{all }s}\zeta_N^{-cs}-c_N(c)=N\mathbf1_{c\equiv0}-c_N(c)$. For $c=0$: $\varphi(N)e^{\beta\alpha}+(N-\varphi(N))e^{\beta\gamma}=0\iff e^{\beta(\alpha-\gamma)}=-\frac{N-\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N)}$; taking moduli gives $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta(\alpha-\gamma)=\log\frac{N-\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N)}$ and the argument gives the odd-multiple imaginary spacing. For $c\ne0$: $c_N(c)\big(e^{\beta\alpha}-e^{\beta\gamma}\big)=0\iff e^{\beta(\alpha-\gamma)}=1\iff\mathrm{Re}\,\beta=0$. The density is the two-frequency case of Thm. 2.1 with $\Delta_y=|\alpha-\gamma|$. $\square$

**Remark 6.2 (The missing $\log$ is the archimedean place). [C]** Theorem 6.1 isolates the exact sense in which arithmetic enters the additive apex: it tilts the **line position** through unequal coefficient moduli ($\varphi$ vs $N-\varphi$ here; $\sqrt q$ Frobenius weights in §7), but it never changes the **linear density**. The number-theoretic critical-line object $\zeta$ has zero count $\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T)$ — a density that *grows*. No finite exponential sum can produce that $\log$; it is the signature of the **archimedean place** (the $\Gamma$-factor / completed-$\xi$ growth), the one place the finite categorical apex over $\mathbb Z/N$ — the residue ring of $\mathbb Q$ — does not see. This is the paper's single conjectural seam, tying to the finite-to-archimedean completion programme (\heb{ל}15; the apex–archimedean identification; the $N$-series Archimedean operator); it is firewalled as [C] and used nowhere in §§1–10. (Certificate **C8**: $c=0$ off-line $\mathrm{Re}_*$ to $\sim10^{-31}$, growth along primorials as above; $c\ne0$ back on $\mathrm{Re}=0$; density slope $0.3157$ vs $\Delta_y/\pi=0.3183$, $T\log T$ misfit $3.8\times$.)

## 7. The tilt ladder and Weil's line {#sec:7}

### 7.1 One formula, three rungs {#sec:7-1}

Theorems 3.3, 6.1 and the function-field lift below are three values of one quantity. $$\mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*=\frac{\log R}{\Delta}=\frac{\log|\text{coefficient ratio}|}{\log(\text{base})}.$$

| Rung | Base ring | Coefficient moduli | $R$ | $\mathrm{Re}\,\beta_*$ | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| additive characters | $\mathbb Z/N$ | $|a_s|=1$ (flat) | $1$ | $0$ — trivial/symmetry line | [P] (Thm. 3.3) |

### 7.2 The function-field apex and Weil's theorem {#sec:7-2}

Change the base ring from $\mathbb Z/N$ (residues of $\mathbb Q$) to a finite field $\mathbb F_q$ with $N\mid q-1$, and pass to multiplicative characters $\chi$ of $\mathbb F_q^\times$ of order $N$ (the \heb{ל}8 multiplicative apex, now over the geometric place). The apex coefficients become genuine **Gauss sums** $g(\chi)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_q^\times}\chi(t)\psi_{\mathrm{add}}(t)$ and **Jacobi sums** $J(\chi^a,\chi^b)=\sum_t\chi^a(t)\chi^b(1-t)$.

**Theorem 7.1 (Weil's $\tfrac12$ as the $\sqrt q$ tilt). [A] (apex-side identities [P])** For nontrivial characters, $|g(\chi)|=\sqrt q$ and $|J(\chi^a,\chi^b)|=\sqrt q$ (nontrivial product). The Jacobi sums are the Frobenius eigenvalues $\alpha_i$ on $H^1$ of the Fermat curve $C_N:x_0^N+x_1^N+x_2^N=0$ over $\mathbb F_q$, so that
$$Z(C_N/\mathbb F_q,T)=\frac{P(T)}{(1-T)(1-qT)},\quad P(T)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_iT),\quad \#C_N(\mathbb F_{q^n})=q^n+1-\sum_i\alpha_i^n,\quad g=\binom{N-1}{2}.$$
**Weil's RH for curves** (Weil 1948, 1949; Deligne 1974 in general) is $|\alpha_i|=q^{1/2}$; under $T=q^{-s}$ this reads
$$\mathrm{Re}\,s=\frac{\log|\alpha_i|}{\log q}=\frac{\log\sqrt q}{\log q}=\frac12\quad\text{exactly}$$
— a genuine, proved critical line. It is the third rung of the tilt ladder: Frobenius **supplies the $\sqrt q$ tilt** that the additive apex (unitary coefficients) cannot and that $\zeta$ must manufacture archimedean-ly.

*Proof.* The modulus identities $|g|=|J|=\sqrt q$ are classical and verified exactly on the apex side (C9). The identification of Jacobi sums with Fermat-curve Frobenius eigenvalues is Weil (1949); $|\alpha_i|=q^{1/2}$ is Weil's theorem for curves (the [A] input). The $\mathrm{Re}\,s=\tfrac12$ reading is the substitution $T=q^{-s}$. $\square$

**Worked instance (Fermat cubic, $g=1$).** For $N=3$ the curve is an elliptic curve; brute point-counting gives $a_q$, the Frobenius $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=\sqrt q$, $\mathrm{Re}\,s=0.500000$, and $\alpha=-J(\chi,\chi)$ (matched to $\sim10^{-16}$); the extension count $\#E(\mathbb F_{q^2})=q^2+1-(\alpha^2+\bar\alpha^2)$ is reproduced (e.g. $63,171$), confirming Frobenius on the extension. ## 9. The two places of $\tfrac12$ {#sec:9}

The Newton polygon of §4 is the **archimedean** ($v=\infty$) indicator diagram — the critical line lives there. There is a companion polygon at each **finite** place $p$.

**Theorem 9.1 ($p$-adic Newton companion; $\tfrac12$ at both places). [A] (instantiation [P])** Let $\mathrm{NP}$ be the $p$-adic Newton polygon of $P(T)$ — the lower convex hull of $\{(k,v_p(c_k))\}$ — and $\mathrm{HP}$ the Hodge polygon, the segment $(0,0)\to(2g,g)$ of slope $\tfrac12$. Then:

*Proof.* Statements (2)–(4) are the classical ordinary/supersingular dichotomy and Mazur's "Newton above Hodge" theorem (Mazur 1972); (1) is the total-slope identity $\sum\text{slopes}=v_p(\prod\alpha_i)=v_p(q^g)=g$ over the $2g$ roots. $\square$
