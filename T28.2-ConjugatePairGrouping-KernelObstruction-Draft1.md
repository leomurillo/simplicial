## §1. The conjugate-pair decomposition

### §1.1 Real and complex characters at primorial $P_n$

For $N = P_n = \prod_{i=1}^n p_i$, the group $(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times \cong \prod_i (\mathbb{Z}/p_i)^\times$ has Pontryagin dual $\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times$. Characters split into:

- **Real characters** ($\chi = \bar\chi$): the 2-torsion of $\widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times}$. For each odd prime $p_i \mid N$, the 2-torsion of $(\mathbb{Z}/p_i)^\times$ has order 2 ($\{1, -1\}$); for $p_1 = 2$, $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^\times$ is trivial. Total real characters: $2^{n-1}$.
- **Complex characters** ($\chi \ne \bar\chi$): paired as $\{\chi, \bar\chi\}$, with $(\varphi(N) - 2^{n-1})/2$ pairs.

Concrete counts:
- $P_2 = 6$: 2 real ($\chi_0, \chi_3$), 0 complex pairs.
- $P_3 = 30$: 4 real, 2 complex pairs.
- $P_4 = 210$: 8 real, 20 complex pairs.
- $P_5 = 2310$: 16 real, 232 complex pairs.

### §1.2 The kernel under conjugate-pair grouping

Recall from T28 the kernel decomposition at rung $k$:

$$\sum_{c \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c; N) \;=\; \mathrm{real}.$$

Split by conjugacy class:

$$\sum_c \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c; N) \;=\; \underbrace{\sum_{c: \chi_c = \bar\chi_c} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c; N)}_{\text{real characters: real}} \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{\{c, c'\}: \chi_{c'} = \bar\chi_c, c' = c^{-1}} \left[\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle S_k(c; N) + \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_{c^{-1}}\rangle S_k(c^{-1}; N)\right]}_{\text{complex pairs: must combine to real}}$$

For each complex pair $\{c, c^{-1}\}$: with $\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle = a_c + i b_c$ (real Helmert vector against complex character), $\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_{c^{-1}}\rangle = \overline{\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle} = a_c - i b_c$:

$$(a_c + i b_c) S_k(c) + (a_c - i b_c) S_k(c^{-1}) \;=\; a_c (S_k(c) + S_k(c^{-1})) \;+\; i b_c (S_k(c) - S_k(c^{-1})).$$

By overall realness (both $h_{k+1}$ and $S_k(c)$ are real, so the inner product $\sum_c \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle S_k(c)$ is real), the imaginary parts CANCEL across pairs:

$$\sum_{\{c, c^{-1}\}} b_c (S_k(c) - S_k(c^{-1})) \;=\; 0 \quad \text{(automatic)}.$$

So the kernel becomes:

$$\sum_c \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c) \;=\; \sum_{c \text{ real}} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c) \;+\; \sum_{\{c, c^{-1}\}} a_c \cdot [S_k(c) + S_k(c^{-1})].$$

### §1.3 The "even" prime-sum components

Define the **conjugate-averaged per-coset prime sum**:

$$\tilde S_k(\{c, c^{-1}\}; N) \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\big[S_k(c; N) + S_k(c^{-1}; N)\big].$$

This is the prime sum averaged over the conjugate pair. By Dirichlet equidistribution:

$$\tilde S_k(\{c, c^{-1}\}; N) \;=\; \frac{\bar S_k(N)}{\varphi(N)} \;+\; \tilde\epsilon_k(\{c, c^{-1}\}; N),$$

with the equidistribution residual $\tilde\epsilon_k = (\epsilon_k(c) + \epsilon_k(c^{-1}))/2$. By Siegel–Walfisz, $|\tilde\epsilon_k|$ is bounded by the worst per-coset residual, so the conjugate-averaging gives a **factor-of-2 noise reduction at best** — not a structural change.

### §1.4 The real-character contribution

For real $\chi_c$: $\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle$ is real, and $c = c^{-1}$ (since 2-torsion). The contribution is:

$$\sum_{c \text{ real}} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle\, S_k(c; N).$$

The real characters at primorial $P_n$ are products of single-prime Legendre characters: $\chi_c = \prod_{i: c_i \ne 0} \chi_{p_i}$ where $c = (c_1, \ldots, c_n)$ with $c_i \in \{0, 1\}$ (binary index of real characters).

## §2. The conjugate-pair main term

### §2.1 Applying character orthogonality on the real-character subgroup

The real characters form a subgroup $G_{\mathbb{R}} \subset \widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times}$ of order $2^{n-1}$. Character orthogonality **on $G_{\mathbb{R}}$** gives, for $s \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times$:

$$\sum_{\chi \in G_{\mathbb{R}}} \chi(s) \;=\; |G_{\mathbb{R}}|\, \mathbf{1}[s \in (G_{\mathbb{R}})^\perp].$$

Here $(G_{\mathbb{R}})^\perp = \{s : \chi(s) = 1 \text{ for all real } \chi\} = \{s : s \text{ is a square in every prime factor}\} = (\mathrm{Sqr})$, the squares subgroup.

So:

$$\sum_{c \text{ real}} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle \;=\; \sum_s (h_{k+1})_s \sum_{c \text{ real}} \chi_c(s) \;=\; 2^{n-1} \sum_{s \in (\mathrm{Sqr})} (h_{k+1})_s.$$

Denote $\mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{(\mathrm{Sqr})}(N) := \sum_{s \in (\mathrm{Sqr})} (h_{k+1})_s$.

This is **NOT $\varphi(N) R_{k+1}$** as in T28's full-character average. It's a partial sum of Helmert coordinates restricted to the squares subgroup — a more delicate object.

### §2.2 The complex-pair "even" structure

For the complex pairs, $\sum_{\{c, c^{-1}\}} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle + \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_{c^{-1}}\rangle = \sum_{c \in G_{\mathbb{R}}^c} \mathrm{Re}\,\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle$ where $G_{\mathbb{R}}^c$ is the complex-character complement.

By character orthogonality on the full $\widehat{}$ minus orthogonality on $G_{\mathbb{R}}$:

$$\sum_{c \in G_{\mathbb{R}}^c} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle \;=\; \sum_c \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle \;-\; \sum_{c \text{ real}} \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle \;=\; \varphi(N) R_{k+1} \;-\; 2^{n-1} \mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{(\mathrm{Sqr})}(N).$$

For the "Re" (sum of real parts of pairs):

$$\sum_{\{c, c^{-1}\}: c \ne c^{-1}} 2 a_c \;=\; \sum_{c \in G_{\mathbb{R}}^c} 2 \mathrm{Re}\,\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle \;=\; \sum_{c \in G_{\mathbb{R}}^c} (\langle h_{k+1}, \chi_c\rangle + \langle h_{k+1}, \chi_{c^{-1}}\rangle) \;=\; \varphi(N) R_{k+1} - 2^{n-1} \mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{(\mathrm{Sqr})}(N).$$

### §2.3 The full conjugate-pair main term

After equidistribution averaging:

$$\boxed{\;\mu_{k+1}^{\mathrm{prime, main, conj}}(N) \;=\; \bar S_k(N) \left[2^{n-1} \mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{(\mathrm{Sqr})}(N) \;+\; (\varphi(N) R_{k+1} - 2^{n-1} \mathcal{H}_{k+1}^{(\mathrm{Sqr})}(N))\right] \;=\; \varphi(N) R_{k+1} \bar S_k(N).\;}$$

**The conjugate-pair main term is identical to the full-coset main term of T28.** The conjugate-pair regrouping does not change the equidistribution main term.

### §2.4 Why this is structural

The reason: both groupings (full-coset and conjugate-pair) sum over the SAME character set, and equidistribution averages out across cosets uniformly. The two groupings differ only in how the SUB-CONTRIBUTIONS are organized, not in the final sum.

The character orthogonality identity $\sum_c \langle h_k, \chi_c\rangle = \varphi(N) R_k$ holds at the FULL Pontryagin dual; it doesn't matter whether we extract it via uniform average or via real+complex partition.

**Conjugate-pair grouping does not give a new main term.** The Cauchy-Schwarz obstruction from T28 §4 transfers directly.

## §3. Where the obstruction actually lives

### §3.1 The kernel ratio is intrinsic

The obstruction in T28 §4 was:

$$\frac{g_k(t)^2}{g_{k-1}(t) g_{k+1}(t)} \;=\; \frac{(2k+5)(2k+4)}{(2k+3)(2k+2)} \;>\; 1 \quad \text{for all } k \ge 1.$$

This ratio depends only on the **integrand kernel** $g_k(t) = t^{2k+3} e^{-t/2}/(2(2k+3)!)$ — the cumulant Mellin-alignment-shifted kernel — and is **independent of the prime measure $d\nu_N$, of the character grouping, and of the equidistribution residual**.

The "21/10" at $k=1$, "12/7" at $k=2$, "55/36" at $k=3$, etc., are dimensional constants of the Mellin-Gamma alignment, not artifacts of any decomposition choice.

### §3.2 The factorial denominator is the culprit

The ratio $\rho_k = (2k+5)(2k+4)/((2k+3)(2k+2))$ comes from the factorial $1/(2k+3)!$ in $g_k$:

$$\rho_k \;=\; \frac{(2k+3)!^2}{(2k+1)!(2k+5)!} \cdot \frac{t^{2(2k+3)}}{t^{2k+1} t^{2k+5}} \;=\; \frac{(2k+3)!^2}{(2k+1)!(2k+5)!} \;=\; \frac{(2k+5)(2k+4)}{(2k+3)(2k+2)}.$$

A kernel of the form $g_k(t) = t^{ak+b} e^{-ct}$ (without factorial) gives $g_k^2/(g_{k-1}g_{k+1}) = 1$ identically — pure Stieltjes positivity. The factorial breaks this by introducing a $k$-dependent normalization that is **not multiplicative in $k$**.

### §3.3 Why the factorial is there

The factorial $1/(2(2k+3)!)$ in $g_k$ comes from T9.4's Mellin-Gauss alignment: the cumulant index $k$ corresponds to integrating against $s^{2k+1}$ on the spectral line $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 1/2$, and the Taylor expansion picks up $1/(2k+3)!$ at the index shift. This is the **cumulant–Mellin–Gamma correspondence**, structurally tied to the Riemann zeta's functional equation through the $\Gamma(s/2)$ archimedean factor.

The factorial is therefore **not negotiable within the simplicial program** — it's how cumulants relate to Mellin transforms in the standard Riemann–Mellin framework.

### §3.4 What would close the obstruction

To get a PSD main term, we would need either:

**(A)** A different cumulant alignment that gives a kernel ratio $\le 1$. This means changing the Mellin pairing — i.e., using a different relationship between cumulants and the Mellin transform of $\xi$. Such a change would re-found the simplicial framework on a different analytic side.

**(B)** A Gram-form re-expression of $\mu_k$: $\mu_{i+j+1} = \sum_n A(n) f_i(n) f_j(n)$ for some positive measure $A$ and some functions $f_i$. Hankel structure ($i+j$-only) requires $f_i(n) = \alpha(n)^i \beta(n)$, giving $\mu_{k+1} = \sum_n A(n) \alpha(n)^k \beta(n)^2$ — a **power-sum representation**.

For our $\mu_{k+1}^{\mathrm{prime,main}} = \varphi(N) R_{k+1} \int g_k(t) d\nu_N(t)$, we'd need $g_k(t) = \alpha(t)^k \beta(t)$ for some $\alpha, \beta$. Computing: $g_k = t^{2k+3} e^{-t/2}/(2(2k+3)!)$ → not of the form $\alpha^k \beta$ because of the factorial.

## §4. The Gram-form route is Paper III Pass-1's angle

### §4.1 What a Gram-form re-expression would give

If $\mu_{k+1}^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}$ could be re-expressed as $\sum_n A(n) f_i(n) f_j(n)$ (Gram form) with $i + j = k$, then $J^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}_n = (\mu_{i+j+1})_{ij}$ would be the Gram matrix of $\{f_i\}$ in $\ell^2(A)$ — automatically PSD.

For Hankel structure (the $i+j$-only dependence), this requires $f_i(n) = \alpha(n)^i \beta(n)$, giving the power-sum form $\mu_{k+1} = \int \alpha(n)^k A(n)\beta(n)^2$. This IS the Stieltjes representation.

### §4.2 Paper III's Pass-1 gives exactly this on the Bochner side

Paper III §1 Pass-1 reformulation:

$$\mathcal{Q}_n^{\mathrm{Gram}}(f; y) \;:=\; \|\Psi_n(f; y)\|_{L^2}^2, \qquad \Psi_n(f; y) = \sum_{p, r} \sqrt{\log p}\, e^{-y r \log p}\, T_{r \log p} f.$$

This is **manifestly Gram-form** and **manifestly PSD**. It samples $(f * f^*)$ at prime-power differences, which is the right Bochner-positive structure for the Riemann explicit formula.

The Bochner-side cumulants at finite primorial $N$, defined via the Gram form $\mathcal{Q}_n^{\mathrm{Gram}}$, are unconditionally non-negative (Paper III T7.3). **This is the natural simplicial-side positivity statement.**

### §4.3 The (GBP) bridge is exactly the obstruction

The Hankel-side $J^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}_n$ uses the standard cumulant–Mellin alignment (with the factorial weight). The Gram-side $\mathcal{Q}_n^{\mathrm{Gram}}$ uses the squared-norm structure. The two are different number-theoretic objects (Paper III §1 lines 2671–2674): the Gram side uses $\sqrt{\log p}$ inside an absolute square; the Hankel side uses $\log p$ linearly via $-\zeta'/\zeta$.

**(GBP) is the spectral-measure identity bridging them.** Closing (GBP) would equate the Gram positivity (unconditional) with the Hankel positivity (equivalent to RH).

### §4.4 What T28.2 confirms

T28.2's structural finding: **the simplicial Hankel-side $J^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}_n$ has an intrinsic Cauchy-Schwarz obstruction inherited from the factorial-weighted Mellin alignment.** No character-organization choice (uniform coset average, conjugate-pair grouping, real-vs-complex partition, Gamma identity reorganization) removes this obstruction.

The unconditional PSD is therefore NOT achievable from the Hankel-side analysis alone; it requires the Gram-side cross-over (GBP). And (GBP) is equivalent to RH.
