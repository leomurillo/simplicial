Tier-A item A5 asks for the **Helmert-Fourier prime-side analysis at primorial $N$** — concretely, an explicit decomposition of the prime piece of the joint primorial cumulant
$$\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N) := \mu_k^{\mathrm{joint}}(N) - \mu_k^{\mathrm{arch,joint}}(N)$$
in the Helmert basis, sharp enough to bound the prime-side contribution to the Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ Hankel matrices.

The empirical work (`Apex-H-Attack-Draft1.md`, `Arch-Structural-Theorems-Draft1.md`, `Arch-Prime-N210-Duality-Draft1.md`) established:

T9.4's Gauss-sum decomposition is the structural tool that turns these empirical observations into analytical bounds: by writing the prime cumulants in the Helmert basis weighted by Dirichlet characters, the resulting expressions involve **Gauss sums + Helmert radicals**, both of which have explicit closed forms. This is the simplicial-program version of the Bombieri–Lagarias / Bettin–Chandee primorial-scale prime-sum estimates — but with the simplicial framework providing the *structural* form that classical analytic NT has to discover empirically.

## 2. Target theorems

The paper should establish (in roughly this order of importance):

### Theorem A (Core: Gauss-sum form of the unit-restricted Helmert row sum)

For each unit-index Helmert vector $h_k$ (with $1 \le k \le N-1$) and each Dirichlet character $\psi \in \widehat{(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times}$, the Dirichlet-weighted sum over the **conductor-$N$ block** $C_N = \{c : \gcd(c, N) = 1\}$
$$S_k(\psi) := \sum_{c \in C_N} \psi(c)\, \langle h_k, \chi_c \rangle$$
admits the closed form
$$S_k(\psi) = -k \tau(\psi, k) + (\text{partial-Gauss-sum residual from } (1-\omega^{kc})/(1-\omega^c))$$
where $\tau(\psi, k) = \sum_{c \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times} \psi(c) \omega^{kc}$ is the classical Gauss sum (T8 §4.1) and the residual term has its own explicit Gauss-sum expansion via geometric-series manipulation. The full closed form should track each piece arithmetically, with cleaner sub-cases for $\psi$ primitive vs. induced, and for $\gcd(k, N) = 1$ vs. $\gcd(k, N) > 1$.

### Theorem B (Conductor-$d$ sub-block decomposition for $d < N$)

For divisors $d \mid N$ with $1 < d < N$, the conductor-$d$ block $U^{(d)}$ involves additive characters $\chi_c$ with $\mathrm{cond}(\chi_c) = d$, which are *induced* from primitive characters mod $d$. Restate the Theorem-A Gauss-sum form for each such block, with the depth-filtration accounting from T0 §4.2 (Proposition 4.1 prime-power locality) tracking which primes contribute. The natural statement is a Gauss-sum identity at level $d$, lifted to level $N$ via the induction map.

### Theorem C (Bilateral matrix factorization)

Reorganize $U$ as a product of two factors:
$$U = U^{\mathrm{rad}} \cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$$
where $U^{\mathrm{rad}}$ is a diagonal matrix of Helmert radicals (the $R_k = 1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}$ factors of T9 Corollary 2.4) and $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ has entries given by Gauss sums of the explicit form derived in Theorems A and B. This is the precise statement of "the transfer matrix factors entry-wise into radical-cyclotomic content" (T9 Corollary 2.4) made *structural* at the matrix level via Gauss sums.

### Theorem D (Application: prime-side cumulant decomposition at primorial $N$)

Apply Theorems A–C to express the joint prime cumulant $\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N)$ at primorial $N$ as a sum over primes $p \le N$ of explicit Gauss-sum-weighted Helmert contributions. This is the simplicial-side statement of A5; the GRH-attack significance is that it makes the prime-side Hankel positivity question into a *finite explicit inequality* on Gauss-sum-weighted radical sums.

Specifically, the target is an identity of the form
$$\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N) = \sum_{p \le N} \sum_{j \ge 1} \frac{\Lambda(p^j) \cdot S_N(p^j) \cdot W_{k, N}(p^j)}{\sqrt{p^j}} + (\text{boundary})$$
where the weight kernels $W_{k, N}(p^j)$ are expressible in the Helmert-Gauss-sum form derived in Theorem C, and the character sum $S_N(n) = \sum_{\chi \in \hat{X}_N} \chi(n)$ has its standard Möbius-Mertens evaluation.

### Theorem E (Optional: Spectral consequences)

If the Gauss-sum factorization makes the spectrum of $U^{(d)}$ tractable, derive consequences for the eigenvalues of $U$. Connects to forward door T9.5 (Spectral Theory of the Helmert-Fourier Transfer Matrix). This is *optional* — only include if the analysis falls out naturally; don't force it.

## 3. Concrete starting computation

A worked sketch to anchor the analysis:

Starting from T9 Theorem 2.1 in rational-polynomial form:
$$\langle h_k, \chi_c \rangle = \frac{1 - \omega^{kc}}{1 - \omega^c} - k \omega^{kc} = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega^{cj} - k \omega^{kc}$$

The Dirichlet-weighted sum over $C_N = (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times$:
$$S_k(\psi) = \sum_{c \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times} \psi(c) \langle h_k, \chi_c \rangle = \sum_c \psi(c) \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \omega^{cj} - k \sum_c \psi(c) \omega^{kc}$$

The second term is $-k \tau(\psi, k)$ (classical Gauss sum at additive-index $k$). The first term splits termwise:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \sum_c \psi(c) \omega^{cj} = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \tau(\psi, j)$$
(another sum of Gauss sums, at additive-indices $j = 0, 1, \ldots, k-1$).

So:
$$\boxed{S_k(\psi) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \tau(\psi, j) - k \tau(\psi, k).}$$

Beautiful: the Dirichlet-weighted Helmert row sum is **a finite signed combination of $k+1$ Gauss sums** at consecutive additive indices. Theorem A is essentially proving and elaborating this identity, plus the special cases:

- **$\psi = \psi_0$ principal:** $\tau(\psi_0, j) = \sum_{c \in (\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times} \omega^{cj}$ is the Ramanujan sum $c_N(j)$. So $S_k(\psi_0)$ is a finite combination of Ramanujan sums — classical arithmetic.

- **$\psi$ primitive of conductor $N$:** $|\tau(\psi, j)|$ has the classical bound $\sqrt{N}$ when $\gcd(j, N) = 1$, and clean special-value structure when $\gcd(j, N) > 1$.

- **$\psi$ induced from $\psi'$ of conductor $d < N$:** $\tau(\psi, j)$ relates to $\tau(\psi', j)$ via the standard induction formula (Iwaniec–Kowalski §3.5).

The full Theorem A is just this boxed identity plus the case analysis for the three character types, with explicit closed forms in each case.
