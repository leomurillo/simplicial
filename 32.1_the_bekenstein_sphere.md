## Abstract

*The Bekenstein bound is the dimensionful clothing of a fact the categorical-simplex framework already owns.*

The Bekenstein bound states that a finite region of space holding finite energy holds at most finitely much information, $S \le 2\pi k R E/(\hbar c)$, with both sides — written as $S/k \le 2\pi R E/(\hbar c)$ — pure numbers. We show that Paper 32's one functional $\Phi=\log Z$ contains this statement at its dimensionless core, and that the "little sphere" of the bound is *literal*, not analogical. Four results, all firewall-clean (Paper 32 §12) and all backed by deterministic exact-arithmetic audits:

1. **[P] The state space is a little sphere.** The categorical row $R$, $Q=|R|$, embeds through the Hellinger map $p\mapsto 2\sqrt p$ as a *sector of a round sphere of constant curvature $K=\tfrac14$ (radius $2$)*, dimension $D=Q-1$, occupying solid-angle fraction exactly $1/2^{Q}$ — the structural integer of Paper 32 §13.2. We verify $K=\tfrac14$ symbolically (identically in coordinates) and the sector fraction by independent Monte-Carlo.

2. **[P] Capacity = the finite Bekenstein bound, saturated at the centroid.** The information capacity is $S_{\max}=\log Q=\log(\dim)$, attained at the centroid $p_\ast=(1/Q,\dots,1/Q)$ — the maximally-mixed/thermal state. It is the $\beta=0$ face of the finite Bekenstein inequality $S(\rho)-S(\sigma)\le\beta(\langle H\rangle_\rho-\langle H\rangle_\sigma)$, which is the Fenchel–Young inequality of $\Phi$ (Paper 54, Theorem 4); so the bound is an internal relation of $\Phi$ in an exact sense, not a comparison of two independent objects. The sphere and the capacity themselves are classical (Čencov; Amari–Nagaoka; Shannon). The Fisher–Rao "size" of the region stays bounded ($R_F<\pi$, diameter $=\pi$ exactly) while $S_{\max}=\log Q$ grows — the strong-holographic signature: *information grows, geometric size does not*.

3. **[P, cited] Why exactly $\hbar$ and $c$, and not $G$.** The dimensionless core of the bound is Casini's inequality $\Delta S\le\Delta\langle K\rangle$ for the modular Hamiltonian $K$ of the vacuum, a KMS state (§6). For a Rindler wedge, Bisognano–Wichmann give $K=2\pi\int x\,T_{00}\,d^3x/(\hbar c)$: $\hbar c$ converts the boost generator into dimensionless modular energy, and $2\pi$ is the KMS inverse temperature. $G$ enters only at black-hole saturation, where the entropy is read as a *dimensionful area* — permanently firewall-external (Paper 32 Principle 12.1). The Planck area $\ell_P^2=\hbar G/c^3$ is then the framework's **information cell** read in dimensionful units: the framework fixes the holographic pixel *count* (the conductor lattice, a pure number) and disowns the pixel *size* $\ell_P^2$ (§10.1).

4. **[A]/[P-structural] Boundary-localization on the arithmetic (unit-group) boundary — not a spatial boundary, not a geometric area.** The closure residual is supported on the conductor-fusion boundary $M=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$ (Paper 32 Cor 5.1d.3). A ladder audit ($N\in\{6,30,210,2310\}$) reproduces §6's $18/20$ at $N=6$ and establishes a ladder-wide parity theorem: $T_{\mathrm{unit}}=0$, the boundary is cubically *self-inert* — **the gravitating residual is boundary-supported but bulk-sourced**. The dimensional boundary share $\varphi(N)/(N-1)\to\prod_{p\mid N}(1-1/p)$ is *number-theoretic*, not a geometric codimension-1 area exponent. A third audit settles what kind of object the boundary is: the geometric area-law negative *survives the Fisher$\to$Bures metric upgrade* (the metric is isotropic, $g_{p_\ast}=Q\,I_H$, at the saturating centroid, so no metric reweights it), yet under the framework's intrinsic *conductor* (arithmetic-mass) weighting the boundary carries a **non-vanishing** share $\to\prod_p(1-\tfrac1{p^2-p+1})\approx 0.515$. The boundary is arithmetic mass, not geometric area; that limit has the firewall-clean closed form $\zeta(6)/(\zeta(2)\zeta(3))=2\pi^4/(315\,\zeta(3))$ (§11).

**The Bekenstein bound is not a new physical input: it is relative-entropy positivity against a KMS state (Casini; Paper 54), and its $\beta=0$ face is the framework's capacity $\log Q$.** Its dimensionless core is exact; its dimensionful magnitude (and the area law $S=A/4G$) is permanently external by the same firewall principle.

\nabla^{(2)}\Phi = g \;=\; \text{Fisher metric} \;=\; \text{space} \;=\; \text{inertia.}

The Bekenstein bound compares *a region of space* with *the information in it*. In ordinary physics these are two things. Here, by **Theorem 3.1 (Paper 32)**, the region's geometry *is* the second cumulant of the same functional whose zeroth-order content is the entropy. So:

**Proposition 2.1 (The bound is internal). [P, via Paper 54, Theorems 1 and 4]**

## 3. The little sphere, made literal — [P]

"This little sphere" is not a figure of speech. Under the Hellinger map $\phi(p)=2\sqrt p$, the simplex $\Delta_R^\circ$ maps onto a region of the Euclidean sphere $\{x\in\mathbb R^{Q}: |x|=2\}$, because $\sum_a (2\sqrt{p_a})^2 = 4\sum_a p_a = 4$. The Fisher–Rao metric is exactly the pullback of the round metric:
\[
g_p(u,u)=\sum_a \frac{u_a^2}{p_a}=\sum_a\Big(\frac{u_a}{\sqrt{p_a}}\Big)^2=\big|d\phi(u)\big|^2,\qquad u\in H_R .
\]

**Theorem 3.1 (The state space is a constant-curvature little sphere). [P]**
*The categorical state space is a geodesically convex **sector of the round sphere of radius $2$**, hence of constant sectional curvature $K=\tfrac14$ and scalar curvature $R=D(D-1)/4$, $D=Q-1$. The sector is the all-positive orthant: of the $2^{Q}$ sign-orthants of the ambient sphere exactly one is compatible with the positive cone, so the sector occupies solid-angle fraction exactly $1/2^{Q}$ — Paper 32 §13.2's admissible structural integer.*

*Proof.* The pullback identity above is exact; a Euclidean sphere of radius $r=2$ has constant sectional curvature $1/r^2=\tfrac14$, and a $D$-manifold of constant sectional curvature $\kappa$ has scalar curvature $D(D-1)\kappa$. The orthant count is combinatorial. $\blacksquare$

**Audit anchor (§8.1).** Audit 1 computes the Gaussian curvature of the $Q=3$ row *symbolically* and returns $K(x,y)=\tfrac14$ **identically** in coordinates; verifies $|\,2\sqrt p\,|^2=4$ and the Fisher = pullback identity to machine $\varepsilon$ on $Q\in\{3,4,6,12,30\}$; and corroborates the sector fraction $1/2^{Q}$ by isotropic Monte-Carlo (e.g. $Q=6$: exact $0.015625$ vs MC $0.01556$). The little sphere is literal.

**Theorem 4.1 (Capacity law). [P]**
*The categorical capacity is $S_{\max}=\log Q$, a pure number (the log of the structural integer $Q$), saturated at the centroid. The saturating state is the maximal-Fisher-volume point and the metric maximum $g_{p_\ast}=Q\,I_H$.*
