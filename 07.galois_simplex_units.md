## Abstract

This note records the standard cyclotomic Galois action in the regular simplex frame and uses that coordinate realization to expose a geometric model for cyclotomic units.  The group-theoretic starting point is classical: the normalizer of a cyclic shift in the symmetric group is the affine normalizer, or holomorph, of the cyclic group.  In simplicial coordinates this normalizer acts by Euclidean isometries of the zero-sum hyperplane.  If

$$
H_N=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\sum_i x_i=0\}
$$

and \(C_N\) is the cyclic shift of the projected vertex frame \(v_i=e_i-\frac1N\mathbf 1\), then the normalizer of the cyclic shift in \(S_N\) acts on \(H_N\) by simplex vertex permutations.  The quotient

$$
N_{S_N}(\langle C_N\rangle)/\langle C_N\rangle
\cong
(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times
$$

is the cyclotomic Galois group.  The automorphism

$$
\zeta_N\mapsto \zeta_N^a
$$

is represented on each cyclotomic block by the vertex multiplier

$$
v_i\mapsto v_{ai\bmod N}.
$$

This makes complex conjugation a literal simplex isometry.  On any two-dimensional cyclotomic block, it is an orientation-reversing reflection; the \(N=4\) Janus orientation is the \(\Phi_4\) instance of this phenomenon.

The new geometric content begins with the unit dictionary.  Cyclotomic units become vertex-arc sums.  For prime \(p\),

$$
u_a=\frac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}
=1+\zeta_p+\cdots+\zeta_p^{a-1}
$$

corresponds to

$$
v_0+v_1+\cdots+v_{a-1}.
$$

Real cyclotomic units correspond to palindromic arc-stars centered at a vertex.  For \(p=5\), the fundamental unit of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) is the 3-star \(v_{-1}+v_0+v_1\).  For \(p=7\), the two basic real cyclotomic units are the 3-star and 5-star, whose Galois orbits sweep the skip classes around the 7-cycle.

Thus the normalizer/Galois theorem is used here as a coordinate bridge, not claimed as new finite group theory.  The research question opened by the note is whether the Kummer-Sinnott finite-index obstruction can be read as a polytopal or regulator-volume deficiency of the arc-star lattice.

**Keywords:** cyclotomic fields, Galois group, regular simplex, zero-sum hyperplane, cyclotomic units, circular units, Janus orientation, stella octangula, simplex ETF, trace form.

## 1. Setup

Index coordinates modulo \(N\).  Let

$$
v_i=e_i-\frac1N\mathbf 1,\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z.
$$

Then

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}v_i=0
$$

and the \(v_i\) span the zero-sum hyperplane

$$
H_N=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\sum_i x_i=0\}.
$$

Their Gram matrix is

$$
\langle v_i,v_j\rangle
=
\delta_{ij}-\frac1N.
$$

The cyclic shift is

$$
C_Nv_i=v_{i+1}.
$$

It satisfies \(C_N^N=I\), fixes the gauge direction in \(\mathbb R^N\), and preserves \(H_N\).

The basic cyclotomic decomposition is

$$
H_{N,\mathbb Q}
=
\bigoplus_{\substack{d\mid N\\d>1}}H_N^{[d]},
\qquad
H_N^{[d]}:=\ker \Phi_d(C_N).
$$

For prime \(p\),

$$
H_{p,\mathbb Q}\cong\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)
$$

as a \(\mathbb Q[C_p]\)-module after choosing the correspondence

$$
v_i\longleftrightarrow \zeta_p^i.
$$

This is a module identification, not an intrinsic field multiplication on \(H_{p,\mathbb Q}\).

## 2. Galois as Simplex Isometries

Let \(C_N=(0\,1\,2\,\cdots\,N-1)\) be the \(N\)-cycle on vertex labels.  Its normalizer in \(S_N\) is the affine group

$$
N_{S_N}(\langle C_N\rangle)
=
\{i\mapsto ai+b:\ a\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times,\ b\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\}.
$$

The translation part \(b\) is the cyclic subgroup \(\langle C_N\rangle\).  Hence

$$
N_{S_N}(\langle C_N\rangle)/\langle C_N\rangle
\cong
(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times.
$$

This is the standard holomorph calculation for a cyclic group.  The contribution of the present note is not the normalizer classification itself, but the way the resulting affine multipliers act on the regular simplex frame and its cyclotomic spectral blocks.

For \(a\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\), define

$$
\sigma_a(v_i)=v_{ai}.
$$

### Theorem 2.1: Galois-Simplex Realization

The classical multiplier action \(a\mapsto\sigma_a\) realizes the cyclotomic Galois action as Euclidean isometries of the simplex frame.  More precisely:

1. \(\sigma_a\) is a vertex permutation and hence a Euclidean isometry of \(H_N\).
2. It normalizes the cyclic shift:

$$
\sigma_a C_N\sigma_a^{-1}=C_N^a.
$$

3. On each \(d\)-th cyclotomic block, it corresponds to the Galois automorphism

$$
\zeta_d\mapsto\zeta_d^a.
$$

   For prime \(N=p\), this is the module dictionary \(v_i\leftrightarrow\zeta_p^i\).

4. It preserves the cyclotomic decomposition, and on \(H_N^{[d]}\) the action factors through

$$
(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)^\times.
$$

**Proof.**  Because \(a\) is a unit modulo \(N\), \(i\mapsto ai\) is a permutation of the vertex labels.  Therefore \(\sigma_a\) preserves the standard Euclidean inner product on \(\mathbb R^N\), preserves \(\mathbf 1\), and restricts to an isometry of \(H_N\).

For the conjugation identity,

$$
\sigma_a C_N\sigma_a^{-1}(v_i)
=
\sigma_a C_N(v_{a^{-1}i})
=
\sigma_a(v_{a^{-1}i+1})
=
v_{i+a}
=
C_N^a(v_i).
$$

On the \(d\)-th cyclotomic summand, this is exactly

$$
\zeta_d^i\mapsto\zeta_d^{ai}.
$$

If a vector lies in the \(d\)-th cyclotomic block, its complex eigenvalues under \(C_N\) have exact order \(d\).  Raising those eigenvalues to the \(a\)-th power preserves their order after reducing \(a\) modulo \(d\).  Thus the block is preserved, and the action depends only on the image of \(a\) in \((\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)^\times\). \(\square\)

## 3. Complex Conjugation and Janus

Complex conjugation is the Galois element \(a=-1\).  In the simplex frame it is

$$
\sigma_{-1}(v_i)=v_{-i}.
$$

On a real rotation plane corresponding to a conjugate pair

$$
\zeta_d^k,\zeta_d^{-k},
$$

this is reflection.  Hence it reverses orientation on that plane.  On the full \(d\)-th cyclotomic block, where \(d>2\), it is a product of \(\varphi(d)/2\) commuting plane reflections, so

$$
\det(\sigma_{-1}|_{H_N^{[d]}})
=
(-1)^{\varphi(d)/2}.
$$

### Corollary 3.1: Janus Scope

The "single orientation bit" picture is clean exactly when \(\varphi(d)=2\), namely

$$
d\in\{3,4,6\}.
$$

In these cases \(H_N^{[d]}\) is a real 2-plane and complex conjugation is its orientation-reversing involution.

For \(d=4\), this is the Gaussian \(\mathbb Q(i)\) plane inside the \(N=4\) tetrahedral system.  Coupled to the parity line \(H_4^{[2]}\), this gives the cyclotomic part of the Janus orientation: Janus is complex conjugation on the \(\Phi_4\) component made geometric, together with the choice of tetrahedral orientation in \(H_4\).

For \(\varphi(d)>2\), conjugation is not a single Janus bit.  It is a simultaneous reflection of several cyclotomic planes, and only the parity of their number determines the determinant of the block.

This is the same arithmetic mechanism that appears in the classical crystallographic restriction theorem.  A lattice-preserving planar rotation has integral trace, so its eigenvalues can only come from cyclotomic data of degree at most two.  In the present language, the "single Janus bit" is exactly the case where the relevant cyclotomic summand is a real two-plane.

## 4. Cyclotomic Trace Form Recalled

For prime \(p\), the trace-Hermitian pairing on the redundant root-of-unity generators is

$$
B(\zeta^i,\zeta^j)
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)/\mathbb Q}
(\zeta^i\overline{\zeta^j})
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\zeta^{i-j}).
$$

Since

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(1)=p-1,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\zeta^m)=-1
\quad(m\not\equiv0\pmod p),
$$

the trace Gram matrix is

$$
G_{\mathrm{trace}}=pI-J.
$$

The simplex Gram matrix on \(v_i=e_i-\frac1p\mathbf 1\) is

$$
G_{\mathrm{simp}}=I-\frac1pJ.
$$

Therefore

$$
G_{\mathrm{simp}}=\frac1pG_{\mathrm{trace}}.
$$

This is the metric reason the simplex model is arithmetic rather than merely pictorial.

## 5. Cyclotomic Units as Arc Sums

For prime \(p\), define the cyclotomic unit

$$
u_a
=
\frac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}
=
1+\zeta_p+\cdots+\zeta_p^{a-1},
\qquad
1\leq a\leq p-1.
$$

Under the simplex dictionary,

$$
u_a
\longleftrightarrow
A_a:=v_0+v_1+\cdots+v_{a-1}.
$$

Thus cyclotomic units are consecutive vertex-arc sums.

### Proposition 5.1: Arc-Sum Realization of Cyclotomic Units

For prime \(p\), the standard cyclotomic units

$$
u_a=\frac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p},
\qquad
1\leq a\leq p-1,
$$

are represented in the simplex module by consecutive vertex arcs:

$$
u_a\longleftrightarrow A_a=v_0+v_1+\cdots+v_{a-1}.
$$

Together with the roots of unity, these generate the cyclotomic unit group \(C\subset \mathbb Z[\zeta_p]^\times\).  In the maximal real subfield, their real symmetrizations generate the real cyclotomic unit group \(C^+\).  Sinnott's theorem gives the finite-index caveat:

$$
[E^+:C^+]=h_p^+
$$

up to the standard power-of-two convention depending on the precise definition of real circular units.  Here \(E^+\) is the full real unit group and \(h_p^+\) is the class number of the maximal real subfield.

Thus the arc-star lattice is not asserted to be the entire real unit lattice in all primes.  Its index is the real class-number obstruction.

**Proof.**  The algebraic identity

$$
\frac{1-\zeta_p^a}{1-\zeta_p}
=
1+\zeta_p+\cdots+\zeta_p^{a-1}
$$

is immediate.  Applying the module dictionary \(v_i\leftrightarrow\zeta_p^i\) gives the arc sum.  The generation and finite-index statement is the standard cyclotomic-unit theorem of Kummer-Sinnott. \(\square\)

The Galois action sends

$$
\sigma_b(A_a)
=
v_0+v_b+v_{2b}+\cdots+v_{(a-1)b}.
$$

So the Galois orbit of a consecutive arc is the family of equal-length arcs with all possible skip lengths.

### 5.2 Real Cyclotomic Units as Palindromic Arc-Stars

Let \(a=2r+1\) be odd.  Define

$$
\eta_a
=
\zeta^{-r}u_a
=
\zeta^{-r}+\cdots+1+\cdots+\zeta^r.
$$

Then \(\eta_a\) lies in the maximal real subfield \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1})\), and

$$
\eta_a
\longleftrightarrow
S_a:=v_{-r}+\cdots+v_0+\cdots+v_r.
$$

These \(S_a\) are palindromic arc-stars centered at \(v_0\).

For \(p\) prime, the real cyclotomic units \(C^+\) are generated, up to the finite index above, by these palindromic arc-stars.

This gives the unit group a body: its standard cyclotomic generators are concrete polytopal configurations in the simplex frame.

## 6. Worked Case: \(p=5\)

The first prime with nontrivial real unit rank is \(p=5\).  The unit

$$
u_2=1+\zeta
$$

corresponds to the edge sum

$$
v_0+v_1.
$$

The trace norm and simplex norm match by the conductor normalization:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}((1+\zeta)(1+\zeta^{-1}))=6,
$$

while

$$
\langle v_0+v_1,v_0+v_1\rangle
=
\frac65.
$$

The ratio is \(5\), as predicted by \(G_{\mathrm{simp}}=\frac15G_{\mathrm{trace}}\).

The real fundamental unit is

$$
\varphi
=
1+\zeta+\zeta^{-1}
=
\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},
$$

and geometrically

$$
\varphi
\longleftrightarrow
v_{-1}+v_0+v_1.
$$

The real Galois group

$$
(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}
$$

swaps the two real spectral planes \(V_1,V_2\).  This is the geometric Galois swap of the golden pair.

## 7. Worked Case: \(p=7\)

For \(p=7\), the maximal real subfield has degree \(3\) and unit rank \(2\).  Two basic palindromic arc-stars are

$$
\eta_3
=
\zeta^{-1}+1+\zeta
\longleftrightarrow
v_6+v_0+v_1,
$$

and

$$
\eta_5
=
\zeta^{-2}+\zeta^{-1}+1+\zeta+\zeta^2
\longleftrightarrow
v_5+v_6+v_0+v_1+v_2.
$$

Using the zero-sum relation, the 5-star is also

$$
v_5+v_6+v_0+v_1+v_2
=
-(v_3+v_4).
$$

So the second real unit is the negative of the opposite pair to the centered 5-arc.

The real Galois group is

$$
(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}
\cong
\mathbb Z/3.
$$

Its orbit on \(\eta_3\) is

$$
v_{-1}+v_0+v_1,\qquad
v_{-2}+v_0+v_2,\qquad
v_{-3}+v_0+v_3.
$$

Thus Galois changes the skip length of the symmetric pair around \(v_0\).  The orbit on \(\eta_5\) is the corresponding orbit of negative symmetric pairs:

$$
-(v_{-1}+v_1),\qquad
-(v_{-2}+v_2),\qquad
-(v_{-3}+v_3).
$$

This is the first case where the real unit lattice is visibly two-dimensional in the simplex frame.

## 8. Regulator Geometry

Let

$$
r=\frac{p-3}{2}.
$$

Choose real cyclotomic units \(\eta_{a_1},\ldots,\eta_{a_r}\), and choose \(r\) of the \((p-1)/2\) real embeddings.  The real cyclotomic regulator is a determinant of logarithms:

$$
R_p^+
=
\left|\det\left(\log|\sigma_b(\eta_{a_j})|\right)\right|.
$$

In the simplex realization, each \(\eta_{a_j}\) is a palindromic arc-star.  Therefore the regulator is the volume of the logarithmic parallelotope traced by these arc-stars under the real Galois embeddings.

This is not a new formula for the regulator.  It is Dirichlet's regulator in simplicial coordinates, with the usual logarithmic embedding doing the arithmetic work.  The potential new question is whether the arc-star geometry exposes frame-theoretic or polytopal structure in the regulator or in the Sinnott index

$$
[E^+:C^+],
$$

the index of real cyclotomic units in the full real unit group.

Equivalently:

> Can the real class number obstruction be read as a geometric deficiency of the arc-star lattice inside the simplex realization?

This is the first genuinely arithmetic question opened by the framework.  The intended claim is modest but concrete: Sinnott's theorem supplies the algebraic index, while the simplex model supplies a candidate geometric body whose finite-index failure may be measured as a deficiency of arc-star-generated logarithmic parallelotopes.

## 10. Next Problem: Parity-Dual Simplices

The next geometric test is the parity-dual simplex problem.  For even \(N\), the cyclotomic decomposition contains the one-dimensional \(\Phi_2\) parity component

$$
H_N^{[2]}=\ker(C_N+I).
$$

Let \(\rho_2\) be the canonical involution that negates \(H_N^{[2]}\) and fixes every other cyclotomic block.  This separates the original \(N=4\) intuition into three claims with different status.

1. **Canonical parity involution.**  For even \(N\), \(\rho_2\) exists and produces a second regular simplex ETF \(\rho_2(V_N)\).  For odd \(N\), no \(\Phi_2\) block exists, so no such cyclic parity dual exists.
2. **Stella-octangula structure.**  For \(N=4\), \(\rho_2(V_4)=-V_4\) up to a cyclic half-turn, giving the complementary tetrahedron of the stella octangula.  This is a low-dimensional accident.
3. **Higher compound structure.**  For even \(N\geq6\), \(V_N\cup\rho_2(V_N)\) is a genuine \(2N\)-vertex compound in \(H_N\), not the union of a simplex and its antipode.  Its extra symmetry, if any, is the open question.

> For even \(N\), the \(\Phi_2\) parity component determines a parity-dual regular simplex ETF in \(H_N\).  For odd \(N\), no such canonical dual exists.

The standalone companion note verifies this theorem and tests the first non-stella case.  For \(N=6\),

$$
\rho_2(v_0)
=
\left(\frac12,\frac16,-\frac12,\frac16,-\frac12,\frac16\right),
$$

which is neither \(v_k\) nor \(-v_k\).  The verifier finds that the coordinate-permutation symmetries of \(V_6\cup\rho_2(V_6)\) have order \(72=2(3!)^2\), exactly the group preserving or swapping the even/odd coordinate classes.  The follow-up census in the companion note extends this pattern through \(N=12\).  Thus the canonical-involution statement generalizes, while the stella-octangula enhancement appears to be genuinely \(N=4\)-special.

## 11. Conclusion

The simplicial-cyclotomic ladder packages a classical finite theorem in a useful coordinate form:

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)/\mathbb Q)
$$

is realized by simplex vertex permutations normalizing the cyclic shift.  This is the affine normalizer/holomorph story expressed on the zero-sum hyperplane, where the Galois group acts by Euclidean isometries of the regular simplex frame.

This theorem absorbs the Janus idea: complex conjugation is the reflection \(v_i\mapsto v_{-i}\), and for the \(\Phi_4\) plane it is precisely the orientation-reversing bit behind the \(N=4\) tetrahedral Janus choice.

The main research value of the note is the unit geometry.  Cyclotomic units are consecutive vertex arcs; real cyclotomic units are palindromic arc-stars.  For \(p=5\) this recovers the golden unit as a 3-star.  For \(p=7\) it produces a two-generator arc-star lattice.

The research direction is now specific:

> Study regulators, class-number indices, and cyclotomic unit deficiencies as geometry of arc-star lattices inside the simplex frame.

That is a concrete arithmetic-geometric problem, not just a metaphor.
