## 1. Abstract

This paper promotes the residual side of the participation metric of Paper 29
to its own object.  In the preceding paper, identity was treated as
whole-relative participation:
\[
  SN_W(A,B)
  =
  \text{participation components}
  +
  \text{residual components}.
\]
The present paper studies the second term.  The thesis is that obstruction is
not merely failure.  When a quotient, real simplex, probe, conductor frame,
classical shadow, cubic closure, or low-cumulant model cannot carry the full
structure, the excess should be measured as a typed residual budget.

The mature target is a residual calculus for constrained overflow.  Overflow
is the part of a whole that exceeds a projection.  Constraint is the declared
structure that keeps that excess typed, bounded, and falsifiable.  Thus an
incompatibility is non-explosive precisely when it does not license arbitrary
explanation: it remains packet-typed, quotient-aware, lift-aware, dynamically
bounded or explicitly unbounded, stable under controls, and open to
falsification.

The physics bridge is a research program, not a result imported by analogy.
When a projection claims to carry dynamics, its residual certificate should
also check time-translation closure: the Noether energy, current, or form
associated to the admitted autonomous law must be preserved, or its failure
must be recorded as a typed bounded residual.  In the Nambu-Fisher setting the
parallel algebraic closure test is the fundamental/Filippov identity.  Failure
of that identity after projection is a residual coordinate to be bounded, not
automatic evidence for a new force.

The proposed whole-relative residual object has the form
\[
  R_W(A,B)
  =
  \bigl(
    R_{\mathrm{quotient}},
    R_{\mathrm{Cartan}},
    R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}},
    R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}},
    R_{\mathrm{cubic}},
    R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge 4},
    R_{\mathrm{recursive}}
  \bigr),
\]
where \(W\) declares the whole, observer, quotient, lift, conductor frame,
order cutoff, and prediction horizon.  The higher-cumulant component is not
terminated at jerk.  Rather,
\[
  R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge 4}(X)
  =
  \bigl(R^{(4)}(X),R^{(5)}(X),R^{(6)}(X),\ldots\bigr),
\]
with \(R^{(4)}\) the jerk or fourth-cumulant pointer, \(R^{(5)}\) the next
fifth-cumulant residual, and so on.  The familiar names jerk, snap, crackle,
and pop may help intuition for orders \(4,5,6,7\), but the theorem-facing
notation is \(R^{(n)}\).

The framework unifies several earlier obstructions: exact quotient packet
loss from Paper 26, the real-simplex Cartan obstruction of Papers 14 and 24,
Bures diagonal loss and recovery from Papers 15 and 18, filtered
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry from Papers 10, 11, and 13, cubic leakage
from Papers 17, 19, 21, and 24, and recursive-feedback identity from Paper 29.
The ideal theorem package would prove a bounded-overflow result: under
declared hypotheses, residuals may be nonzero while remaining controlled under
quotients, lifts, finite jets, and prediction horizons.  This draft does not
claim that theorem is closed.  It separates definitions, inherited theorems,
immediate propositions, conjectures, programmatic claims, research gates, and
falsifiers.

The downstream application is causal shadows.  A causal model can be a
legitimate quotient shadow of a richer Fisher-conductor whole when the
observer-relevant residual budgets remain controlled under recursive
prediction.  Paper 31 will study that classification.  Paper 30 supplies the
residual calculus needed before causal shadows can be trusted.  The world can
exceed a projection without exploding the theory.

## 3. Introduction

Paper 28 rejected the pairwise Newtonian blindspot at the level of dynamics.
If the primitive object is the whole ordered triplet, then the force should
not be decomposed into pairwise channels unless the decomposition is proved to
be faithful.  The result was not a declaration that every physical interaction
is ternary.  It was a replacement problem: define the non-pairwise primitive,
state its conservation laws, and make its projection failures measurable.

Paper 29 rejected the analogous blindspot at the level of identity.  It
defined \(SN_W(A,B)\) as a whole-relative, packet-resolved,
Bures-aware participation object.  The subscript \(W\) was essential.  It
declared the whole, observer, task, packet frame, quotient maps, lift, and
residual ledger inside which \(A\) and \(B\) appear as projections.  Thus
\(SN_W(A,B)\) is not an absolute pairwise similarity score.  It is a
structured participation calculus inside a declared whole.

Paper 30 asks what happens to the parts of \(SN_W\) that do not fit the
declared projection.  A quotient may erase packets.  A real simplex may forbid
nonabelian directions that become legal in the Bures lift.  A conductor
associated graded may forget filtered carry.  A cubic closure may leak into a
higher cumulant.  A low-order predictive model may work for one horizon and
fail under recursive feedback.  These are not the same defect, and treating
them as one generic error destroys information.

The proposed answer is a typed residual calculus.  A residual is not merely
"what the model got wrong."  It is the structured excess of a whole over a
declared projection:
\[
  \text{whole}
  =
  \text{projection-readable part}
  +
  \text{typed residual}.
\]
The residual is useful only when its type is declared.  Hidden conductor
packet energy, diagonal dephasing loss, filtered carry, cubic leakage, and
higher-cumulant mismatch have different algebraic causes and different
controls.  They should not be collapsed into an undifferentiated distance.

This turns obstruction into a positive object.  The Cartan obstruction is not
only the statement that \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\) fails to
act on the real simplex.  It is also a coordinate saying which nonabelian
directions require a lift.  Quotient probe blindness is not only a failed
probe.  It is an exact packet ledger determined by the divisor rule.  Jerk is
not a mystical extra force.  It is the first visible higher-cumulant overflow
pointer after cubic closure.  And jerk is not the end of overflow; it is the
first named member of the higher-cumulant residual tower.

The philosophical motivation is close to the non-explosive incompatibility
language in Kajetan Mlynarski's 2025 causality work: contradiction need not
mean arbitrary inference if it is held inside constraints.  This paper uses
that language as motivation only.  The mathematical discipline is internal:
packet support, quotient survival, lift recovery, finite-jet closure,
prediction horizon, and empirical controls decide whether overflow is
constrained or uncontrolled.

The final purpose is practical.  Causal models, linear probes, classical
shadows, low-order cumulant closures, and quotient readouts are often useful.
This paper does not discard them.  It asks when they are honest shadows.  A
projection remains usable when its residual budget is typed and controlled.  A
projection becomes ontology by mistake when its overflow is untyped, hidden,
or unbounded.

## 4. What Is Constrained Overflow?

This section fixes the vocabulary.

**Definition 4.1 (Projection).**  
A projection is any declared map or reduction
\[
  \Pi:X\to Y
\]
that makes a full object \(X\) readable in a smaller space \(Y\).  In this
paper, projections include quotient maps \(C_N\to C_k\), diagonal
measurements \(\rho\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(\rho)\), associated-graded
forgetful maps from filtered conductor algebras, low-cumulant truncations,
finite-jet cutoffs, classical readouts, probes, and prediction-horizon
reductions.

**Definition 4.2 (Overflow).**  
Given a full object \(X\), projection \(\Pi\), and declared reconstruction or
comparison convention \(s\) when one is available, the overflow is the part of
\(X\) not carried by \(\Pi\):
\[
  \operatorname{Overflow}_{\Pi,s}(X)
  =
  X - s\Pi X,
\]
when such subtraction is meaningful.  More generally, overflow is the
structured failure of \(\Pi\) to preserve the components of \(X\) required by
the context \(W\).

The second formulation is necessary because several residuals are not simple
linear differences.  A quotient residual is naturally a packet-support vector.
A Bures residual may be a coherence lost under a channel.  An
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) residual is a filtered carry class modulo section
coboundaries.  A recursive-feedback residual is a mismatch between a predicted
whole and an updated whole after action.

**Definition 4.3 (Constraint).**  
A constraint is the declared structure that makes overflow legible.  In the
Fisher-conductor setting, constraints may include:

1. packet type;
2. quotient map;
3. conductor frame or moving-frame convention;
4. Bures or Fisher lift;
5. finite cumulant/order cutoff;
6. finite-jet ledger;
7. prediction horizon;
8. admissible controls;
9. falsification criterion.

**Definition 4.4 (Budget).**  
A residual budget is a quantitative or qualitative ledger of typed overflow:
\[
  R_W(X;\Pi)
  =
  \bigl(r_1,\ldots,r_m\bigr),
\]
where each \(r_i\) has a declared type, measurement convention, and claim
status.  A budget may contain exact norms, support indicators, cohomology
classes, bounds, open entries, and explicitly unbounded entries.

**Definition 4.5 (Constrained overflow).**  
Overflow is constrained relative to \(W\) when its residual budget is:

1. packet-typed or lift-typed;
2. quotient-aware;
3. dynamically bounded or explicitly marked unbounded;
4. stable under the controls specified by \(W\);
5. falsifiable by a stated mathematical or empirical test.

An incompatibility that satisfies these conditions is non-explosive.  It does
not imply "anything goes."  It says that two shadows are incompatible because
they omit different typed parts of the whole.

This is the central distinction:
\[
  \text{unconstrained overflow}
  =
  \text{excuse for missing structure},
\]
whereas
\[
  \text{constrained overflow}
  =
  \text{typed residual requiring a budget}.
\]

The world can exceed a projection without exploding the theory.

## 5. The Residual Object \(R_W\)

The residual object is whole-relative, just as participation was
whole-relative in Paper 29.

**Definition 5.1 (Residual context).**  
A residual context is a tuple
\[
  W=(\mathcal X,\mathcal Q,\mathcal L,\mathcal K,\mathcal T,\mathcal F,\mathcal C),
\]
where:

1. \(\mathcal X\) is the class of full states, objects, signals, or dynamics;
2. \(\mathcal Q\) is the family of quotients, readouts, probes, or
   projections under study;
3. \(\mathcal L\) is the lift or ambient geometry, such as Fisher, Bures, or a
   filtered conductor algebra;
4. \(\mathcal K\) is the order cutoff, cumulant cutoff, or finite-jet
   convention;
5. \(\mathcal T\) is the prediction horizon or dynamical window;
6. \(\mathcal F\) is the chosen frame, packet transport, or section convention;
7. \(\mathcal C\) is the set of controls and falsifiers.

For dyadic participation problems we write \(R_W(A,B;\Pi)\).  For a general
state or dynamics \(X\), we write
\[
  R_W(X;Q,L,K,T),
\]
where \(Q\) is a quotient/readout/projection, \(L\) is the lift or ambient
geometry, \(K\) is the order cutoff, and \(T\) is the horizon.

**Definition 5.2 (Structured residual object).**  
The default residual object is tuple-valued:
\[
  R_W(A,B;\Pi)
  =
  \bigl(
    R_{\mathrm{quotient}},
    R_{\mathrm{Cartan}},
    R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}},
    R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}},
    R_{\mathrm{cubic}},
    R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4},
    R_{\mathrm{recursive}}
  \bigr)_W.
\]
Entries may be zero, nonzero, undefined, unobserved, or outside the declared
context.  Undefined does not mean zero.  If \(W\) has no Bures lift, then
\(R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}}\) should be marked unobserved rather than
invented.  If \(W\) has no conductor frame, packet residuals are not
well-defined.  If \(W\) has no recursive prediction loop, the recursive entry
is programmatic rather than measured.

**Proposition 5.3 (Whole-relativity).**  
There is no absolute residual \(R(A,B)\) independent of \(W\).

**Reason.**  Residuals are failures of declared projections, and projections
are context-dependent.  A quotient residual depends on the quotient.  A Bures
residual depends on whether the density-matrix lift is part of the model.  A
higher-cumulant residual depends on the order cutoff.  A recursive-feedback
residual depends on the prediction horizon and update protocol.  Therefore the
same pair \(A,B\) can have vanishing residuals in one task context and large
residuals in another.  \(\square\)

**Guardrail 5.4 (No scalar by default).**  
The invariant datum is the structured residual tuple.  A scalar incompatibility
score can be introduced only after \(W\) supplies weights, norms, and task
losses.  Without those choices, scalar aggregation hides the type of overflow
and can turn a useful projection into an ontology.

## 6. Quotient Overflow

The cleanest residual is quotient overflow because Paper 26 gives an exact
theorem.

Let \(R=C_N\) and let
\[
  q_k:C_N\to C_k,\qquad k\mid N,
\]
be the canonical cyclic quotient.  Let \(P_{q_k}\) be the orthogonal
projection onto functions factoring through \(q_k\).  In conductor packets,
Paper 26 proves:
\[
  P_{q_k}H_d=H_d\quad\text{iff}\quad d\mid k,
\]
and
\[
  P_{q_k}H_d=0\quad\text{iff}\quad d\nmid k.
\]

**Definition 6.1 (Quotient residual).**  
For a conductor-decomposed signal
\[
  x=\sum_{d\mid N}x_d,\qquad x_d\in H_d,
\]
define the exact quotient residual under \(q_k\) by
\[
  R_{\mathrm{quotient}}^{(k)}(x)
  =
  \sum_{d\nmid k}\|x_d\|^2,
\]
with norm taken in the declared conductor/Fisher frame.  For a pair \(A,B\),
the residual may be measured on their difference, their participation
mismatch, or their hidden packet participation, depending on \(W\):
\[
  R_{\mathrm{quotient}}^{(k)}(A,B)
  =
  \sum_{d\nmid k}
  \operatorname{budget}_d(A,B).
\]

**Immediate proposition 6.2 (Exact vanishing criterion).**  
In the exact cyclic quotient setting, \(R_{\mathrm{quotient}}^{(k)}(x)=0\) iff
the nontrivial packet support of \(x\) lies entirely in packets \(H_d\) with
\(d\mid k\).

This is the finite model of constrained overflow.  A quotient can be perfectly
valid for \(C_k\)-tasks while losing packets that matter for a \(C_N\)-task.
High quotient participation is therefore not full identity.  It is identity
relative to a readout whose blindness is mathematically known.

For the month row \(C_{12}\), the canonical packet ledger is:

| quotient \(k\) | visible packets | hidden packets |
|---:|---|---|
| 2 | \(H_2\) | \(H_3,H_4,H_6,H_{12}\) |
| 3 | \(H_3\) | \(H_2,H_4,H_6,H_{12}\) |
| 4 | \(H_2,H_4\) | \(H_3,H_6,H_{12}\) |
| 6 | \(H_2,H_3,H_6\) | \(H_4,H_{12}\) |
| 12 | \(H_2,H_3,H_4,H_6,H_{12}\) | none |

This residual is not a psychological statement about a probe.  It is a packet
budget forced by the quotient algebra.

## 7. Cartan and Bures Overflow

Papers 14, 15, 18, and 24 establish the obstruction-completion pattern for
the minimal mixed row
\[
  R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z
  \cong
  \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
On the real simplex, preserving positivity, Fisher volume, and CRT
compatibility forces the continuous symmetry algebra to the Cartan sector
\[
  \mathfrak u(1)^2
  \subset
  \mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]
The nonabelian root directions do not act as admissible real-simplex
symmetries.  In the density-matrix lift
\[
  \mathcal D(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3),
\]
the Bures-Helstrom metric recovers Fisher on the diagonal and admits the
nonabelian directions as unitary coherences.

**Definition 7.1 (Cartan residual).**  
Let \(G_{\mathrm{full}}\) denote the declared full local algebra or symmetry
content in the lift, and let \(G_{\mathrm{real}}\) be the admissible
real-simplex algebra.  The Cartan residual is the component
\[
  R_{\mathrm{Cartan}}
  =
  G_{\mathrm{full}}/G_{\mathrm{real}},
\]
understood not as a literal quotient in every setting, but as the typed budget
of directions forbidden by the real simplex and recovered only after a lift.
For the \(R=6\) cubic row, this is the nonabelian complement of
\(\mathfrak u(1)^2\) inside
\(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\), subject to the exact hypotheses of
Papers 14 and 15.

**Definition 7.2 (Bures/dephasing residual).**  
Let \(\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal D(\mathcal H)\) and let
\[
  D(\rho)=\operatorname{diag}(\rho)
\]
be the diagonal measurement channel in the declared basis.  The
Bures/dephasing residual is the mismatch between participation in the lifted
state and participation after diagonal measurement:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}}(\rho,\sigma)
  =
  P_B(\rho,\sigma)-P_H(D\rho,D\sigma),
\]
when the signed convention is useful, or
\[
  |P_B(\rho,\sigma)-P_H(D\rho,D\sigma)|
\]
when the budget records magnitude only.  More refined versions decompose the
same residual by CRT packet or local unitary direction.

**Immediate proposition 7.3 (Diagonal vanishing).**  
If \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\) are diagonal commuting density matrices in the
declared basis, then the Bures participation recovers Hellinger participation
and
\[
  R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}}(\rho,\sigma)=0.
\]

This makes Bures a completion layer, not optional decoration.  If a residual
is caused by noncommuting coherence that the real simplex cannot represent,
then a purely diagonal Fisher account is not wrong because it is approximate.
It is wrong if it refuses to record the missing lift.

The same logic also prevents overclaiming.  A nonzero Bures/dephasing residual
does not by itself prove a physical quantum interpretation for an empirical
system.  It proves that the declared diagonal projection has lost coherence in
the lifted model.  The interpretation depends on the rest of \(W\).

## 8. \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) and Conductor Carry Overflow

Quotient residuals record packet loss under a quotient.  Cartan/Bures
residuals record directions forbidden by the real simplex and recovered by a
lift.  \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) records a different phenomenon: filtered
carry that cannot be split canonically by passing to the associated graded
conductor shadow.

Papers 10 and 11 develop the quotient-character adjacency algebra and its
filtered local multiplication law.  Paper 13 imports this into simplectic
mechanics as a finite arithmetic anomaly of the conductor current.  The
important point for Paper 30 is:
\[
  \text{associated graded agreement}
  \not\Rightarrow
  \text{filtered conductor agreement}.
\]
The missing term is carry.

**Definition 8.1 (Filtered carry residual).**  
Fix a filtered conductor algebra, an associated graded projection
\[
  \operatorname{gr}:\mathcal A_{\mathrm{filtered}}\to
  \mathcal A_{\mathrm{graded}},
\]
and a section convention \(s\) when one is available.  The
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) residual is the section-invariant carry class
\[
  R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}}
  =
  [\,x-s\operatorname{gr}(x)\,]_{\mathrm{carry}},
\]
modulo the coboundaries generated by changing filtered section.

In words: \(R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}}\) is the residual produced when tame
residue motion and wild depth motion cannot be split canonically.  It is a
filtered carry budget.

**Guardrail 8.2 (Not every support residual is \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)).**  
A support mismatch, a closure defect, and an \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry
class are related but not identical.  The Ext-facing object must be invariant
under the allowed change of filtered section.  Otherwise it is a coordinate
artifact.

For Paper 30, the scalar normalization of
\(R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}}\) remains programmatic.  A useful budget may
record:

1. raw carry atoms;
2. normalizer orbits;
3. residual support after coboundary reduction;
4. signed normalized carry mass;
5. whether the residual disappears, converges, or obstructs a continuum
   scaling limit.

The key conceptual move is that filtered carry is not a failure of the
conductor formalism.  It is one of the things the conductor formalism was
built to see.

## 9. Cubic Leakage and Higher-Cumulant Overflow

Papers 17 and 24 identify the cubic information-geometric force as the raised
Amari-Chentsov tensor
\[
  K^{CH}(v,v).
\]
The cubic Noether theorem gives a closed variational object inside its
admissible real-simplex sector.  Papers 19 and 20 then interpret the next
covariant derivative
\[
  \nabla^{(e)}T=T^{(4)}
\]
as the leading leakage or jerk pointer when the cubic closure is projected,
truncated, or forced through a classical channel.

The Noether part matters for the projection certificate.  If the admitted
cubic or \(n\)-ary law is autonomous, time translation supplies an energy,
current, or form that must close in the shadow.  A projection that preserves
force shape but leaks this Noether quantity has not closed dynamically; it has
created a typed residual inside \(R_{\mathrm{cubic}}\) or the downstream
closure certificate of Paper 31.

Paper 30 keeps that insight but removes a possible misunderstanding: the
residual tower does not stop at jerk.

**Definition 9.1 (Cubic residual).**  
Let \(X\) be a state, path, or projected dynamics with a declared cubic
closure model \(K^{CH}\).  The cubic residual
\[
  R_{\mathrm{cubic}}(X;W)
\]
records the failure of the declared projection or model to preserve the cubic
Noether/Fisher-conductor closure.  Depending on \(W\), this may be measured as
leakage outside declared conductor support, mismatch in cubic energy, failure
of an admissibility condition, or residual mass transported into undeclared
packets or lifts.

**Definition 9.2 (Higher-cumulant residual tower).**  
The higher-cumulant residual is the sequence
\[
  R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}(X)
  =
  \bigl(R^{(4)}(X),R^{(5)}(X),R^{(6)}(X),\ldots\bigr),
\]
where \(R^{(n)}\) records the order-\(n\) cumulant overflow relative to the
declared cutoff.  The leading member
\[
  R^{(4)}
\]
is the fourth-cumulant residual, or jerk pointer, after cubic closure.  The
next members \(R^{(5)},R^{(6)},R^{(7)}\) correspond to the higher derivative
intuition sometimes called snap, crackle, and pop, but the sober notation is
\(R^{(n)}\).  When the shorthand \(R^{(\ge4)}\) is used, it means this typed
tower \(R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}\), not a single scalar obstruction.

**Guardrail 9.3 (Jerk is not terminal).**  
Jerk is not the final obstruction.  It is the first visible
higher-cumulant overflow pointer after cubic closure.  Treating it as the last
residual would repeat the same low-order blindspot that Papers 28 and 29 were
designed to avoid.

The tower should be read with claim-status discipline.

1. \(R^{(4)}\) has inherited support from Papers 17, 19, 20, 21, 24, and 28 as
   the jerk/fourth-cumulant pointer.
2. \(R^{(5)}\) and above require higher-jet variational and stability
   analysis.
3. A nonzero \(R^{(n)}\) may indicate a real missing lift, a wrong projection,
   a finite-order truncation artifact, a numerical artifact, or a task-irrelevant
   coordinate.
4. Promoting \(R^{(n)}\) to an independent dynamical variable requires its own
   boundedness or degeneracy theorem, especially in higher-derivative
   variational settings.

**Guardrail 9.4 (Bianchi-Filippov is structural).**  
The Einstein comparison is only a structural guide.  In general relativity,
the contracted Bianchi identity enforces the integrability of the field
equation with covariantly conserved stress-energy.  In the Nambu-Fisher
program, the corresponding closure obligation is the fundamental/Filippov
identity for the admitted bracket.  Under a projection \(\Pi\), its defect may
be recorded schematically as
\[
  R_{\mathrm{Filippov}}^\Pi
  =
  \Pi(\text{Filippov left side})
  -
  \Pi(\text{Filippov right side}),
\]
or by the equivalent packet, lift, or finite-jet ledger supplied by \(W\).
The claim is not that the Bianchi identity has been derived from the
Nambu-Fisher bracket.  The claim is that both identities play the same kind of
role in a closure certificate: they prevent a projected dynamics from
inventing unconstrained sources.

**Conjectural target 9.5 (Higher-cumulant compatibility).**  
For well-declared Fisher-conductor contexts \(W\), the higher-cumulant tower
admits a compatible finite-jet budget:
\[
  \|R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge m}(X)\|_{W,T}
  \le
  \Phi_m(\delta_m(W),T,\operatorname{support}(X)),
\]
where \(\delta_m(W)\) is the order-\(m\) closure defect and \(T\) is the
declared horizon.  This is a conjectural template, not a theorem of this
paper.

## 10. Boundedness and Non-Explosion

Residual calculus is useful only if it separates controlled overflow from
uncontrolled failure.

**Definition 10.1 (Controlled residual budget).**  
A residual budget \(R_W(X)\) is controlled on a horizon \(T\) if each declared
component is assigned one of the following statuses:

1. **zero:** proved absent under the hypotheses;
2. **bounded:** equipped with a norm, support, or finite ledger bound;
3. **stable:** nonzero but invariant or controlled under the declared
   transformations;
4. **unobserved:** not measured because \(W\) lacks the needed lift or frame;
5. **unbounded:** explicitly identified as outside the model's validity;
6. **programmatic:** named but awaiting definition or measurement.

Unobserved and zero must never be conflated.

There are several useful forms of boundedness.

**Norm bound.**
\[
  \|R_i(X)\| \le \epsilon_i(T).
\]

**Packet support bound.**
\[
  \operatorname{supp}R_{\mathrm{quotient}}
  \subseteq
  \{H_d:d\in S_{\mathrm{hidden}}\}.
\]

**Quotient survival bound.**
\[
  \sum_{d\mid k}\|x_d\|^2
  \ge
  1-\epsilon.
\]

**Lift recovery bound.**
\[
  |P_B(\rho,\sigma)-P_H(D\rho,D\sigma)|
  \le
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{dephase}}.
\]

**Finite-jet bound.**
\[
  \sum_{n\ge m}\|R^{(n)}(X)\|
  \le
  \Phi_m(\delta_m,T).
\]

**Noether time-translation bound.**
\[
  |E_\Pi(t)-E_\Pi(0)|
  \le
  \epsilon_E(T),
\]
or, for the appropriate current or form, the corresponding conserved quantity
is zero, bounded, or explicitly outside the projected model.

**Filippov closure bound.**
\[
  \|R_{\mathrm{Filippov}}^\Pi(X)\|_{W,T}
  \le
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{Fil}}(T).
\]

**Prediction-horizon bound.**
\[
  \|R_{\mathrm{recursive}}(\widehat W_t,W_{t+1})\|
  \le
  \epsilon(T).
\]

**Recurrence/heteroclinic bound.**  
Using the language of Paper 19, leakage along regular Nambu-Fisher leaves is
controlled by finite-jet closure defects, with trajectories classified as
interior periodic, boundary-stratum periodic, or heteroclinic under the stated
hypotheses.  Paper 30 imports this as a boundedness pattern, not as a universal
theorem for every residual component.

**Conjecture 10.2 (Bounded overflow theorem).**  
Let \(W\) be a Fisher-conductor residual context with:

1. exact or approximately controlled quotient maps;
2. a declared Fisher or Bures lift;
3. finite conductor packet frame or transport;
4. finite-jet closure ledger through order \(m\);
5. time-translation closure for the admitted Noether energy, current, or
   form, or a typed residual marking its failure;
6. a fundamental/Filippov identity ledger for Nambu-Fisher brackets when such
   brackets are part of \(W\);
7. prediction horizon \(T\);
8. admissible controls excluding frame artifacts and shortcut explanations.

Then there exists a typed budget
\[
  R_W(X)
  =
  \bigl(R_{\mathrm{quotient}},R_{\mathrm{Cartan}},
  R_{\mathrm{Bures/dephase}},R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}},
  R_{\mathrm{cubic}},R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4},
  R_{\mathrm{recursive}}\bigr)
\]
such that nonzero residuals do not imply arbitrary failure.  They are either
zero, bounded, stable, unobserved, explicitly unbounded, or programmatic in
the sense of Definition 10.1.

This conjecture is intentionally modest.  It does not say every projection is
valid.  It says that a projection can remain valid when the overflow it creates
is typed and controlled.  If the overflow is untyped or unbounded, the
projection has exceeded its warrant.

## 11. AI and Empirical Residuals

Papers 12, 26, and 29 supply the empirical side of the residual calculus.
The point is not to declare that transformer internals are literally the same
as the formal conductor object.  The point is to use finite tasks where the
output algebra, quotient maps, and packet predictions are explicit enough to
test residual claims.

### 11.1 Quotient Probe Blindness

For calendar tasks such as \(C_7\) and \(C_{12}\), a quotient-tied probe can
perform well while being blind to forbidden packets.  The residual budget is:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{quotient}}^{(k)}
  =
  \text{native packet energy not visible to the }C_k\text{ readout}.
\]

Controls:

1. shuffled labels;
2. same-rank random subspaces;
3. non-equivariant probes allowed to break the quotient;
4. comparison against native logits before binning.

The falsifier is a quotient-respecting readout that reliably recovers
forbidden packets without violating the hypotheses.

### 11.2 Native Logit Packet Loss

The native output logits can be decomposed into conductor packets.  A quotient
readout may erase a packet that the model's native behavior actually uses.
The residual is not model error; it is readout error:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{native}\to\mathrm{quotient}}
  =
  \Pi_{\mathrm{hidden}}^{(k)}(\text{native logits}).
\]

The empirical question is whether hidden packet mass predicts failures,
generalization differences, or shortcut behavior beyond ordinary distance
metrics.

### 11.3 Activation-to-Behavior Residual

Let
\[
  F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ
\]
be an activation-to-behavior map.  The pullback metric
\[
  J_F^\top g J_F
\]
from Paper 12 compares activation geometry to behavior geometry.  The
residual version asks:

1. which activation directions map to behavior packet directions;
2. which behavior packets have no stable activation-side chart;
3. which activation similarities collapse under the output quotient;
4. which residuals survive layer, prompt, and model-family controls.

### 11.4 Hidden-State Shortcut Residual

Paper 26 records a live issue: hidden-state probes may solve a task through
shortcuts, including nonlinear decimal sum/carry features, even when quotient
packet predictions remain exact for the output algebra.  The residual budget
must therefore distinguish:

1. true conductor packet structure;
2. linear input shortcut residuals;
3. nonlinear decimal shortcut residuals;
4. base-10 ablation effects;
5. non-equivariant escape readouts.

A residual that fails these controls is not a conductor residual.  It is a
shortcut artifact.

### 11.5 Bures/Dephasing Residual in Models

Papers 15 and 18 motivate a pre-softmax or residual-stream geometry richer
than the final diagonal output simplex.  Empirically this can be tested only
with caution.  A constructed density matrix, covariance coherence, or
nonabelian-vs-Cartan squash signature is not proof of physical quantum
structure.  It is evidence for a dephasing residual if it:

1. is stable under basis and layer controls;
2. distinguishes Cartan from nonabelian perturbations;
3. predicts output behavior after the final measurement channel;
4. fails in shuffled or random controls.

### 11.6 Persistent Feedback Identity Residual

Paper 29 ends with recursive participation.  A future agent diagnostic should
compare:
\[
  SN_W(I_t,I_{t+1}),\qquad
  SN_W(\widehat W_t,W_{t+1}),
\]
and track whether action consequences change durable identity packets rather
than merely accumulating context.

The residual is:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{recursive}}
  =
  \text{prediction/update mismatch after feedback}.
\]

Controls include memory ablation, counterfactual feedback, shuffled
consequences, delayed updates, and non-persistent context accumulation.  This
is programmatic in Paper 30 and belongs to the Paper 31 causal-shadow agenda.

## 12. Research Gates / Falsifiers

The following gates separate the ideal abstract from a closed theorem package.

### 12.1 Mathematical Gates

**Gate M1: exact residual definitions.**  
Define each residual component as an object in its natural category: packet
norm, quotient support, Bures coherence, filtered carry class, finite-jet
closure defect, or recursive prediction mismatch.

**Gate M2: compatibility among residuals.**  
Determine when quotient residuals, Bures residuals, and conductor carry
residuals commute, refine each other, or interfere.  In particular, prove
packet-resolved diagonal recovery for Bures lifts when a CRT packet
decomposition is declared.

**Gate M3: off-centroid frame discipline.**  
Compare fixed square-root packet frames, transported Fisher-orthogonal frames,
and quotient-character jet frames.  Identify which residuals are invariant and
which are frame artifacts.

**Gate M4: \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) budget normalization.**  
Give a scalar or measure-valued carry budget that is invariant under section
change and compatible with the normalized carry-measure trichotomy in the
finite-to-continuum setting.

**Gate M5: higher-cumulant tower.**  
Define \(R^{(n)}\) for \(n\ge4\) in a way that separates true missing
higher-order structure from projection artifacts and numerical derivatives.
Prove at least one finite-jet boundedness result beyond \(R^{(4)}\).

**Gate M6: bounded overflow theorem.**  
Prove a non-explosive overflow theorem under explicit hypotheses, or identify
which residual component prevents such a theorem.

**Gate M7: causal-shadow interface.**  
State the criterion for a causal model to be a legitimate quotient shadow:
which residual budgets must vanish, which may remain bounded, and which make
the causal shadow invalid for a horizon \(T\).

### 12.2 Empirical Gates

**Gate E1: residual atlas for \(C_7\) and \(C_{12}\).**  
Compute Hellinger participation, packet participation, quotient residuals,
native logit residuals, and hidden-state residuals for existing calendar
tasks.

**Gate E2: quotient residual comparison across models.**  
Run the Paper 26 quotient-probe checks across model families, layers, token
sets, and prompt templates.

**Gate E3: higher-cumulant signal tests.**  
Build finite-difference or jet-based diagnostics for \(R^{(4)}\) and candidate
\(R^{(5)}\) signals.  Test whether they add predictive value beyond cubic
leakage and ordinary curvature.

**Gate E4: shortcut controls.**  
Audit source/offset residualization, base-10 decimal sum/carry ablation,
random same-rank subspaces, shuffled labels, and non-equivariant probes.

**Gate E5: Bures/dephasing controls.**  
Test whether noncommuting lift diagnostics matter in tasks designed to require
noncommuting structure.  If diagonal Hellinger diagnostics always suffice, the
Bures residual remains mathematically valid but empirically idle for those
tasks.

**Gate E6: persistent feedback loop audit.**  
Construct a controlled recursive prediction setting and measure whether
identity packets change after action consequences.  Separate durable identity
revision from context-length accumulation.

### 12.3 Falsifiers

**F1: residuals collapse to ordinary distance.**  
If packet residuals, Bures residuals, carry residuals, and higher-cumulant
residuals add no predictive or explanatory power beyond Hellinger, KL, or
Euclidean distance, the residual calculus is mostly terminology.

**F2: residuals are frame artifacts.**  
If residual values vary wildly under reasonable packet frames or lift choices
without invariant content, the framework must restrict itself to the settings
where the residuals are invariant.

**F3: hidden packet residuals fail controls.**  
If quotient-hidden packet mass does not distinguish true quotient blindness
from shuffled labels, random same-rank subspaces, or shortcut features, the
interpretability claim fails.

**F4: higher-cumulant tower adds no value.**  
If \(R^{(4)}\) and higher terms do not improve prediction, boundedness, or
classification beyond cubic leakage, the tower should be treated as formal
bookkeeping rather than an active empirical object.

**F5: Bures residual never matters where it should.**  
If tasks designed to require noncommuting lift structure are always fully
explained by diagonal classical residuals, the Bures component remains a
formal completion but loses empirical motivation in that domain.

**F6: recursive residual reduces to ordinary memory.**  
If feedback identity diagnostics are fully explained by context accumulation,
without durable packet-level identity revision, the strong recursive identity
claim should be rejected.

## 13. Bridge to Paper 31: Causal Shadows

Paper 30 does not classify causal models.  It prepares the residual object
needed for that classification.

A causal model is often a quotient shadow:
\[
  \text{full whole }W
  \xrightarrow{\Pi_{\mathrm{causal}}}
  \text{causal graph, mechanism, or intervention model}.
\]
This shadow can be legitimate.  It becomes illegitimate only when it forgets
that it is a shadow.

The residual criterion suggested by Paper 30 is:
\[
  \text{causal shadow valid on horizon }T
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  R_W^{\mathrm{observer}}(T)
  \text{ is controlled for the task}.
\]
Here "controlled" does not mean zero.  It means the residuals relevant to the
task are typed, bounded, and stable under recursive prediction.

The Paper 31 projection certificate should therefore include more than a
prediction score.  For dynamical shadows it must ask whether time-translation
Noether closure survives the projection, whether any Nambu-Fisher bracket
still satisfies its fundamental/Filippov identity after projection, and
whether failures of \(R^{(4)}\), \(R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}\), recursive
feedback, or typed overflow stay within the declared horizon budget.

If a future gravity- or dark-matter-adjacent shadow uses a residual as an
effective extra-attraction term, Paper 30 permits only that guarded reading:
an effective residual test with bounds and falsifiers.  It is not a claim of
particle dark matter, modified gravity, or emergent gravity without the
separate conservation, lensing, weak-field, and structure-growth checks.

Examples:

1. A quotient causal model may be valid if hidden packets do not affect the
   target intervention horizon.
2. A classical diagonal model may be valid if Bures/dephasing residuals remain
   below task tolerance.
3. A low-cumulant dynamical model may be valid if
   \(R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}\) is bounded for the prediction window.
4. A feedback model may fail if recursive residuals grow after action,
   revealing that the causal shadow has mistaken a temporary projection for a
   stable identity.

Paper 31 should therefore study causal shadows as typed quotient models with
residual budgets, not as all-or-nothing replacements for wholes.

## 14. Conclusion

Paper 29 defined identity as participation inside a whole.  Paper 30 defines
incompatibility as residual overflow from a projection of a whole.

The central claim is not that obstruction disappears.  The claim is that
obstruction becomes more useful when it is typed.  Quotient packet loss,
Cartan obstruction, Bures dephasing, filtered \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
carry, cubic leakage, higher-cumulant mismatch, and recursive feedback error
are different residual coordinates.  They should not be compressed into one
undifferentiated failure.

The discipline is equally important.  Overflow is meaningful only when it is
constrained.  A residual budget must say what type of excess is present, which
projection created it, whether it is bounded, what controls it survives, and
what would falsify it.  Otherwise "overflow" becomes a license to explain
anything after the fact.

Projection failure is not automatically failure of theory.  It is a demand
for a residual budget.  If the overflow is typed and bounded, the projection
remains usable.  If the overflow is untyped or unbounded, the projection has
mistaken itself for ontology.

Jerk is not the end of overflow; it is the first named member of the
higher-cumulant residual tower.
