## 1. Abstract

This paper names the framework that Papers 29 and 30 were preparing.
Paper 29 introduced identity as whole-relative participation:
\[
  SN_W(A,B)
  =
  \text{participation components}
  +
  \text{residual components}.
\]
Paper 30 promoted the residual side to a typed calculus of constrained
overflow.  The present paper asks the next question: when is a projection,
shadow, quotient, readout, causal model, classical state, quantum observable,
low-arity force law, or finite-jet truncation allowed to function as a model?

The proposed answer is projection-closure theory.  A projection is not invalid
because it is incomplete.  It is invalid when it cannot account for its
incompleteness.  Equivalently, a shadow is legitimate when the participation
needed by the task survives the projection and the residuals created by the
projection are typed, bounded, stable under controls, and falsifiable on the
declared horizon.

The central object is a closure certificate
\[
  {\mathcal C}_W(\Pi;T,H,K)
  =
  \bigl(
    {\mathsf P}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf R}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf B}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf S}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf E}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf F}_W^\Pi
  \bigr),
\]
where \(W\) declares the whole, \(\Pi\) is the projection or shadow, \(T\) is
the task, \(H\) is the prediction or observation horizon, and \(K\) is the
order, arity, cumulant, quotient, or jet cutoff.  The entries record retained
participation, typed residuals, residual bounds, stability conditions,
time-translation/Noether-energy closure, and falsifiers.  A projection is
closure-valid for \((W,T,H,K)\) only after this certificate is specified.

The theory unifies several lessons already present in the sequence.
Classicality is commutative projection closure.  Quantumness is the failure of
one classical quotient to carry incompatible observables sharply; the
covariance-commutator matrix \(\Gamma+i\Omega\succeq0\) is the first exact
multi-probe residual budget.  N-arity is the failure of lower-arity shadows to
reconstruct a whole interaction.  Acceleration, jerk, snap, and higher
dynamicity probes are finite-jet closure tests for residual force, residual
force drift, residual force concavity, and higher residual bending.  Causality
is the low-dimensional predictive shadow of a richer whole, valid exactly when
observer-relevant residuals remain controlled under recursive prediction.

The paper separates theorem-facing content from programmatic reach.  The
Robertson-Schrodinger uncertainty matrix, Hellinger/Bures diagonal recovery,
Paper 26 quotient packet survival, Paper 28 arity-resolution discipline, and
Paper 30 residual budgets are inherited or standard.  Projection-closure
validity, causal-shadow classification, and a general bounded-closure theorem
are definitions and conjectural research gates.  Gravity is not claimed.  The
framework explains what a gravity claim would have to prove: a conserved,
universal, lensing-compatible geometric residual with the Newtonian two-body
and relativistic weak-field limits recovered.

The Einstein/Bianchi bridge is therefore structural.  In GR, the contracted
Bianchi identity enforces stress-energy integrability.  In the Nambu-Fisher
program, the fundamental/Filippov identity is the bracket-closure obligation.
Projection failure of that identity may be typed and bounded as a residual;
it is not, by itself, a gravity or dark-matter claim.

The guiding sentence is:

> Classical, quantum, causal, N-ary, and finite-jet models are not rival
> ontologies at first contact.  They are projection regimes distinguished by
> what they can carry, what they must lift, and what residuals remain bounded.

## 3. Introduction

The simplicial sequence has repeatedly encountered the same shape of problem.
A lower shadow works locally, then fails when treated as ontology.  Pairwise
force channels work inside dyadic mechanics but do not exhaust irreducible
three-body replacement problems.  Diagonal probability simplexes work for
commuting observations but cannot carry the full nonabelian directions restored
by the Bures density-matrix lift.  Quotient probes work for packets that
survive the quotient and fail exactly on forbidden conductor packets.  A
3-order closure can have a leading fourth-cumulant leakage pointer, but jerk
does not terminate the higher residual tower.  A causal graph may predict a
task while hiding residuals that reappear under feedback.

Papers 29 and 30 gave this pattern two names.  First, identity is
participation relative to a declared whole \(W\).  Second, projection failure
is constrained overflow when its residuals are typed, bounded, and falsifiable.
The missing third name is closure.  A projection is a model only if it closes
for the declared purpose.

This paper therefore proposes projection-closure theory:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{a projection is legitimate exactly to the extent that its required
  participation survives and its residuals close.}
  }
\]

The phrase "projection" is intentionally broad.  It includes ordinary
coordinate choices, quotient maps, coarse readouts, causal graphs, diagonal
classical shadows of density matrices, lower-arity force decompositions, and
finite-jet truncations.  The phrase "closure" is also broad but disciplined.
It means that the projection's missing pieces have been accounted for as
zero, bounded, stable, explicitly unbounded, or outside the declared model.

Projection-closure theory is not a license to claim that every obstruction is
secretly a new force.  It is a refusal to throw away the information carried
by obstruction.  The question is always:

\[
  \text{What can the declared shadow carry, what overflows, and is the overflow bounded?}
\]

This single question organizes classicality, quantumness, arity, causality,
and dynamicity probes.

## 4. Projection Contexts and Shadows

**Definition 4.1 (Projection context).**  
A projection context is a tuple
\[
  W
  =
  ({\mathcal X},{\mathcal O},{\mathcal T},{\mathcal Q},{\mathcal L},
   {\mathcal K},{\mathcal H},{\mathcal N}),
\]
where:

1. \({\mathcal X}\) is the full object, state space, dynamics, or whole;
2. \({\mathcal O}\) is the observer or allowed readout family;
3. \({\mathcal T}\) is the task or comparison objective;
4. \({\mathcal Q}\) is the quotient, projection, coarse-graining, or
   observable map family;
5. \({\mathcal L}\) is the lift or ambient geometry, such as Fisher, Bures,
   density-matrix, Nambu-Fisher, or conductor geometry;
6. \({\mathcal K}\) is the order, arity, cumulant, packet, or finite-jet
   cutoff;
7. \({\mathcal H}\) is the prediction, recurrence, observation, or feedback
   horizon;
8. \({\mathcal N}\) is the norm, tolerance, or acceptance convention.

**Definition 4.2 (Projection or shadow).**  
A projection is a declared map
\[
  \Pi:{\mathcal X}\to{\mathcal Y}
\]
or a declared model class \({\mathcal M}_\Pi\) that represents the full whole
inside a lower space \({\mathcal Y}\).  The output is called a shadow when the
paper wants to emphasize that \(\Pi(X)\) is not the whole.

Examples include:

1. a diagonal map from density matrices to probability vectors;
2. a quotient \(C_N\to C_k\);
3. a causal graph readout from a richer dynamical system;
4. a lower-arity force model \(F_{\le n}\);
5. a finite-jet truncation retaining force and jerk but not snap;
6. a behavior-simplex probe from model activations.

**Definition 4.3 (Projection-readable component).**  
Given \(X\in{\mathcal X}\), the projection-readable component is the part of
the whole whose relevant participation, dynamics, or observable consequence is
carried by \(\Pi(X)\) inside \(W\).

**Definition 4.4 (Projection overflow).**  
The projection overflow is the typed excess of \(X\) over \(\Pi(X)\) relative
to \(W\).  It is recorded as a residual object
\[
  R_W^\Pi(X).
\]
This is not necessarily a linear difference.  It may be a packet-support
ledger, a commutator matrix, a Bures/dephasing residual, a filtered carry
class, a missing arity certificate, or a finite-jet replay bound.

## 5. The Closure Certificate

**Definition 5.1 (Closure certificate).**  
For a projection \(\Pi\), task \(T\), horizon \(H\), and cutoff \(K\), define
\[
  {\mathcal C}_W(\Pi;T,H,K)
  =
  \bigl(
    {\mathsf P}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf R}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf B}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf S}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf E}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf F}_W^\Pi
  \bigr).
\]
The entries are:

1. \({\mathsf P}_W^\Pi\): participation retained by the projection;
2. \({\mathsf R}_W^\Pi\): typed residuals created by the projection;
3. \({\mathsf B}_W^\Pi\): residual bounds or explicitly unbounded entries;
4. \({\mathsf S}_W^\Pi\): stability conditions under recurrence, feedback,
   controls, perturbations, or horizon extension;
5. \({\mathsf E}_W^\Pi\): time-translation and Noether-energy closure, or the
   typed residual marking its failure;
6. \({\mathsf F}_W^\Pi\): falsifiers and controls.

The closure certificate is tuple-valued.  A scalar quality score may be useful
for a task, but it is never the invariant datum unless the context declares
the weights and tolerances.

**Definition 5.2 (Closure-valid projection).**  
A projection \(\Pi\) is closure-valid for \((W,T,H,K)\) if:

1. all task-required participation components survive \(\Pi\);
2. all forbidden residuals vanish;
3. all allowed residuals are bounded by the declared tolerance on \(H\);
4. residuals remain stable under the declared controls and feedback protocol;
5. time-translation closure is preserved for the admitted Noether energy,
   current, or form, or its failure is typed and bounded;
6. any declared Nambu-Fisher bracket preserves its fundamental/Filippov
   identity, or the projected defect is typed and bounded;
7. the falsifiers in \({\mathsf F}_W^\Pi\) are passed;
8. unobserved residual entries are marked unobserved, not silently set to zero.

The notation is:
\[
  \Pi\in\operatorname{ClValid}(W,T,H,K).
\]

**Guardrail 5.3.**  
Closure-validity is not truth.  It is permission to use a projection as a
model for a declared purpose.  A projection may be closure-valid for a short
horizon and invalid for a longer one; valid for a quotient task and invalid
for a full-conductor task; valid for diagonal measurements and invalid for a
noncommuting lift.

**Remark 5.3a (Relation to Spekkens generalized noncontextuality).**  
The six-slot closure certificate of Definition 5.1 is the arithmetic-substrate
cousin of Spekkens's generalized noncontextuality framework (Spekkens,
*Phys. Rev. A* 71:052108, 2005; cf. also Spekkens 2007 on epistemic
restrictions). Spekkens replaces sharp-projective-measurement Kochen-Specker
noncontextuality with an operational equivalence requirement: equivalent
preparations or measurements must receive equivalent ontological
representations, with noise-robust formulations applicable to unsharp and
sequential implementations. The closure certificate plays the same role with
two changes of substrate:

1. **Operational equivalence is replaced by quotient-respecting readout
   (Paper [26](26.conductor_probe_obstruction_theorem.md) T1):** two
   observables are "equivalent at resolution \(k\)" iff they project to the
   same element of the conductor packets \(\bigoplus_{d\mid k} H_d\).  The
   identity of an observable across contexts is the survival of its
   packet content under the declared readout, not a primitive ontological
   equality.
2. **Ontic representation is replaced by typed bounded residual
   (\({\mathsf R}_W^\Pi, {\mathsf B}_W^\Pi\)):** the closure certificate does
   not require any underlying ontology; it requires that whatever the
   projection \(\Pi\) discards is named, typed, and bounded on the declared
   horizon, with falsifiers \({\mathsf F}_W^\Pi\) that can fail.

The two frameworks coincide in spirit at the maximally entropic point: at
\(p_*\), the cubic Amari-Chentsov tensor \(T_{k\ell m}\) is the
quantum-foundations-style witness, and the closure certificate's \(\mathsf R\)
slot is exactly the bounded budget for what the projection cannot carry.
Dutra, Baldijão & Terra Cunha (arXiv:2604.24735, Apr 2026) §V close their
analysis by recommending the Spekkens framework as better suited to noisy
sequential implementations than the KS framework. The closure certificate is
the same recommendation realized on the categorical-simplex / conductor-lattice
substrate: closure-validity at horizon \(H\) under controls \(\mathsf F\) is
the noise-robust noncontextual reconstruction relation, with the cubic and
quartic obstructions (Papers [17](17.the_cubic_noether_theorem.md),
[21](21.quartic_force_law_n4_obstruction.md), [44](44_quantization_forced_cubic_to_bures_bridge.md))
playing the role of contextuality witnesses.

The simplicial program is *not* a derivation of Spekkens noncontextuality;
it is a parallel construction on a finite arithmetic substrate that arrives
at the same operational concern (noise-robust observable identity across
contexts) by different means (Pontryagin character orthogonality on
\((\mathbb Z/N)^\wedge\)).

**Proposition 5.4 (Whole-relativity).**  
There is no absolute closure-validity independent of \(W\).

**Reason.**  
The same projection can preserve all task-required participation in one
context and hide an essential residual in another.  Paper 26 quotient maps,
Paper 29 participation objects, and Paper 30 residual budgets are all
context-indexed.  Closure-validity inherits that dependence.  \(\square\)

## 6. The One-Body Lesson: Gauge Before Dynamics

The one-body problem is not trivial.  It is the first warning that state
variables are not automatically ontology.

For a free single body with no external field,
\[
  F_1=0,
  \qquad
  p=\text{constant},
  \qquad
  q(t)=q(0)+vt.
\]
But the absolute origin is gauge:
\[
  q\mapsto q+a.
\]
The inertial frame is also gauge:
\[
  v\mapsto v+b.
\]
Even mass has no internal dynamical meaning in a one-body universe unless a
comparison, collision, gravitational field, measuring device, or external
coupling is declared.  A lone free body does not reveal its mass from inside
its own one-body world.

Thus the first projection-closure lesson is:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{a variable is real only relative to a declared transfer, probe, or
  comparison context.}
  }
\]

This does not make position, velocity, or mass fake.  It makes them
contextual.  Classical mechanics already knows this through Galilean
invariance.  Projection-closure theory generalizes the lesson: if a coordinate
is not invariant under the declared context, then the coordinate is a shadow,
not the whole.

## 7. Two Bodies and the First Relational Closure

Two bodies are the first nontrivial relational system.  The relative coordinate
\[
  r=q_2-q_1
\]
survives translations.  The relative velocity survives boosts.  For Newtonian
gravity or any central pair potential, the two-body reduction closes:
\[
  F_{12}^{(2)}
  =
  -F_{21}^{(2)},
  \qquad
  F_{12}^{(2)}
  =
  f(\|r\|)\,{r\over \|r\|}.
\]
Total momentum, angular momentum, and energy are conserved.  In the
gravitational inverse-square case, Kepler structure appears.

Projection-closure theory does not reject this.  It explains why this shadow
is excellent in its proper regime.  Pairwise closure is valid when its
residual budget is controlled:
\[
  R_{\le2}
  =
  F_{\mathrm{truth}}-F_{\le2}
  \approx 0
\]
on the declared horizon and under the declared probes.

The Newtonian two-body limit therefore remains a boundary condition for any
gravity-adjacent theory.  It is not Newtonian ontology.  It is the empirical
fact that the dyadic shadow closes extremely well in the isolated weak-field
slow-motion regime.

## 8. Quantum as Noncommuting Projection Nonclosure

The uncertainty principle is the one-body prototype of residual budgets.  It
does not say merely that measurement is clumsy.  It says that certain
projections cannot be made jointly sharp inside one classical quotient.

Let
\[
  X=(X_1,\ldots,X_m)
\]
be observables, and write
\[
  \delta X_i=X_i-\langle X_i\rangle.
\]
Define
\[
  \Gamma_{ij}
  =
  {1\over2}\langle\{\delta X_i,\delta X_j\}\rangle,
  \qquad
  \Omega_{ij}
  =
  {1\over2i}\langle[X_i,X_j]\rangle.
\]
The general covariance-commutator budget is
\[
  \boxed{
  \Gamma+i\Omega\succeq0.
  }
\]

For two observables this gives the Robertson-Schrodinger inequality:
\[
  (\Delta A)^2(\Delta B)^2-\operatorname{Cov}(A,B)^2
  \ge
  {1\over4}|\langle[A,B]\rangle|^2.
\]
The usual Heisenberg relation
\[
  \Delta q\,\Delta p\ge {\hbar\over2}
\]
is the special case \(A=q\), \(B=p\), with \([q,p]=i\hbar\) and no covariance
term displayed.

For three observables \(A,B,C\), uncertainty is not controlled by one scalar
triple product.  It is controlled by the full matrix and its principal minors,
including
\[
  \det(\Gamma+i\Omega)\ge0.
\]
Pairwise Heisenberg inequalities are two-dimensional shadows of this matrix.

If force is admitted as a probe and \(F=F(q)\), then
\[
  [q,F(q)]=0,
  \qquad
  [p,F(q)]=-i\hbar\,\nabla F(q).
\]
Thus
\[
  \Delta p\,\Delta F
  \ge
  {\hbar\over2}\left|\langle\nabla F\rangle\right|
\]
in the one-dimensional covariance-free display.  There is no new universal
"force-bar" constant.  There is \(\hbar\) multiplied by the local force slope.

Projection-closure interpretation:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{quantum means noncommuting projection nonclosure requiring a lift.}
  }
\]

It does not mean "tiny."  It means that no single classical shadow carries all
declared observables sharply.  Papers 15 and 18 supply the compatible program
language: the density-matrix/Bures lift restores participation that the
diagonal simplex cannot carry.

## 9. N-ary Force Closure and the Conservation Crescendo

Write an admitted force expansion schematically as
\[
  F_i
  =
  F_i^{(1)}
  +
  \sum_{j\ne i}F_{ij}^{(2)}
  +
  \sum_{j<k,\; j,k\ne i}F_{ijk}^{(3)}
  +
  \cdots.
\]
Here:

1. \(F^{(1)}\) is one-body, external, inertial, or body-local;
2. \(F^{(2)}\) is pairwise or edge-level;
3. \(F^{(3)}\) is triangular or 3-ary;
4. \(F^{(4)}\) is tetrahedral;
5. \(F^{(5)}\) is circuit-attached in ordinary 3-space;
6. higher terms require their own arity certificates.

The admitted residual after arity \(n\) is
\[
  R_{\le n}
  =
  F_{\mathrm{truth}}
  -
  \sum_{k\le n}F^{(k)}.
\]

The conservation generalization is:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{conservation is not pairwise action-reaction; it is closure of a
  declared whole under its admissible symmetries.}
  }
\]

For a closed \(n\)-ary force, momentum balance is imposed globally:
\[
  \sum_i F_i^{(n)}=0.
\]
Angular momentum balance is imposed by
\[
  \sum_i q_i\times F_i^{(n)}=0.
\]
The order-appropriate internal action supplies a Noether energy, current, or
form:
\[
  Q_n=\text{conserved Noether object of the admitted }n\text{-ary law}.
\]
For an autonomous admitted law, this time-translation closure is part of the
certificate rather than an optional afterthought:
\[
  {\mathsf E}_W^\Pi
  =
  \text{Noether energy/current/form survives }\Pi
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  R_{\mathrm{time}}^\Pi\text{ is typed and bounded.}
\]

For Nambu-Fisher brackets there is a second closure obligation.  The
fundamental/Filippov identity is the higher-bracket analogue of the Jacobi
closure condition: it says that the bracket-generated flow acts as a
derivation of the bracket.  A projection that preserves a force-looking term
but breaks this identity has created a bracket residual
\[
  R_{\mathrm{Filippov}}^\Pi,
\]
which must be zero, bounded, or outside the model.  This is the precise sense
in which the later Bianchi analogy is being used.

This is the conservation-laws crescendo.  Each rung must bring a closure law,
not merely a new force term.  Pairwise antisymmetry is one route to momentum
conservation, not the ontology of conservation.

| rung | primitive closure | conservation meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1-body | inertial/gauge closure | momentum is constant only relative to a chosen inertial context |
| 2-body | pair/central closure | total momentum, angular momentum, energy, and Kepler-type structure in special potentials |
| 3-body | irreducible triangular closure | global momentum, angular momentum, 3-order Noether energy, and Nambu volume |
| 4-body | tetrahedral closure | lower edge/face laws are insufficient unless tetrahedral residuals vanish |
| 5-body | circuit closure | overlapping tetrahedra must close through a five-body circuit budget |
| 6-body | carry/quotient closure | composite \(2\times3\) carry residuals must be priced |

Jerk is therefore not the next conservation law by default.  It is the first
visible derivative of a force residual after 3-order closure.  Snap is not the
next ontology by default.  It is the first visible time-concavity of a residual
force after force and force drift have been admitted or bounded.

## 10. Space-Time Transfer and Dynamicity Probes

The proper snap test must be preceded by the proper velocity, momentum, and
acceleration tests.  Let
\[
  R(t)=F_{\mathrm{truth}}(t)-F_{\mathrm{ad}}(t)
\]
be the residual force of the admitted model, and let \(M\) be the mass matrix.
Define
\[
  A(t)=M^{-1}R(t),
  \qquad
  J(t)=M^{-1}D_tR(t),
  \qquad
  S(t)=M^{-1}D_t^2R(t).
\]
These are residual acceleration, residual jerk, and residual snap.
In the Paper 30 notation, residual jerk is the leading fourth-cumulant pointer
\(R^{(4)}\), and the full finite-jet remainder is
\[
  R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}
  =
  (R^{(4)},R^{(5)},R^{(6)},\ldots).
\]
When the shorthand \(R^{(\ge4)}\) is used, it denotes this same typed tower.
Thus the dynamicity words are diagnostics inside typed bounded overflow.  A
large jerk signal means the \(R^{(4)}\) budget must be carried or bounded; it
does not by itself promote jerk to a new ontology.

Momentum error is impulse:
\[
  e_p(H)=\int_0^H R(t)\,dt.
\]
Velocity error is mass-normalized impulse:
\[
  e_v(H)=\int_0^H M^{-1}R(t)\,dt.
\]
Position error is the double integral:
\[
  e_q(H)=\int_0^H(H-t)M^{-1}R(t)\,dt.
\]

If force and force drift have already been admitted or budgeted, the snap-only
Taylor remainder gives
\[
  \|e_q(H)\|
  \le
  {H^4\over24}\|S\|_{\infty,H},
\]
\[
  \|e_v(H)\|
  \le
  {H^3\over6}\|S\|_{\infty,H},
\]
\[
  \|e_a(H)\|
  \le
  {H^2\over2}\|S\|_{\infty,H}.
\]

Thus snap is mandatory only if these exceed the declared tolerances after
force-level and jerk-level residuals have already been handled:
\[
  {H^4\|S\|_{\infty,H}\over24}>\varepsilon_q,
  \qquad
  {H^3\|S\|_{\infty,H}\over6}>\varepsilon_v,
  \qquad
  {H^2\|S\|_{\infty,H}\over2}>\varepsilon_a.
\]

The diagnostic ladder is:

| residual failure | time word | mathematical object | response |
|---|---|---|---|
| missing force | residual acceleration | \(M^{-1}R\) | admit missing arity or force coordinate |
| missing force drift | residual drift | \(M^{-1}D_tR\) | track mass-weighted jerk if horizon demands it |
| missing force concavity | residual concavity | \(M^{-1}D_t^2R\) | track mass-weighted snap if horizon demands it |
| missing higher bending | higher residual bending | \(M^{-1}D_t^kR\), \(k\ge3\) | extend finite-jet budget |

The body-count view is:

| transfer | 1 body | 2 bodies | 3 bodies | 4 bodies | 5 bodies | 6 bodies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| position \(q\) | origin gauge unless a frame is declared | relative position closes | triangular whole may affect transfer | tetrahedral shape can affect transfer | circuit coordinate can affect transfer | carry channels can affect transfer |
| velocity/momentum | boost gauge for one free body | relative momentum and total momentum close | internal redistribution need not be pairwise-compressible | tetrahedral residual can move momentum through 4-body shape | circuit residual can move momentum through five-body shape | \(2\times3\) carry can move momentum through composite structure |
| acceleration | zero or external | pair force complete in dyadic regime | lower pair shadow can miss 3-ary force | lower \(\le3\) shadow can miss tetrahedral force | lower \(\le4\) shadow can miss circuit force | lower shadows can miss carry force |
| jerk | zero if free | determined by admitted pair law | first visible force-drift nonclosure after lower posture | diagnostic, not automatically stateful | diagnostic unless residual drift breaks horizon | useful but not necessarily enough |
| snap | zero if free | determined by admitted pair law | not first correction | usually a horizon diagnostic after tetrahedral admission | mandatory only if circuit residual concavity breaks horizon | important when force/jerk fit but long replay fails |

The rule is:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{snap is not a body-count tax; it is the price of unmodeled
  time-concavity in a residual force.}
  }
\]

## 11. Classical, Quantum, Causal, and N-ary as Projection Regimes

Projection-closure theory does not begin by asking which ontology is true.  It
asks which shadow closes.

| regime | projection | closure condition | residual when it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical | commutative coordinate/readout shadow | all declared observables jointly assignable within tolerance | noncommuting/Bures residual |
| Quantum | lifted noncommuting observable calculus | \(\Gamma+i\Omega\succeq0\) and lift-compatible dynamics | diagonal/dephasing residual under classical projection |
| Causal | low-dimensional predictive graph or intervention shadow | prediction-relevant residuals controlled under feedback | recursive residual, hidden confound/carry residual |
| N-ary | lower-arity reconstruction shadow | whole interaction reconstructible from admitted hyperedges | arity residual \({\mathsf C}_n\) |
| Finite-jet | truncated dynamicity shadow | residual force derivatives bounded on \(H\) | jerk, snap, and higher residual tower |
| Quotient | packet-forgetting readout | required conductor packets survive quotient | forbidden-packet residual |

The same object can sit in several rows.  A causal graph may be a classical
shadow, a quotient shadow, and a finite-jet shadow simultaneously.  A quantum
measurement may be a Bures lift followed by a diagonal projection.  A
multi-body simulation may be an arity projection and a finite-jet projection.
Closure certificates exist to keep these types separate.

## 12. Causal Shadows

**Definition 12.1 (Causal shadow).**  
A causal shadow is a projection \(\Pi_C\) from a richer whole \(W\) to a
predictive/interventional model \(C\) such that \(C\) represents selected
dependencies as causes for a task \(T\).

The causal shadow is closure-valid when:

1. the observer-relevant participation needed for \(T\) survives \(\Pi_C\);
2. hidden quotient, Bures, conductor, arity, and finite-jet residuals are
   zero, bounded, or irrelevant on \(H\);
3. recursive prediction does not amplify hidden residuals beyond tolerance;
4. interventions do not reveal a forbidden packet or hidden lift that the
   causal shadow erased;
5. controls distinguish genuine causal stability from shortcut correlation.

This gives a precise version of the Paper 29 and Paper 30 bridge:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{causality is a legitimate quotient shadow when residual budgets remain
  controlled under recursive prediction.}
  }
\]

It also prevents a common overcorrection.  A causal model is not false because
it is incomplete.  It is false, for the declared task, when the incompleteness
it creates is untyped, unstable, unbounded, or hidden from its own diagnostics.

**Conjecture 12.2 (Causal shadow classification).**  
For a class of Fisher-conductor dynamical systems with declared observer
family \({\mathcal O}\), quotient family \({\mathcal Q}\), and feedback
horizon \(H\), causal shadows split into:

1. **closed shadows:** required packets and lift data survive; residuals vanish;
2. **bounded shadows:** residuals are nonzero but stay within task tolerance;
3. **fragile shadows:** residuals are controlled for one horizon or
   intervention family but fail under recurrence or feedback;
4. **false ontologies:** residuals are hidden, untyped, or unbounded while the
   shadow is treated as complete.

This is a conjectural classification, not a theorem of the present draft.

## 13. Gravity Guardrail

Projection-closure theory is naturally gravity-adjacent because gravity is a
theory of relational geometry, closure, and universal constraints.  But the
framework does not explain gravity merely by naming overflow.

The Einstein comparison is structural and limited.  In general relativity, the
contracted Bianchi identity
\[
  \nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu}=0
\]
is what makes the Einstein equation compatible with covariant stress-energy
conservation
\[
  \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0.
\]
In this program, the Nambu-Fisher fundamental/Filippov identity plays the
analogous role for higher-bracket dynamics: it is the identity that prevents a
bracket projection from producing unconstrained sources.  The residual object
is the projected failure of that identity, not an Einstein equation.

A gravity claim would need a map
\[
  R_W^{\mathrm{geometry}}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{effective metric, connection, or stress-energy}
\]
and then it would have to pass hard boundary conditions:

1. Newtonian two-body weak-field limit;
2. equivalence principle or a precise replacement;
3. universal coupling to mass-energy or a tested substitute;
4. local conservation laws;
5. light bending and gravitational lensing;
6. perihelion precession and Solar System bounds;
7. gravitational-wave propagation;
8. cosmological structure-growth constraints;
9. cluster collision and Bullet-Cluster-type tests;
10. distinction from particle dark matter and modified-gravity alternatives.

Dark-matter-adjacent language is allowed only in this guarded sense: a
projected residual may behave like an effective extra-attraction term in a
shadow model, and that is a testable residual hypothesis.  It is not a claim
of particle dark matter, modified gravity, or emergent gravity unless it also
passes the conservation, lensing, weak-field, and structure-growth conditions
above.

The Newtonian two-body limit matters because it is the first nontrivial
relational closure and is empirically excellent.  In projection-closure
language a future gravity-adjacent theory must satisfy
\[
  R_{\mathrm{PCT}}(2\text{-body weak field})\approx0
\]
or keep its corrections below observational bounds.

The defensible present claim is:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{projection-closure theory explains the form of the gravity question,
  not gravity itself.}
  }
\]

Gravity should not be claimed as a metaphorical prize for overflow.  It
becomes a target only if the residual budget induces a conserved, universal,
lensing-compatible spacetime geometry with known limits recovered.

## 14. AI and Empirical Closure Tests

The empirical side of the program already contains projection-closure tests.
The point of Paper 31 is to name their common structure.

**Quotient packet closure.**  
Paper 26 proves that under \(C_N\to C_k\), exactly packets \(H_d\) with
\(d\mid k\) survive.  The empirical quotient-probe and native-logit audits are
closure tests: the projection is valid for quotient-visible packets and blind
to forbidden packets by theorem.

**Activation-to-behavior closure.**  
An activation probe is closure-valid only if activation-side distances,
behavior-side participation, and packet residuals remain aligned under
controls.  Otherwise the probe is a useful shadow but not an ontology of the
model state.

**Hidden shortcut closure.**  
A learned shortcut may perform well on a task while hiding packet or arity
residuals.  Projection closure asks whether those residuals remain bounded
under distribution shift, recursive prediction, or intervention.

**N-body arity closure.**  
The three-body, four-body, five-body, and six-body audits are not claims about
real gravitation.  They are controlled closure experiments.  They ask whether
lower shadows fit short-horizon force while failing replay, whether arity
coordinates recover the planted channel, and whether jerk or snap budgets
correctly predict horizon failure.

**Feedback identity closure.**  
For persistent AI systems, the relevant object is not a one-step score:
\[
  SN_W(I_t,I_{t+1};a_t,y_t),
  \qquad
  R_W(I_{t+1},\widehat I_{t+1}).
\]
The closure test is whether identity-relevant participation persists and
whether prediction residuals remain bounded under feedback.

**Worked open F-slot example: hypothesis-driven circuit discovery
(DCD).**  
Rai, Geva & Yao 2026, "Data-driven Circuit Discovery for Interpretability of
Language Models"
([arXiv:2605.09129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09129)), demonstrate that
hypothesis-driven circuit-discovery methods (EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, E-ACT) on
GPT-2 / Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct / Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct return circuits that
fail cross-dataset consistency (in-distribution faithfulness 0.73 on 2-person
IOI dropping to 0.42 cross-dataset on 3-person; mean pairwise Jaccard
0.30–0.66 across complexity/syntax/domain axes; single circuit at 87%
faithfulness on two structurally distinct mechanisms after 10% mixture
injection). Their proposed remedy, Data-driven Circuit Discovery (DCD),
clusters per-example attribution vectors via PCA + K-means and returns one
circuit per cluster. The remedy works empirically — DCD achieves 86%
faithfulness at circuit size 0.05 on GPT-2 vs. 64% for EAP-IG, a 22-point
improvement — but the cited work flags an open question in its §8: "one must
characterize what each cluster represents, either by inspecting its examples
or by interpreting the circuit itself; open questions remain about how to
effectively assign new examples to clusters at test time, and how to handle
clusters that resist human-legible description." This is precisely an
*open F-slot* in the six-slot closure certificate \((P, R, B, S, E, F)\) of
§5: the projection (cluster a dataset, attribute per cluster) is *operational*
and *predictive* in DCD's own evaluation, but the *falsifier specification*
for what each cluster means and how new examples are routed is left unstated.

The closure certificate ingredients for the DCD projection in the
program's vocabulary:

- **P (participation-relevant content survives):** the per-cluster
  attribution vectors recover the task-relevant signal, measured by
  best-of-K faithfulness at circuit size 0.05.
- **R (residual is typed):** the residual is the cross-packet cubic content
  the second-order EAP-IG attribution cannot couple at \(p_*\)
  ([Paper 34](34_the_conductor_blind_spot.md) Corollary 3.11); the type is
  the conductor packet of the unrepresented cross-coupling.
- **B (residual is bounded):** Paper 34 Theorem 3.5 + Theorem 5.6 give the
  bound as the off-centroid Fourier-convolution expansion of the cross-packet
  Fisher entries, summable along any displacement ray.
- **S (residual is stable):** the smooth Jaccard ridge $0.79$–$0.89$ across
  adjacent mixture ratios in the cited work's Fig 3c is the empirical
  stability statement.
- **E (declared evaluation horizon):** the per-cluster faithfulness threshold
  $\geq 0.80$ that DCD inherits from the hypothesis-driven workflow.
- **F (falsifier specification):** *not declared by DCD*; this is the slot
  the cited work's §8 leaves open. The program supplies the missing
  specification in two pieces. On the structural side, the divisor-table
  prediction $K^* = \tau(N)$ on cyclic sub-tasks (Paper 26 T1 + Paper 45
  CCSAE) names what the cluster *should* be by independent algebraic
  reasoning; if the recovered \(K^*\) does not match \(\tau(N)\) on a cyclic
  task, the closure certificate fails. On the operational side, the
  Algebraic Mechanism Certificate of Paper 40 (BNSA), with survival ratio
  \(\sigma_{S,\ell}\) computed on each cluster's representative examples,
  supplies the per-cluster mechanism-class falsifier: a cluster with
  $\sigma < 0.7$ on its claimed mechanism class is using a different
  mechanism than the cluster label asserts. The combined F-slot
  specification is therefore *divisor-table cluster count + per-cluster
  survival-ratio mechanism class*, both predictable in advance, both
  testable post-DCD, and both reportable with positive-and-negative branches
  pre-claimed. This is the off-the-shelf F-slot completion of an
  externally-published projection-closure open question; the closure
  certificate is the framework in which DCD's open question and its
  resolution are stated as one object.

## 15. Bounded Projection-Closure Conjecture

**Conjecture 15.1 (Bounded projection closure).**  
Let \(W\) be a projection context and let \(\Pi\) be a projection with closure
certificate
\[
  {\mathcal C}_W(\Pi;T,H,K)
  =
  (
    {\mathsf P}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf R}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf B}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf S}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf E}_W^\Pi,
    {\mathsf F}_W^\Pi
  ).
\]
Assume:

1. task-required participation components in \({\mathsf P}_W^\Pi\) survive;
2. each residual coordinate in \({\mathsf R}_W^\Pi\) is typed;
3. each task-relevant residual is zero, bounded by \({\mathsf B}_W^\Pi\), or
   explicitly marked outside the model;
4. bounds are stable under the recurrence, feedback, quotient, lift, and
   perturbation protocols in \({\mathsf S}_W^\Pi\);
5. time-translation/Noether closure in \({\mathsf E}_W^\Pi\) is preserved, or
   its projected failure is typed and bounded;
6. any Nambu-Fisher fundamental/Filippov defect created by \(\Pi\) is zero,
   bounded, or explicitly outside the model;
7. the controls and falsifiers in \({\mathsf F}_W^\Pi\) are passed.

Then \(\Pi\) is closure-valid for \((W,T,H,K)\).  Conversely, if a
task-relevant residual is untyped, hidden, unstable, or unbounded, then
\(\Pi\) is not closure-valid for that context.

This conjecture is deliberately formal.  The hard work is not the wording; it
is proving useful instances with explicit norms, packet ledgers, commutator
budgets, arity certificates, and finite-jet estimates.

## 17. Falsifiers

**F1: Projection closure collapses to ordinary error.**  
If the structured certificate adds no predictive or explanatory value beyond a
generic error norm, the framework should shrink to ordinary model validation.

**F2: Residual types are frame artifacts.**  
If packet, Bures, carry, arity, or finite-jet residuals disappear under
reasonable equivalent frames without invariant content, their claimed type
must be weakened.

**F3: Scalar scores hide essential failures.**  
If a scalar closure score repeatedly marks projections valid while typed
residuals predict failure, scalar aggregation is not theorem-facing.

**F4: Quantum residuals are empirically idle.**  
If Bures/dephasing residuals never matter in tasks where noncommuting lifts
should matter, the quantum closure layer remains mathematically valid but
empirically idle for those tasks.

**F5: N-ary rungs collapse to lower shadows.**  
If a claimed \(n\)-ary coordinate can be reconstructed from lower-arity
features under the declared controls, it is not an \(n\)-ary discovery.

**F6: Dynamicity probes fail horizon scaling.**  
If jerk and snap budgets do not predict replay error with the expected
\(H^3\) and \(H^4\) scaling after lower residuals are controlled, the
finite-jet closure test is wrong or incomplete.

**F7: Causal shadows do not survive intervention.**  
If a causal projection performs under passive prediction but fails under
declared interventions while hiding the relevant residual, it is a false
ontology for that task.

**F8: Gravity overreach.**  
If a gravity-adjacent residual cannot recover the known two-body, weak-field,
lensing, conservation, and structure-growth limits, it is not a gravity
theory.  If an effective extra-attraction residual fails the same controls, it
is not dark-matter evidence.

## 18. Conclusion

Projection-closure theory names the common structure behind the last stage of
the simplicial sequence.  A projection can be useful without being complete.
It can be incomplete without being false.  It becomes false for a declared
task when it cannot account for its incompleteness.

Paper 29 supplied participation: what survives in a shadow.  Paper 30
supplied residual overflow: what the shadow cannot carry.  Paper 31 supplies
the admissibility question: when does the shadow close?

The answer is not one scalar.  It is a certificate.  A legitimate projection
declares its whole, observer, task, horizon, lift, quotient, arity, and
cutoff.  It records retained participation.  It names residual types.  It
bounds what must be bounded, marks what is unobserved, and gives falsifiers
that can force the model to shrink.

This is why the one-body problem, the uncertainty principle, the two-body
limit, the three-body arity gate, the snap test, causal modeling, quotient
probes, and Bures lifts belong to one paper.  They are all projection-closure
questions.

Final slogan:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{The world may exceed any shadow.  A theory begins when the shadow can
  say how.}
  }
\]
