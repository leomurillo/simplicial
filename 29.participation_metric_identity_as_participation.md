## 1. Abstract

This paper adds a participation layer to Fisher-conductor geometry.  The aim is
to turn gradable identity, inspired by Kajetan Mlynarski's \(SN\) function, into
an operational object inside the simplicial research program.  Instead of
treating identity as binary equality or treating non-identity as mere distance,
we define a structured participation score \(SN_W(A,B)\) between states,
objects, signals, readouts, or subsystems relative to an observer/task context
\(W\).  The score records how two objects participate in the same whole, and at
which levels that participation survives or fails.

The proposed object combines four compatible components.  On categorical
simplexes it uses the Hellinger/Bhattacharyya affinity
\[
  P_H(p,q)=\sum_{a\in R}\sqrt{p_aq_a},
\]
which is the inner product after the square-root Fisher embedding.  On
noncommuting lifts it uses Bures/Uhlmann root fidelity
\[
  P_B(\rho,\sigma)
  =
  \operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}},
\]
whose diagonal commuting restriction recovers \(P_H\).  In quotient-character
coordinates it refines identity by conductor packets, producing a vector of
packet overlaps, energy fractions, quotient survivals, and hidden residuals.
Finally, under observable maps or quotient readouts it records task-conditioned
participation, making explicit the way a coarse observer can identify two
states that remain distinct in the full conductor geometry.

The ideal theorem package is a calculus of participation, constrained
overflow, and causal shadows.  Causal identity appears as the low-dimensional
case where all observer-relevant packets survive a quotient and no Bures
coherence or conductor residual remains hidden.  Obstruction classes such as
Cartan leakage, \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\), and quotient-probe blindness become
structured non-identity budgets rather than undifferentiated defects.  The
resulting \(SN\) object is not an absolute scalar ontology.  A scalar score
requires explicit observer/task weights; the invariant datum is the structured
tuple.

This draft makes the formal core precise and marks the remaining agenda.  The
Hellinger, Fisher tangent, Bures, quotient, and packet definitions are
immediate mathematical objects.  Their normalization, diagonal recovery,
coarse-graining monotonicity, and Paper 26 divisor behavior are formal or
inherited.  The stronger claims, including functorial conductor naturality
off-centroid, dynamical obstruction bounds, and persistent AI feedback identity,
are stated as conjectures, programmatic claims, and falsifiable research gates.
The abstract is therefore an iteration pointer: it states the mature target
that the body begins to build, not a claim that every component has already
been closed.

## 3. Introduction

Papers 10--28 developed a mathematical spine: categorical Fisher geometry,
conductor decompositions, quotient-character adjacency algebras,
Nambu-Fisher/simplectic mechanics, Cartan obstruction, Bures density-matrix
completion, finite-to-Archimedean limits, quotient probe-obstruction, and
Paper 28's irreducible ternary mechanics.  The common movement is away from an
atomistic picture in which parts first exist independently and then interact
externally.  In the Fisher-conductor program, local categorical states are
organized by global constraints: conductor packets, quotient maps, cubic
tensors, Bures lifts, and Nambu volume forms decide which distinctions survive
and which are artifacts of a chosen readout.

Mlynarski's *The Limits of Causality* gives a philosophical version of the
same pressure point.  Causality is not rejected, but it is treated as a
partial regime.  A whole, represented there by dynamic constraints
\(\Gamma\), selects actual coherent states from a space of possibilities
\(\Omega\).  Local objects are projections of the whole, and identity is not
binary.  Two objects may be "each other" to some degree, described by an
\(SN\) function in \([0,1]\).  Contradiction is not simply logical explosion:
under constraints it can be non-explosive overflow.  Intelligence, human or
artificial, becomes more mature when it can mark white spots rather than
hallucinating causal completeness.

This paper does not import that philosophy as a theorem.  It uses it as a
diagnostic: the simplicial program already has mathematical machinery for the
question Mlynarski poses.  Hellinger geometry gives a canonical state-level
overlap on categorical simplexes.  Bures geometry gives the corresponding
noncommuting overlap after the density-matrix lift.  Conductor packets say
which arithmetic modes of participation are shared.  Quotient readouts explain
why two states can be identical to a coarse observer while remaining distinct
in the full geometry.  Cartan obstruction and leakage identify which
directions are missing from a classical shadow.

Paper 28 made the "whole before pairwise parts" issue concrete by formulating
irreducible ternary mechanics for a three-body prototype.  Paper 29 asks the
next question: once a whole is primary, how do we measure the degree to which
two projections participate in that same whole?

This question has an immediate trap.  If \(SN_W(A,B)\) is read as an absolute
similarity between two isolated objects, then Paper 29 would fall back into
the same prime-2 blindspot that Papers 13 and 28 were designed to escape:
binary symplectic structure, pairwise force channels, and scalar
same/not-same bookkeeping.  That is not the intended reading.  The subscript
\(W\) is primary.  It names the whole, the observer, the packet frame, the
quotient maps, the lift, and the residual ledger inside which \(A\) and \(B\)
appear as projections.  The dyadic notation is a low-order interface to a
whole-relative object, not a return to pairwise ontology.

The answer proposed here is not a single distance.  A distance says how far
two states are.  A participation object says what they share, which packets
carry the sharing, which observer maps preserve it, which lifts reveal hidden
coherence, and which residuals mark constrained overflow.  The right object is
therefore structured:
\[
  SN_W(A,B)
  =
  \bigl(
    \text{base Fisher/Hellinger participation},
    \text{conductor packet participation},
    \text{Bures participation},
    \text{observable/quotient participation},
    \text{obstruction residuals}
  \bigr).
\]
Only after a context \(W\) declares a task, observer, packet frame, and weight
system may this tuple be compressed to a scalar.

## 4. The Problem: Identity Beyond Binary Separation

Classical equality answers a sharp question:
\[
  A=B \quad\text{or}\quad A\ne B.
\]
For many mathematical tasks this is exactly the right question.  The problem
begins when the same binary logic is used as an ontology of complex states.
In a categorical, arithmetic, or quantum setting, two objects can agree under
one observer, disagree under another, share some conductor packets, differ in
hidden coherences, and become indistinguishable after a quotient.  Binary
separation collapses that structure.

The same collapse can reappear in a friendlier form if one replaces
same/not-same by a single similarity score.  A scalar overlap between two
objects is still prime-2 bookkeeping unless it is explicitly derived from a
whole-relative participation object.  Paper 28's lesson is therefore imported
as a methodological rule for identity: do not explain a whole by pairwise
channels, and do not explain participation by an absolute pairwise scalar.
The scalar is a readout; the structured \(SN_W\) object is the datum.

Mlynarski calls attention to a related failure through radical negation.  A
statement such as "A is not B" is informative only relative to a specified
space of alternatives.  Without such a context it creates an informational
void.  In this program, the analogue is a readout without a declared quotient,
packet support, or measurement channel.  Saying that two states are "different"
does not tell us whether the difference lives in \(H_2\), \(H_{12}\), a Bures
coherence, a forbidden nonabelian direction, a fourth-cumulant leakage term,
or a task-irrelevant coordinate.

Distance improves on binary separation but is still insufficient.  A scalar
distance \(d(A,B)\) usually forgets the decomposition of the difference.  It
can say that two month distributions are near in Hellinger geometry while
hiding the fact that their full-period \(H_{12}\) components are misaligned.
It can say that two density matrices are close after diagonal measurement
while hiding noncommuting coherences.  It can say that a quotient probe
performs well while Paper 26 tells us exactly which packets the probe cannot
see.

Participation is the positive version of the same measurement.  Instead of
asking only how much two objects differ, it asks:

1. what part of their Fisher/Hellinger state is shared;
2. what conductor packets carry that sharing;
3. what a Bures lift reveals beyond the commuting classical shadow;
4. what an observer/readout preserves or erases;
5. what residuals remain as structured non-identity.

In this sense causal descriptions become quotient shadows.  They are valid
when the observer-relevant packets close under the quotient and the hidden
residuals do not affect the declared task.  They become misleading when the
quotient creates the appearance of identity by erasing the packets that carry
the real distinction.

## 5. Participation on the Categorical Simplex

Let \(R\) be a finite outcome set, usually a finite quotient ring, with
\(|R|=Q\).  Let
\[
  \Delta_R
  =
  \left\{
    p:R\to\mathbb R_{\ge 0}:\sum_{a\in R}p_a=1
  \right\},
  \qquad
  \Delta_R^\circ
  =
  \left\{
    p:R\to\mathbb R_{>0}:\sum_{a\in R}p_a=1
  \right\}.
\]
The Fisher metric on the interior is
\[
  g_p(u,v)
  =
  \sum_{a\in R}{u_av_a\over p_a},
  \qquad
  \sum_a u_a=\sum_a v_a=0.
\]

### 5.1 State participation

**Definition 5.1 (Hellinger participation).**  
For \(p,q\in\Delta_R\), define
\[
  P_H(p,q)
  =
  A_H(p,q)
  =
  \sum_{a\in R}\sqrt{p_aq_a}.
\]
This is the Hellinger affinity, also called the Bhattacharyya coefficient.  We
use it as the base state-level participation score.  The squared Hellinger
distance is
\[
  H^2(p,q)=1-P_H(p,q).
\]
The classical fidelity convention is
\[
  F_{\mathrm{cl}}(p,q)=P_H(p,q)^2.
\]
In this paper \(P_H\) means the root fidelity/affinity, not the squared
fidelity.

The square-root Fisher embedding is
\[
  \varphi:\Delta_R\to S^{Q-1}_+,\qquad
  \varphi(p)_a=\sqrt{p_a}.
\]
Then
\[
  P_H(p,q)=\langle \varphi(p),\varphi(q)\rangle_{\mathbb R^Q}.
\]
Thus participation is literally spherical overlap after the square-root
embedding.

**Proposition 5.2 (Basic properties).**  
\(P_H\) satisfies:

1. \(0\le P_H(p,q)\le 1\);
2. \(P_H(p,q)=P_H(q,p)\);
3. \(P_H(p,q)=1\) iff \(p=q\);
4. \(P_H(p,q)=0\) iff \(p\) and \(q\) have disjoint support;
5. if \(p\in\Delta_R^\circ\) and \(q(t)=p+t u+O(t^2)\), with
   \(\sum_a u_a=0\), then
   \[
     P_H(p,q(t))
     =
     1-{t^2\over 8}g_p(u,u)+O(t^3).
   \]

**Proof sketch.**  
The first four items follow from Cauchy-Schwarz under the square-root
embedding.  For the local expansion,
\[
  \sqrt{p_a(p_a+t u_a)}
  =
  p_a\sqrt{1+t u_a/p_a}
  =
  p_a+{t\over2}u_a-{t^2\over8}{u_a^2\over p_a}+O(t^3).
\]
Summing over \(a\), the linear term vanishes because \(u\) is tangent to the
simplex.

This proposition is the first reason \(P_H\) is the right base component of
\(SN\).  It is a normalized overlap globally and the Fisher metric
infinitesimally.

### 5.2 Tangent participation

State participation compares points.  Sometimes the relevant objects are
directions: updates, gradients, currents, activation-to-behavior velocities,
or Nambu-Fisher vector fields.

**Definition 5.3 (Signed Fisher tangent participation).**  
For nonzero tangent vectors \(u,v\in T_p\Delta_R^\circ\), define
\[
  C_{F,p}(u,v)
  =
  {g_p(u,v)\over \sqrt{g_p(u,u)g_p(v,v)}}\in[-1,1].
\]
If a score in \([0,1]\) is required, define the affine alignment
\[
  P^{\mathrm{tan}}_{F,p}(u,v)
  =
  {1+C_{F,p}(u,v)\over 2}.
\]

This tangent score should not be confused with identity of states.  It is a
directional participation score.  Negative values are meaningful: two updates
may participate in the same Fisher tangent geometry while being oppositely
oriented.  For projective tasks one may instead use
\[
  P^{\mathrm{proj}}_{F,p}(u,v)=C_{F,p}(u,v)^2,
\]
but that quotient by sign is a declared observer choice.

### 5.3 Support and boundary conventions

The state score \(P_H\) is defined on the closed simplex \(\Delta_R\).  The
Fisher metric \(g_p\) is defined on the interior and on relative interiors of
fixed support strata.  When a boundary state appears, one must choose one of
three conventions:

1. work on the common support stratum;
2. add a declared smoothing floor \(p_a\mapsto (1-\epsilon)p_a+\epsilon/Q\);
3. use only the square-root embedding and avoid tangent Fisher formulas.

This convention must be part of the observer context \(W\).  Otherwise a scalar
SN score near the boundary can hide a numerical regularization choice.

## 6. Conductor-Resolved Participation

The Hellinger score gives a base overlap, but it does not say which arithmetic
packets are shared.  Conductor-resolved participation supplies that missing
decomposition.

Assume now that \(R\) is a finite quotient ring or finite abelian label algebra
with additive character group \(\widehat R\).  Let
\[
  H_R
  =
  \left\{
    x\in\mathbb C^R:\sum_{a\in R}x_a=0
  \right\}
\]
be the nontrivial tangent representation at the centroid.  The conductor
decomposition from Papers 8--11 is
\[
  H_R=\bigoplus_{f\in\mathcal C(R)} H_f,
\]
where \(H_f\) is the packet spanned by characters of conductor \(f\).  Let
\(\Pi_f\) denote the orthogonal projection onto \(H_f\) in the centroid
Fourier/Fisher normalization.

### 6.1 Square-root packet coordinates

Let
\[
  c=Q^{-1/2}{\bf 1}\in\mathbb R^R
\]
be the square-root image of the uniform distribution.  For
\(p\in\Delta_R\), define the centered square-root contrast
\[
  x_p
  =
  \varphi(p)-\langle \varphi(p),c\rangle c.
\]
Then \(x_p\in H_R\otimes\mathbb R\) and
\[
  x_{p,f}=\Pi_f x_p.
\]
The packet energy of \(p\) is
\[
  E_f(p)=\|x_{p,f}\|^2.
\]
If \(p=p_*\), then \(x_p=0\); all packet energies vanish.  This is not a
failure.  It says the maximum-entropy state has no nontrivial conductor
identity yet.

**Definition 6.1 (Packet alignment and packet participation).**  
For \(p,q\in\Delta_R\), define the signed packet alignment
\[
  \alpha_f(p,q)
  =
  { \operatorname{Re}\langle x_{p,f},x_{q,f}\rangle
    \over
    \|x_{p,f}\|\,\|x_{q,f}\| }
\]
when both denominators are nonzero.  If both packet components vanish, the
packet is silent and \(\alpha_f\) is marked \(\mathrm{silent}\).  If exactly one
vanishes, set \(\alpha_f=0\).

Define the packet mass weight
\[
  m_f(p,q)
  =
  {2\|x_{p,f}\|\,\|x_{q,f}\|
   \over
   \|x_p\|^2+\|x_q\|^2},
\]
with \(m_f=0\) when \(x_p=x_q=0\).  The positive packet participation is
\[
  \pi_f^+(p,q)
  =
  m_f(p,q)\max(\alpha_f(p,q),0).
\]
The signed packet overlap is
\[
  o_f(p,q)
  =
  \operatorname{Re}\langle x_{p,f},x_{q,f}\rangle.
\]

The pair
\[
  \Pi_{\mathrm{cond}}(p,q)
  =
  \bigl(
    (E_f(p),E_f(q),\alpha_f(p,q),\pi_f^+(p,q),o_f(p,q))
  \bigr)_{f\in\mathcal C(R)}
\]
is the conductor packet participation vector.

This vector is deliberately richer than a scalar.  A packet can be silent,
present and aligned, present and anti-aligned, present only in one state, or
erased by a quotient.  These cases should not be collapsed unless the task
declares that collapse.

### 6.2 Tangent packet participation

For tangent vectors \(u,v\in T_{p_*}\Delta_R^\circ\cong H_R\), define
\[
  u_f=\Pi_f u,\qquad v_f=\Pi_f v.
\]
The tangent packet alignment is
\[
  \alpha^{\mathrm{tan}}_f(u,v)
  =
  {\operatorname{Re}\langle u_f,v_f\rangle
  \over
  \|u_f\|\,\|v_f\|},
\]
with the same zero-denominator conventions as above.

At the centroid this is exact in the Fisher-conductor decomposition.  Away
from the centroid, Papers 10 and 11 show that conductor orthogonality is not
literally fixed: the Fisher tensor develops off-centroid coupling coefficients
controlled by quotient-character convolution.  Therefore any off-centroid
packet participation must declare one of the following:

1. the fixed centroid packet frame applied to square-root contrasts;
2. a transported Fisher-orthogonal moving frame;
3. an \(e\)-flat or \(m\)-flat conductor foliation;
4. a quotient-character adjacency algebra expansion to a specified jet order.

This is an important guardrail.  There is no context-free packet scalar
off-centroid.

### 6.3 Quotient-conditioned participation

Let \(q:R\to R_k\) be a quotient map and let \(P_q\) be the corresponding
orthogonal projection onto functions that factor through \(q\).  Paper 26
proves the cyclic divisor rule: for \(R=C_N\) and \(q:C_N\to C_k\),
\[
  P_q H_d = H_d \quad\text{iff}\quad d\mid k,
  \qquad
  P_q H_d =0 \quad\text{iff}\quad d\nmid k.
\]

Therefore the quotient-visible packet participation vector is
\[
  \Pi_{\mathrm{cond}}^{(k)}(p,q)
  =
  \bigl(
    (E_f(p),E_f(q),\alpha_f,\pi_f^+,o_f)
  \bigr)_{f\mid k}.
\]
The quotient-hidden residual is
\[
  \Pi_{\mathrm{hidden}}^{(k)}(p,q)
  =
  \bigl(
    (E_f(p),E_f(q),\alpha_f,\pi_f^+,o_f)
  \bigr)_{f\nmid k}.
\]

The corresponding one-state survival fraction is
\[
  S_k(p)
  =
  {\sum_{f\mid k}E_f(p)\over\sum_f E_f(p)},
\]
with \(S_k(p)=1\) by convention when \(x_p=0\).  A pair survival can be
recorded as
\[
  S_k(p,q)
  =
  {2\sum_{f\mid k}\|x_{p,f}\|\|x_{q,f}\|
  \over
  \|x_p\|^2+\|x_q\|^2},
\]
again with the uniform-uniform case declared separately.

High quotient participation is therefore not high full identity.  It can mean
that the observer has erased every forbidden packet.

### 6.4 Coarse-graining monotonicity

For an observable map \(\phi:R\to S\), let \(\phi_\#p\) be the pushforward
distribution:
\[
  (\phi_\#p)_s=\sum_{a:\phi(a)=s}p_a.
\]
Then
\[
  P_H(\phi_\#p,\phi_\#q)\ge P_H(p,q).
\]
This is the classical data-processing property of fidelity.  Coarse observers
make states at least as similar as they were before coarse graining.  The
participation residual
\[
  r_\phi^H(p,q)
  =
  P_H(\phi_\#p,\phi_\#q)-P_H(p,q)\ge 0
\]
measures identity created by forgetting.  In the conductor setting, Paper 26
tells us which packets caused that residual for quotient readouts.

## 7. Bures Participation and Noncommuting Identity

The categorical simplex is the commuting classical sector.  Papers 14, 15, 18,
and 24 show that the real simplex cannot carry all nonabelian directions
compatible with the CRT skeleton.  The missing directions become legal after
lifting to density matrices with Bures geometry.

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and let
\[
  \mathcal D^\circ(\mathcal H)
  =
  \{\rho>0:\rho=\rho^\dagger,\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1\}
\]
be the strictly positive density-matrix manifold.

### 7.1 Bures root participation

**Definition 7.1 (Bures participation).**  
For \(\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal D^\circ(\mathcal H)\), define
\[
  P_B(\rho,\sigma)
  =
  \operatorname{Tr}
  \sqrt{
    \sqrt{\rho}\,\sigma\,\sqrt{\rho}
  }.
\]
This is the Bures/Uhlmann root fidelity.  The squared fidelity is
\[
  F_B(\rho,\sigma)=P_B(\rho,\sigma)^2.
\]
This paper uses \(P_B\), the root fidelity, so that the diagonal classical
restriction matches \(P_H\).

The Bures distance convention compatible with this choice is
\[
  d_B^2(\rho,\sigma)=2(1-P_B(\rho,\sigma)).
\]

**Proposition 7.2 (Diagonal recovery).**  
If
\[
  \rho=\operatorname{diag}(p),\qquad
  \sigma=\operatorname{diag}(q),
\]
then
\[
  P_B(\rho,\sigma)=P_H(p,q).
\]

**Proof sketch.**  
The matrices commute, so
\[
  \sqrt{\rho}\,\sigma\,\sqrt{\rho}
  =
  \operatorname{diag}(p_aq_a),
\]
and the trace of the square root is
\[
  \sum_a\sqrt{p_aq_a}.
\]

### 7.2 Noncommuting participation residuals

Let \(D\) be a diagonal measurement or dephasing channel in a declared
classical basis.  Since fidelity is monotone under completely positive
trace-preserving maps,
\[
  P_B(D\rho,D\sigma)\ge P_B(\rho,\sigma).
\]
When \(D\rho=\operatorname{diag}(p)\) and \(D\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(q)\),
this becomes
\[
  P_H(p,q)\ge P_B(\rho,\sigma).
\]
The residual
\[
  r_D^B(\rho,\sigma)
  =
  P_H(D\rho,D\sigma)-P_B(\rho,\sigma)
  \ge 0
\]
measures identity created by diagonal measurement.  It is a hidden-coherence
residual: two states may appear more identical after the noncommuting degrees
of freedom have been erased.

This residual is the Bures analogue of quotient-probe blindness.  In one case
a quotient erases conductor packets; in the other a measurement channel erases
coherences.  Both create a causal or classical shadow in which identity looks
sharper than it is in the full object.

### 7.3 Relation to the Cartan obstruction

Papers 14 and 24 prove that the real simplex closes only in the Cartan sector
under the positivity, Fisher-volume, and CRT compatibility requirements.
Papers 15 and 18 show that the Bures density-matrix lift restores the missing
nonabelian directions as legal unitary coherences.

The participation reading is:
\[
  \text{Cartan classical identity}
  \quad\subset\quad
  \text{Bures noncommuting participation}.
\]
The classical shadow can be perfectly coherent inside its Cartan quotient while
still failing to participate in the full nonabelian identity upstairs.  The
failure is not an undefined metaphysical gap.  It is a residual coordinate in
\(SN_W\).

## 8. The SN Object

Mlynarski's \(SN\) function is introduced as a degree of identity:
\[
  A=B\ \mathrm{SN}\ z,\qquad z\in[0,1].
\]
This paper operationalizes that idea inside Fisher-conductor geometry.  The
main adjustment is that \(SN\) is tuple-valued before it is scalar-valued.

### 8.1 Observer/task context

An observer/task context \(W\) consists of the declared data needed to measure
participation:
\[
  W
  =
  (R,\mathcal H,\Phi,\mathcal P,\mathcal O,\mathcal R,\omega).
\]
Here:

- \(R\) is the categorical output algebra or label set.
- \(\mathcal H\) is an optional Hilbert-space lift.
- \(\Phi\) maps objects into representations, such as probability states
  \(p_A\in\Delta_R\), density matrices \(\rho_A\), activation states, or
  signals.
- \(\mathcal P=\{\Pi_f\}\) is the conductor packet frame or declared moving
  packet convention.
- \(\mathcal O\) is a family of observable maps, quotient readouts, probes, or
  measurement channels.
- \(\mathcal R\) is the list of residuals to record: quotient-hidden packets,
  Bures dephasing residual, Cartan residual, \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry,
  leakage, or task-specific missingness.
- \(\omega\) is an optional scalar weighting system.

Without \(W\), there is no honest scalar participation score.

The order of this notation matters.  The object is not first a pair
\((A,B)\), and then a context \(W\) added for convenience.  The context \(W\)
is the declared whole within which \(A\) and \(B\) become comparable
projections.  This is the identity-level analogue of Paper 28's ternary
guardrail: just as a three-body force should not be decomposed into three
binary conversations when the primitive is the whole triplet, a participation
claim should not be decomposed into an absolute two-object similarity when
the primitive is the whole constraint system.

### 8.2 Structured definition

**Definition 8.1 (Structured participation object).**  
Let \(A,B\) be two objects represented in context \(W\).  When the relevant
components are available, define
\[
  SN_W(A,B)
  =
  \left(
    P_H(p_A,p_B),
    \Pi_{\mathrm{cond}}(p_A,p_B),
    P_B(\rho_A,\rho_B),
    \{P_o(A,B)\}_{o\in\mathcal O},
    \mathcal R_W(A,B)
  \right).
\]
The entries are interpreted as follows:

1. \(P_H(p_A,p_B)\): base categorical participation.
2. \(\Pi_{\mathrm{cond}}(p_A,p_B)\): conductor-resolved participation vector.
3. \(P_B(\rho_A,\rho_B)\): noncommuting participation in the density-matrix
   lift.
4. \(P_o(A,B)\): observer-conditioned participation after quotient, readout,
   measurement, or task map \(o\).
5. \(\mathcal R_W(A,B)\): residuals showing what the observer has forgotten or
   what the classical shadow cannot close.

If a component is not available, it is not filled with a fake number.  It is
marked undefined relative to \(W\).  This is the "white spot" discipline:
missing participation data should remain missing rather than be hallucinated
as identity or distance.

### 8.3 Scalar aggregation

Scalar aggregation is allowed only after a context declares weights.  A typical
task scalar has the form
\[
  sn_\omega(A,B)
  =
  \omega_H P_H(p_A,p_B)
  +
  \omega_B P_B(\rho_A,\rho_B)
  +
  \sum_f \omega_f \pi_f^+(p_A,p_B)
  +
  \sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\omega_o P_o(A,B)
  -
  \sum_r \lambda_r R_r(A,B),
\]
with nonnegative weights normalized by the task.  Other aggregators are
possible, including minimum-gate scores, lexicographic packet priorities, or
Pareto fronts.

The scalar \(sn_\omega\) is not the ontology.  It is a task readout from the
structured \(SN_W\) object.  Two objects can have high scalar participation for
one task and low full-conductor participation for another.  That is not a
contradiction; it is the point of the construction.

### 8.4 Naturality desiderata

A mature \(SN\) calculus should satisfy the following requirements.

**N1. Normalization.**  
Self-participation is maximal:
\[
  SN_W(A,A)
\]
has \(P_H=1\), \(P_B=1\), aligned non-silent packets, and zero residuals
except for residuals attached to the observer itself.

**N2. Symmetry.**  
For identity-as-participation,
\[
  SN_W(A,B)=SN_W(B,A)
\]
up to orientation-sensitive tangent components, which must be marked as
signed.

**N3. Relabeling invariance.**  
Packet-preserving relabelings, Galois symmetries, and unitary changes of basis
inside the declared lift should preserve the structured score.

**N4. Coarse-graining monotonicity.**  
Observable participation should not decrease under forgetting:
\[
  P_H(\phi_\#p,\phi_\#q)\ge P_H(p,q),
  \qquad
  P_B(\mathcal E\rho,\mathcal E\sigma)\ge P_B(\rho,\sigma)
\]
for stochastic maps \(\phi\) and CPTP channels \(\mathcal E\).  The increase is
not "more true identity"; it is a forgetting residual.

**N5. Conductor transparency.**  
When a quotient map kills packets, \(SN_W\) should show exactly which packets
were killed.  In cyclic quotient rows, Paper 26's divisor rule is the model.

**N6. Lift compatibility.**  
On diagonal density matrices, Bures participation must recover Hellinger
participation.  Off the diagonal, Bures residuals should record the identity
hidden by classical measurement.

**N7. No scalar without weights.**  
There is no absolute scalar \(SN(A,B)\) unless the observer/task context
declares what counts.

The formal definitions above already satisfy N1, N2, N4, and N6 componentwise.
N3 and N5 are inherited at the centroid and under quotient maps, but require a
cleaner off-centroid naturality theorem.  N7 is a methodological axiom.

### 8.5 Higher-arity and recursive participation

The notation \(SN_W(A,B)\) is useful because many measurements are dyadic:
two probability states, two density matrices, two activation centroids, or two
readout distributions.  But dyadic measurement should not be confused with
dyadic ontology.

The higher-arity extension is:
\[
  SN_W(A_1,\ldots,A_m)
\]
where the entries record common participation, packet-wise shared support,
quotient survivals, Bures coherences, and residuals across an \(m\)-tuple of
projections.  For \(m=3\), this is the identity-side companion to Paper 28's
irreducible ternary mechanics.  The question is not only whether \(A_1\) is
close to \(A_2\), or \(A_2\) to \(A_3\), but whether the triple carries a
participation component that vanishes under every pairwise marginalization.

This matters because the cubic row is not the end of the story.  The raised
Amari-Chentsov cubic is the first nonlinear momentum-gap term, not the final
arity of the universe.  Higher cumulants, conductor towers, hyper-adjacency
supports, and finite-to-Archimedean limits all require participation objects
that can be indexed by more than two projections and more than one time step.

For predictive systems the recursive form is the more natural object.  A
system with identity state \(I_t\), action \(a_t\), world feedback \(y_t\),
and updated identity \(I_{t+1}\) should be audited by objects of the form
\[
  SN_W(I_t,I_{t+1};a_t,y_t),
  \qquad
  SN_W(\widehat W_t,W_{t+1}),
\]
where \(\widehat W_t\) is the predicted whole and \(W_{t+1}\) is the updated
whole after feedback.  The participation question becomes recursive: which
packets of identity persist, which packets change, which residuals were
predicted, and which residuals were hallucinated away by a coarse quotient?

This is the precise place where the "recursive universe for prediction"
enters the paper.  The dyadic \(SN_W(A,B)\) score is a projection of a
feedback-sensitive participation calculus, not its endpoint.

## 9. Participation, Obstruction, and Constrained Overflow

The preceding definitions make it possible to reinterpret several earlier
obstructions.

### 9.1 From defect to residual coordinate

An obstruction is often described negatively: a symmetry does not close, a
packet leaks, a quotient loses information, a classical real cone cannot carry
nonabelian directions.  The participation layer asks for the positive content
of that failure:

- Which participation component remains high?
- Which packet, coherence, or quotient component fails?
- Which observer created the appearance of identity?
- Is the residual stable under controls?

Thus an obstruction becomes a residual coordinate in \(SN_W\), not merely a
defect label.

### 9.2 Cartan obstruction

The Cartan obstruction says that the real simplex cannot support the full
nonabelian symmetry algebra while preserving positivity, Fisher volume, and
CRT compatibility.  In participation language:

\[
  \text{classical Cartan participation}
  \ne
  \text{full Bures participation}.
\]

The diagonal measurement residual \(r_D^B\) measures how much identity is
created by collapsing the noncommuting lift to the classical shadow.  A high
classical score with a large \(r_D^B\) is not a contradiction.  It is constrained
overflow: the classical observer sees identity because the noncommuting
distinction has been forced out of view.

### 9.3 Quotient probe-obstruction

Paper 26 gives the exact finite readout version.  A quotient probe may score
well inside \(C_k\) while annihilating every \(H_d\) with \(d\nmid k\).  The
participation layer records this as:
\[
  SN_{C_k}(A,B)\ \text{high},
  \qquad
  \Pi_{\mathrm{hidden}}^{(k)}(A,B)\ \text{nonzero}.
\]
This is identity under a causal or task quotient, not identity in the full
conductor object.

### 9.4 \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) and filtered carry

Paper 11's \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) records filtered arithmetic data that
the associated graded conductor shadow cannot see.  The participation reading
is that two objects can agree in the graded conductor identity while differing
in filtered carry.  Therefore \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) should enter
\(\mathcal R_W\) as a higher residual:
\[
  R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}}(A,B)
  =
  \text{filtered carry mismatch after graded packet agreement}.
\]
This is not yet a closed scalar formula.  It is a research gate: define the
minimal carry-sensitive participation residual that distinguishes supports
with equal graded packet scores.

### 9.5 Jerk and long-time leakage

Papers 17, 19, 20, 21, and 24 interpret the jerk or fourth-cumulant derivative
as the next leakage pointer after cubic closure.  Participation language should
not romanticize this leakage.  It may indicate a real missing lift, a wrong
projection, a numerical artifact, or a higher-order effect.  The proposed
residual is:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{leak}}(t;A,B)
  =
  \text{participation mass transported into undeclared packets or lifts}.
\]
A future theorem should bound \(R_{\mathrm{leak}}\) along Nambu-Fisher and
Bures flows using Paper 19's heteroclinic and finite-jet leakage estimates.
That theorem is not proved here.

### 9.6 Non-explosive contradiction

Mlynarski describes superposition as a kind of non-explosive contradiction:
the state participates in alternatives without collapsing the whole system
into arbitrary inference.  In the present program, the disciplined version is:

\[
  \text{apparent contradiction}
  =
  \text{high participation in incompatible observer shadows}
  +
  \text{bounded residuals in the full object}.
\]

The word "bounded" is essential.  Without residual bounds, "overflow" becomes
an excuse for anything.  With residual coordinates and falsifiers, it becomes
a mathematical object.

## 10. AI Diagnostics and Feedback Identity

Papers 12 and 26 already use Fisher-conductor geometry to analyze learned
behavior manifolds, activation pullbacks, quotient probes, hidden-state
shortcuts, and native logit packet structure.  The participation layer makes
the diagnostic question more direct:

> Which states, prompts, behaviors, or internal representations participate in
> the same learned whole, and which observer map creates that identity?

### 10.1 Behavior-simplex participation

For a model output distribution \(p_x\in\Delta_R\) at prompt or state \(x\),
define
\[
  SN_W(x,y)
  =
  SN_W(p_x,p_y)
\]
using Hellinger participation and conductor packet decomposition.  This
records whether two prompts are behaviorally close, which packet modes carry
the closeness, and which packets are anti-aligned or silent.

For calendar tasks, a prime row such as \(C_7\) has one nontrivial conductor
packet, so conductor-resolved participation is coarse.  A composite row such
as \(C_{12}\) has packets \(H_2,H_3,H_4,H_6,H_{12}\), making participation
multi-channel.  This is exactly where a scalar distance can hide the structure.

### 10.2 Activation-to-behavior pullback

Let
\[
  F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ
\]
be an activation-to-behavior map, with Jacobian \(J_F\).  Paper 12 studies the
pullback metric
\[
  J_F^\top g_y J_F.
\]
The participation version compares:

1. activation-side similarity in \(A\);
2. behavior-side \(P_H(F(a),F(b))\);
3. conductor packet participation of \(F(a),F(b)\);
4. tangent participation of \(J_F u,J_F v\).

When activation distance and Hellinger participation agree, the representation
is close to an isometric behavior chart.  When they disagree, the residual
should be decomposed by conductor packets before it is interpreted.

### 10.3 Quotient-probe blindness

For a quotient probe \(C_N\to C_k\), Paper 26 predicts exactly which packets
are blind:
\[
  H_d\ \text{visible iff}\ d\mid k.
\]
The participation diagnostic is therefore:

1. compute full native behavior packet participation;
2. compute quotient-conditioned participation after binning or probe readout;
3. record the hidden residual \(\Pi_{\mathrm{hidden}}^{(k)}\);
4. compare with shuffled-label and same-rank random-subspace controls.

A successful quotient probe does not prove the model lacks forbidden packets.
It may prove only that the readout erased them.

### 10.4 Feedback identity

Mlynarski's AI critique is not merely that AI lacks sensory grounding.  It is
that action does not return as a durable change in identity.  In this paper's
terms, the missing object is a closed feedback loop:
\[
  I_t
  \xrightarrow{\text{action}}
  W_t
  \xrightarrow{\text{world response}}
  I_{t+1},
\]
where the identity state \(I_t\) itself changes and constrains later action.

A future participation diagnostic for feedback identity would track:
\[
  SN_W(I_t,I_{t+1}),
  \qquad
  SN_W(I_{t+1},\mathrm{trace}(A_t,W_t)),
  \qquad
  \Delta\Pi_{\mathrm{cond}}(I_t,I_{t+1}).
\]
The question is not whether an agent's output changes.  It is whether durable
identity packets change in response to consequences and then alter future
behavior under controls.

This is programmatic.  The current repo has behavior and activation tools for
static and local pullback audits.  Persistent feedback identity requires a new
experimental design with memory, intervention, counterfactual controls, and
longitudinal packet tracking.

### 10.5 White spots as an output requirement

The \(SN_W\) object should permit missing entries.  If the Bures lift is not
observed, do not infer \(P_B\).  If the conductor frame is not declared, do not
invent packet participation.  If a quotient readout is blind by theorem, mark
the hidden packets.  This is the technical version of the "white spot"
discipline: an advanced diagnostic should know when it does not know.

### 10.6 Worked example: circuit identity is whole-relative, not absolute

A direct empirical instance of identity-as-participation in mechanistic
interpretability is supplied by Rai, Geva & Yao 2026, "Data-driven Circuit
Discovery for Interpretability of Language Models"
([arXiv:2605.09129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09129)). They report that
hypothesis-driven circuit-discovery methods (EAP, EAP-IG, ACDC, E-ACT) on
GPT-2 / Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct / Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct return circuits that drift
sharply under task-semantics-preserving dataset variations: in-distribution
faithfulness 0.73 on the 2-person IOI variant collapses to 0.42 cross-dataset
on the 3-person variant (31-point drop on GPT-2; 33-point on Llama); the
top-5% edge Jaccard similarity between circuits discovered on syntax-axis
variants falls to 0.26 at the extreme endpoints (mean pairwise 0.30–0.66
across complexity / syntax / domain axes); entity-binding circuits drift
position-conditionally from $P_2$ at $111\%$ in-distribution faithfulness on
Qwen2.5 to $P_8$ at $33\%$.

The standard reading of these findings is *negative*: hypothesis-driven
methods are flawed because they fail to return a single coherent
*task-general* circuit. The identity-as-participation reading is *positive*:
the circuit's identity is the structured \(SN_W(C^{(i)}, C^{(j)})\) object
between the circuit \(C^{(i)}\) discovered on dataset \(\mathcal{D}_i\) and
the circuit \(C^{(j)}\) discovered on dataset \(\mathcal{D}_j\), with the
observer/task context \(W\) given by the cyclic task algebra and the
syntax/domain/complexity axis along which the datasets differ. The reported
pairwise Jaccard $0.26$–$0.89$ across mixture ratios is the *scalar
aggregation* (§8.3) of an underlying packet-resolved \(SN_W\): the high
overlap on adjacent variants is the V-sector / single-prime-packet survival
(stable under small displacements of the centered logit); the smooth falloff
to $0.26$ is the M-sector activation (the cross-packet content that the
second-order attribution cannot couple at $p_*$, by Paper 34 Corollary 3.11).

"The IOI circuit" or "the entity-binding circuit," used as an absolute noun
phrase, is therefore an unjustified universal in the binary-equality sense
this paper diagnoses (§4). The participation-respecting alternative is the
\(SN_W\) tuple of (per-dataset circuit, per-axis Jaccard, per-position
faithfulness, per-mixture-ratio packet-projection profile) — exactly the
structured object §8.2 specifies. The DCD pipeline's own remedy (cluster the
per-example attribution vectors; report one circuit per cluster) is the
empirical operationalization of the *coarse-graining monotonicity* of §6.4:
collapsing the per-example identity into the per-cluster aggregate is a
quotient projection on the observer context \(W\), with the participation
preserved or lost in a measurable, theorem-bounded way (Paper 26 T1).

## 11. Research Gates / Falsifiers

The following gates separate the ideal abstract from a preprint-ready theorem
package.

### 11.1 Mathematical gates

**Gate M1: off-centroid conductor naturality.**  
Define and compare the three off-centroid packet conventions: fixed
square-root packet frame, transported Fisher-orthogonal frame, and
quotient-character jet expansion.  Prove when they agree to first or second
order and identify the first obstruction term.

**Gate M2: scalar aggregation theorem or rejection.**  
Determine whether any scalar aggregation of \(SN_W\) is canonical under
reasonable axioms.  The expected answer is negative except under strong task
weights.  If so, prove the no-free-scalar theorem.

**Gate M3: conductor/Bures compatibility.**  
For CRT lifts \(\mathbb C[R]\cong\bigotimes_p\mathbb C[R_p]\), define
packet-resolved Bures participation and prove diagonal recovery packet by
packet.

**Gate M4: obstruction residual bounds.**  
Relate \(R_{\mathrm{leak}}\), \(r_D^B\), and
\(R_{\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}}\) to the finite-jet leakage and heteroclinic
bounds of Paper 19.  The target is a non-explosive overflow theorem:
residuals may be nonzero but remain controlled under declared dynamics.

**Gate M5: quotient participation theorem.**  
Extend Paper 26 from exact quotient projections to noisy, learned,
approximately quotient-respecting readouts.  Bound forbidden packet leakage as
a function of equivariance error and rank.

**Gate M6: higher-arity participation theorem.**  
Define \(SN_W(A_1,\ldots,A_m)\) for finite tuples and prove which components
are genuinely \(m\)-ary rather than recoverable from pairwise marginals.  The
minimal test case is \(m=3\), where the target is an identity-side analogue of
Paper 28's irreducibility condition: a ternary participation component that
cannot be reconstructed from the three dyadic participation profiles.

### 11.2 Empirical gates

**Gate E1: native-logit participation atlas.**  
For existing \(C_7\) and \(C_{12}\) outputs, compute full \(SN_W\) tables:
Hellinger participation, packet alignments, quotient-hidden residuals, and
controls.

**Gate E2: activation pullback participation.**  
Compare activation-side distances to behavior-side participation and packet
participation across layers.  Test whether high behavior participation is
explained by actual activation geometry or by readout collapse.

**Gate E3: quotient blindness replication.**  
Run the Paper 26 quotient-probe checks across model families, prompts, and
tasks.  The falsifier is a quotient-respecting readout that reliably recovers
forbidden packets without violating the hypotheses.

**Gate E4: non-equivariant escape controls.**  
Build readouts that intentionally break quotient equivariance and verify that
they can recover packets forbidden to quotient-respecting probes.  This guards
against overreading the obstruction as an all-probe impossibility.

**Gate E5: feedback identity pilot.**  
Construct a persistent agent or model wrapper with durable memory and
controlled world feedback.  Track whether identity packets change after action
consequences.  The falsifier for the strong feedback claim is stable task
improvement without durable \(SN_W(I_t,I_{t+1})\) structure beyond ordinary
context accumulation.

**Gate E6: recursive prediction audit.**  
For a prediction-action-feedback loop, compare participation between the
predicted whole \(\widehat W_t\) and the updated whole \(W_{t+1}\).  The useful
observable is not only accuracy, but packet-resolved identity revision:
which conductor packets were preserved, which were corrected by feedback, and
which residuals were hidden by the task quotient.

### 11.3 Conceptual falsifiers

**F1: Participation collapses to ordinary distance.**  
If conductor packet vectors, Bures residuals, and quotient residuals add no
predictive or explanatory power beyond \(P_H\) or KL distance, the participation
layer is mostly terminology.

**F2: Packet participation is frame artifact.**  
If off-centroid packet scores vary wildly under reasonable moving-frame
choices without invariant residuals, the conductor \(SN\) component must be
restricted to centroid/local statements or redesigned.

**F3: Obstruction residuals fail controls.**  
If leakage and hidden-packet residuals do not distinguish real quotient
structure from shuffled labels, random same-rank subspaces, or base-10
shortcuts, the constrained-overflow interpretation fails.

**F4: Bures lift is irrelevant for observed tasks.**  
If density-matrix participation never changes the diagnostic compared with
classical diagonal Hellinger scores under tasks that should require
noncommuting identity, the Bures component remains mathematically valid but
empirically unmotivated for AI diagnostics.

## 12. Conclusion

This paper proposes a participation metric layer for the simplicial program.
The core move is simple: identity should be measured as structured
participation, not as binary equality and not merely as scalar distance.

On the categorical simplex, the base participation score is Hellinger affinity,
which is globally normalized and infinitesimally Fisher.  In conductor
coordinates, participation decomposes into packet alignments, packet energies,
quotient survivals, and hidden residuals.  In the density-matrix lift, Bures
root fidelity extends the same idea to noncommuting identity and recovers the
classical Hellinger score on diagonal states.  Under quotient readouts,
participation becomes observer-conditioned, and Paper 26 tells us exactly
which cyclic conductor packets survive.

The resulting \(SN_W(A,B)\) object operationalizes Mlynarski's gradable
identity inside Fisher-conductor geometry.  It does not make identity vague.
It makes identity decomposable.  A and B may participate strongly in one
observer's quotient, weakly in the full conductor object, and differently again
in the Bures lift.  That is not a flaw in the metric.  It is the mathematical
content of identity-as-participation.

The draft also reframes obstruction.  Cartan obstruction, quotient blindness,
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\), and leakage are not merely signs that a model
failed.  They are coordinates of constrained overflow: places where a local
shadow cannot carry the full participation structure.  The caution is equally
important.  Overflow becomes meaningful only when bounded by definitions,
controls, and falsifiers.

The ideal abstract points to a future theorem package: a calculus of
participation, constrained overflow, and causal shadows.  The anchored body
begins that package with definitions and formal properties, while leaving the
hard work visible: off-centroid naturality, packet-resolved Bures lifts,
obstruction bounds, quotient-noise stability, and persistent feedback identity
experiments.
