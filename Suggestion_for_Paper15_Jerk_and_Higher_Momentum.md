## Proposed Addition (new short subsection after Section 5 or as Remark 5.2)

**Remark 5.2 (The Jerk as Higher-Order Momentum and Projection Leakage).**  

The classical cumulant tower shows that the e-geodesic in expectation coordinates has acceleration given by the cubic Amari-Chentsov tensor \(T = K_{CH}\) and jerk given by the fourth cumulant:
\[
\dddot\eta = \nabla^{(e)}T(v,v,v) = T^{(4)}(v,v,v).
\]
The jerk is the first derivative of the cubic force and therefore points to quartic (higher-order) momentum. On the real simplex this higher momentum cannot be absorbed into any finite set of classical macro-Hamiltonians without violating real-cone preservation or CRT-tensor compatibility (see the cubic Noether theorem and Remark 3.2 of Paper 17).

On the density-matrix manifold the situation is qualitatively different. The quantum Nambu-Bures flow generated by a linear observable plus Casimir invariants reduces exactly to the unitary von Neumann equation
\[
\dot{\rho} = -i[H,\rho]
\]
(Theorem 3.2). This flow is linear in the coadjoint action on the Hermitian matrices. Consequently all higher derivatives vanish identically:
\[
\frac{d^k}{dt^k}\rho(t) = (-iH)^k \rho(0) \quad \text{(up to the adjoint action)},
\]
so the jerk — and every higher cumulant — is identically zero upstairs.

The classical jerk therefore appears **only** in the lossy projection from \(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3)\) back to the diagonal (commuting) submanifold \(\Delta_R^\circ\). Its leading-order rate is the leakage budget identified by the cubic/Bures completion. In this sense the unitary flow provides exact prediction upstairs, while the classical higher-momentum difficulty is reinterpreted as a quantifiable projection artifact controlled by \(T^{(4)}\).

This remark makes explicit the connection between the higher-order momentum intuition and the clean resolution supplied by the Bures lift, without altering any existing theorem.
