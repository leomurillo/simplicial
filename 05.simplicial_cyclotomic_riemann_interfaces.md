## Abstract

This note maps three cautious interfaces between the simplicial-cyclotomic framework and the Riemann Hypothesis.  It does not propose a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, nor a new zeta function meant to replace the classical one.  The aim is narrower: to identify which parts of the framework touch already-existing RH-adjacent mathematics, and where a future contribution would have to become genuinely new.

The first interface is the **prime-exponent lattice**.  Unique factorization embeds positive rationals into the free abelian group generated by primes,

$$
n=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)},
\qquad
\log n=\sum_p v_p(n)\log p.
$$

On this lattice the Euler product becomes a bosonic partition function with one energy \(\log p\) for each prime.  This places the simplicial-cyclotomic emphasis on characters, quotienting, and exponent lattices near Tate's thesis and the Berry-Keating/Connes/Bost-Connes spectral programs, but it does not by itself construct the Hilbert-Polya operator.

The second interface is **optimal frame theory**.  The simplicial ladder uses regular simplex equiangular tight frames (ETFs), while the deepest number-theoretic frame connections appear for complex SIC-POVMs.  SICs are not the same objects as real regular simplices; nevertheless, SIC work has produced striking links to ray class fields, Hilbert's twelfth problem, and Stark units.  This suggests a frame-theoretic bridge to class-field arithmetic, not a direct RH implication.

The third interface is **simplicial zeta analogues**.  Connection Laplacians and other operators on finite simplicial complexes give spectral zeta functions with functional equations and reciprocal spectral symmetries in special cases.  These models are useful laboratories for zeta phenomena, but their functional equations are finite/combinatorial analogues, not the analytic continuation and critical-line problem of \(\zeta(s)\).

The conclusion is a programmatic one: the simplicial-cyclotomic framework meets RH-adjacent mathematics at exponent lattices, spectral operators, optimal frames, and finite zeta analogues.  Its next defensible task is to make one of those interfaces produce a concrete operator, determinant, trace formula, or limiting theorem.

**Keywords:** Riemann Hypothesis, prime exponent lattice, Euler product, Berry-Keating Hamiltonian, Bost-Connes system, equiangular tight frames, SIC-POVMs, class field theory, Stark conjectures, connection Laplacian, simplicial zeta functions.

## 1. Scope and Caution

The Riemann Hypothesis concerns the nontrivial zeros of the meromorphic continuation of

$$
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}
$$

and asserts that they all lie on the line \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\) [Bombieri; Clay].

This note does not claim to prove, reduce, or reformulate the Riemann Hypothesis.  It only asks where the simplicial-cyclotomic program genuinely touches the established landscape around zeta functions and primes.

The answer has three layers:

1. arithmetic exponent lattices and spectral/dynamical models;
2. optimal frames and class-field arithmetic;
3. finite simplicial zeta functions and spectral symmetries.

Each layer is real.  Each also has a boundary where analogy stops.

## 2. Prime-Exponent Lattice

Unique factorization identifies the multiplicative group of positive rationals with the free abelian group on the primes:

$$
\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\cong \bigoplus_p \mathbb Z e_p,
$$

where

$$
n=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
v(n)=(v_2(n),v_3(n),v_5(n),\ldots).
$$

The logarithm turns this multiplicative lattice into an additive energy:

$$
\log n=\sum_p v_p(n)\log p.
$$

For \(\operatorname{Re}(s)>1\),

$$
n^{-s}=\exp(-s\log n)
=\exp\left(-s\sum_p v_p(n)\log p\right).
$$

Thus the Euler product can be written as a partition function over finite nonnegative prime-occupation vectors:

$$
\zeta(s)
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb N^{(\mathcal P)}}\exp\left(-s\sum_p k_p\log p\right)
=\prod_p\frac{1}{1-e^{-s\log p}}.
$$

This is the cleanest bridge between prime number theory and exponent-lattice thinking.  The prime logarithms \(\log p\) play the role of one-particle energies; the integers are finite occupation states.  This is the "free Riemann gas" viewpoint of Julia [Julia].  The phase

$$
n^{-it}=e^{-it\log n}
$$

is a character of the multiplicative group.

### 2.1 Relation to Dimensional Analysis

This is structurally parallel to dimensional analysis.  A physical dimension

$$
M^aL^bT^c
$$

is encoded by the exponent vector \((a,b,c)\).  Buckingham's Pi theorem finds dimensionless products by computing kernels of exponent matrices [Buckingham].

The number-theoretic analogue uses prime valuations instead of physical base dimensions.  Multiplicative relations among integers become integer relations among valuation columns.

The analogy is useful but limited.  Prime valuation matrices presuppose knowledge of prime factorizations.  They are not, by themselves, a factoring method.  The value here is conceptual: both physical scaling and arithmetic factorization become character theory on exponent lattices.

## 3. Spectral Interface: Berry-Keating, Connes, and Bost-Connes

The Hilbert-Polya idea asks for a self-adjoint operator whose spectrum encodes the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\).  The Berry-Keating proposal focuses attention on the classical Hamiltonian

$$
H_{BK}=xp,
$$

whose flow is dilation:

$$
x(t)=e^t x(0),\qquad p(t)=e^{-t}p(0).
$$

Berry and Keating showed that suitable semiclassical regularizations of \(xp\) reproduce the smooth counting asymptotics of the Riemann zeros [Berry-Keating].  Tate's thesis gives the \(GL(1)\) adelic framework in which local zeta integrals, local characters, and Fourier transforms assemble into global \(L\)-functions and functional equations [Tate].  Connes gave a spectral interpretation in noncommutative geometry in which the zeros appear as an absorption spectrum and the explicit formulas become a trace formula on adele classes [Connes].  The Bost-Connes system gives a quantum statistical-mechanical system whose partition function is the Riemann zeta function and whose symmetry breaking involves class-field-theoretic arithmetic [Bost-Connes].

The prime-exponent lattice touches this world because

$$
\log n=\sum_p v_p(n)\log p
$$

is the natural additive coordinate for multiplicative scaling.  In this sense, logarithm is the position/energy readout of arithmetic phase space.

But this is only an interface.  The exponent lattice does not automatically produce a self-adjoint Hilbert-Polya operator.  A future contribution would need to specify:

1. a Hilbert space built from truncated or completed prime-exponent states;
2. an operator whose trace/determinant has a controlled zeta-like expansion;
3. a limiting procedure compatible with analytic continuation;
4. a spectral symmetry corresponding to the functional equation.

Without these ingredients, the connection remains programmatic rather than a result.

## 4. Simplicial-Cyclotomic Operators and Euler Products

The simplicial-cyclotomic ladder supplies finite cyclic operators

$$
C_N^N=I,
$$

with the trivial/gauge mode removed on

$$
H_N=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\sum_i x_i=0\}.
$$

On \(H_N\),

$$
1+C_N+\cdots+C_N^{N-1}=0.
$$

For prime \(N=p\), this is the cyclotomic law

$$
\Phi_p(C_p)=0.
$$

This is a finite additive/cyclic symmetry, while the Euler product is an infinite multiplicative prime product.  The bridge is not equality of structures but character theory:

- \(C_p\) has roots of unity as finite additive/cyclic characters;
- \(n^{-it}\) is a multiplicative character along logarithmic scale;
- the zeta function packages all prime multiplicative modes through the Euler product.

The tempting synthesis is to attach to each prime \(p\) a finite cyclotomic block \(H_p\), then build a global object from the direct sum/product of these blocks.  The caution is that \(\zeta(s)\) sees primes through their logarithms and multiplicative independence, not through the internal regular-simplex geometry of \(H_p\).  A nontrivial result would have to show that the \(H_p\)-blocks contribute to a determinant, transfer operator, or trace formula in a way that recovers known zeta data.

## 5. Frame-Theoretic Interface: Regular Simplices and SICs

The simplicial framework uses the regular simplex ETF: the projection of the standard basis of \(\mathbb R^N\) onto \(H_N\).  This gives \(N\) equiangular unit vectors in an \((N-1)\)-dimensional real space.

The deepest known number-theoretic frame connections, however, occur not for these real regular simplices but for complex SIC-POVMs.  A SIC in complex dimension \(d\) is a set of \(d^2\) equiangular complex lines in \(\mathbb C^d\).  SICs are central in quantum information and are the subject of Zauner's conjecture.

Appleby, Flammia, McConnell, and Yard describe remarkable connections between SICs and algebraic number theory, especially Hilbert's twelfth problem [Appleby-Flammia-McConnell-Yard].  Kopp relates SIC-POVM constructions to Stark conjectures and ray class fields, using zeta-derivative data at \(s=0\) in the construction [Kopp].

This gives a legitimate RH-adjacent contact surface:

1. optimal frame geometry;
2. class fields and explicit class field theory;
3. Stark units and zeta/L-function derivatives.

But the bridge must be stated carefully.  The real regular simplex ETF in \(H_N\) is elementary and exists in every dimension.  It is not a SIC-POVM and does not inherit the SIC class-field phenomena automatically.  The useful research question is instead:

> Which parts of the simplicial-cyclotomic bookkeeping survive when one moves from real regular simplices to complex equiangular systems with Weyl-Heisenberg or other finite abelian symmetry?

This is a frame-theoretic path toward class-field arithmetic, not a direct path to RH.

## 6. Simplicial Zeta Analogues

There is also a literal simplicial zeta direction.  For a finite abstract simplicial complex \(G\), one can form operators such as Hodge Laplacians, Dirac operators, or connection Laplacians.  Given a suitable spectrum \(\{\lambda_j\}\), one can define a spectral zeta function

$$
\zeta_G(s)=\sum_j \lambda_j^{-s}.
$$

Knill's work on connection Laplacians gives a particularly relevant example.  For a finite abstract simplicial complex, the connection Laplacian is defined on simplices by intersection.  In one-dimensional cases, the squared connection operator exhibits reciprocal spectral symmetry

$$
\sigma(L^2)=\sigma(L^{-2}),
$$

which yields a functional equation of the form

$$
\zeta_{L^2}(s)=\zeta_{L^2}(-s)
$$

and an equivalent palindromic characteristic polynomial [Knill].

This looks RH-like in the right way: spectrum, zeta function, functional equation, symmetry.  But it is a finite/combinatorial analogue.  The Riemann zeta function requires analytic continuation, an Euler product, and a critical-line zero statement.  The finite simplicial zeta setting gives a laboratory for functional equations and spectral reciprocity, not a proof template by itself.

The natural research problem is to connect the two directions:

> Can a sequence of simplicial complexes or cyclic-simplicial operators produce a limiting zeta function whose functional equation and zero geometry survive the limit in a controlled way?

This is the kind of statement that would turn analogy into mathematics.

## 7. Three Candidate Research Programs

### 7.1 Prime-Exponent Truncations

Let \(P_X=\{p\leq X\}\).  Consider the finite prime-exponent box

$$
\mathcal E_X(K)=\{(k_p)_{p\leq X}:0\leq k_p\leq K\}.
$$

Define the arithmetic energy

$$
E(k)=\sum_{p\leq X}k_p\log p.
$$

The truncated zeta partition function is

$$
Z_{X,K}(s)=\sum_{k\in\mathcal E_X(K)}e^{-sE(k)}.
$$

This is elementary, but it gives a controlled finite model.  The next step would be to add cyclic/simplicial internal degrees of freedom at each prime and ask whether the determinant or trace of the resulting operator has a meaningful limit.

### 7.2 SIC-Class-Field Translation

Use the existing SIC-class-field literature as the number-theoretic side, and ask whether the real simplex ETF \(H_N\) supplies any useful shadow or limiting case.  The likely answer is modest: the real simplex is too elementary, but the gauge/zero-sum bookkeeping may help organize finite abelian symmetry in more complicated complex ETFs.

### 7.3 Simplicial Zeta Limits

Choose a family of one-dimensional complexes \(G_m\), such as cycle graphs or barycentric refinements, and study the connection zeta functions

$$
\zeta_{G_m}(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(L_m^2)}\lambda^{-s}.
$$

The test is whether a limiting function keeps a functional equation and develops meaningful zero geometry.  This should be compared to Knill's dyadic and circular-graph zeta models, where such limiting questions are already explicit [Knill].

## 8. What Would Count as Progress?

The current framework becomes genuinely RH-relevant only if it produces one of the following:

1. **An operator:** a natural self-adjoint or trace-class construction whose spectrum or determinant sees zeta zeros.
2. **A determinant:** an explicit Fredholm, transfer, or finite-limit determinant whose zero set converges to known zeta data.
3. **A trace formula:** a formula matching arithmetic prime sums to spectral data.
4. **A limiting theorem:** a sequence of simplicial/cyclotomic finite models whose zeta functions converge with controlled zero behavior.
5. **A class-field construction:** a frame-theoretic object that produces ray class fields or Stark units beyond what is already known from SICs.

Without at least one of these, the framework remains a suggestive language rather than a new theorem about RH.

---

## 9. Conclusion

The simplicial-cyclotomic framework touches the Riemann Hypothesis through three existing mathematical doors.

First, the prime-exponent lattice turns the Euler product into a partition function over prime occupation states.  This naturally meets Tate's thesis and the Berry-Keating, Connes, and Bost-Connes programs, where local characters, scaling, trace formulas, and spectral interpretations of zeta are central.

Second, optimal frame theory has a real number-theoretic frontier in SIC-POVMs, class fields, and Stark units.  Regular simplex ETFs are not SICs, but they sit in the same broad frame-theoretic universe and provide the elementary real baseline.

Third, finite simplicial zeta functions show that Laplacian spectra on combinatorial objects can satisfy functional equations and reciprocal symmetries.  These are laboratories for zeta behavior, not replacements for the Riemann zeta function.

The safe thesis is therefore:

> The simplicial-cyclotomic program is not a route to RH by analogy alone.  It is a bookkeeping framework that points to precise RH-adjacent structures: exponent lattices, spectral operators, optimal frames, and simplicial zeta models.  The next step is to make one of those interfaces produce an operator, determinant, trace formula, or limiting theorem.
