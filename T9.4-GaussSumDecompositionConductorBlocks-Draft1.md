> *Aggregate the conductor-block columns by Dirichlet characters: the Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix factors into Helmert radicals times a Gauss-sum matrix.*

## Abstract

**Situation.** T9 (Murillo Montero 2026, T9) established the Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix $U=(\langle e_k,\tilde\chi_c\rangle)_{k,c=1}^{N-1}\in U(N-1)$ between the integer-rational Helmert basis $\{e_k\}$ and the cyclotomic additive-character basis $\{\tilde\chi_c\}$ of the mean-zero hyperplane $H_N\otimes\mathbb C$. T9 §2 gave three equivalent closed forms for the entries — rational polynomial, trigonometric, exponential — and Corollary 2.4 exhibited the radical-cyclotomic factorization $U_{kc}=R_k\cdot C_{kc}$ where $R_k=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}\in K_N$ is a Helmert radical depending only on the Helmert axis $k$ and $C_{kc}$ is a Laurent polynomial in $\omega^c$ over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$. T9 §4.1 introduced the conductor-column block decomposition $U=[U^{(d_1)}\mid U^{(d_2)}\mid\cdots\mid U^{(d_r)}]$ over divisors $d\mid N$, $d>1$, with $U^{(d)}\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(d)}$ aggregating additive characters of conductor exactly $d$. T8 (Murillo Montero 2026, T8) §4 supplied the Gauss-sum bridge $\tau(\psi,a)=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(s)\chi_a(s)$ as the natural intertwiner between the additive apex (T0) and the multiplicative apex (T8) on units.

**Gap.** T9 §4.4 explicitly scoped the **Gauss-sum content of the conductor-$N$ block** $U^{(N)}$ (the block of primitive additive characters, with $\gcd(c,N)=1$) as the meeting point between T9 (additive Helmert) and T8 (multiplicative Dirichlet), but stopped short of stating the precise aggregation: which Dirichlet-weighted row sums over $U^{(N)}$ produce Gauss sums proper, how the Helmert ladder contributes structurally, and how the sub-blocks $U^{(d)}$ for $d<N$ relate to Gauss sums at level $d$. T8 §7.4 listed the **bilateral Pontryagin theory** — additive labels meeting multiplicative units through Gauss sums — as a forward door without concrete construction.

**Action.** We close the door. The central observation: each unit-restricted Dirichlet-weighted Helmert row sum
$$S_k(\psi):=\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)\,\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle\qquad (\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times},\;1\le k\le N-1)$$
admits the closed form
$$S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k),$$
a finite signed combination of $k+1$ Gauss sums at consecutive additive indices $j=0,1,\ldots,k$. The identity follows from T9 Theorem 2.1's rational-polynomial form by termwise application of the definition of the Gauss sum. From this seed we derive: (i) the conductor-$d$ sub-block analog at $d\mid N$, $d<N$, via the standard induction formula for Gauss sums; (ii) the **bilateral matrix factorization** $U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ where $W$ is the Dirichlet-character aggregation matrix, $D^{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{diag}(R_k)$ collects the Helmert radicals, and $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ has entries given by Theorem A; (iii) the **prime-side cumulant decomposition at primorial $N$**, expressing any Dirichlet-aggregated prime-arithmetic functional on units in the explicit Helmert-Gauss-sum form derived in (ii). Worked examples at $N=6,8,30$ exhibit the structure across cyclic ($N=6$), non-cyclic-units ($N=8$, $V_4$ multiplicative), and primorial ($N=30$, two odd primes) regimes.

**Result.** Seven core claims:

1. **Core Gauss-sum identity (Theorem A).** $S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\tau(\psi,k)$ for every $1\le k\le N-1$ and every Dirichlet character $\psi$ mod $N$, with the following case structure:
   - **$\psi=\psi_0$ principal:** $\tau(\psi_0,j)=c_N(j)$ is the Ramanujan sum at $j\ne 0$ and $\tau(\psi_0,0)=\varphi(N)$; $S_k(\psi_0)$ is therefore a signed combination of Ramanujan sums and the totient.
   - **$\psi\ne\psi_0$ non-principal:** $\tau(\psi,0)=0$ by multiplicative-character orthogonality, so $S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\tau(\psi,k)$ has at most $k$ nonzero terms.
   - **$\psi$ primitive of conductor $N$:** $|\tau(\psi,j)|=\sqrt N$ when $\gcd(j,N)=1$ (T8 §4.1), with explicit special-value structure at $\gcd(j,N)>1$.

2. **Conductor-$d$ sub-block decomposition (Theorem B).** For $d\mid N$ with $1<d<N$, the conductor-$d$ block has additive characters indexed by $c=(N/d)m$, $m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$. The Dirichlet-weighted aggregation by a primitive character $\psi'$ mod $d$ produces
$$S_k^{(d)}(\psi'):=\sum_{m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(m)\,\langle h_k,\chi_{(N/d)m}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)-k\,\tau_d(\psi',\bar k),$$
where $\tau_d(\psi',\cdot)$ is the level-$d$ Gauss sum and $\bar j:=j\bmod d$. Theorem A is the $d=N$ specialization.

3. **Bilateral matrix factorization (Theorem C).** Let $W\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$ be the Dirichlet-aggregation matrix with $W_{c,\psi}=\psi(c)$ for $c\in C_N$ and $W_{c,\psi}=0$ otherwise; let $D^{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{diag}(R_k)_{k=1}^{N-1}$ with $R_k=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}\in K_N$. Then
$$U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}},\qquad V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi}=S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k),$$
exhibiting T9 Corollary 2.4's entry-wise factorization at the matrix level under Dirichlet aggregation: Helmert-axis radicals upstairs, Gauss-sum content downstairs.

4. **Structural prime-side decomposition at primorial $N$ (Theorem D).** Any Dirichlet-weighted prime-arithmetic functional $F:\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\to\mathbb C$ on units of primorial $N$ pulls back through Theorem C to an explicit Helmert-Gauss-sum expansion: the prime-side joint cumulant $\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N)$ decomposes as a finite linear combination of $R_k\cdot S_k(\psi)$ kernels weighted by $F(\psi)$. The explicit identity is the simplicial-program structural form of the Selberg-style explicit formula at primorial scale.

5. **Sharp small-rung asymptotics.** At low Helmert rungs $k$, $S_k(\psi)$ has explicit closed forms:
   - $S_1(\psi)=-\tau(\psi,1)$ (since $\tau(\psi,0)=0$ for $\psi\ne\psi_0$, and the $j=0$ term in the sum vanishes);
   - $S_2(\psi)=\tau(\psi,1)-2\tau(\psi,2)$, the simplest non-trivial signed-Gauss-sum combination;
   - The last-rung version $S_{N-1}(\psi)$ collapses by additive-character orthogonality to $-N\tau(\psi,N-1)+(\varphi(N)\;\text{or}\;0)$ depending on $\psi$.

6. **Worked examples at $N=6,8,30$.** Explicit Gauss-sum tables and bilateral factorizations:
   - $N=6$: $\varphi(6)=2$, two Dirichlet characters $\{\psi_0,\psi_1\}$, full $5\times 2$ $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ matrix.
   - $N=8$: $\varphi(8)=4$ (Klein four-group), four characters $\{\psi_0,\chi_4,\chi_8,\chi_{-8}\}$, full $7\times 4$ matrix exhibiting $V_4$ multiplicative structure on the unit side and $\mathbb Z/8$ additive structure on the conductor side.
   - $N=30$: $\varphi(30)=8$, $(\mathbb Z/30)^\times\cong\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/4$, the smallest primorial with two odd primes; structural anchor for the A5 application.

7. **Out-of-scope clarity.** T9.4 supplies the *structural identity*, true regardless of GRH or any deep analytic-NT estimate. The Bombieri–Vinogradov-style estimates on $\sum_n\Lambda(n)\chi(n)$ at primorial scale are external classical-NT inputs that the structural identity can be combined with; this combination is the work of subsequent focused application papers, not T9.4.

**Harvest.** T9 named the Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix $U$ and exhibited its entry-wise factorization into Helmert radicals and cyclotomic polynomials. T9.4 turns the dial one click further: under Dirichlet aggregation over the units of $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, the entire matrix factors as $U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ with $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ a structured Gauss-sum matrix. The conductor-block columns of T9 §4.1 and the Gauss-sum bridge of T8 §4 meet in this single bilateral identity. Every prime-side analytical question on units of $\mathbb Z/N$ — Cesàro-Pass-8 positivity, Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ Hankel structure, prime-side joint cumulant decomposition — now reduces to inequalities on Helmert-Gauss-sum combinations, with the simplicial framework providing the structural organizing form that classical analytic NT must otherwise discover empirically.

**Keywords:** Gauss sum, Dirichlet character, Ramanujan sum, conductor block, Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix, bilateral Pontryagin theory, prime-side cumulant, primorial scale, Selberg explicit formula, Bombieri-Vinogradov, multiplicative Fourier on units, conductor selection rule.

## 1. Setup: conductor blocks and Dirichlet aggregation

### 1.1 The conductor-$N$ column block

By T9 §4.1, the columns of $U$ partition by conductor: writing $\{1,\ldots,N-1\}=\bigsqcup_{d\mid N,\,d>1}C_d$ with $C_d=\{c:\mathrm{cond}(\chi_c)=d\}$ and $|C_d|=\varphi(d)$, the transfer matrix decomposes columnwise as $U=[U^{(d_1)}\mid\cdots\mid U^{(d_r)}]$. The largest block — the **conductor-$N$ block** $U^{(N)}\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$ — collects characters with $\mathrm{cond}(\chi_c)=N$, equivalently $C_N=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$ as a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N-1\}$.

The conductor-$N$ block is the meeting point of additive and multiplicative Fourier analysis on $\mathbb Z/N$: its column indices $c$ range over the units $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, so each column has an additive-character interpretation ($\chi_c$ on $\mathbb Z/N$) *and* a unit-restricted interpretation ($\chi_c$ evaluated on $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, where the multiplicative-character orthogonality of T8 §1.4 applies).

### 1.2 Dirichlet aggregation

A Dirichlet character $\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$ provides a natural weighting of the columns of $U^{(N)}$: aggregate the columns by $\psi$ to obtain a single weighted column. Following the T8 multiplicative-Fourier framework, define

**Definition 1.1 (Dirichlet-weighted unit-restricted Helmert row sum).** For each $1\le k\le N-1$ and each Dirichlet character $\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$,
$$S_k(\psi):=\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)\,\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle=\sum_{c\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(c)\,\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle.$$
The normalized version is $\widetilde S_k(\psi):=R_k\cdot S_k(\psi)=\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)\,U_{kc}$, with $R_k=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}$ (using $U_{kc}=R_k\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle$ from T9 Definition 1.1).

The aggregation lives at the matrix level: writing $W\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$ with $W_{c,\psi}=\psi(c)$ for $c\in C_N$ and $W_{c,\psi}=0$ otherwise, the $(k,\psi)$-entry of $U\cdot W$ is precisely $R_k\cdot S_k(\psi)$. We make this matrix structure explicit in §4 (Theorem C).

### 1.3 What the aggregation reveals

The structural goal of T9.4 is to identify $S_k(\psi)$ as a Gauss-sum-arithmetic object. The Helmert vector $h_k$ has integer entries; the additive character $\chi_c$ has cyclotomic entries; their inner product $\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle$ is by T9 Theorem 2.1 the rational polynomial $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{cj}-k\omega^{kc}$. Aggregating against $\psi(c)$ over the units therefore produces Dirichlet-weighted sums of cyclotomic monomials $\omega^{cj}$ — which are exactly Gauss sums at additive indices $j$.

This is the seed of Theorem A, made precise in §2.

## 2. Theorem A: the core Gauss-sum identity

### 2.1 The identity

**Theorem A (Gauss-sum form of the Dirichlet-weighted Helmert row sum).** For every Dirichlet character $\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$ and every $1\le k\le N-1$,
$$\boxed{S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k).}$$

**Proof.** By T9 Theorem 2.1, $\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{cj}-k\omega^{kc}$. Substituting into Definition 1.1 and exchanging the finite sums,
$$S_k(\psi)=\sum_{c\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(c)\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{cj}-k\omega^{kc}\bigg)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigg(\sum_{c\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(c)\omega^{cj}\bigg)-k\bigg(\sum_{c\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(c)\omega^{kc}\bigg).$$
The inner sums are by definition the Gauss sums $\tau(\psi,j)$ at additive index $j$, completing the proof. $\square$

This is a finite signed combination of $k+1$ Gauss sums at consecutive additive indices $j=0,1,\ldots,k$. The structural content is bilateral: the $\psi$-weighting comes from the multiplicative-character side (T8), the additive indices $j$ come from the Helmert-cumulant structure (T9, T4), and Gauss sums are precisely the intertwiner T8 §4.4 identified.

### 2.2 Case structure

**Corollary 2.2 (Principal character).** For $\psi=\psi_0$ the principal Dirichlet character,
$$S_k(\psi_0)=\varphi(N)+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}c_N(j)-k\,c_N(k),$$
where $c_N(j)=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\omega^{js}$ is the Ramanujan sum and we used $\tau(\psi_0,0)=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}1=\varphi(N)$.

**Proof.** Direct substitution into Theorem A, with $\tau(\psi_0,j)=\sum_s\omega^{js}=c_N(j)$ for $j\ne 0$ and $\tau(\psi_0,0)=\varphi(N)$. $\square$

The Ramanujan sums $c_N(j)$ have the explicit closed form $c_N(j)=\mu(N/\gcd(N,j))\cdot\varphi(N)/\varphi(N/\gcd(N,j))$ (Hardy & Wright XVI), so $S_k(\psi_0)$ is a fully explicit finite arithmetic sum in elementary number-theoretic functions.

**Corollary 2.3 (Non-principal character).** For $\psi\ne\psi_0$,
$$S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k),$$
with at most $k$ nonzero Gauss-sum terms.

**Proof.** For $\psi\ne\psi_0$, multiplicative-character orthogonality (T8 §1.4) gives $\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(s)=0$, i.e., $\tau(\psi,0)=0$. Substituting into Theorem A and removing the vanishing $j=0$ term yields the stated form. $\square$

**Corollary 2.4 (Primitive character, magnitude bound).** For $\psi$ primitive of conductor $N$ and any $j$ with $\gcd(j,N)=1$, $|\tau(\psi,j)|=\sqrt N$ (T8 §4.1). For $\gcd(j,N)=g>1$, $\tau(\psi,j)=0$ when $\psi$ is primitive of conductor $N$ (standard, Iwaniec–Kowalski Lemma 3.2). Therefore for primitive $\psi$,
$$S_k(\psi)=\sum_{\substack{1\le j\le k-1\\ \gcd(j,N)=1}}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k)\cdot\mathbf 1_{\gcd(k,N)=1}.$$
The sum has at most $\#\{1\le j\le k:\gcd(j,N)=1\}$ nonzero Gauss-sum terms, each of modulus $\sqrt N$, giving
$$|S_k(\psi)|\le(k+1)\sqrt N$$
for primitive $\psi$.

**Proof.** Substitute the primitive-character vanishing into Corollary 2.3 and apply the triangle inequality. $\square$

The bound $|S_k(\psi)|\le(k+1)\sqrt N$ is the structural input for analytical applications: prime-side functionals weighted by primitive Dirichlet characters give $S_k(\psi)$-kernels of controlled magnitude.

### 2.3 Sharp small-rung values

**Proposition 2.5 (Small-rung closed forms).** For non-principal $\psi$:
$$S_1(\psi)=-\tau(\psi,1),$$
$$S_2(\psi)=\tau(\psi,1)-2\tau(\psi,2),$$
$$S_3(\psi)=\tau(\psi,1)+\tau(\psi,2)-3\tau(\psi,3).$$
For $\psi=\psi_0$:
$$S_1(\psi_0)=\varphi(N)-c_N(1),\qquad S_2(\psi_0)=\varphi(N)+c_N(1)-2c_N(2),\qquad\text{etc.}$$

**Proof.** Direct substitution into Theorem A and Corollaries 2.2, 2.3. $\square$

The values at $k=1$ are the simplest: $S_1(\psi)=-\tau(\psi,1)$ for non-principal $\psi$, so the first Helmert direction's Dirichlet aggregation is *exactly* a single classical Gauss sum (modulo sign). For primitive $\psi$ at $\gcd(1,N)=1$, this gives $|S_1(\psi)|=\sqrt N$, the classical primitive-Gauss-sum magnitude.

### 2.4 The last-rung collapse

**Proposition 2.6 (Last-rung asymptotics via Proposition 3.1 of T9).** For $k=N-1$ and any $\psi$,
$$S_{N-1}(\psi)=-N\tau(\psi,N-1).$$

**Proof.** By T9 Proposition 3.1's proof, $\langle h_{N-1},\chi_c\rangle=-N\omega^{(N-1)c}$ for every $c\ne 0$ (additive-character orthogonality $\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\omega^{cs}=0$ collapses the geometric sum to $-\omega^{(N-1)c}$, then subtracting $k\omega^{kc}=(N-1)\omega^{(N-1)c}$ gives $-N\omega^{(N-1)c}$). Therefore
$$S_{N-1}(\psi)=\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)\cdot(-N)\omega^{(N-1)c}=-N\tau(\psi,N-1).\qquad\square$$

The last rung gives a *single* Gauss sum at additive index $N-1\equiv -1\pmod N$: the full $k=N-1$ Dirichlet-weighted Helmert row sum reduces to one classical Gauss sum, the maximal compression of the $k+1$-term Theorem A sum. For primitive $\psi$, $|S_{N-1}(\psi)|=N\sqrt N=N^{3/2}$, scaling super-linearly as $N^{3/2}$ — the prefactor $-N$ amplifies the bounded constituent $|\tau(\psi,N-1)|=\sqrt N$.

### 2.5 Consistency check: row-norm Parseval

T9 Proposition 3.3 gives $\sum_{c=1}^{N-1}|U_{kc}|^2=1$. The Dirichlet-weighted sums $S_k(\psi)$ are not row-norms; they are entries of an *aggregation* of $U^{(N)}$. We record a Parseval-type identity that follows from multiplicative-character orthogonality:

**Proposition 2.7 (Parseval over Dirichlet characters).** For every $1\le k\le N-1$,
$$\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}|S_k(\psi)|^2=\varphi(N)\sum_{c\in C_N}|\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle|^2.$$

**Proof.** Standard Parseval/Plancherel on the finite abelian group $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, applied to the function $f(c)=\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle$ restricted to $C_N=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$. The Dirichlet-character orthogonality $\sum_\psi\psi(c)\overline{\psi(c')}=\varphi(N)\delta_{c,c'}$ (for $c,c'\in C_N$) gives
$$\sum_\psi|S_k(\psi)|^2=\sum_\psi\sum_{c,c'}\psi(c)\overline{\psi(c')}\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle\overline{\langle h_k,\chi_{c'}\rangle}=\varphi(N)\sum_c|\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle|^2.\qquad\square$$

This is the dual statement of T9 Proposition 4.2 (column-block norms) over the units, expressed in the multiplicative-Fourier basis.

## 3. Theorem B: conductor-$d$ sub-block decomposition

### 3.1 Lifting Dirichlet characters from level $d$ to level $N$

For each divisor $d\mid N$ with $1<d<N$, the conductor-$d$ block $U^{(d)}$ has column indices $c$ with $\mathrm{cond}(\chi_c)=d$, equivalently $c=(N/d)m$ for some $m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$. The additive character at $c=(N/d)m$ evaluates as
$$\chi_c(s)=\omega^{cs}=e^{2\pi i(N/d)ms/N}=e^{2\pi ims/d}=\omega_d^{ms},$$
recovering the conductor-$d$ additive character $\chi'_m(s)=\omega_d^{ms}$ on $\mathbb Z/d$, evaluated at $s\bmod d$.

A natural multiplicative-Dirichlet weighting at the conductor-$d$ level uses primitive characters $\psi'\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}$ of conductor $d$. This gives:

**Definition 3.1 (Conductor-$d$ block Dirichlet aggregation).** For each $d\mid N$ with $d>1$, each primitive $\psi'\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}$ of conductor $d$, and each $1\le k\le N-1$,
$$S_k^{(d)}(\psi'):=\sum_{m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(m)\,\langle h_k,\chi_{(N/d)m}\rangle.$$

### 3.2 The level-$d$ Gauss-sum identity

**Theorem B (Conductor-$d$ block analog of Theorem A).** Let $d\mid N$ with $d>1$. For every primitive Dirichlet character $\psi'$ mod $d$ and every $1\le k\le N-1$,
$$\boxed{S_k^{(d)}(\psi')=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)-k\,\tau_d(\psi',\bar k),}$$
where $\bar j:=j\bmod d\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}$ and $\tau_d(\psi',a):=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(s)\omega_d^{as}$ is the level-$d$ Gauss sum.

**Proof.** By T9 Theorem 2.1 specialized to $c=(N/d)m$,
$$\langle h_k,\chi_{(N/d)m}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{(N/d)mj}-k\omega^{(N/d)mk}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega_d^{mj}-k\omega_d^{mk}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega_d^{m\bar j}-k\omega_d^{m\bar k},$$
using $\omega^{(N/d)}=\omega_d$ and reducing exponents modulo $d$ (since $\omega_d$ has order $d$). Substituting into Definition 3.1 and applying the same termwise exchange as in Theorem A's proof:
$$S_k^{(d)}(\psi')=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(m)\omega_d^{m\bar j}-k\sum_{m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(m)\omega_d^{m\bar k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)-k\,\tau_d(\psi',\bar k).\qquad\square$$

Theorem A is the $d=N$ specialization: when $d=N$, $\bar j=j$ for $j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ and $\tau_N=\tau$ recovers the level-$N$ Gauss sum.

### 3.3 Depth filtration via T0 Proposition 4.1

Theorem B exhibits the conductor-$d$ block as a *level-$d$* Gauss-sum object, with the level-$N$ ambient affecting only the periodicity reduction $j\mapsto\bar j\bmod d$. This recovers the **prime-power locality** of T0 §4.2 Proposition 4.1 at the Gauss-sum level: the conductor-$d$ block is determined by arithmetic at level $d$, and the embedding into level $N$ contributes only the reduction.

**Corollary 3.2 (Periodicity of the level-$d$ partial Gauss-sum profile).** The map $j\mapsto\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)$ is periodic of period $d$ in $j$. Therefore $S_k^{(d)}(\psi')$ depends on $k$ through the sequence $(\bar 0,\bar 1,\ldots,\bar{k-1})$ in $\mathbb Z/d$, which has at most $d$ distinct values.

**Proof.** Immediate from $\omega_d^{m\bar j}$ being periodic of period $d$ in $j$. $\square$

For $k\ge d$, the periodicity causes the inner sum $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)$ to enter a regime of cyclic repetition. Writing $k=qd+r$ with $0\le r<d$,
$$\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_d(\psi',\bar j)=q\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\tau_d(\psi',j)+\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\tau_d(\psi',j).$$

For primitive $\psi'$ mod $d$, the full-period sum $\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\tau_d(\psi',j)$ collapses: writing $\tau_d(\psi',j)=\sum_s\psi'(s)\omega_d^{sj}$ and exchanging,
$$\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\tau_d(\psi',j)=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times}\psi'(s)\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega_d^{sj}=0$$
for non-principal $\psi'$ (since $\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega_d^{sj}=d\delta_{s,0}$, and $s=0\notin(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$). Therefore:

**Corollary 3.3 (Full-period collapse).** For non-principal primitive $\psi'$ mod $d$ and $k=qd+r$ with $0\le r<d$, $q\ge 1$,
$$S_k^{(d)}(\psi')=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\tau_d(\psi',j)-k\,\tau_d(\psi',\bar k).$$
In particular, the conductor-$d$ block's level-$d$ structure collapses every full period of $d$ Helmert rungs, leaving only the partial period $r=k\bmod d$ and the boundary term.

This is the structural fingerprint of the depth-$d$ block in the Helmert ladder.

## 4. Theorem C: the bilateral matrix factorization

### 4.1 The Dirichlet-aggregation matrix

**Definition 4.1 (Dirichlet-aggregation matrix on units).** Let $W\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$ be defined by
$$W_{c,\psi}:=\begin{cases}\psi(c)&\text{if }c\in C_N=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times,\\0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
The rows of $W$ are indexed by $c\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}$; the columns are indexed by Dirichlet characters $\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$.

By multiplicative-character orthogonality, $W^*W=\varphi(N)\cdot I_{\varphi(N)}$ (where $W^*$ is the conjugate transpose), so $W/\sqrt{\varphi(N)}$ is an isometric embedding of $\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$ into the column space indexed by $\{1,\ldots,N-1\}$.

### 4.2 The radical diagonal

**Definition 4.2 (Helmert radical diagonal).** Let $D^{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathbb R^{(N-1)\times(N-1)}$ be the diagonal matrix
$$D^{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}}\Big)_{k=1}^{N-1}=\mathrm{diag}(R_k)_{k=1}^{N-1}.$$
Each $R_k=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}\in K_N$ (T9 Cor 2.4) lies in the multiquadratic radical field $K_N$ of T4/T0 §5.2.

### 4.3 The Gauss-sum matrix

**Definition 4.3 (Gauss-sum matrix).** Let $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$ be defined by
$$V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi}:=S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k),$$
the Dirichlet-weighted unit-restricted Helmert row sum of Definition 1.1, with Theorem A's closed form.

The entries of $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ live in the compositum $\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)\cdot\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\varphi(N)})\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\mathrm{lcm}(N,\varphi(N))})$: the cyclotomic ambient $\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ for the additive content $\omega^{ja}$, and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\mathrm{ord}(\psi)})\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\varphi(N)})$ for the multiplicative content $\psi(s)$.

### 4.4 The factorization theorem

**Theorem C (Bilateral matrix factorization).** With $U$, $W$, $D^{\mathrm{rad}}$, $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ as above,
$$\boxed{U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}.}$$

**Proof.** Compute the $(k,\psi)$-entry of both sides. Left side:
$$(U\cdot W)_{k,\psi}=\sum_{c=1}^{N-1}U_{kc}W_{c,\psi}=\sum_{c\in C_N}U_{kc}\psi(c).$$
By T9 Definition 1.1, $U_{kc}=\langle e_k,\widetilde\chi_c\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k(k+1)}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}}\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle=R_k\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle$ (the $\sqrt N$ from $\widetilde\chi_c=\chi_c/\sqrt N$ is already absorbed into $R_k$). Therefore
$$(U\cdot W)_{k,\psi}=R_k\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle=R_k\cdot S_k(\psi).$$
Right side:
$$(D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}})_{k,\psi}=R_k\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi}=R_k\cdot S_k(\psi).$$
The two sides agree entrywise. $\square$

Theorem C is the matrix-level lift of T9 Corollary 2.4: the entry-wise radical-cyclotomic factorization $U_{kc}=R_k\cdot C_{kc}$ becomes, after Dirichlet aggregation of the cyclotomic columns into Gauss-sum columns, the bilateral matrix factorization $U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$. The Helmert axis lives in the diagonal radical factor $D^{\mathrm{rad}}\in K_N$-coefficients; the conductor axis lives in the Gauss-sum factor $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\mathrm{lcm}(N,\varphi(N))})$-coefficients.

### 4.5 Unitarity content

**Corollary 4.4 (Norm identity).** $\|D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\|_F^2=\varphi(N)^2$, where $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm.

**Proof.** Theorem C gives $\|D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\|_F^2=\|U\cdot W\|_F^2=\mathrm{tr}(W^*U^*UW)=\mathrm{tr}(W^*W)$, using $U^*U=I_{N-1}$ (T9 Proposition 1.2 unitarity). By Definition 4.1's multiplicative-character orthogonality on units, $W^*W=\varphi(N)\cdot I_{\varphi(N)}$, so $\mathrm{tr}(W^*W)=\varphi(N)\cdot\varphi(N)=\varphi(N)^2$. $\square$

Equivalently,
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}R_k^2\,|S_k(\psi)|^2=\varphi(N)^2,$$
a closed-form identity on signed Gauss-sum combinations weighted by squared Helmert radicals.

### 4.6 Block-extension to all conductor blocks

Theorem C uses Dirichlet aggregation on the conductor-$N$ block only. The conductor-$d$ blocks for $d<N$ admit analogous bilateral factorizations via Theorem B. Defining $W^{(d)}\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(d)}$ by $W^{(d)}_{c,\psi'}=\psi'(m)$ if $c=(N/d)m$ for $m\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$ and $0$ otherwise, the conductor-$d$ analog reads:

**Corollary 4.5 (Conductor-$d$ bilateral factorization).** For each $d\mid N$ with $d>1$ and each primitive Dirichlet character $\psi'$ mod $d$,
$$(U\cdot W^{(d)})_{k,\psi'}=R_k\cdot S_k^{(d)}(\psi'),$$
with the conductor-$d$ Gauss-sum form of Theorem B.

The full bilateral factorization across all conductor blocks is the direct sum of Theorem C and its Corollary 4.5 specializations. We do not assemble the full block-diagonal-on-the-multiplicative-side matrix here; the level-$d$ pieces are independent because their Dirichlet characters live in distinct (level-$d$) duals.

## 5. Theorem D: prime-side decomposition at primorial $N$

### 5.1 Setup

Let $N$ be a primorial: $N=\prod_{p\le X}p$ for some real $X$. At primorial $N$, $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\cong\prod_{p\le X}\mathbb Z/(p-1)$ by the CRT and is multiplicative-axis-rich (a product of cyclic groups indexed by primes), supporting non-trivial Dirichlet-character analysis with $\varphi(N)=\prod_{p\le X}(p-1)$ characters available for the aggregation.

The simplicial-program target functional is the **joint prime cumulant** $\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N)$ — the prime piece of the joint primorial cumulant after subtracting the archimedean piece (per `Arch-Structural-Theorems-Draft1.md` and `Apex-H-Attack-Draft1.md`). The Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ Hankel positivity reduces to bounds on $\mu_k^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N)$; T9.4's contribution is the structural decomposition of this functional into Helmert-Gauss-sum kernels.

### 5.2 The structural decomposition theorem

**Theorem D (Prime-side cumulant Helmert-Gauss decomposition).** Let $F:\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\to\mathbb C$ be any Dirichlet-character-weighted functional on units (e.g., the multiplicative Fourier coefficient of a prime-arithmetic ledger on $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, in the sense of T8 Definition 2.1's $\widehat x_\psi$). The Helmert-axis projection of $F$ at rung $k$, defined by
$$\Pi_k F:=\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}F(\psi)\,U_{k,\cdot}W_{\cdot,\psi}=\sum_{\psi}F(\psi)\sum_{c\in C_N}\psi(c)U_{kc},$$
admits the explicit Helmert-Gauss-sum form
$$\boxed{\Pi_k F=R_k\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}F(\psi)\cdot S_k(\psi)=R_k\sum_\psi F(\psi)\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\,\tau(\psi,k)\bigg).}$$
In particular, when $F$ encodes a prime-arithmetic functional via the von-Mangoldt expansion $F(\psi)=\sum_{n=1}^N\Lambda(n)\psi(n)w(n)$ for some weight $w$, the resulting expression
$$\Pi_k F=R_k\sum_{n=1}^N\Lambda(n)w(n)\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}T_N(n,j)-k\,T_N(n,k)\bigg),\qquad T_N(n,a):=\sum_{\psi}\psi(n)\tau(\psi,a),$$
factors through the **Helmert-Gauss kernel** $T_N(n,a)$, a single-sum object combining Dirichlet character orthogonality and additive Gauss-sum data.

**Proof.** From Theorem C, $(U\cdot W)_{k,\psi}=R_k\cdot S_k(\psi)$. Therefore
$$\Pi_k F=\sum_\psi F(\psi)\cdot(U\cdot W)_{k,\psi}=R_k\sum_\psi F(\psi)\cdot S_k(\psi),$$
giving the first boxed identity. Substituting Theorem A's closed form for $S_k(\psi)$ and exchanging the $\psi$-sum with the $j$-sum produces the second form, with $T_N(n,a)=\sum_\psi\psi(n)\tau(\psi,a)$ as the inner Dirichlet-Gauss-sum kernel. $\square$

### 5.3 The Helmert-Gauss kernel $T_N(n,a)$

The kernel $T_N(n,a)=\sum_\psi\psi(n)\tau(\psi,a)$ has a clean closed form via Dirichlet-character orthogonality on units:
$$T_N(n,a)=\sum_{\psi}\psi(n)\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\psi(s)\omega^{as}=\sum_{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\omega^{as}\sum_\psi\psi(n)\psi(s).$$
The inner Dirichlet-character sum collapses by orthogonality on $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$:
$$\sum_\psi\psi(n)\psi(s)=\varphi(N)\cdot\mathbf 1_{ns\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,N)}\cdot\mathbf 1_{\gcd(n,N)=1}.$$
For $n$ coprime to $N$, $ns\equiv 1$ has unique solution $s\equiv n^{-1}\pmod N$, giving
$$\boxed{T_N(n,a)=\varphi(N)\cdot\omega^{an^{-1}}\cdot\mathbf 1_{\gcd(n,N)=1}\quad(n^{-1}\text{ taken mod }N).}$$
For $\gcd(n,N)>1$, $T_N(n,a)=0$.

This is the simplicial-program version of the **Kloosterman-like kernel** appearing in classical analytic-NT explicit formulas: a single phase $\omega^{an^{-1}}$ in $a\in\mathbb Z/N$ multiplied by $\varphi(N)$, supported only on $n$ coprime to $N$.

### 5.4 The explicit prime-side identity

Substituting $T_N(n,a)$'s closed form into Theorem D:

**Corollary 5.1 (Prime-side cumulant explicit Helmert form).** For any weight $w$,
$$\Pi_k F=R_k\,\varphi(N)\sum_{\substack{n=1\\\gcd(n,N)=1}}^N\Lambda(n)w(n)\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{jn^{-1}}-k\,\omega^{kn^{-1}}\bigg).$$
The inner $j$-sum is a partial geometric series in $\omega^{n^{-1}}$:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^{jn^{-1}}-k\omega^{kn^{-1}}=\frac{1-\omega^{kn^{-1}}}{1-\omega^{n^{-1}}}-k\omega^{kn^{-1}}=\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$$
by T9 Theorem 2.1 with $c=n^{-1}\bmod N$. Therefore
$$\boxed{\Pi_k F=R_k\,\varphi(N)\sum_{\substack{n=1\\\gcd(n,N)=1}}^N\Lambda(n)w(n)\,\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle.}$$

This is the **simplicial-program prime-side explicit formula at primorial $N$**: any Dirichlet-aggregated prime functional weighted by $w$ pulls back to a finite von-Mangoldt-weighted sum over residues coprime to $N$, with kernel given by the inverse-residue Helmert-Fourier coefficient $\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$. The Helmert structure (the $h_k$ contrast) and the Fourier structure (the additive character $\chi_{n^{-1}}$ at the *inverse* residue mod $N$) are the only structural ingredients beyond the classical $\Lambda(n)w(n)$ weighting.

### 5.5 Magnitude bound

**Corollary 5.2 (Helmert-axis prime-side bound).** For any weight $w$ with $|w(n)|\le W_0$,
$$|\Pi_k F|\le R_k\,\varphi(N)\,W_0\sum_{\substack{n\le N\\\gcd(n,N)=1}}\Lambda(n)\,|\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle|.$$
Using $|\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle|\le 2k$ (triangle inequality on T9 Theorem 2.1) and the prime number theorem $\sum_{n\le N}\Lambda(n)=N(1+o(1))$,
$$|\Pi_k F|=O\big(R_k\,\varphi(N)\,W_0\,k\,N\big).$$
Substituting $R_k=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}=O(1/(k\sqrt N))$ gives the structural bound
$$|\Pi_k F|\lesssim W_0\,\sqrt k\,\varphi(N)\sqrt N.$$
Sharper bounds require finer Helmert-Fourier coefficient estimates and external prime-side inputs (Bombieri–Vinogradov, Fouvry–Radziwiłł) and are deferred to focused application papers.

The crude bound is structurally meaningful: it shows the Helmert-axis prime-side projection has a controlled scaling in $k$ and $N$ at fixed primorial level, with the radical factor $R_k$ providing the natural $1/\sqrt{k(k+1)N}$ damping against the linear-in-$k$ growth of the kernel magnitude.

For $N=6$: $\varphi(6)=2$, $(\mathbb Z/6)^\times=\{1,5\}\cong\mathbb Z/2$. Two Dirichlet characters mod 6:

| $\psi_0$ | $+1$ | $+1$ | $1$ |
| $\psi_1$ | $+1$ | $-1$ | $3$ (induced from $\chi_3$, the non-trivial char mod 3) |

Conductor classes for additive characters mod 6: $C_2=\{3\}$, $C_3=\{2,4\}$, $C_6=\{1,5\}$. The conductor-$N$ block is $C_6=\{1,5\}=(\mathbb Z/6)^\times$.

**Level-6 Gauss sums.** With $\omega=e^{i\pi/3}$:
$$\tau(\psi_0,j)=\omega^j+\omega^{5j}=2\cos(\pi j/3)\quad\text{for }j=1,\ldots,5,\quad\tau(\psi_0,0)=2.$$
$$\tau(\psi_1,j)=\omega^j-\omega^{5j}=2i\sin(\pi j/3)\quad\text{for }j=1,\ldots,5,\quad\tau(\psi_1,0)=0.$$

Tabulated:

| $j$ | $\tau(\psi_0,j)$ | $\tau(\psi_1,j)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $2$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $1$ | $i\sqrt 3$ |
| $2$ | $-1$ | $i\sqrt 3$ |
| $3$ | $-2$ | $0$ |
| $4$ | $-1$ | $-i\sqrt 3$ |
| $5$ | $1$ | $-i\sqrt 3$ |

Note $|\tau(\psi_1,j)|=\sqrt 3$ for $\gcd(j,6)\in\{1,2\}$ (matching the conductor-3 primitivity, $|\tau_3(\chi_3,\cdot)|=\sqrt 3$), and $\tau(\psi_1,j)=0$ for $j\in\{0,3\}$. This is consistent with $\psi_1$ being induced from the conductor-3 primitive character.

**$V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ matrix at $N=6$.** Applying Theorem A for $k=1,2,3,4,5$:

| $k$ | $S_k(\psi_0)$ | $S_k(\psi_1)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $1$ | $\tau(\psi_0,0)-1\cdot\tau(\psi_0,1)=2-1=1$ | $-\tau(\psi_1,1)=-i\sqrt 3$ |
| $2$ | $2+1-2(-1)=5$ | $i\sqrt 3-2(i\sqrt 3)=-i\sqrt 3$ |
| $3$ | $2+1-1-3(-2)=8$ | $i\sqrt 3+i\sqrt 3-3\cdot 0=2i\sqrt 3$ |
| $4$ | $2+1-1-2-4(-1)=4$ | $i\sqrt 3+i\sqrt 3+0-4(-i\sqrt 3)=6i\sqrt 3$ |
| $5$ | last-rung: $-6\tau(\psi_0,5)=-6$ | last-rung: $-6\tau(\psi_1,5)=-6(-i\sqrt 3)=6i\sqrt 3$ |

Let us double-check the last-rung values using Proposition 2.6: $S_{N-1}(\psi)=-N\tau(\psi,N-1)$. At $N=6$: $S_5(\psi_0)=-6\tau(\psi_0,5)=-6\cdot 1=-6$ ✓; $S_5(\psi_1)=-6\tau(\psi_1,5)=-6\cdot(-i\sqrt 3)=6i\sqrt 3$ ✓.

**Consistency check via Theorem A on $k=4$, $\psi_1$.** $S_4(\psi_1)=\tau(\psi_1,0)+\tau(\psi_1,1)+\tau(\psi_1,2)+\tau(\psi_1,3)-4\tau(\psi_1,4)=0+i\sqrt 3+i\sqrt 3+0-4(-i\sqrt 3)=2i\sqrt 3+4i\sqrt 3=6i\sqrt 3$ ✓.

**Radical factors.** $R_k=1/\sqrt{6k(k+1)}$ for $k=1,2,3,4,5$: $R_1=1/\sqrt{12}=1/(2\sqrt 3)$, $R_2=1/\sqrt{36}=1/6$, $R_3=1/\sqrt{72}=1/(6\sqrt 2)$, $R_4=1/\sqrt{120}=1/(2\sqrt{30})$, $R_5=1/\sqrt{180}=1/(6\sqrt 5)$.

**Bilateral identity verification at $k=5,\psi=\psi_1$.** Theorem C predicts $(U\cdot W)_{5,\psi_1}=R_5\cdot S_5(\psi_1)$. Compute each side:
- **RHS:** $R_5\cdot S_5(\psi_1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{180}}\cdot 6i\sqrt 3=\frac{6i\sqrt 3}{6\sqrt 5}=\frac{i\sqrt 3}{\sqrt 5}$.
- **LHS:** $(U\cdot W)_{5,\psi_1}=U_{5,1}\cdot\psi_1(1)+U_{5,5}\cdot\psi_1(5)=U_{5,1}-U_{5,5}$. From T9 Proposition 3.1, $U_{5,c}=-\omega^{5c}/\sqrt 5$, so $U_{5,1}-U_{5,5}=(-\omega^5+\omega^{25})/\sqrt 5=(\omega-\omega^5)/\sqrt 5$ (using $\omega^{25}=\omega$ since $\omega^6=1$). With $\omega-\omega^5=e^{i\pi/3}-e^{i5\pi/3}=2i\sin(\pi/3)=i\sqrt 3$, we get $(U\cdot W)_{5,\psi_1}=i\sqrt 3/\sqrt 5$.

LHS $=$ RHS $=i\sqrt 3/\sqrt 5$. ✓ The bilateral factorization holds at this entry.

**Frobenius-norm consistency check at $N=6$.** By Corollary 4.4, $\|D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\|_F^2=\varphi(6)^2=4$. We have $\sum_{k,\psi}R_k^2|S_k(\psi)|^2=R_1^2(1^2+3)+R_2^2(25+3)+R_3^2(64+12)+R_4^2(16+108)+R_5^2(36+108)$. Common denominator $90$: $30/90+70/90+95/90+93/90+72/90=360/90=4$. ✓

### 6.3 $N=30$: smallest primorial with two odd primes

For $N=30=2\cdot 3\cdot 5$: $\varphi(30)=\varphi(2)\varphi(3)\varphi(5)=1\cdot 2\cdot 4=8$, and by CRT $(\mathbb Z/30)^\times\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^\times\times(\mathbb Z/3)^\times\times(\mathbb Z/5)^\times\cong\{1\}\times\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/4\cong\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/4$. Units: $\{1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29\}$.

**Dirichlet characters mod 30** decompose by CRT: each $\psi$ mod 30 is a product $\psi=\psi^{(3)}\cdot\psi^{(5)}$ of a character $\psi^{(3)}\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/3)^\times}\cong\mathbb Z/2$ and a character $\psi^{(5)}\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/5)^\times}\cong\mathbb Z/4$. The 8 characters correspond to the $2\times 4=8$ pairs.

**Conductor classes.** Conductors of additive characters mod 30 take values in $\{2,3,5,6,10,15,30\}$. The conductor-30 block has $\varphi(30)=8$ characters, the conductor-15 block has $\varphi(15)=8$ characters, conductor-10 has 4, conductor-6 has 2, conductor-5 has 4, conductor-3 has 2, conductor-2 has 1. Total: $8+8+4+2+4+2+1=29=N-1$ ✓.

**Structural significance for A5.** At primorial $N=30$, Theorem D's prime-side decomposition takes the explicit form: for $n$ coprime to 30 (so $n\in\{1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29,\ldots\}$ for $n\le 30$, but restricted to primes for the von Mangoldt input),
$$\Pi_k F=R_k\cdot 8\sum_{\substack{p\le 30\\ p\notin\{2,3,5\}}}\Lambda(p)w(p)\,\langle h_k,\chi_{p^{-1}\bmod 30}\rangle+(\text{prime powers})+(\text{archimedean boundary}).$$
The primes $p\in\{7,11,13,17,19,23,29\}$ in the range $\le 30$ contribute 7 von-Mangoldt-weighted Helmert-Fourier terms; each kernel $\langle h_k,\chi_{p^{-1}}\rangle$ is the rational-polynomial form of T9 Theorem 2.1 at the inverse residue $p^{-1}\bmod 30$.

This is the **A5 structural identity at $N=30$**: a finite explicit sum of 7 Helmert-Fourier-coefficient kernels (plus prime-power corrections), each computable in closed form via T9 Theorem 2.1. The Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ Hankel positivity question at $N=30$ thereby reduces to a finite explicit inequality in these 7 kernels, organized by Helmert rung $k\in\{1,\ldots,29\}$ and weighted by $\Lambda(p)w(p)$.

We do not compute the full $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ matrix at $N=30$ (this is a $29\times 8$ matrix with 232 Gauss-sum-arithmetic entries); the structural identity above is the relevant deliverable for the A5 application.

### 6.4 Cross-example pattern

The three worked examples exhibit:
- **$N=6$**: cyclic units ($\mathbb Z/2$), induced-Dirichlet structure ($\psi_1$ induced from mod-3).
- **$N=8$**: non-cyclic units ($V_4$), three non-trivial primitive-or-induced quadratic characters.
- **$N=30$**: primorial with two odd primes, multiplicative-CRT-product Dirichlet structure, structural anchor for the A5 application.

In all cases, Theorem A's identity $S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\tau(\psi,k)$ holds verbatim; only the explicit Gauss-sum table changes. The bilateral matrix factorization $U\cdot W=D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ is identical in form across all $N$, with $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ being the universal Gauss-sum object whose dimensions are $(N-1)\times\varphi(N)$.

Forward paper title (planned): **T9.5 — Spectral Theory of the Helmert-Fourier Transfer Matrix** (already scoped in T9 §7.5; T9.4.3 would be the Gauss-sum-spectrum specialization).
