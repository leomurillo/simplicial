## Abstract

We construct the native quantum lift of the Nambu-Fisher mechanics of Paper 14. The classical phase space \(\Delta_R^\circ\) (the categorical probability simplex for the minimal mixed ring \(R = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\)) is replaced by the manifold \(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3)\) of strictly positive trace-1 Hermitian operators on the CRT tensor product Hilbert space. The Fisher volume form \(\Omega_R^F\) is canonically extended to the Bures-Helstrom volume form \(\Omega^B\) on this 35-dimensional manifold.

The resulting quantum Nambu-Bures bracket is well-defined and inherits global existence from the unitary invariance of the Bures geometry. We prove that the maximal continuous Lie subalgebra of Bures-volume-preserving symmetries that respects the CRT-tensor structure is now precisely the full local gauge algebra \(\mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3)\). The classical Cartan obstruction of Paper 14 (Theorem 2.2′) is thereby lifted: nonabelian rotations, previously forbidden by the real positive cone, are admitted because off-diagonal coherences in Hermitian density matrices absorb the mixing while preserving positivity and trace.

The classical Nambu-Fisher dynamics on \(\Delta_R^\circ\) is recovered exactly as the restriction of the quantum theory to the diagonal (commuting) submanifold. Quantization is therefore not an ad-hoc completion but the geometrically forced resolution of the representation-theoretic obstruction identified in Paper 14. The framework supplies the precise information-geometric bridge from classical probability to the local Standard Model gauge structure.

This final proof-level version expands the three technical bridges needed by the cubic information-geometric synthesis: the Bures-Helstrom restriction to the diagonal simplex, the reduction from Nambu-Bures volume data to unitary von Neumann flow, and the maximality of the effective local \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\) algebra under CRT locality. It also sharpens the slogan "no projected obstruction upstairs": the density-matrix lift has no projected classical cumulant-defect obstruction of the kind measured by the real-simplex jerk \(\nabla^{(e)}T\). Ambient higher time derivatives of a unitary orbit may be nonzero; what disappears upstairs is the need to force a nonabelian direction into a forbidden real-simplex macro-Hamiltonian.

As a first empirical audit of this lift, we test whether final residual-stream geometry in two transformer families separates CRT-local nonabelian rotations from Cartan rotations before the final LayerNorm-unembedding-Softmax measurement. On a synthetic \(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\) modular-addition probe, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-v0.3 show lower post-measurement survival for nonabelian CRT-local perturbations than for Cartan perturbations in layers 28--30. ## 1. The Density-Matrix Manifold

Let \(R = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\). By the Chinese Remainder Theorem the group algebra decomposes as \(\mathbb{C}[R] \cong \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3\). We work throughout with the composite Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3\) of dimension \(Q = 6\).

**Definition 1.1 (Density-matrix manifold).**  
The quantum phase space is the manifold
\[
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}) = \{ \rho \in \Herm(\mathcal{H}) : \Tr(\rho) = 1,\ \rho > 0 \}
\]
of strictly positive trace-1 Hermitian operators. This is a smooth manifold of dimension \(Q^2 - 1 = 35\).

The classical simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ\) embeds canonically as the diagonal submanifold
\[
\Delta_R^\circ \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}),\qquad p \mapsto \diag(p),
\]
where the diagonal is taken in the character basis of \(\mathbb{C}[R]\). Under this embedding the classical probability vectors are precisely the commuting density matrices.

## 2. The Bures-Helstrom Volume Form

The Fisher-Rao metric on the classical simplex admits a unique unitary-invariant extension to the quantum setting: the Bures-Helstrom metric (equivalently, the quantum Fisher information metric).

**Definition 2.1 (Bures-Helstrom metric).**  
For tangent vectors \(X,Y \in T_\rho \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\) (traceless Hermitian operators), the Bures metric is
\[
g^B_\rho(X,Y) = 2 \Tr\bigl( X \, L_\rho^{-1}(Y) \bigr),
\]
where \(L_\rho\) is the superoperator \(L_\rho(Z) = \rho Z + Z \rho\) (the symmetric logarithmic derivative equation). The factor \(2\) fixes the Paper 14 Fisher scale: on commuting diagonal states this metric restricts to the classical Fisher metric without an additional global rescaling. The associated Riemannian volume form is denoted \(\Omega^B \in \Omega^{35}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}))\).

**Theorem 2.2 (Classical recovery).**  
Under the diagonal embedding \(\Delta_R^\circ \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\), the Bures volume form restricts exactly to the Fisher volume form of Paper 14:
\[
\iota^*\Omega^B = \Omega_R^F,
\]
where \(\iota : \Delta_R^\circ \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\) is the diagonal inclusion. Consequently, all classical Nambu-Fisher theorems (Theorem 1.1 of Paper 14) are recovered as special cases of the quantum theory on the commuting submanifold.

*Proof.* Let \(\rho=\operatorname{diag}(p_1,\ldots,p_Q)\) with \(p_a>0\) and \(\sum_a p_a=1\). A tangent vector to the diagonal simplex has the form \(X=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_Q)\) with \(\sum_a x_a=0\), and similarly \(Y=\operatorname{diag}(y_1,\ldots,y_Q)\).

For diagonal \(Y\), the symmetric logarithmic derivative equation
\[
L_\rho(Z)=\rho Z+Z\rho=Y
\]
is diagonal and gives \(Z_{aa}=y_a/(2p_a)\). Hence
\[
g^B_\rho(X,Y)
=2\sum_a x_a {y_a\over 2p_a}
=\sum_a {x_a y_a\over p_a}.
\]
This is exactly the Fisher-Rao metric on the probability simplex in the Paper 14 normalization, restricted to the tangent hyperplane \(\sum_a x_a=0\). In redundant coordinates this is the usual Fisher form modulo the constant-vector gauge direction; in a \((Q-1)\)-coordinate chart it is the standard Fisher matrix.

Because the diagonal embedding has identical tangent metric after this identification, and because the diagonal orientation is chosen to agree with the Paper 14 Fisher orientation, the induced Riemannian top form is exactly the Fisher volume form:
\[
\iota^*\Omega^B=\Omega_R^F.
\]
Consequently, if \(f,C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\) are functions depending only on the diagonal probabilities, their Bures-Nambu bracket restricted to the diagonal is the Paper 14 Nambu-Fisher bracket. The classical theory is therefore recovered as the commuting-sector restriction of the density-matrix theory. \(\blacksquare\)

## 3. The Quantum Nambu-Bures Bracket

On the 35-dimensional manifold \(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\) the Nambu structure is defined by the top-degree volume form exactly as in the classical case, but now with 34 macro-Hamiltonians.

**Definition 3.1 (Quantum Nambu-Bures bracket).**  
For smooth functions \(f, C_1, \dots, C_{34} : \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}) \to \mathbb{R}\), the quantum Nambu-Bures bracket is the unique function satisfying
\[
\{f, C_1, \dots, C_{34}\}_B \, \Omega^B = df \wedge dC_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dC_{34}.
\]
The associated vector field \(X_{\mathbf{C}}\) obeys
\[
\iota_{X_{\mathbf{C}}} \Omega^B = dC_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dC_{34}.
\]

**Theorem 3.2 (Unitary von Neumann reduction).**  
Let \(H \in \Herm(\mathcal{H})\) be any traceless Hermitian observable and define
\[
Y_H(\rho)=-i[H,\rho].
\]
On any regular tube where \(Y_H\neq 0\), choose smooth functions
\[
C_1(\rho)=\Tr(\rho H),\qquad C_2,\ldots,C_{34}
\]
whose differentials are independent and whose wedge product satisfies
\[
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{34}
=\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B.
\]
Then the Nambu-Bures vector field generated by \(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{34})\) is exactly
\[
\dot{\rho}=Y_H(\rho)=-i[H,\rho].
\]

Equivalently, the unitary von Neumann dynamics is the Nambu-Bures flow whose Fisher-dual momentum form is the contraction of the Bures volume by the unitary vector field.

*Proof.* The Bures-Helstrom metric is invariant under unitary conjugation:
\[
\rho\mapsto U\rho U^\dagger,\qquad X\mapsto UXU^\dagger.
\]
Therefore its Riemannian volume form \(\Omega^B\) is also invariant. The infinitesimal generator \(Y_H(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]\) is consequently divergence-free:
\[
\mathcal L_{Y_H}\Omega^B=0.
\]
By Cartan's formula and \(d\Omega^B=0\),
\[
d(\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B)=\mathcal L_{Y_H}\Omega^B-\iota_{Y_H}d\Omega^B=0.
\]
Thus \(\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B\) is a closed \(34\)-form. On a regular tube for the one-dimensional foliation generated by \(Y_H\), the flow-box theorem supplies local coordinates \((s,c_1,\ldots,c_{34})\) with \(Y_H=\partial_s\). Since \(C_1=\Tr(\rho H)\) is constant along \(Y_H\),
\[
Y_H C_1=\Tr(-i[H,\rho]H)=0,
\]
we may choose \(C_1\) as one of the transverse coordinates wherever \(dC_1\) is independent of the remaining transverse differentials. The remaining \(C_2,\ldots,C_{34}\) complete the transverse chart and can be normalized so that their wedge product equals \(\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B\).

The Nambu-Bures vector field \(X_{\mathbf C}\) is defined by
\[
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega^B=dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{34}.
\]
By the chosen normalization, the right-hand side is \(\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B\). Nondegeneracy of the top-degree volume form gives \(X_{\mathbf C}=Y_H\), hence
\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho].
\]
This proves the reduction. The statement is local on regular tubes; global factorization is available on any region where the closed \(34\)-form admits a global decomposable primitive of the displayed form. \(\blacksquare\)

**Remark 3.3 (Lindblad terms are not part of this theorem).**  
Completely positive trace-preserving dissipative terms can be studied as additional non-unitary currents on \(\mathcal D(\mathcal H)\), but they are not used in the gauge-recovery theorem or in the cubic information-geometric synthesis. The proof above establishes only the conservative unitary von Neumann reduction.

The precise statement is that the obstruction called jerk in the real-simplex theory is a projected classical cumulant-defect obstruction: it appears when a nonabelian density-matrix trajectory is forced through the diagonal measurement channel and read as a path in \(\Delta_R^\circ\). Upstairs, the same nonabelian direction is an ordinary unitary tangent direction preserving positivity, trace, and Bures volume. Thus the density-matrix lift removes the need to encode that direction as a forbidden real-simplex higher-momentum macro-Hamiltonian; the defect reappears only after projection back to the commuting sector.

## 4. The Gauge Recovery Theorem — Lifting the Classical Cartan Obstruction

**Theorem 4.1 (Quantum Gauge Recovery).**  
Let \(\mathfrak{g}\) be the effective Lie algebra, modulo scalar local phases, of the maximal continuous Lie algebra of Bures-volume-preserving symmetries acting on \(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3)\) that satisfies:

1. **CRT-tensor compatibility:** Every element of \(\mathfrak{g}\) is a derivation of the form \(D = D_2 \otimes I_3 + I_2 \otimes D_3\) with \(D_i \in \mathfrak{u}(i)\).
2. **Centroid-fixing / trace preservation:** \(\mathfrak{g}\) annihilates the maximally mixed state \(\rho_* = I_6/6\).
3. **Positivity preservation:** The flow \(\exp(tX)\) for \(X \in \mathfrak{g}\) maps strictly positive density matrices to strictly positive density matrices.

Then \(\mathfrak{g}\) is exactly the full local gauge algebra:
\[
\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3).
\]

**Proof.**  
First, the advertised algebra acts. For \(U_2\in U(2)\) and \(U_3\in U(3)\), define
\[
(U_2, U_3)\cdot \rho
  =
(U_2\otimes U_3)\rho (U_2\otimes U_3)^\dagger .
\]
This action preserves Hermiticity, strict positivity, and trace. The Bures-Helstrom metric is unitarily invariant because the symmetric logarithmic derivative equation
\[
X={1\over 2}(\rho L_X+L_X\rho)
\]
is transported by conjugation: \(L_{UXU^\dagger,\,U\rho U^\dagger}=UL_{X,\rho}U^\dagger\). Hence the action preserves \(g^B\) and its Riemannian volume form \(\Omega^B\).

The infinitesimal CRT-local generators have the form
\[
A=A_2\otimes I_3+I_2\otimes A_3,
\qquad
A_2\in \mathfrak u(2),\quad A_3\in \mathfrak u(3),
\]
and act by \(X_A(\rho)=-i[A,\rho]\). The scalar parts \(i\alpha I_2\otimes I_3\) and \(I_2\otimes i\beta I_3\) contribute only a scalar multiple of the identity on \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3\), so their commutator with every \(\rho\) vanishes. After quotienting these ineffective local phase directions, the effective local algebra is therefore
\[
\mathfrak{pu}(2)\oplus \mathfrak{pu}(3)
\cong
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]

It remains to show maximality under the stated hypotheses. Condition 1 already restricts every admissible infinitesimal symmetry to a CRT-local element of \(\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(3)\); no generator with a genuine \(2\)-by-\(3\) cross term is in the candidate class. The local unitary algebra decomposes as
\[
\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(3)
=
\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus i\mathbb R I_2\bigr)
\oplus
\bigl(\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus i\mathbb R I_3\bigr).
\]
Under the commutator action on density matrices, both scalar summands map to scalar multiples of \(I_6\) and therefore vanish. Hence the entire effective quotient of the largest CRT-local unitary algebra is already \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\).

Consequently any strictly larger effective Lie algebra would have to add either a nonlocal \(\mathfrak{su}(6)\) direction or a non-unitary local direction. The former violates CRT-tensor compatibility. The latter is outside the \(\mathfrak u(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(3)\) derivation class in condition 1 and, in any case, does not preserve the Bures-Helstrom metric as a unitary conjugation symmetry. Therefore the largest effective continuous algebra satisfying CRT locality, Bures-volume preservation, and positivity preservation is exactly
\[
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]

Finally, this is precisely where the Paper 14 real-cone obstruction is lifted. On the real simplex, the off-Cartan root directions leave the positive orthant or mix CRT packets in a forbidden way. On the density-matrix manifold, the same nonabelian directions are represented as unitary coherences; they preserve spectrum, trace, positivity, and Bures volume. The obstruction therefore vanishes only after enlarging the phase space from real probabilities to Hermitian density matrices. \(\blacksquare\)

**Remark 4.2 (Hypercharge clarification — quantum version).**  
The recovered \(\mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3)\) contains the Cartan subalgebra \(\mathfrak{u}(1)^2\) of Paper 14 as its maximal torus, but now augmented by the full nonabelian root spaces. ## 5. Empirical Audit: Transformer Residual Streams and the Softmax Measurement

This section records an operational audit of the quantum-lift hypothesis in real transformer residual streams. It is not used in the proof of Theorem 4.1. Its purpose is to test whether the same Cartan/nonabelian distinction that is exact in the formal density-matrix geometry has a measurable analogue in the pre-unembedding behavior manifold of large language models.

### 5.1 Protocol

The task space is the minimal mixed ring \(R=\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\). We generated all \(36\) ordered modular-addition cases \((x+y)\bmod 6\) under six prompt templates, for \(216\) prompts total. For each prompt we scored the next-token distribution over the six residue answers \(0,\dots,5\) and captured the last-token residual stream at decoder layers \(28,29,30,31\).

For each layer, the six digit-answer unembedding directions define a six-dimensional readout subspace. The empirical density matrix \(\rho_h\) has:

- diagonal entries given by the mean six-bin Softmax probabilities;
- off-diagonal entries obtained from the covariance of residual coordinates in the answer-readout subspace, scaled to the largest positive-semidefinite coherence level allowed by the pre-registered \(\gamma=0.35\) cap.

We then applied two classes of matched small rotations in the six-residue coordinate system:

- **Cartan controls:** real Fourier-plane rotations corresponding to the two diagonal torus directions visible to the classical \(C_6\) output simplex;
- **CRT-local nonabelian probes:** real shadows of \(SU(2)\) and \(SU(3)\) generators acting on the \(\mathbb{C}^2\otimes\mathbb{C}^3\) CRT decomposition.

For each perturbation we measured the hidden-space chord distance before the final measurement and the Hellinger distance between six-bin output distributions after LayerNorm, unembedding, and Softmax. The reported squash ratio is
\[
\frac{d_H(p(h),p(h'))}{\|h-h'\|_{\mathrm{readout}}}.
\]
Lower values mean that more pre-softmax motion is lost under the classical measurement channel.

### 5.3 Interpretation and Limitations

The audit supports the qualitative hypothesis that transformer residual streams contain a richer pre-measurement geometry than the final output simplex exposes. In the tested layers, CRT-local nonabelian perturbations are more strongly squashed by LayerNorm-unembedding-Softmax than Cartan perturbations, while off-diagonal covariance energy remains measurable before the projection.

This should be read as exploratory empirical support, not as a proof that the model implements \(SU(2)\times SU(3)\) gauge symmetry. The modular-addition task is weakly learned by Llama-Instruct and only moderately learned by Mistral. Stronger follow-up controls are required: shuffled-label density controls, random skew-generator baselines, exact suffix reinjection through downstream layers, and higher-accuracy arithmetic prompts or fine-tuned probes.
