## Abstract

The $N$-vertex simplicial frame, stripped to its combinatorics, is the complete graph $K_N$. We show that one object — the $K_N$ graph Laplacian $M_N=NI-J$ — and its higher-dimensional companions organize the whole series, and that nearly every structure the program needs is a classical fact about complete complexes, newly identified across the layers.

**(1) The frame metric is the complete-graph Laplacian.** $M_N=NI-J=d_0^\top d_0=L_0(K_N)$, with spectrum $\{0^{(1)},N^{(N-1)}\}$: the gauge line $\mathbf 1=\ker d_0=H^0$ at $0$, and $N$ on the hyperplane $H$. Its heat trace is $1+(N-1)e^{-Nt}$ and its pseudo-determinant $N^{N-1}$ counts (Matrix–Tree/Cayley) the $N^{N-2}$ spanning trees of $K_N$.

**(2) $N$ is a universal eigenvalue.** The frame metric (Paper I), the order-2 cumulant / Fisher metric at the center (Paper III), and the combinatorial Hodge Laplacian $L_k$ on the complete complex at every degree (Horak–Jost) all carry the nonzero eigenvalue $N$. At second order the frame is one object — the complete-graph Laplacian — wearing three hats.

**(3) Order and degree are the two axes of the FEEC bicomplex.** The cumulant tower (order $n$, symmetric) and the Hodge tower (degree $k$, antisymmetric) are the column and row of the polynomial-differential-form bicomplex $P_n\Lambda^k$ of finite element exterior calculus, with the Koszul/Euler contraction $\kappa$ and the de Rham $d$ satisfying the Cartan identity $d\kappa+\kappa d=(n+k)\,\mathrm{Id}$. The universal eigenvalue $N$ is *not* a bicomplex invariant: it is the degree-axis Hodge eigenvalue, while the order axis grows as $N^{n-1}(n-2)!$; the towers meet only at the corner $(2,0)$. Its finite element realization on the integer Quadray lattice is moreover *exact*: every lowest-order element matrix is rational (no quadrature, no $\sqrt{\;}$), the $P_1$ stiffness on the regular cell being the graph Laplacian $\tfrac16(4I-J)$ itself, so Betti numbers — and the §5 arrow — are decided by exact rational rank rather than eigenvalue thresholding.

**(4) The arrow of time is the first Betti number.** On a simplicial complex, the discrete Hodge decomposition $C^1=\operatorname{im}d_0\oplus\operatorname{im}d_1^\top\oplus\ker L_1$ realizes the symmetric/antisymmetric ("$S+A$") reversibility split — exact $=$ gradient $=$ curvature, coexact $=$ curl $=$ current — and the harmonic sector, the topologically protected current, has dimension $b_1$. A self-adjoint instrument is blind to it; a two-sided predictor recovers it.

**(5) The gauge curvature is discrete.** The continuous abelian $\mathbb{R}\mathbf 1$ connection has curvature identically zero (the gauge line is fixed by the relabeling group $S_4$ and globally subtractable); the genuine gauge content is the orientation $\mathbb{Z}/2=S_4/A_4$ lattice field, whose gauge-invariant plaquette holonomy is the first Stiefel–Whitney class $w_1$ — the orientability obstruction, equal to Paper 01's "extra datum beyond the Gram matrix."

The unifying datum is $N=4$'s triple exceptionality, all tracing to $d=N-1=3$. The spectra, the bicomplex, the Hodge arrow, and $w_1$ are classical [Chung, Horak–Jost, Arnold–Falk–Winther, Eckmann, Lim, Whitney]; the contribution is their unification on the frame and the mapping of the program's two "blind spots" onto the $S+A$ split.

**Keywords:** complete graph, graph Laplacian, combinatorial Hodge Laplacian, simplicial complex, finite element exterior calculus, Koszul complex, polynomial differential forms, Betti number, Hodge decomposition, Stiefel–Whitney class, lattice gauge theory, spanning trees, Matrix–Tree theorem, simplicial frame.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Everything is the complete graph

Papers I–III built the frame's algebra, calculus, and information geometry, and at each turn a single object kept surfacing: the cyclic-difference generator $\widetilde K$, the integer metric $M=4I-J$, the gauge line $\mathbf 1$. This paper names the combinatorial source. The metric $M_N=NI-J$ is the graph Laplacian of the complete graph $K_N$ (the frame's $1$-skeleton); the gauge line is its kernel; the cross product is the Hodge dual of its coboundary (Theorem 01b.10.1); the cumulant tower and the Hodge complex are the two axes of the complete complex's FEEC bicomplex; the arrow of time and the orientation class are its topological invariants. The simplicial frame, combinatorially, *is* the complete graph $K_N$, and its spectrum and topology are the spine of the whole construction.

The honest consequence — confirmed by a literature pass once the full shape was known — is that most of the structure is classical. The spectra of complete complexes are Horak–Jost; the polynomial-form bicomplex is Arnold–Falk–Winther; the Hodge decomposition and Betti numbers are Eckmann and Lim; the orientation class is Whitney. The contribution of this paper is not new spectral theory but the *identification* — that these separate classical facts are one object seen from the program's four layers — together with two genuinely new readings: the mapping of the program's conductor and reversibility blind spots onto the symmetric/antisymmetric split, and the precise localization of the universal eigenvalue $N$ within the bicomplex.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. **The frame metric is $L_0(K_N)$ (§2):** spectrum, heat trace, Matrix–Tree, general-$N$ calculus.
2. **The universal eigenvalue $N$ (§3):** metric $=$ order-2 cumulant $=$ Hodge-$L_k$.
3. **The FEEC bicomplex (§4):** order $\times$ degree $=P_n\Lambda^k$, Cartan identity, $N$ localized to the degree axis; the reference cell is exact — rational element matrices on the IVM, with the $P_1$ stiffness $=\tfrac16 M$ (§4.3).
4. **The Betti arrow (§5):** $S+A$ on a complex, arrow $=b_1$, self-adjoint blindness.
5. **The orientation gauge (§6):** abelian-trivial, discrete-$\mathbb{Z}/2$, $w_1$.
6. **The triple exceptionality of $N=4$ (§7)** and the five-role synthesis of $\widetilde K$ (§8).

### 1.3 Novelty versus organization

Classical, cited not reproved: the $K_N$ Laplacian spectrum and Matrix–Tree/Cayley [Chung, Kirchhoff, Cayley]; the integrality of complete-complex Hodge spectra [Horak–Jost]; the polynomial-form/Koszul bicomplex and the homotopy identity $d\kappa+\kappa d=(r+k)\mathrm{Id}$ [Arnold–Falk–Winther]; the Hodge decomposition and $\dim\ker L_k=b_k$ [Eckmann, Lim]; the Stiefel–Whitney orientation class and $\mathbb{Z}/2$ lattice gauge theory [Whitney, Wilson]. New: **(a)** the unification — that the frame metric, the order-2 cumulant, and the degree-$k$ Hodge Laplacian are the single eigenvalue $N$; **(b)** the mapping of the program's two blind spots (conductor $=$ order axis, irreversible $=$ symmetric/antisymmetric) onto the $S+A$ split, with $\widetilde K$ the dictionary; **(c)** the localizations (the universal $N$ is degree-axis only; the towers meet at one corner). All displayed identities are exact-certified (Appendix A).

## 2. The frame metric is the complete-graph Laplacian

Let $K_N$ be the complete graph on the $N$ frame rays, with coboundary $d_0:C^0=\mathbb{R}^N\to C^1=\mathbb{R}^{\binom N2}$, $(d_0u)([ij])=u_j-u_i$.

**Theorem 2.1 (Metric $=$ Laplacian).** *The integer simplicial metric $M_N=NI-J$ of §01.7.5 equals the graph Laplacian $L_0(K_N)=d_0^\top d_0$. Its spectrum is $\{0^{(1)},N^{(N-1)}\}$, with $\ker M_N=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}=H^0(K_N)$ (the gauge line) and $M_N|_H=N\,I_H$. Consequently the entire frame calculus needs only the single scalar $N$ as metric data.*

*Proof.* $L_0=d_0^\top d_0$ has diagonal $\deg=N-1$ and off-diagonal $-1$, i.e. $(N-1)I-(J-I)=NI-J=M_N$. A connected graph has $\ker L_0=$ constants. The remaining eigenvalue is $N$ with multiplicity $N-1$ (the complete-graph spectrum [Chung]). Certified for $N=3,\dots,7$ in Appendix A. $\square$

**Spectral data.** The heat trace is $\operatorname{Tr}e^{-tM_N}=1+(N-1)e^{-Nt}$; the spectral zeta is $\zeta_{M_N}(s)=(N-1)N^{-s}$; the pseudo-determinant (product of nonzero eigenvalues) is $\det{}'(M_N)=N^{N-1}$, and the Matrix–Tree theorem then gives the spanning-tree count $\tau(K_N)=\det{}'(M_N)/N=N^{N-2}$, recovering Cayley's formula. The single gauge mode at $0$ plus the $N-1$ physical modes at $N$ is the spectral form of "one redundancy, $N-1$ dimensions."

**General-$N$ calculus.** Because $M_N|_H=N\,I_H$, the differential operators of Paper 01b extend verbatim to all $N$: $\operatorname{grad}=\tfrac1N d$, $\operatorname{div}=\sum_i\partial_i$, $\Delta=\tfrac1N\sum_i\partial_i^2$, and the coboundary identities $d_{k+1}d_k=0$ give $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$, $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$ at every $N$ (the single-axis binary cross product remains $N=4$-only; §7). In the integer normalization all operators are rational on rational data.

## 3. The universal eigenvalue $N$

The complete *simplicial complex* on $N$ vertices (all faces present) carries combinatorial Hodge Laplacians $L_k=d_{k-1}d_{k-1}^\top+d_k^\top d_k$ at every degree.

**Theorem 3.1 (Universal eigenvalue).** *For every $N\ge3$, the nonzero eigenvalue $N$ is shared by three a-priori-different objects:*
1. *(Paper I.) the frame metric $M_N|_H$;*
2. *(Paper III.) the order-2 cumulant — the Fisher metric at the center $p_*=\tfrac1N\mathbf 1$, $g_{p_*}=N\sum_i u_iv_i=M_N|_H$;*
3. *(Hodge.) the combinatorial Hodge Laplacian $L_k$ on the complete complex, whose nonzero spectrum is identically $N$ at every degree $k$ [Horak–Jost; the complete complex is Laplacian-integral].*

*Certified for $N=4,5,6$, all $k$, in Appendix A.*

At second order, then, the algebra's metric, the statistics' Fisher information, and the topology's Hodge Laplacian are one number, $N$ — the complete-graph Laplacian seen from three layers. This is the precise sense in which the simplicial frame is, at second order, a single combinatorial object.

## 4. Order and degree: the FEEC bicomplex

Two graded towers run on $H$: the **order axis** — the cumulant tower $T^{(n)}=\nabla^n\varphi$ (symmetric $n$-tensors, Paper III) — and the **degree axis** — the Hodge complex ($k$-cochains, $L_k$, antisymmetric). They are the two coordinates of one classical object.

**Theorem 4.1 (The bicomplex).** *Let $P_n\Lambda^k=\mathrm{Sym}^n(H^*)\otimes\Lambda^k(H^*)$ be the polynomial differential $k$-forms of degree $n$ — the bigraded space of finite element exterior calculus [Arnold–Falk–Winther]. It carries the de Rham $d:P_n\Lambda^k\to P_{n-1}\Lambda^{k+1}$ and the Koszul/Euler contraction $\kappa=\iota_E:P_n\Lambda^k\to P_{n+1}\Lambda^{k-1}$, with*
$$d\kappa+\kappa d=(n+k)\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad d^2=0,\qquad \kappa^2=0$$
*(Cartan's identity for the Lie derivative along the Euler field; the AFW Koszul homotopy). The cumulant tower is the $k=0$ column $P_n\Lambda^0$ (symmetric tensors $\cong$ polynomial functions); the Hodge tower is the form-degree axis; the de Rham $d$ is the bridge $P_n\Lambda^0\to\Lambda^1$. Certified for $d=3$, $n,k\le3$, in Appendix A.*

**Theorem 4.2 (Localization of $N$).** *The universal eigenvalue $N$ is not a bicomplex invariant. It is the degree-axis Hodge eigenvalue (Theorem 3.1.3); the order axis grows, $\nabla^n\varphi|_{p_*}=(-1)^n(n-2)!\,N^{n-1}$ (Paper III); and the two towers coincide at exactly one bidegree, the corner $(2,0)$, where the order-2 cumulant equals the degree-0 graph Laplacian. The unifying invariant of the bicomplex is the total degree $n+k$, not $N$.*

**The two blind spots are two moves in $P_n\Lambda^k$.** The program's *conductor blind spot* is the failure to climb the order axis past $n=2$ (a second-order instrument misses the cumulants); the *irreversible blind spot* is the failure to leave the $k=0$ symmetric column for the exterior part (a self-adjoint instrument misses the current, §5). They are distinct moves in one bicomplex, which is why the program needs both a higher-order probe and a non-self-adjoint probe — neither subsumes the other.

### 4.3 The reference cell is exact: rational FEEC element matrices

The bicomplex $P_n\Lambda^k$ is metric-free; its *finite element* realization is not. Standard FEEC on a Cartesian mesh pays a quadrature and round-off price at the reference cell, because the regular simplex has irrational Cartesian vertices — the $\sqrt3$ of §01.1, "the cost of describing tetrahedral truth in a cubic language." In the integer Quadray normalization of §2 that price is not reduced but removed.

**Theorem 4.3 (Exact-rational element matrices).** *Let $\tau$ be a $3$-simplex whose vertices have rational Quadray (equivalently rational Tom-Ace Cartesian) coordinates. Then its barycentric coordinates $\lambda_i$ are rational-affine, its gradient Gram $[\langle\nabla\lambda_i,\nabla\lambda_j\rangle]$ and its tetravolume are rational (read from the metric $M=4I-J$ of §2, with no $\sqrt{\;}$), and the barycentric moments are rational in closed form,*
$$\int_\tau \prod_{i}\lambda_i^{a_i}\,dV \;=\; \mathrm{vol}(\tau)\,\frac{3!\,\prod_i a_i!}{\bigl(3+\sum_i a_i\bigr)!}.$$
*Consequently every lowest-order finite element matrix on $\tau$ — the Lagrange $P_1$ mass and stiffness ($k=0$) and the Whitney $\mathcal P_1^-\Lambda^k$ mass and stiffness for edges, faces, and the cell ($1\le k\le 3$) — has exactly rational entries. No quadrature is required and no $\sqrt{\;}$ is incurred.*

*Proof.* Each named entry is a finite sum of products of gradient-Gram entries with barycentric moments (each moment being the exact integral of a monomial over a simplex). Rational vertices give rational $\lambda_i$ by inverting the integer augmented vertex matrix, hence rational constant gradients $\nabla\lambda_i$, rational Gram, and rational $\mathrm{vol}(\tau)=\tfrac16\lvert\det[v_1-v_0,\,v_2-v_0,\,v_3-v_0]\rvert$. Certified in Appendix A for the regular tetrahedron and an IVM octahedron-quarter cell. $\square$

**Corollary 4.4 (The stiffness matrix is the frame metric; IVM exactness; exact Betti numbers).**
*(i) On the regular tetrahedron the $P_1$ stiffness matrix is a rational multiple of the frame metric, $S=\tfrac16(4I-J)=\tfrac16 M$; by the symmetry and zero-row-sum argument ($\sum_i\nabla\lambda_i=0$) the regular $N$-simplex gives $S\propto M_N=NI-J=L_0(K_N)$. The lowest-order FEEC stiffness is the complete-graph Laplacian of Theorem 2.1, up to a rational scalar.*
*(ii) Every cell of the IVM octet-truss — the regular tetrahedra and the tetrahedra of a triangulated regular octahedron — has rational Quadray coordinates, so by Theorem 4.3 the entire assembled mass/stiffness system on an IVM mesh is exactly rational.*
*(iii) The discrete Hodge–de Rham operators $L_k$ therefore have rational entries, so $\dim\ker L_k=b_k$ (Theorem 5.1) is decided by exact rational linear algebra: the Betti numbers — and hence the topological arrow $b_1$ of §5 — are computed without the eigenvalue thresholding that makes a near-zero floating-point mode ambiguous.*

Part (iii) is the homological face of the discreteness the Synergetics tradition insists on [Thomson, §13]: a Hodge-harmonic dimension is a *combinatorial* fact — the rank of an integer matrix, not the magnitude of a small eigenvalue — and rational element matrices decide it exactly, where a Cartesian-quadrature assembly can mis-count $b_1$ at a near-degenerate mode. The exactness is a reference-cell property; under uniform IVM refinement (Fuller frequency $F\to\infty$) the resulting approximations converge at the standard rates [Arnold–Falk–Winther], the only floating point entering at the final global solve — the program's deferred-$\sqrt{\;}$ boundary. On a generic, irrational-coordinate tetrahedral mesh the per-cell exactness is lost (the cell Gram is no longer rational); it is special to the rational-coordinate Quadray/IVM meshes, which are exactly the octet-truss geometry the Synergetics program privileges. This answers the metric half of open problem E2: the place "a number-theoretic Gram weighting could enter" is the element-matrix assembly, and on the unweighted IVM it enters rationally.

**Remark 4.5 (Scope: irregular meshes).** Theorem 4.3 turns on *rational coordinates*, not on *regularity*, and the two must not be conflated. Regularity is unavailable in any case: the regular tetrahedron does not tile space — its dihedral angle $\arccos\tfrac13\approx70.53^\circ$ is incommensurable with $2\pi$ — so the clean form $S=\tfrac16 M$ cannot hold across a space-filling mesh, and the only space-filling rational lattice (the IVM octet-truss) realizes it through regular tetrahedra *together with* the non-regular but still rational tetrahedra of a split octahedron. Rationality, not regularity, is the operative hypothesis, and it is a property of the embedding — where the vertices sit — not of the mesh quality.

Two consequences delimit the result on a generic boundary-fitted mesh, and they fall on the program's own combinatorial/metric divide (§5; cf. [Thomson, §13]):

- *The combinatorics stays exact unconditionally.* The coboundary operators $d_k$ are integer incidence matrices — entries in $\{0,\pm1\}$ fixed by connectivity and orientation, independent of coordinates — so $d^2=0$ holds and the Betti numbers $b_k=\dim\ker L_k$ (a metric-independent dimension, by the Hodge theorem) are computed by exact integer rank on *any* simplicial mesh, regular or not, rational or not.
- *The metric is irreducibly irrational for arbitrary geometry.* The Hodge star, the mass matrices, the harmonic representatives, and the discrete solution depend on the cell geometry, and the geometry is data: the vertices of a domain-fitted mesh lie wherever the boundary is (rational points on a sphere or a CAD surface are exceptional), so Theorem 4.3's hypothesis fails cell-by-cell and these objects become floating-point.

One therefore cannot "regularize" an arbitrary mesh into the exact regime; the available moves and their costs are:

1. *Regularize the reference element only* — free and real: a regular reference simplex carries the exact rational reference matrices of Theorem 4.3, removing reference-cell quadrature error. It does **not** make the *assembled* matrices exact, because the affine map to each physical cell carries that cell's generically irrational Jacobian. The reference cell is a choice; the physical cell's shape is not.
2. *Snap vertices to a rational lattice* — generally a poor trade: it replaces an $O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}})$ quadrature error with an $O(\text{snap distance})$ *geometric* error (a consistency/variational crime — the discrete problem now lives on a perturbed domain) and can collapse cells into slivers, degrading the shape-regularity constant on which stability depends. It is justified only when the lattice spacing *is* the discretization scale, i.e. when the IVM is itself the mesh.
3. *Use the IVM as the mesh* — exact in the interior (rational cells throughout the octet-truss) and inexact only in the boundary skin, where cells are cut by the true domain boundary; advantageous when the bulk carries the topology or physics of interest.
4. *Carry algebraic coordinates exactly* — when the vertices lie in a fixed number field ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ for octet geometry, $\mathbb{Q}(\varphi)$ for icosahedral, rational points on a quadric), exact arithmetic in that field preserves combinatorial correctness (exact orientation determinants and ranks) *without perturbing the geometry* [Thomson, §11.6]; it helps precisely to the extent the geometry is algebraic.

None of this is required for the method to function: the Arnold–Falk–Winther stability and convergence theory is metric-independent and holds on shape-regular families of irregular simplices [Arnold–Falk–Winther]. The exact-arithmetic regime of §4.3 is thus a *dividend* available on lattice-commensurate geometry (Quadray/IVM, crystalline, periodic), where it makes the Hodge-harmonic computation combinatorially decidable rather than thresholded — not a precondition for FEEC elsewhere. The irrationality lives in the embedding, never in the complex; metric data can be made exact exactly insofar as the geometry is lattice-commensurate, and no further.

## 5. The topological arrow: $S+A$ on a complex

Paper 01b §7.3 split a field's Jacobian $DF=S+A$ into a symmetric (curvature/source) part with $\operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{div}F$ and an antisymmetric (current/vorticity) part $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$. On a simplicial *complex* this becomes the discrete Hodge decomposition, and its harmonic sector is a topological invariant.

**Theorem 5.1 (Arrow $=b_1$).** *On a simplicial complex, $C^1=\operatorname{im}d_0\oplus\operatorname{im}d_1^\top\oplus\ker L_1$ with $L_1=d_0d_0^\top+d_1^\top d_1$, the three sectors mutually orthogonal. Under the identification of Paper 01b: $\operatorname{im}d_0=$ exact $=$ gradient (curvature, $A=0$); $\operatorname{im}d_1^\top=$ coexact $=$ curl (current, $\operatorname{tr}S=0$); $\ker L_1=$ harmonic. The harmonic current sector has dimension $b_1$ (the first Betti number) — the topologically protected arrow. Certified across complexes with $b_1\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ in Appendix A.*

**Self-adjoint blindness.** A symmetric instrument $\langle x,Tx\rangle$ reads only the symmetric ($S$, exact/curvature) sector and is identically blind to an antisymmetric current installed on the harmonic sector; a two-sided predictor $\tfrac12(T-T^\top)$ recovers it (certified). This is the reversibility blind spot on the frame: the arrow of time is the harmonic $b_1$, invisible to any self-adjoint probe. On the frame's own $k$-skeletons the count is explicit, $b_k=\binom{N-1}{k+1}$ — for $K_4$, $b_1=3$ independent currents.

## 6. The orientation gauge

Over a mesh of varying frames, what is the gauge curvature $\mathcal F=dA$? The answer separates a trivial continuous part from a genuine discrete one.

**Theorem 6.1 (Abelian flatness).** *The continuous $\mathbb{R}\mathbf 1$ connection (shift along the gauge line) has $\mathcal F=dA\equiv0$ and trivial holonomy: the relabeling group $S_4$ fixes $\mathbf 1$ ($P_\sigma\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$ for all $24$ $\sigma$), and the mean-subtraction section is global. No continuous gauge curvature exists.*

**Theorem 6.2 (Discrete orientation field).** *The genuine gauge content is the orientation $\mathbb{Z}/2=S_4/A_4$ lattice field, with edge variable $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_e)$. The plaquette holonomy $\mathcal F_f=\prod_{e\in\partial f}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_e)\in\{\pm1\}$ is gauge-invariant under cell-orientation flips and can equal $-1$ — an orientation defect (disclination) no local change removes; the global loop holonomy is the first Stiefel–Whitney class $w_1$ [Whitney, Wilson]. By Proposition 01.2.2, transporting the cross product $\widetilde K$ around a net-odd loop returns it negated, $\widetilde K\mapsto-\widetilde K$: an orientation defect is exactly where Paper 01's oriented algebra cannot be globally trivialized. The frame bundle is a flat $S_4$ bundle classified by its holonomy $\pi_1\to S_4$. Certified in Appendix A.*

So the gauge curvature of the simplicial frame is topological, not differential: no local field strength, but orientation defects ($\mathcal F_f=-1$) and the global $w_1$. This is the formal version of §01.2.2.1's analogy — a flat $S_4$ / orientation-$\mathbb{Z}/2$ gauge theory whose only nonzero invariant is the orientation datum Paper 01 already named essential.

## 7. The triple exceptionality of $N=4$

Each layer re-detects the exceptional status of $N=4$, all tracing to the single coincidence $d=N-1=3$:

- **Algebra (Paper 01).** $\dim\mathfrak{so}(3)=3$ equals the dimension of $H$, so one vector axis parameterizes every rotation — the binary cross product exists only at $N=4$.
- **Hodge star (§2/§6).** The opposite-edge involution $\star_E$ (Theorem 01b.10.1) is an endomorphism of the edge space iff an edge's complementary face is again an edge, i.e. $N-3=1$, i.e. $N=4$ — the Hodge-combinatorial twin of $\dim\mathfrak{so}(3)=3$.
- **Cubic (Paper III).** The cross-product-square realization $I+K(\tau_i)^2$ of the componentwise product is a $d=3$ luxury; the metric (order $2$) exists for all $N$, the cubic's cross-product factorization does not.
- **Topology (§5).** The graph $K_4$ has $b_1=\binom{3}{2}=3=\dim\mathfrak{so}(3)$ independent loops — three tetrahedral currents matching the three rotation axes.

The single-axis cross product, the edge-self-dual Hodge star, the cross-product-square cubic, and the loop-count$=$rotation-axis coincidence are four faces of $d=3$.

## 8. $\widetilde K$: one object, five roles

The cyclic-difference matrix $\widetilde K$ is the spine. It is, successively:

1. the $\mathfrak{so}(3)$/cross-product generator and rotation seed (Paper 01);
2. the curl symbol, $\operatorname{curl}=\widetilde K(\operatorname{grad})$ (Paper 01b §5);
3. the opposite-edge Hodge dual of the coboundary, $\widetilde K(u)=\star_E(d_0u)$ on $K_4$ (Theorem 01b.10.1);
4. the antisymmetric$\leftrightarrow$current dictionary, $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$, and the orientation transporter (§6);
5. the carrier of the cross-product-square realization $I+K(\tau_i)^2$, hence the frozen geometry of the moving Amari–Chentsov cubic (Paper III).

One matrix is the rotation generator, the curl, the coboundary's Hodge dual, the arrow dictionary, and the cumulant cubic's skeleton — which is why the four layers of the series cohere.

## 9. Conclusion

At second order the simplicial frame is the complete-graph Laplacian $NI-J$ — metric, Fisher information, and harmonic flux at once. Above second order the symmetric (cumulant, order) and antisymmetric (Hodge, degree) towers are the two axes of the FEEC bicomplex $P_n\Lambda^k$, the universal eigenvalue $N$ living on the degree axis and the cumulants growing on the order axis. The arrow of time is the harmonic $b_1$, the gauge curvature is the discrete orientation $w_1$, and $N=4$'s exceptionality is the coincidence $d=3$ seen four ways. Everything the series builds sorts into the symmetric and antisymmetric halves of one object, with $\widetilde K$ the dictionary between them.

Open problems, in convergence-friendly order:

- **E1.** The general-$N$ Hodge/curl layer: off $N=4$ the "current" lives in $C^{N-3}$; write the explicit $(N-3)$-input simple-blade generator and its $C^1\to C^{N-3}$ coboundary realization.
- **E2.** The weighted Matrix–Tree dictionary for deformed Gram matrices — the only place a number-theoretic Gram weighting could enter (flagged forward-only; the finite complete-complex spectrum supports no archimedean/apex structure by itself). The *unweighted* baseline is settled by §4.3: on the rational-coordinate IVM the element-matrix assembly is exactly rational (Theorem 4.3, Remark 4.5); the open part is whether a number-theoretic *weighting* of the Gram/edge data carries spectral content.
- **E3.** The higher Hodge Laplacians $L_k$ ($k\ge2$) as higher "currents," $b_k=\binom{N-1}{k+1}$, and whether the degree axis past $k=1$ has a reading on the cumulant order axis past $n=3$.
