## Abstract

**Situation.** T11 proved finite apex vertex rules and conductor-fusion boundaries. T12 proved that apex order towers are finite spectral objects. T13 proved exact simplicial renormalization: projection creates boundary sources, integrating out boundaries produces finite corrections, and the finite RG is the closure operator generated by conductor fusion. The next question is geometric: why do nonlinear curvature, renormalization corrections, and Noether defects keep appearing as if they are different phenomena?

**Gap.** The earlier papers identify the algebra of projection, but not yet the geometry of projection. In probability coordinates, simplex dynamics is nonlinear. In information geometry, curvature and connection defects appear as intrinsic geometric facts. In effective-action language, omitted sectors return as Schur and cumulant corrections. In mechanics, conservation laws fail under truncation. These look like separate effects until the lifted ledger/apex layer is used as the ambient flat space.

**Action.** We prove six structure theorems. First, the **Linear Lift Theorem**: nonlinear simplex dynamics is the normalized shadow of linear multiplication/transport in the ledger/apex algebra. Second, the **Curvature as Projection-Commutator Theorem**: projected curvature is the commutator defect between a projection and a flat lifted connection. Third, the **Boundary Curvature Theorem**: for conductor-packet projections, the universal support of curvature is exactly the conductor-fusion boundary. Fourth, the **Schur Curvature Correction Theorem**: integrating out boundary packets renormalizes curvature by Schur and connected cumulant contractions. Fifth, the **Cubic Noether Defect Theorem**: inherited cubic conservation laws persist exactly on fusion-closed sectors; failure is boundary leakage. Sixth, the **Curvature-Renormalization-Noether Equivalence Theorem**: projected curvature, projection noncommutation, boundary leakage, renormalization source, beta source, and Noether defect are the same obstruction in different languages.

**Result.** The central compression is
\[
\boxed{
\text{curvature}
=
\text{projection noncommutation}
=
\text{boundary leakage}
=
\text{renormalization source}
=
\text{Noether defect}.
}
\]
Thus curvature is not primitive in the finite apex setting. It is the cost of observing a flat lifted algebra through a nonclosed retained sector.

**Harvest.** T14 explains why the "lifting floor" method works. Lift to the ledger/apex algebra and the dynamics becomes linear or multiplicative. Project back to a sector and nonlinearity appears. If the sector is fusion-closed, the projection is flat. If it is not closed, curvature is the visible trace of missing boundary packets.

**Keywords:** curvature linearization, projected flatness, ledger lift, apex algebra, projection commutator, conductor boundary, Schur complement, Noether defect, finite information geometry, simplicial mechanics, fusion closure.

## 1. Why curvature linearizes upstairs

The finite simplex is nonlinear because probabilities are normalized:
\[
\sum_sp_s=1.
\]
But normalization is a quotient operation. If one works in the positive ledger \(q\), many nonlinear probability flows become linear multiplication flows. For example, the escort path
\[
p_s(\beta)=\frac{p_s(0)e^{\beta h_s}}{\sum_up_u(0)e^{\beta h_u}}
\]
is nonlinear in \(p\), but its lift
\[
q_s(\beta)=p_s(0)e^{\beta h_s}
\]
solves a linear diagonal equation.

This is the basic T14 move:
\[
\text{lift}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{linear transport},
\]
\[
\text{project/normalize}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{nonlinearity and curvature}.
\]
T14 then asks: when is the projected geometry still flat? The answer is now visible from T11-T13:
\[
\boxed{
\text{flat after projection}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{the retained sector is fusion-closed}.
}
\]

## 2. Linear lift

### Theorem 1. Linear Lift Theorem

Let \(p(\beta)\in\Delta^\circ(\Omega)\) be the escort flow driven by an observable \(h:\Omega\to\mathbb R\):
\[
p_s(\beta)=\frac{p_s(0)e^{\beta h_s}}{\sum_up_u(0)e^{\beta h_u}}.
\]
Then \(p(\beta)\) satisfies the nonlinear replicator equation
\[
\frac{d}{d\beta}p_s(\beta)
=
p_s(\beta)\big(h_s-\mathbb E_{p(\beta)}[h]\big).
\]
But its unnormalized ledger lift
\[
q_s(\beta)=p_s(0)e^{\beta h_s}
\]
satisfies the linear equation
\[
\frac{d}{d\beta}q(\beta)=M_hq(\beta),
\qquad
(M_hq)_s=h_sq_s.
\]
Thus the nonlinear simplex flow is linear multiplication upstairs followed by normalization downstairs:
\[
p(\beta)=\mathcal N(q(\beta)).
\]
Equivalently, in centered log-ledger coordinates,
\[
y(\beta)=y(0)+\beta\left(h-\frac1{|\Omega|}\sum_sh_s\,\mathbf 1\right),
\]
so the log-ledger lift is affine-linear.

**Proof.** Define
\[
q_s(\beta)=p_s(0)e^{\beta h_s}.
\]
Then
\[
\frac{d}{d\beta}q_s(\beta)=h_sq_s(\beta),
\]
which is the linear equation \(\dot q=M_hq\).

Now normalize:
\[
p_s(\beta)=\frac{q_s(\beta)}{Z(\beta)},
\qquad
Z(\beta)=\sum_uq_u(\beta).
\]
Differentiating,
\[
\dot p_s
=
\frac{\dot q_sZ-q_s\dot Z}{Z^2}.
\]
Since \(\dot q_s=h_sq_s\),
\[
\dot Z=\sum_uh_uq_u.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dot p_s
=
\frac{h_sq_s}{Z}
-
\frac{q_s}{Z}\frac{\sum_uh_uq_u}{Z}.
\]
Using \(p_s=q_s/Z\) and
\[
\frac{\sum_uh_uq_u}{Z}=\sum_uh_up_u=\mathbb E_p[h],
\]
we get
\[
\dot p_s=p_s(h_s-\mathbb E_p[h]).
\]

For the log-ledger statement,
\[
\log q_s(\beta)=\log p_s(0)+\beta h_s.
\]
Centering by subtracting the arithmetic mean gives
\[
y(\beta)=y(0)+\beta\left(h-\frac1{|\Omega|}\sum_sh_s\,\mathbf 1\right).
\]
Thus the apparent nonlinearity is produced by normalization/projectivization; the lifted motion is linear. \(\square\)

## 3. Curvature as projection commutator

### Theorem 2. Curvature as Projection-Commutator Theorem

Let \(E=M\times V\) carry a flat lifted connection \(\nabla\), and let
\[
\Pi:M\to\operatorname{End}(V),
\qquad
\Pi^2=\Pi,
\]
be a smooth projection. Define the projected connection on \(E_S=\operatorname{im}\Pi\) by
\[
\nabla^\Pi_Xs=\Pi\nabla_Xs.
\]
Then the curvature of \(\nabla^\Pi\) is
\[
R^\Pi(X,Y)
=
\Pi\left(
[\nabla_X,\Pi][\nabla_Y,\Pi]
-
[\nabla_Y,\Pi][\nabla_X,\Pi]
\right)\Pi.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
R^\Pi=\Pi\,d\Pi\wedge d\Pi\,\Pi.
\]
Thus, if the projection commutes with lifted flat transport,
\[
[\nabla_X,\Pi]=0
\]
for all \(X\), then the projected curvature vanishes. Curvature is the obstruction to projection commuting with the lifted flat connection.

**Proof.** Let \(s\in\Gamma(E_S)\), so \(\Pi s=s\). The curvature of the projected connection is
\[
R^\Pi(X,Y)s
=
\nabla^\Pi_X\nabla^\Pi_Ys
-
\nabla^\Pi_Y\nabla^\Pi_Xs
-
\nabla^\Pi_{[X,Y]}s.
\]
Substituting \(\nabla^\Pi_Xs=\Pi\nabla_Xs\),
\[
R^\Pi(X,Y)s
=
\Pi\nabla_X(\Pi\nabla_Ys)
-
\Pi\nabla_Y(\Pi\nabla_Xs)
-
\Pi\nabla_{[X,Y]}s.
\]
By the Leibniz rule,
\[
\nabla_X(\Pi\nabla_Ys)
=
(\nabla_X\Pi)\nabla_Ys+\Pi\nabla_X\nabla_Ys.
\]
Hence
\[
R^\Pi(X,Y)s
=
\Pi(\nabla_X\Pi)\nabla_Ys
-
\Pi(\nabla_Y\Pi)\nabla_Xs
+
\Pi(\nabla_X\nabla_Y-\nabla_Y\nabla_X-\nabla_{[X,Y]})s.
\]
Since \(\nabla\) is flat, the final parenthesized term vanishes.

Differentiate \(\Pi s=s\):
\[
(\nabla_X\Pi)s+\Pi\nabla_Xs=\nabla_Xs.
\]
Thus
\[
(1-\Pi)\nabla_Xs=(\nabla_X\Pi)s.
\]
Also differentiating \(\Pi^2=\Pi\) gives
\[
\Pi(\nabla_X\Pi)\Pi=0.
\]
Therefore only the off-sector part of \(\nabla_Xs\) contributes:
\[
\Pi(\nabla_X\Pi)\nabla_Ys
=
\Pi(\nabla_X\Pi)(\nabla_Y\Pi)s,
\]
and similarly with \(X,Y\) reversed. Thus
\[
R^\Pi(X,Y)s
=
\Pi\left(
(\nabla_X\Pi)(\nabla_Y\Pi)
-
(\nabla_Y\Pi)(\nabla_X\Pi)
\right)s.
\]
Since \([\nabla_X,\Pi]=\nabla_X\Pi\) as an endomorphism-valued derivative, the claimed formula follows. \(\square\)

The vertex version has the same form. If \(V\) is the lifted interaction, then
\[
[\Pi V,\Pi V]-\Pi[V,V]
\]
measures the defect between composing after projection and projecting after composition.

## 4. Boundary curvature

### Theorem 3. Boundary Curvature Theorem

Fix an interaction order \(n\). Let
\[
H_S=\bigoplus_{d\in S}H_d
\]
be a retained conductor-packet sector. Define the order-\(n\) projected curvature defect by
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(n)}
=
(1-\Pi_S-\Pi_1)V_n\big|_{H_S^{\otimes n}}.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(n)}(H_S,\ldots,H_S)
\subseteq
\bigoplus_{d\in\partial_nS}H_d.
\]
Moreover, this support is sharp: every packet \(H_d\) with \(d\in\partial_nS\) occurs for some retained character inputs. Thus the universal support of projected curvature is exactly the conductor-fusion boundary.

**Proof.** Take inputs \(u_i\in H_S\). Each has a unique packet expansion
\[
u_i=\sum_{d_i\in S}u_{i,d_i},
\qquad
u_{i,d_i}\in H_{d_i}.
\]
By multilinearity,
\[
V_n(u_1,\ldots,u_n)
=
\sum_{d_1,\ldots,d_n\in S}
V_n(u_{1,d_1},\ldots,u_{n,d_n}).
\]
It is enough to analyze a product of packet elements. Each \(H_d\) is spanned by characters \(\chi_a\) with \(\operatorname{cond}(a)=d\). For basis characters,
\[
\chi_{a_1}\cdots\chi_{a_n}
=
\chi_{a_1+\cdots+a_n}.
\]
Thus the output conductor is
\[
\operatorname{cond}(a_1+\cdots+a_n).
\]
As the input conductors range over \(S\), all possible output conductors are precisely \(\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)\). Therefore
\[
V_n(H_S,\ldots,H_S)
\subseteq
\bigoplus_{e\in\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)}H_e.
\]

The projected theory keeps packets in \(S\), and \(\Pi_1\) removes the trivial constant packet. Hence
\[
(1-\Pi_S-\Pi_1)V_n(H_S,\ldots,H_S)
\subseteq
\bigoplus_{d\in\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)\setminus(S\cup\{1\})}H_d.
\]
By definition, this set is \(\partial_nS\).

For sharpness, let \(d\in\partial_nS\). Then there exist indices \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\) with \(\operatorname{cond}(a_i)\in S\) and
\[
\operatorname{cond}(a_1+\cdots+a_n)=d.
\]
Thus
\[
V_n(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\chi_{a_n})
=
\chi_{a_1+\cdots+a_n}\in H_d.
\]
Since \(d\notin S\cup\{1\}\), this component survives in \(\mathcal R_S^{(n)}\). Individual linear combinations may cancel, but no smaller universal support works. \(\square\)

## 5. Schur curvature correction

### Theorem 4. Schur Curvature Correction Theorem

Let
\[
H=H_S\oplus H_B
\]
split a finite apex sector into retained packets and boundary packets. Suppose the quadratic action kernel is
\[
Q=
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_{SS}&Q_{SB}\\
Q_{BS}&Q_{BB}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q_{BB}\text{ invertible}.
\]
Then integrating out \(H_B\) renormalizes the retained quadratic curvature kernel by the Schur correction
\[
Q^{\mathrm{eff}}_S
=
Q_{SS}-Q_{SB}Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}.
\]
At higher order, every induced retained curvature coefficient is a finite sum of connected cumulant contractions whose internal legs lie in \(H_B\) and whose external legs lie in \(H_S\).

**Proof.** Write
\[
\phi=\phi_S+\phi_B,
\qquad
\phi_S\in H_S,\quad \phi_B\in H_B.
\]
The quadratic action is
\[
\Gamma_2(\phi_S,\phi_B)
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}\phi_S\\\phi_B\end{pmatrix}^T
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_{SS}&Q_{SB}\\
Q_{BS}&Q_{BB}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\phi_S\\\phi_B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Expanding,
\[
\Gamma_2
=
\frac12\phi_S^TQ_{SS}\phi_S
+\phi_S^TQ_{SB}\phi_B
+\frac12\phi_B^TQ_{BB}\phi_B.
\]
Complete the square:
\[
\Gamma_2
=
\frac12\phi_S^T
\left(Q_{SS}-Q_{SB}Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}\right)\phi_S
+
\frac12
(\phi_B+Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}\phi_S)^T
Q_{BB}
(\phi_B+Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}\phi_S).
\]
Integrating out \(\phi_B\), or substituting the stationary value
\[
\phi_B^*=-Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}\phi_S,
\]
leaves the effective retained quadratic kernel
\[
Q^{\mathrm{eff}}_S
=
Q_{SS}-Q_{SB}Q_{BB}^{-1}Q_{BS}.
\]
The correction is finite propagation through the boundary:
\[
S\to B\to S.
\]

For higher terms, write
\[
\Gamma(\phi_S,\phi_B)
=
\Gamma_2(\phi_S,\phi_B)
+
\sum_{m\ge3}\frac1{m!}
\Gamma_m(\phi_S+\phi_B,\ldots,\phi_S+\phi_B).
\]
The exact retained effective action is
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff},S}(\phi_S)
=
-\log\int_{H_B}e^{-\Gamma(\phi_S,\phi_B)}\,d\phi_B,
\]
or, in the finite cumulant formulation,
\[
K_{\mathrm{eff},S}(J_S)=K(J_S,0).
\]
Expanding the logarithm selects connected contractions. Because the packet set is finite, every fixed-order coefficient contains only finitely many packet assignments. By Theorem 3, nonclosed curvature components are supported in \(H_B\). Hence every induced retained coefficient is a finite connected contraction with external legs in \(H_S\) and internal legs in \(H_B\). At quadratic order, this is exactly the Schur term above. \(\square\)

## 6. Cubic Noether defect

### Theorem 5. Cubic Noether Defect Theorem

Let
\[
H=\bigoplus_{d\mid N}H_d
\]
be the conductor-packet decomposition, and let
\[
H_S=\bigoplus_{d\in S}H_d
\]
be a retained sector. Let \(C\) be a full cubic apex tensor with character-selection rule
\[
C(\chi_a,\chi_b,\chi_c)\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a+b+c\equiv0\pmod N.
\]
Let \(A\) be an infinitesimal symmetry of the full cubic theory:
\[
C(Au,v,w)+C(u,Av,w)+C(u,v,Aw)=0
\]
for all \(u,v,w\in H\). Assume \(A\) is inherited by the retained sector, meaning \(A(H_S)\subseteq H_S\oplus H_1\) when \(S\) is fusion-closed, and otherwise its projected action is measured by \(A_S=\Pi_SA\).

Then the retained cubic Noether identity is exact for all retained fields if \(S\) is cubic-fusion-closed:
\[
\operatorname{Fuse}_3(S)\subseteq S\cup\{1\}.
\]
If \(S\) is not cubic-fusion-closed, the Noether defect is supported on the cubic conductor boundary
\[
\partial_3S
=
\operatorname{Fuse}_3(S)\setminus(S\cup\{1\}).
\]
An individual conserved quantity may still persist exactly if its Noether covector annihilates the boundary leakage.

**Proof.** Let
\[
\Pi_\partial=1-\Pi_S-\Pi_1.
\]
For retained fields \(u,v,w\in H_S\), the cubic vertex decomposes as
\[
V_3(u,v,w)
=
\Pi_SV_3(u,v,w)
+
\Pi_1V_3(u,v,w)
+
\Pi_\partial V_3(u,v,w).
\]
By Theorem 3 with \(n=3\),
\[
\Pi_\partial V_3(H_S,H_S,H_S)
\subseteq
\bigoplus_{d\in\partial_3S}H_d.
\]

If \(S\) is cubic-fusion-closed, then \(\partial_3S=\varnothing\). Therefore
\[
V_3(H_S,H_S,H_S)\subseteq H_S\oplus H_1.
\]
Ignoring the trivial gauge/mean packet, the cubic tensor restricts to a closed retained sector. Since \(A\) is a symmetry of the full tensor and is inherited on the retained sector, the identity
\[
C(Au,v,w)+C(u,Av,w)+C(u,v,Aw)=0
\]
holds after restriction to retained fields. Thus the retained Noether law persists.

If \(S\) is not cubic-fusion-closed, then \(\partial_3S\ne\varnothing\). By sharpness of Theorem 3, there exist retained character inputs whose cubic product has a nonzero component in some \(H_d\) with \(d\in\partial_3S\). The full theory satisfies the Noether identity because that boundary component is present. The projected retained theory discards it. Therefore the projected Noether variation is the negative of the omitted boundary variation:
\[
\mathcal N_S(A;u,v,w)
=
-\mathcal N_\partial(A;u,v,w),
\]
where \(\mathcal N_\partial\) is computed using \(\Pi_\partial V_3\). Since \(\Pi_\partial V_3\) is supported on
\[
\bigoplus_{d\in\partial_3S}H_d,
\]
the Noether defect is supported on the same boundary. \(\square\)

Thus conservation fails only by what the projection omitted.

## 7. The equivalence theorem

### Theorem 6. Curvature-Renormalization-Noether Equivalence Theorem

Fix an interaction order \(n\). Let
\[
H_S=\bigoplus_{d\in S}H_d
\]
be a retained sector with projection \(\Pi_S\), and let \(V_n\) be the \(n\)-leg apex vertex. Assume the lifted apex dynamics is flat/linear on \(H\), and all curvature, renormalization, beta, and Noether defects are measured after projection to \(H_S\).

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(S\) is \(n\)-fusion-closed:
\[
\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)\subseteq S\cup\{1\}.
\]
2. The vertex closes on the retained sector:
\[
V_n(H_S,\ldots,H_S)\subseteq H_S\oplus H_1.
\]
3. The projected curvature defect vanishes:
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(n)}=0.
\]
4. No boundary Schur/cumulant correction is universally forced.
5. Every inherited \(n\)-order Noether identity persists without universal boundary defect.
6. The discrete beta source vanishes for every enlargement \(S'\supseteq S\):
\[
\beta^{(n)}_{S\to S'}
=
(\Pi_{S'}-\Pi_S)V_n\big|_{H_S^{\otimes n}}
=0.
\]

When these conditions fail, all failures are supported on the same conductor-fusion boundary
\[
\partial_nS
=
\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)\setminus(S\cup\{1\}).
\]

**Proof.** Condition (1) means
\[
\operatorname{Fuse}_n(S)\subseteq S\cup\{1\}.
\]
By definition of conductor fusion, this is exactly the statement that products of \(n\) retained packet elements land in \(H_S\oplus H_1\). Hence
\[
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2).
\]

By Theorem 3,
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(n)}
=
(1-\Pi_S-\Pi_1)V_n\big|_{H_S^{\otimes n}}
\]
is supported on \(\partial_nS\), and this support is sharp. Thus
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(n)}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_nS=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\text{ is }n\text{-fusion-closed}.
\]
So
\[
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(3).
\]

By Theorem 4, integrating out boundary packets produces Schur corrections at quadratic order and connected cumulant contractions at higher order, with internal legs in the boundary. Such corrections are universally forced exactly when the boundary support is nonempty. Therefore no boundary Schur/cumulant correction is universally forced if and only if
\[
\partial_nS=\varnothing.
\]
Thus
\[
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(4).
\]

For Noether identities, Theorem 5 proves the cubic case, and the same \(n\)-leg argument applies to \(n\)-order inherited identities: the full identity holds upstairs, while the retained projected identity can fail only by the omitted boundary variation. Therefore every inherited \(n\)-order identity persists without universal boundary defect if and only if
\[
\partial_nS=\varnothing.
\]
Thus
\[
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(5).
\]

Finally, define the beta source for \(S\subseteq S'\) by
\[
\beta^{(n)}_{S\to S'}
=
(\Pi_{S'}-\Pi_S)V_n\big|_{H_S^{\otimes n}}.
\]
By T13's beta theorem,
\[
\beta^{(n)}_{S\to S'}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_nS\cap(S'\setminus S)=\varnothing.
\]
If the beta source vanishes for every enlargement, choose
\[
S'=S\cup\partial_nS.
\]
Then vanishing implies \(\partial_nS=\varnothing\). Conversely, if \(\partial_nS=\varnothing\), the beta source vanishes for every enlargement. Hence
\[
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(6).
\]

The same proof identifies the common support when the conditions fail. Curvature defect, Schur/cumulant correction, Noether defect, and beta source all use the same omitted component:
\[
(1-\Pi_S-\Pi_1)V_n\big|_{H_S^{\otimes n}},
\]
whose universal support is \(\partial_nS\). Therefore
\[
\text{curvature}
=
\text{projection noncommutation}
=
\text{boundary leakage}
=
\text{renormalization source}
=
\text{Noether defect}
\]
inside the finite apex setting. \(\square\)

## 8. Worked example: \(N=6\) again

For \(G=\mathbb Z/6\), the nontrivial conductor packets are
\[
H_2=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_3\},
\]
\[
H_3=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_2,\chi_4\},
\]
\[
H_6=\operatorname{span}\{\chi_1,\chi_5\}.
\]
Let
\[
S=\{2,3\}.
\]
From T13,
\[
\partial_3S=\{6\}.
\]
Thus the cubic curvature defect of the retained sector \(H_2\oplus H_3\) is supported exactly on \(H_6\):
\[
\mathcal R_S^{(3)}(H_S,H_S,H_S)\subseteq H_6.
\]
The support is sharp because
\[
\chi_3\chi_2\chi_2=\chi_1\in H_6.
\]

This example now has four readings:

1. **Projection reading.** \(H_2\oplus H_3\) is not cubic-closed.
2. **Curvature reading.** The projected cubic geometry curves into \(H_6\).
