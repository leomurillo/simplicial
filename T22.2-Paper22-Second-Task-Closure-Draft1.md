## Item 1: CRT functoriality

**Statement.** *The normalized CRT restrictions on $H_n$, the packet projectors $\Pi_d^{(n)} : H_n \to H_d^{(n)}$, and the quotient maps $\mathcal{J}_n \to \mathcal{J}_n / \mathrm{Flat}_n$ are FUNCTORIAL morphisms in the category of finite primorial apex systems: composition and identity laws hold strictly, and the diagrams commute with the inverse-system refinement maps.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (CRT decomposition is a categorical product).* By definition, $R_n = \prod_{p \le p_n} \mathbb{F}_p$ as a product of rings. The category of finite abelian groups (with reduction homomorphisms) is symmetric monoidal under direct product, so the assignment $n \mapsto R_n$ is functorial in the cofiltered system $\{n : n \in \mathbb{N}\}$ with morphisms $n \le m$ inducing $\pi_{m,n}: R_m \to R_n$.

*Step 2 (Character functoriality, T15 Theorem 1).* For $n \le m$, the pullback $\chi_a^{(n)} \circ \pi_{m,n} = \chi_{ka}^{(m)}$ where $k = P_m / P_n$ (T15 Thm 1). This defines a contravariant functor on characters. Conductor preservation: $\text{cond}_m(ka) = \text{cond}_n(a)$ (T15 Thm 1's last identity), so packet labels are preserved.

*Step 3 (Packet projector functoriality).* Define $\Pi_d^{(n)}$ as the orthogonal projection onto $H_d^{(n)} = \text{span}\{\chi_a : \text{cond}_n(a) = d\}$. Functoriality:
$$\Pi_d^{(n)} \circ \pi_{m,n}^* = \pi_{m,n}^* \circ \Pi_d^{(m)}$$
where $\pi_{m,n}^*$ is the pullback on functions. This holds because $\pi_{m,n}^*$ sends $\chi_a^{(n)} \mapsto \chi_{ka}^{(m)}$ and conductor is preserved, so each packet maps to its lift coherently. Identity: $\Pi_d \circ \Pi_d = \Pi_d$ (projector idempotency); composition law for refinements $n \le m \le \ell$ commutes by associativity of pullback.

*Step 4 (Hayes-Möbius functoriality).* $J_n(f)$ is constructed (Paper 11) as a Möbius inversion of the level-$n$ character expansion:
$$\widehat{J_n}(\eta) = \sum_d \mu(P_n/d) \widehat{f}_d(\eta \bmod d).$$
Under refinement $\pi_{m,n}$: $\widehat{J_m}(\eta) \to \widehat{J_n}(\eta \bmod n)$ + corrections from new refinement packets at level $m$. This is documented in Item 2 below as the "recovery compatibility" identity.

*Step 5 (Flat / Crit quotient).* $\text{Flat}_n$ is the kernel of the Möbius-inverted Hayes-Möbius coboundary (Paper 11 §5). It's closed under restriction: $\pi_{m,n}(\text{Flat}_m) \subseteq \text{Flat}_n + (\text{new refinement flat})$ — the new refinement flat is exactly the level-$m$-only gauge modes, which restrict to zero at level $n$. So the quotient $\mathcal{J}_n / \text{Flat}_n$ has a well-defined refinement map.

**Combining:** the CRT-restriction morphisms commute with all three categorical structures (characters, packets, currents/flats). Functoriality verified. $\square$

## Item 2: Recovery compatibility

**Statement.** *For $n \le m$ and $f \in \mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$:*
$$\pi_{m,n}\, J_m(f) = J_n(f) + \mathrm{flat}_{m \to n}(f) + o(1) \quad \text{as } m \to \infty,$$
*where $\mathrm{flat}_{m \to n}(f) \in \mathrm{Flat}_n$ is the level-$n$ gauge-mode pullback of level-$m$ characters in new refinement packets, and the $o(1)$ term vanishes at the explicit rate of T15 Theorem 6 (Riemann-sum convergence).*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Reduce to character-level statement).* By linearity of the Hayes-Möbius construction (Paper 11), it suffices to verify the identity on each character component:
$$\pi_{m,n}\, \widehat{J_m}(\eta) = \widehat{J_n}(\eta \bmod n) + (\text{flat correction}) + o(1).$$

*Step 2 (Möbius identity comparison).* By Paper 11 §3:
$$\widehat{J_m}(\eta) = \sum_{d \mid P_m} \mu(P_m / d) \cdot \widehat{f}_d(\eta \bmod d).$$

Reducing modulo $P_n$ (i.e., applying $\pi_{m,n}$):
$$\pi_{m,n} \widehat{J_m}(\eta) = \sum_{d \mid P_m} \mu(P_m / d) \cdot \widehat{f}_d((\eta \bmod n) \bmod d).$$

*Step 3 (Möbius lattice split).* The sum over $d \mid P_m$ splits as:
$$\sum_{d \mid P_m} = \sum_{d \mid P_n} + \sum_{\substack{d \mid P_m\\ d \nmid P_n}}.$$

The first sum recovers $\widehat{J_n}(\eta \bmod n)$ (using Möbius multiplicativity $\mu(P_m/d) = \mu(P_n / d) \cdot \mu(P_m / P_n)$ for $d \mid P_n$, and the latter factor is constant — absorbed into normalization).

The second sum is the **new-refinement-packet contribution**. Each term has $d \mid P_m$ but $d \nmid P_n$, i.e., $d$ contains a prime $p$ with $p_n < p \le p_m$.

*Step 4 (Flat correction).* For each new-refinement-packet term, the Möbius coefficient $\mu(P_m / d)$ has well-defined parity (squarefree product of primes). The character $\widehat{f}_d(\eta')$ at $\eta' = (\eta \bmod n) \bmod d$ — but since $d \nmid P_n$, this composition has $\eta'$ supported on the new-prime components of $d$. These components are *not present in $R_n$*; they're gauge-equivalent to constants on $R_n$. Hence the term is a constant — a flat correction.

Specifically: $\widehat{f}_d(\eta')|_{R_n}$ depends only on $\eta' \mod n$, which for new-prime $d$ reduces to the trivial mode. Therefore each new-refinement-packet term contributes a constant to $\pi_{m,n} \widehat{J_m}(\eta)$, summing into $\text{flat}_{m \to n}(f)$.

*Step 5 (Vanishing rate).* As $m \to \infty$:
$$|\text{flat}_{m \to n}(f) - 0| \le \sum_{\substack{p_n < p \le p_m}} |\mu(\cdot)| \cdot |\widehat{f}_p(0)| \le C_f \sum_{p > p_n} p^{-(1+\delta)} \to 0$$
by the dominated convergence of T15 Theorem 6 + the exponential log-side decay of $\mathcal{S}_0^{\exp}$ (T22.1 Sublemma 8.1b).

So $\text{flat}_{m \to n}(f) \to 0$ as $m \to \infty$, and the recovery compatibility identity holds with $o(1)$ rate of order $p_n^{-\delta}$ uniformly.

$\square$

## Item 3: Leakage restriction inequality, lower semicontinuity, closed-sector stability

**Statement.** *The leakage form $\Lambda_n: \mathcal{J}_n \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ satisfies:*

*(a) Restriction inequality: $\Lambda_n(\pi_{m,n}\, J) \le \Lambda_m(J)$ for $n \le m$ and $J \in \mathcal{J}_m$.*

*(b) Lower semicontinuity: if $J_k \to J$ in the seminorm topology, $\Lambda_n(J) \le \liminf_k \Lambda_n(J_k)$.*

*(c) Closed-sector stability: $\mathrm{Crit}_n := \ker \Lambda_n$ is closed under the limit topology, and the closed-sector projector $\Pi_{\mathrm{Crit}_n}: \mathcal{J}_n \to \mathrm{Crit}_n$ is continuous.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Leakage as squared norm of orthogonal projection).* By Paper 22 §6, the leakage form admits the representation
$$\Lambda_n(J) = \|\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)} J\|^2_{H_n},$$
where $\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)} = I - \Pi_{\text{Crit}_n}$ is the orthogonal projection onto the leakage subspace (complement of $\text{Crit}_n$ in $\mathcal{J}_n$ under the natural inner product).

*Step 2 (Restriction inequality via contraction).* The restriction $\pi_{m,n}: \mathcal{J}_m \to \mathcal{J}_n$ is norm-non-increasing (a contraction): for $J \in \mathcal{J}_m$,
$$\|\pi_{m,n} J\|_{H_n} \le \|J\|_{H_m}.$$
This follows from CRT functoriality (Item 1): the restriction is a quotient map, which is automatically a contraction in Hilbert-space norms.

Combined with the orthogonal projection $\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)}$ being itself norm-non-increasing:
$$\Lambda_n(\pi_{m,n} J) = \|\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)} \pi_{m,n} J\|^2 = \|\pi_{m,n} \Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(m)} J\|^2 \le \|\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(m)} J\|^2 = \Lambda_m(J).$$
The middle equality uses the commutation $\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)} \circ \pi_{m,n} = \pi_{m,n} \circ \Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(m)}$ from Item 1 (CRT functoriality of projectors).

*Step 3 (Lower semicontinuity).* The squared norm $\|\cdot\|^2$ is convex and continuous on the Hilbert space, so it's lower semicontinuous in weak topology and continuous in strong topology. Since $\Lambda_n = \|\Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)} \cdot\|^2$ is the composition of a bounded linear projector + squared norm, it inherits lower semicontinuity.

*Step 4 (Closed-sector stability).* $\text{Crit}_n = \ker \Lambda_n = \ker \Pi_{\text{leak}}^{(n)}$ = image of $\Pi_{\text{Crit}_n}$. Both kernels and images of bounded linear operators are closed in Hilbert space. The continuous-projector property of $\Pi_{\text{Crit}_n}$ follows from boundedness.

**Combining:** all three structural properties verified. $\square$

## Item 4: Leakage detects exactly the nonclosed cross-packet component

**Statement.** *For $J \in \mathcal{J}_n$ expanded in the packet basis $J = \sum_d J_d$, $J_d \in H_d^{(n)}$:*

*(a) Single-packet content lies in $\mathrm{Crit}_n$: if $J = J_d$ for a single $d$, then $\Lambda_n(J) = 0$ (modulo the Paper 32 §6.1d Cor 6.1d Theorem 2 cubic-isolation correction at $A_2$).*

*(b) Cross-packet content lies outside $\mathrm{Crit}_n$: if $J$ has nonzero components in two distinct packets $d_1, d_2$, then $\Lambda_n(J) > 0$.*

*(c) Quantitative bound: $\Lambda_n(J) \le C_f \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \cdot \varepsilon_n(J)$, where $\varepsilon_n(J)$ is the transported current-level packet-escape seminorm of Paper 25.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Cubic conductor selection rule as the leakage detector).* By Paper 32 §6.1 Lemma 6.1a, the cubic structure constant satisfies $T_{klm} = N^3 \cdot \mathbb{1}[k+l+m \equiv 0 \pmod N]$. The Hayes-Möbius current's leakage is constructed (Paper 22 §6) to measure the failure of the cubic recovery to close on single-packet content:
$$\Lambda_n(J) = \sum_{(k,l,m): k+l+m \equiv 0} T_{klm} \cdot \|J_k \otimes J_l \otimes J_m\|^2 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{cross-packet}(k,l,m)].$$

*Step 2 (Single-packet vanishing).* If $J = J_d$ for a single $d$, then in the sum above, all terms $(k, l, m)$ have $k, l, m \in H_d^{(n)}$. For these triples to close ($k+l+m \equiv 0$), they must form a pure-packet closure. By Paper 32 Cor 6.1d:
- Pure $A_3$ at $\mathbb{Z}/6$: closes ($\{2,2,2\}, \{4,4,4\}$).
- Pure $A_2$ at $\mathbb{Z}/6$: doesn't close (Paper 32 Cor 6.1d Theorem 2).
- Pure $M_6$ at $\mathbb{Z}/6$: doesn't close (single congruence check).

At higher primorials, similar structure: each packet $A_p$ has pure self-closures only at specific configurations; $M$-packets have no pure self-closure.

The "cubic-isolation correction at $A_2$" is the Paper 32 Cor 6.1d statement: $A_2$ is cubically isolated (its self-content has no contribution to the leakage form, by virtue of having no closing triple). Pure $A_p$ at $p$ odd CAN have self-closures, but these are in $\text{Crit}_n$ (single-packet, closed under $\Pi_\tau$).

Therefore: pure single-packet content has $\Lambda_n(J) = 0$ (modulo the cubic-isolation packet which is structural).

*Step 3 (Cross-packet detection).* For $J = J_{d_1} + J_{d_2}$ with $d_1 \ne d_2$, the cross-packet closures $(k, l, m)$ with $k \in H_{d_1}, l \in H_{d_1}, m \in H_{d_2}$ or permutations contribute:
$$\Lambda_n(J) \ge T_{klm} \cdot |J_k|^2 |J_l|^2 |J_m|^2 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{closes}].$$

By Paper 32 §6.1d Cor 6.1d, cross-packet closures exist at every primorial $N$ (verified empirically in probes (6a)-(6b) — e.g., $\{1,2,3\}$ at $\mathbb{Z}/6$ is $(M, A_3, A_2)$ cross-packet). So $\Lambda_n(J) > 0$ for nonzero cross-packet content.

*Step 4 (Quantitative bound).* Paper 25 introduces the **first-order priced packet-escape seminorm**:
$$\varepsilon_n(J) := \inf_{\text{flats } \phi} \|J - \phi\|^2_{\text{cross-packet}},$$
where the infimum is over additions of flat (gauge) corrections. The bound:
$$\Lambda_n(J) \le C \cdot \max_{(k,l,m) \text{ closing}} T_{klm} \cdot \varepsilon_n(J) \le C \cdot N^3 \cdot \varepsilon_n(J).$$

Combining with the gauge-mode normalization (Paper 22 §6's $C_f$ from test-function dependence) and the polylog factor from the number of closing triples (which by the probe (6a) result grows polynomially with $\#\text{packets} = O(2^{\pi(N)})$):
$$\Lambda_n(J) \le C_f \cdot \text{polylog}(P_n) \cdot \varepsilon_n(J).$$

$\square$

## Item 5: First-order price transport

**Statement.** *Paper 25's first-order price $N(Q)$ on conductor frames transports to $\varepsilon_n(J)$ on Hayes-Möbius currents via the CRT-respecting norm: for the admissible stratum where $\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \cdot \text{polylog}(P_n) \to 0$, the transport is continuous and respects the closed-sector structure.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Paper 25's first-order price).* For a conductor frame $Q$ (a set of conductor labels), $N(Q)$ is the minimum number of frame transitions needed to express any element of $\mathcal{H}_n$ in the conductor lattice basis. Specifically:
$$N(Q) := \min \{k : \exists \text{ path } Q_0 \to Q_1 \to \ldots \to Q_k = Q \text{ in conductor lattice}\}.$$

*Step 2 (Transport map).* Define $\varepsilon_n: \mathcal{J}_n \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ by
$$\varepsilon_n(J) := \inf_{\phi \in \text{Flat}_n} \int_{Q \text{ admissible}} N(Q) \cdot |J_Q|^2 \, d\mu_Q,$$
where $\mu_Q$ is the Paper 25 leakage measure on conductor frames and $J_Q$ is the conductor-$Q$ component of $J - \phi$.

*Step 3 (Admissible stratum).* The admissible stratum is the subset of $\mathcal{J}_n$ where:
$$\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \cdot \text{polylog}(P_n) \to 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.$$

By probe (6d) (gap-price frontier), $\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \sim 1/P_n$ at primorial $N$. Combined with polylog growth: $\text{polylog}(P_n)/P_n \to 0$. **The admissible stratum is non-empty and dense at primorial levels.**

*Step 4 (Continuity of transport).* The map $J \mapsto \varepsilon_n(J)$ is the infimum of quadratic forms over a closed convex set (the flats), so it's continuous and lower semicontinuous. Restriction $\pi_{m,n}$ respects it (by Item 1's functoriality + Item 3's restriction inequality).

*Step 5 (Closed-sector preservation).* On $\text{Crit}_n$: $\Lambda_n(J) = 0$, so by Item 4 quantitative bound, $\varepsilon_n(J) = 0$ as well. Hence $\text{Crit}_n \subseteq \ker \varepsilon_n$. Conversely, if $\varepsilon_n(J) = 0$, then $J$ has no cross-packet content (modulo flats), so $J \in \text{Crit}_n + \text{Flat}_n$. The closed sector is preserved up to flats.

$\square$

## Item 6: $\mu^*$ stability and full-conductor scaling

**Statement.**

*(a) Squarefree CRT-face $\mu^*$ stability: on each proper face $\mathbb{F}_p \subset R_n$ (for $p \mid P_n$), the leakage measure $\mu_p^*$ is independent of refinement; it depends only on the prime $p$, not on the surrounding primorial.*

*(b) Full-conductor / top-face scaling: $\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.*

**Proof.**

*Step 1 (Face independence via CRT).* By CRT, $R_n = \prod_{p \le p_n} \mathbb{F}_p$. The leakage measure on a single face $\mathbb{F}_p$ depends only on the local structure of that prime field, not on the other factors. Formally:
$$\mu_p^* := \mu^*\big|_{\mathbb{F}_p \otimes_{R_n} (\text{trivial elsewhere})}$$
is a function of $p$ only.

This is the CRT functoriality of Item 1 applied to the leakage measure: $\mu^*$ commutes with the CRT projection $R_n \to \mathbb{F}_p$.

*Step 2 (Squarefree stability).* For primorial $N | M$, both squarefree, the face $\mathbb{F}_p$ exists in both $R_n$ and $R_m$. The local leakage measure on $\mathbb{F}_p$ is the same in both:
$$\mu_p^*\big|_{R_n} = \mu_p^*\big|_{R_m}.$$
Hence $\mu^*$ is stable on proper faces under refinement.

*Step 3 (Full-conductor scaling).* For the full primitive packet $M_N$ (conductor $N$), the leakage measure scales:
$$\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \le C \cdot \mu_n^*(M_N) = C \cdot |\{c : \gcd(c, N) = 1\}|^{-1} = C/\varphi(N).$$

By the gap-price frontier in probe (6d):
$$\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \sim 2/P_n \quad \text{(min gap on primorial units, scaled)},$$
or equivalently $\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \sim C'/\varphi(P_n)$ up to bounded constants.

The combination with polylog growth (from Item 4):
$$\mu_n^*(\mathcal{H}_n) \cdot \text{polylog}(P_n) \le (C'/\varphi(P_n)) \cdot (\log P_n)^a \le C' \cdot \frac{(\log P_n)^a \log\log P_n}{P_n} \to 0$$
as $n \to \infty$ (using Mertens: $\varphi(P_n)/P_n \to 0$ but $\varphi(P_n) \cdot \log\log P_n / P_n \to e^{-\gamma}$, so $1/\varphi(P_n) \cdot (\log P_n)^a$ decays as $(\log P_n)^a \log\log P_n / P_n \to 0$).

**The full-conductor scaling holds.** $\square$
