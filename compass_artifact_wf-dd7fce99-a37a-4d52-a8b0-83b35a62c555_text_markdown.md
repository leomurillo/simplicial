# Surviving the Singular Limit: A Survey of Toolkits for Certifying that a Thin One-Sided Inequality Passes to its Limit

## TL;DR
- **No general "thin-positivity-survives-its-limit" theorem exists; the working instruments are problem-specific, and the three with genuine arithmetic/adelic traction are (1) the Connes–Consani semilocal trace-formula / Weil-positivity program, (2) rigorous renormalization-group + reflection-positivity (Balaban; Bauerschmidt–Brydges–Slade; stochastic quantization à la Chandra–Chevyrev–Hairer–Shen), and (3) Γ/Mosco convergence of Dirichlet forms (the cleanest abstract template for "nonnegativity passes to the limit").**
- **The program's natural home is the Connes–Consani circuit — it already lives at the intersection of "positivity-preserving limit" AND "adelic/spectral," and its 2023–2026 semilocal + q→1 work (the prolate-wave-operator and q-series papers with Moscovici) is precisely the discrete→archimedean-completion machinery the program says it lacks. The program's existing adelic place-completion sub-program is the right vehicle; the *missing* analytic piece is a lower-semicontinuity / compression-stability certificate borrowed from Γ/Mosco or RG theory.**
- **The single transferable theorem-template is a lower-semicontinuity-of-a-compressed-quadratic-form statement: if a quadratic functional Q_Λ is ≥0 at every finite cutoff Λ, and the family {Q_Λ} is equi-coercive / Γ-(or Mosco-)convergent to a limit Q_∞ with no positive mass escaping to the cutoff scale, then Q_∞ ≥ 0. Instantiating this for the Weil functional as the phase-space cutoff Λ→∞ is the shared certificate the three hard problems need.**

## Key Findings

1. **There is no off-the-shelf theorem that a marginal one-sided inequality survives a singular limit.** Every successful instance couples a *monotone/coercive structure* (entropy, energy, transfer-matrix positivity, lower-semicontinuous functional) to a *compactness or equi-coercivity* statement that prevents the margin from leaking at the singular scale. Where the margin is *strict*, survival is routine; where it is *marginal/critical*, survival is exactly where the open problems live (Yang–Mills mass gap, RH, Collatz).
2. **Reflection positivity (RP) is the cleanest "survives-the-limit" property when it survives** — it is a family of inequalities ⟨ΘP₊, P₊⟩ ≥ 0 testable on a dense set and therefore closed under weak limits of states — but it is famously *fragile*: RP is not preserved by all discretizations, and establishing it for the *limit* measure (not just lattice approximants) typically requires an independent argument (Jaffe: "one needs an independent method to establish RP for the limit").
3. **Rigorous RG is the most battle-tested machinery for carrying a bound through a continuum limit**, but its successes (4D hierarchical φ⁴; 2D/3D Yang–Mills via stochastic quantization; 3D φ⁴) are in *sub-critical or super-renormalizable* regimes; the *marginal* (just-renormalizable, 4D) case is precisely the frontier.
4. **Γ-convergence/Mosco convergence is the abstract skeleton** of "nonnegativity/Markov property passes to the limit": the liminf inequality gives lower semicontinuity of nonnegative functionals, and Mosco convergence preserves the sub-Markovian (positivity-preserving, contraction) structure of Dirichlet forms. This is the most directly *transferable* abstract template, but it is silent about *which* topology makes the arithmetic functional equi-coercive.
5. **The Connes–Consani program is already at the target intersection.** Their archimedean Weil-positivity theorem reduces positivity of the Weil quadratic functional to positivity of a *compressed* scaling-action trace (the Sonin space, phase-space cutoff Λ=1), and the 2023–2026 papers with Moscovici extend the prolate-wave-operator machinery toward the *semilocal* case (several primes) and a *q = 1/p → 1* limit. This is the literal "carry a finite Weil-positivity form toward a q→1/archimedean limit" the program describes.
6. **Monotonicity/entropy methods (Perelman, Huisken, Almgren, modulated free energy)** are the paradigm of "a monotone quantity controls a singular limit," and Serfaty-type modulated-energy methods are the closest analogue to the program's "cumulants of a categorical log-partition," because they control a *positive* energy/relative-entropy distance uniformly in N.

## Details

### Toolkit 1 — Reflection positivity, Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction, constructive QFT, stochastic quantization
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** Reflection positivity (RP/OS-positivity) is the inequality ⟨Θ F, F⟩ ≥ 0 for observables F supported in a half-space, where Θ is time reflection; it is the one axiom (OS2) that makes the Euclidean→Lorentzian reconstruction produce a Hilbert space with a *positive* self-adjoint Hamiltonian H ≥ 0 (transfer matrix T = e^{−aH}). The theorem-type is: *if RP holds at each lattice spacing a and the Schwinger functions converge with uniform clustering/temperedness bounds, the limit satisfies OS0–OS4 and reconstructs a Wightman QFT with H ≥ 0 (a mass gap if clustering is exponential, uniformly in a).* Stochastic quantization replaces the static measure by a Langevin SPDE whose invariant measure is the QFT; regularity structures (Hairer) and paracontrolled distributions (Gubinelli–Imkeller–Perkowski; Gubinelli–Hofmanová) renormalize the singular limit. Chandra–Chevyrev–Hairer–Shen build the 2D and 3D Yang–Mills Langevin dynamics gauge-covariantly: "by leveraging symmetry arguments in the small noise limit, we show that there is a unique choice of renormalisation counterterms such that these solutions are gauge covariant in law," defining a canonical Markov process on the gauge-orbit space.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook for the framework (Glimm–Jaffe; Osterwalder–Schrader 1973/75); active-to-frontier for survival under continuum limits (3D YM is frontier; 4D is open).
**(c) Key references:**
- M. Hairer, "A theory of regularity structures," Invent. Math. 198 (2014) 269–504, **arXiv:1303.5113** (verified).
- A. Chandra, I. Chevyrev, M. Hairer, H. Shen, "Langevin dynamic for the 2D Yang–Mills measure," Publ. IHÉS 136 (2022) 1–147, **arXiv:2006.04987** (verified via multiple reference lists; ID not personally seen on the abstract page — please confirm).
- A. Chandra, I. Chevyrev, M. Hairer, H. Shen, "Stochastic quantisation of Yang–Mills–Higgs in 3D," Invent. Math. 237 (2024) 541–696, **arXiv:2201.03487** (verified).
- M. Gubinelli, M. Hofmanová, "A PDE construction of the Euclidean Φ⁴₃ quantum field theory," Comm. Math. Phys. 384 (2021) 1–75, **arXiv:1810.01700** (verified via reference lists).
- I. Chevyrev, H. Shen, "Invariant measure and universality of the 2D Yang–Mills Langevin dynamic," **arXiv:2302.12160** (2023, verified via reference lists).
- A. Jaffe, "Reflection Positivity Then and Now" (Oberwolfach survey).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** The discrete framework over Z/N could be cast so that "positivity at every finite stage" is literally RP/transfer-matrix positivity, and OS reconstruction would then deliver a positive limiting operator — exactly the s→1−s involution flavor the program already has (RP's reflection Θ is, concretely, the Tomita–Takesaki modular reflection J, so the analogy is structural, not cosmetic). What must hold: a genuine reflection symmetry of the finite data and *uniform* clustering bounds as N→∞. Where it breaks: RP is not automatically inherited by limits, and a *dimensionless/firewall* finite theory may not present a half-space reflection structure at all — RP needs a geometric reflection, which an information-geometric Z/N model may lack.

### Toolkit 2 — Rigorous renormalization group
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** Rigorous RG integrates out scales via a finite-range decomposition of the Gaussian covariance and a fixed-point/stable-manifold analysis of the effective-action flow, controlling large-field regions by hand. Theorem-type: *if the RG map has a controllable (stable + finitely many unstable) flow with uniform bounds along the trajectory, the continuum/thermodynamic limit exists and inherits the bounds (stability, mass gap, positivity of the effective potential) tracked at every scale.* Balaban's program does this for lattice gauge theories (UV stability in d=3, coupling-constant renormalization in d=4); Bauerschmidt–Brydges–Slade do it rigorously for the 4D hierarchical |φ|⁴ model and weakly self-avoiding walk; Kupiainen reframes singular SPDEs as an RG flow.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (BBS book; Dimock's expositions of Balaban); frontier for full 4D non-abelian YM.
**(c) Key references:**
- R. Bauerschmidt, D. Brydges, G. Slade, *Introduction to a Renormalisation Group Method*, Lecture Notes in Math. 2242 (2019), **arXiv:1907.05474** (verified).
- T. Balaban, "Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories I," Comm. Math. Phys. 109 (1987) 249–301 (journal-only primary source).
- J. Dimock, "The Renormalization Group According to Balaban I–III" (expository; e.g. Rev. Math. Phys. 25 (2013) 1330010).
- A. Kupiainen, "Renormalization Group and Stochastic PDEs," Ann. Henri Poincaré 17 (2016) 497–535.
**(d) Transfer assessment.** RG is the most plausible *analytic* import. The program's "mean multiplier just below 1 in log" is structurally a *marginal* RG situation — the same near-criticality that makes Collatz and 4D YM hard. If the conductor-packet cumulants of the categorical log-partition can be organized as a scale decomposition with a contraction, an RG fixed-point argument could carry positivity to the limit. What must hold: a genuine multi-scale (renormalization) structure on Z/N with a coercive quadratic part and controllable remainder. Where it breaks: RG needs a *Gaussian reference measure with a decomposable covariance*; a purely arithmetic/dimensionless finite model may not supply one, and the *marginal* case has no complete 4D theory even for the experts.

### Toolkit 3 — Γ-convergence and Mosco convergence; Dirichlet forms
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** Γ-convergence (De Giorgi–Franzoni): Eₙ Γ→E if liminf Eₙ(fₙ) ≥ E(f) for fₙ→f strongly and a recovery sequence attains E(f). Mosco convergence strengthens the liminf to *weak* convergence of fₙ and is equivalent to convergence of the associated semigroups/resolvents/spectral families. Theorem-type, exactly the one requested: *nonnegativity of a functional is preserved under Γ/Mosco limits (the liminf gives E(f) ≥ 0 whenever all Eₙ ≥ 0), and the sub-Markovian / positivity-preserving / contraction property of a sequence of Dirichlet forms passes to the Mosco limit* (Mosco; Kuwae–Shioya). This is the cleanest abstract embodiment of "a one-sided inequality survives the limit."
**(b) Maturity:** textbook (Dal Maso, *An Introduction to Γ-Convergence*; Fukushima–Oshima–Takeda, *Dirichlet Forms and Symmetric Markov Processes*; Mosco 1994); active for metric-measure/fractal and graph→continuum limits.
**(c) Key references:**
- U. Mosco, "Composite media and asymptotic Dirichlet forms," J. Funct. Anal. 123 (1994) 368–421 (journal primary).
- G. Dal Maso, *An Introduction to Γ-Convergence*, Birkhäuser 1993 (textbook).
- M. Fukushima, Y. Oshima, M. Takeda, *Dirichlet Forms and Symmetric Markov Processes*, de Gruyter (textbook).
- "Dirichlet forms on metric measure spaces as Mosco limits of Korevaar–Schoen energies," **arXiv:2301.08273** (verified); "Korevaar–Schoen p-energies and their Γ-limits on Cheeger spaces," **arXiv:2401.15699** (verified).
- Caution from the literature: Mosco convergence preserves the *form-positivity/semigroup* structure but global path properties (recurrence/transience, conservativeness) are **not** preserved.
**(d) Transfer assessment.** This is the most directly transferable *abstract template*. If the program's finite Weil-positivity quadratic form is realized as a Dirichlet-form-like functional Eₙ on a space that converges (in L² over an adelic/archimedean measure) to the limit object, then E∞ ≥ 0 follows from the liminf inequality — provided the family is **equi-coercive** and the recovery sequence exists. Where it breaks: one must choose the right ambient L²-space and prove equi-coercivity; the cited warning (path properties not preserved) means *which* positivity survives must be pinned down — form-positivity yes, but spectral-gap survival needs the stronger Mosco (resolvent) convergence plus a uniform gap.

### Toolkit 4 — Monotonicity formulas and entropy methods
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** A monotone quantity (Perelman's W-entropy/F-functional for Ricci flow; Huisken's Gaussian-weighted area for mean curvature flow; Almgren's frequency function; entropy–entropy-dissipation) is non-increasing/non-decreasing along the flow and therefore *controls the blow-up/singular limit*: the limit of the monotone quantity exists and constrains the limiting object (blow-ups are self-similar shrinkers; "no entropy is lost at infinity," Mantegazza–Müller). Theorem-type: *a sign-definite monotone functional passes to the rescaled/singular limit and forces rigidity of the limit (soliton/shrinker), so a one-sided bound on it survives.* Serfaty's modulated energy / modulated free energy controls a *positive* distance between an N-point configuration and its mean-field limit uniformly in N (mean-field convergence for Coulomb/Riesz flows).
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (Perelman 2002–03; Huisken 1990; Almgren); very active 2023–2026 for modulated-energy mean-field limits.
**(c) Key references:**
- G. Perelman, "The entropy formula for the Ricci flow and its geometric applications," **arXiv:math/0211159** (2002, standard).
- S. Brendle, "A monotonicity formula for mean curvature flow with surgery," **arXiv:1312.0262** (verified).
- C. Mantegazza, R. Müller, "Perelman's entropy functional at Type I singularities of the Ricci flow," **arXiv:1205.4143** (verified).
- D. Bresch, P.-E. Jabin, Z. Wang, "Modulated free energy and mean field limit," Séminaire Laurent Schwartz (2019), **arXiv:1912.03152** (verified).
- M. Rosenzweig, S. Serfaty, "Sharp commutator estimates of all order for Coulomb and Riesz modulated energies," **arXiv:2407.15650** (2024, verified); S. Serfaty, "Lectures on Coulomb and Riesz gases," **arXiv:2407.21194** (2024, verified).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Strong conceptual fit: the program's "cumulants of a categorical log-partition" are morally a free-energy/entropy functional, and a *monotone* such functional under discrete→continuum refinement would be the ideal certificate. What must hold: identify a quantity that is monotone under "place-completion"/refinement and sign-definite. Where it breaks: monotonicity formulas are tied to a *flow* (a semigroup/evolution); the arithmetic completion is not obviously a flow, so the program would first need to manufacture a one-parameter scaling flow — the scaling site's R₊ action is the natural candidate (Toolkit 7).

### Toolkit 5 — Lyapunov / Foster–Lyapunov drift methods; Collatz machinery
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** A Foster–Lyapunov drift condition asks for V ≥ 0 with E[V(Xₙ₊₁)|Xₙ] ≤ V(Xₙ) − f(Xₙ) + b·1_C; theorem-type (Meyn–Tweedie): *a per-step drift inequality at every stage yields positive recurrence / geometric ergodicity / convergence in the long-time (iteration) limit.* The Collatz analogue: a per-step *expected* contraction (the log-multiplier ½log(½)+½log(3/2) < 0, mean multiplier just below 1) heuristically forces orbits down; Tao makes this rigorous for *almost all* orbits in logarithmic density.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook (Meyn–Tweedie, *Markov Chains and Stochastic Stability*); the Collatz state-of-the-art is a single deep paper.
**(c) Key references:**
- T. Tao, "Almost all orbits of the Collatz map attain almost bounded values," Forum Math. Pi 10 (2022), **arXiv:1909.03562** (verified). Theorem 1.3, verbatim: *"Let f : N+1 → R be any function with lim_{N→∞} f(N) = +∞. Then one has Col_min(N) < f(N) for almost all N ∈ N+1 (in the sense of logarithmic density). Thus for instance one has Col_min(N) < log log log log N for almost all N."* This improves Korec's prior natural-density bound Col_min(N) ≤ N^θ for any θ > log3/log4 ≈ 0.7924. The proof establishes an approximate transport property for a Syracuse first-passage random variable via characteristic-function estimates of a 3-adic skew random walk.
- I. Krasikov, J. C. Lagarias, "Bounds for the 3x+1 problem using difference inequalities," Acta Arith. 109 (2003) 237–258, **arXiv:math/0205002** (verified): for any fixed positive integer a not divisible by 3 and large enough x, at least x^{0.84} of the integers below x have a in their forward orbit under the 3x+1 function.
- J. C. Lagarias, "The 3x+1 problem: An annotated bibliography," **arXiv:math/0309224** and II **arXiv:math/0608208** (verified).
- S. R. Meyn, R. L. Tweedie, *Markov Chains and Stochastic Stability* (textbook).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** The "marginal contraction" is the *exact* signature the program flags, and drift methods are the canonical instrument. What must hold: a Lyapunov function on the finite state space whose drift is uniformly negative and survives N→∞. Where it breaks: drift gives *almost-all / generic* (measure-theoretic) conclusions, not the *every-orbit* statement — precisely Tao's stated barrier — so for a *deterministic every-stage* arithmetic positivity, drift alone is structurally insufficient; it controls typical, not extremal, behavior. This is the pseudo-random-dynamics regime named in the prompt.

### Toolkit 6 — de Branges spaces, Beurling–Nyman, the Weil quadratic functional, Connes–Consani spectral/adelic approach
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** The Weil explicit-formula quadratic functional Q(f) is positive semidefinite iff RH holds. Bombieri studied its finite truncations, proving (verbatim): *"if the Riemann Hypothesis is false but only with finitely many non-trivial zeros off the critical line we show that the number of negative eigenvalues is precisely one-half of the number of zeros failing to satisfy the Riemann Hypothesis, provided the truncation is big enough."* The Beurling–Nyman criterion recasts RH as a density (a closure/limit statement) in L²; de Branges spaces of entire functions give a positivity-condition route (though Conrey–Li showed de Branges' specific positivity hypotheses fail for ζ). Connes–Consani's theorem-type is the one the program wants: *positivity of the (global/semilocal) Weil functional is equivalent to RH, and at the archimedean place it reduces to "the positivity of the trace of the scaling action compressed onto the orthogonal complement of the range of the cutoff projections associated to the cutoff in phase space, for Λ=1"*, with the discrepancy from the Weil distribution controlled by prolate spheroidal wave functions and Hermitian Toeplitz matrices; "all the concepts and tools used in this paper make sense in the general semi-local case, where Weil positivity implies RH."
**(b) Maturity:** textbook (Weil; de Branges 1968; Bombieri 2000); active-to-frontier (Connes–Consani–Moscovici 2023–2026).
**(c) Key references:**
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Weil positivity and Trace formula, the archimedean place," Selecta Math. 27 (2021) Paper 77, **arXiv:2006.13771** (verified).
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Spectral triples and ζ-cycles," Enseign. Math. 69 (2023) 93–148, **arXiv:2106.01715** (verified).
- A. Connes, C. Consani, H. Moscovici, "Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators," Ann. Funct. Anal. 15 (2024) Art. 87, **arXiv:2310.18423** (reported by research assistant; ID not personally re-verified — please check) — extends the prolate-operator machinery toward the **semilocal** case and stability of the semilocal Sonin space.
- A. Connes, C. Consani, H. Moscovici, "On q-series and the moment problem," **arXiv:2403.01247** (reported by research assistant; ID not personally re-verified — please check) — treats the semilocal case S = {p, ∞} with parameter **q := 1/p**, the explicit q→1 / several-primes direction.
- E. Bombieri, "Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers, I," Rend. Lincei Mat. Appl. 11 (2000) 183–233 (journal-only).
- L. de Branges, *Hilbert Spaces of Entire Functions*, Prentice-Hall 1968; J. B. Conrey, X.-J. Li counterexample to de Branges' positivity (1998/2000).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** This **is** the program's natural home: simultaneously (i) a positivity-preservation problem and (ii) arithmetic/adelic/spectral, and the Λ=1 cutoff → semilocal → q=1/p→1 ladder is literally the "carry a finite Weil-positivity form toward a q→1/archimedean limit" the program states it has. What must hold: realize the program's "conductor-packet" finite quadratic form as (a discretization of) the compressed scaling-action trace, and prove the compression-positivity is *stable as the cutoff Λ→∞ and as more places are adjoined*. Where it breaks: the open step is exactly the *uniform* control of the prolate/Toeplitz discrepancy as Λ→∞ — i.e., the limit step is unsolved even here, which is why the program's analytic deficit is real and worth attacking.

### Toolkit 7 — Arakelov geometry, the archimedean fiber, F₁ programs, function-field→number-field transfer
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** Over function fields, RH is a *theorem* (Weil; Deligne) because one has a genuine curve, the Frobenius, intersection theory, and the **Hodge index theorem on a surface** (positivity of an intersection form) to run Weil's positivity proof. Arakelov geometry compactifies Spec Z by adding an archimedean fiber, aiming to import this positivity. Theorem-type sought: *a positivity (Hodge index / Castelnuovo–Severi) that holds geometrically over F_q descends/survives to the arithmetic completion at the archimedean place.* Connes–Consani's arithmetic site and scaling site realize the adele class space of Q as a tropical curve with an R₊ scaling flow, and they prove a Riemann–Roch theorem for the Arakelov compactification of Spec Z using Segal's Γ-rings / the sphere spectrum as "absolute base" F₁.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook (Weil conjectures, Deligne; Arakelov, Faltings); frontier-speculative for the F₁/absolute approach to RH.
**(c) Key references:**
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Geometry of the scaling site," Selecta Math. 23 (2017) 1803–1850, **arXiv:1603.03191** (verified).
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Riemann–Roch for $\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb Z}$," Bull. Sci. Math. 187 (2023), **arXiv:2205.01391** (reported by research assistant; ID not personally re-verified — please check).
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Riemann–Roch for the ring Z," C.R. Acad. Sci. Paris 362 (2024) 229–235, **arXiv:2306.00456** (reported by research assistant; ID not personally re-verified — please check).
- A. Connes, C. Consani, "Segal's Gamma rings and universal arithmetic," Q. J. Math. 72 (2021), **arXiv:2004.08879** (reported by research assistant; ID not personally re-verified — please check).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Deep structural fit with the program's F₁/adelic ambitions, and the scaling site's R₊ action supplies the *flow* that monotonicity methods (Toolkit 4) need. What must hold: a genuine *positive* intersection-type form on the arithmetic surface whose positivity is the Weil functional. Where it breaks: the missing "arithmetic surface Spec Z ×_{F₁} Spec Z" and its Hodge-index positivity remain conjectural — a decades-old wall — so this toolkit supplies *language and geometry* more than a deployable limit-survival theorem.

### Toolkit 8 — Concentration-compactness / profile decomposition
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** For a sequence saturating a critical threshold, the Lions concentration-compactness principle / Bahouri–Gérard profile decomposition writes it as a sum of rescaled/translated profiles plus a dispersive remainder; the Kenig–Merle method extracts a *minimal non-scattering critical element* with compactness, then kills it by rigidity. Theorem-type: a *trichotomy* — at the critical level the mass/margin either (i) is attained by a compact extremizer, (ii) splits into separating bubbles, or (iii) disperses — telling you precisely *whether* the positive margin is preserved or lost in the limit.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (Lions 1984–85; Bahouri–Gérard 1999; Kenig–Merle 2006–08).
**(c) Key references:**
- C. Kenig, F. Merle, Acta Math. 201 (2008) 147–212 (energy-critical NLW); Invent. Math. 166 (2006) (energy-critical NLS).
- H. Bahouri, P. Gérard, Amer. J. Math. 121 (1999) 131–175.
- P.-L. Lions, "The concentration-compactness principle," Ann. IHP 1 (1984).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Diagnostic value: it is the right framework to ask *where the positive margin of the Weil functional goes* as the cutoff is removed — does it concentrate at the archimedean place, split across places, or disperse? It is, concretely, the "no-mass-loss" hypothesis in the master template below. What must hold: a scaling/translation symmetry group and a critical variational quantity. Where it breaks: it is built for *dispersive PDE* with explicit symmetry groups; porting it to an arithmetic functional requires identifying the analogue of the bubbling symmetries, which is unclear.

### Additional Toolkit A — Random-matrix universality / GUE statistics
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** The Montgomery–Odlyzko law: the pair correlation of zeta zeros matches the GUE sine-kernel; Erdős–Schlein–Yau / Bourgade–Erdős–Yau universality for Dyson Brownian motion shows local eigenvalue statistics are *universal* (independent of the initial law) after a short relaxation time. Theorem-type: *local statistics of a self-adjoint/log-gas system converge to a universal scaling limit* — a limit-survival statement for *correlation structure*, not for a one-sided inequality per se.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (Montgomery 1973; universality program 2009–2017+); the zeta connection remains conjectural (Montgomery's conjecture is open; recent unconditional partial results exist).
**(c) Key references:**
- H. Montgomery (1973) pair-correlation conjecture; Odlyzko's numerical confirmations.
- L. Erdős, H.-T. Yau, *A Dynamical Approach to Random Matrix Theory* (textbook).
- Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya, Turnage-Butterbaugh, "An unconditional Montgomery theorem for pair correlation of zeros of the Riemann zeta function," **arXiv:2306.04799** (verified).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Suggestive (Hilbert–Pólya) but indirect: universality preserves *statistics*, which is not the program's target property (the sign of a functional). Likely a source of *heuristic calibration* rather than a limit-survival certificate.

### Additional Toolkit B — KMS states, Tomita–Takesaki modular theory, Bost–Connes systems
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** A Bost–Connes-type C*-dynamical system (A, σ_t) has equilibrium (KMS_β) states whose structure changes at a critical inverse temperature β=1: a unique state above, a symmetry-broken family below, with partition function ζ. Tomita–Takesaki modular theory supplies a canonical modular automorphism and a reflection J (the operator-algebra avatar of OS reflection). Theorem-type: *a phase transition / spontaneous symmetry breaking in the infinite-volume (thermodynamic) limit of KMS states*, with arithmetic (Galois) symmetries acting.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (Bost–Connes 1995; Connes–Marcolli; Laca–Raeburn; Neshveyev's ergodicity proof).
**(c) Key references:**
- J.-B. Bost, A. Connes, Selecta Math. 1 (1995) 411–457.
- S. Neshveyev, "Ergodicity of the action of Q*₊ on the finite adeles and the Bost–Connes phase transition theorem," **arXiv:math/0002141** (verified).
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Real structural resonance: the program's s→1−s involution and adelic structure are exactly the BC/modular world, and KMS-state survival in the thermodynamic limit is a positivity-of-state survival theorem. What must hold: realize the finite model as a sequence of finite-volume Gibbs states converging to a KMS state. Where it breaks: BC gives *existence/uniqueness/symmetry-breaking* of states, not the *Weil-functional sign* — the bridge from "phase transition at β=1" to "Q(f) ≥ 0" is not established.

### Additional Toolkit C — Berkovich / non-archimedean and tropical analytic limits
**(a) Definition + theorem-type.** Berkovich analytic spaces give a connected, Hausdorff non-archimedean analytic geometry; tropical limits (degeneration of amoebas) convert algebraic positivity into piecewise-linear/convexity statements. Theorem-type: *convexity/positivity is preserved under tropicalization/non-archimedean limits.* The scaling site's tropical structure (Toolkit 7) is in this family.
**(b) Maturity:** textbook/active (Berkovich 1990; tropical geometry); speculative for RH transfer.
**(c) Key references:** V. Berkovich, *Spectral Theory and Analytic Geometry over Non-Archimedean Fields* (1990); Connes–Consani scaling-site papers above.
**(d) Transfer assessment.** Provides the right *category* for the archimedean-as-limit-of-non-archimedean intuition and dovetails with the scaling site. Where it breaks: no current theorem converts tropical convexity into Weil positivity.

## Recommendations

**Top 3 toolkits to invest in (with the first concrete object to build):**

1. **Connes–Consani semilocal trace-formula / Weil-positivity program (Toolkit 6).** *This is the program's home — invest here first.* **First object to learn/build:** the *compressed scaling-action trace on the Sonin space at cutoff Λ* and the prolate-spheroidal/Hermitian-Toeplitz control of its discrepancy from the Weil distribution (arXiv:2006.13771), then the *semilocal* extension (arXiv:2310.18423) and the *q=1/p* moment-problem formulation (arXiv:2403.01247). The RG and stochastic-quantization toolkits are valuable for the *marginal-contraction* difficulty but are secondary to getting the lower-semicontinuity certificate right.

## The ONE transferable theorem-template

**Template (Compressed-Form Lower-Semicontinuity Certificate).**
*Let H be a Hilbert space with a one-parameter family of cutoff projections P_Λ (phase-space/scale cutoffs) and a positive "scaling/transfer" action. For each finite cutoff Λ let Q_Λ(f) := ⟨f, (compression of the action to the range of (1−P_Λ)) f⟩ be the finite-stage quadratic functional, and suppose Q_Λ(f) ≥ 0 for every Λ and every admissible f (the finite/local positivity, established by hand). Suppose further that the family {Q_Λ} is (i) equi-coercive on the relevant test space and (ii) Γ- (equivalently, for the resolvents, Mosco-) convergent to a limit functional Q_∞ as Λ→∞, with no positive mass concentrating at the cutoff scale (a no-loss / tightness condition ruling out the concentration-compactness "bubbling" alternative). Then Q_∞(f) ≥ 0 for all f.*

Instantiating this with H the semilocal Sonin/Hilbert space, P_Λ the phase-space cutoffs, Q_Λ the compressed scaling-action trace, and Q_∞ the global Weil quadratic functional **is** the certificate that "thin positivity survives its limit." It is simultaneously the template behind (a) RP→OS positivity surviving a→0 in Yang–Mills (P_Λ = lattice cutoff, no-loss = uniform clustering), and (b) the Collatz per-step contraction surviving the iteration limit (Q_Λ = drift at horizon Λ, no-loss = tightness of the first-passage transport). The two hard hypotheses — **equi-coercivity** and **no-mass-loss at the cutoff** — are exactly where all three problems are currently open, and proving them for the arithmetic instance is the program's target.

## Caveats
- **No universal theorem exists.** Each toolkit's "survival" result carries hypotheses (equi-coercivity, uniform clustering, a monotone quantity, a flow, a symmetry group) that are nontrivial and often *fail* exactly in the marginal/critical case. I have flagged where each breaks.
- **Established vs. frontier.** Established: OS reconstruction; RG for 4D hierarchical φ⁴ and the 2D/3D YM Langevin dynamics; Γ/Mosco preservation of form-positivity; Tao's almost-all Collatz result; Connes–Consani archimedean Weil positivity (Λ=1). Frontier/open: 4D YM mass gap survival; RH (the global/semilocal Weil positivity); every-orbit Collatz; the F₁ "arithmetic surface" and its Hodge-index positivity.
