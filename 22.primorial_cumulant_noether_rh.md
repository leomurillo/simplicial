## Abstract

We use the all-order cumulant viewpoint opened by Paper 17 and the primorial
pattern suggested by Paper 21 to formulate the sharpest current route from the
simplicial-cyclotomic program to the Riemann Hypothesis.  The central object is
the primorial tower
\[
P_n=p_n\#=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i,
\qquad
R_n=\mathbb Z/P_n\mathbb Z,
\]
whose conductor lattice contains exactly the squarefree interactions among the
first \(n\) prime axes.  The \(k\)-th Lagrangian/cumulant order naturally probes
the row \(P_{k-1}\): quadratic order sees the first prime axis, cubic order sees
\(2\cdot3\), with the first nonabelian rung forced by Paper 18, Theorem B,
quartic order sees \(2\cdot3\cdot5\), quintic order sees
\(2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\), and the all-order limit sees the natural-number
prime-exponent lattice.

The proposed bridge to the Riemann Hypothesis is not a direct zero calculation.
It is a positivity theorem.  In the language of Paper 20, this is the
all-order version of the same boundedness-below requirement: finite
projection-closure residuals are nonnegative inside the information geometry,
and the limiting cumulant tower should identify that lower bound with the Weil
quadratic form.  The all-order Noether energy on the primorial critical leaf
should converge to the Weil explicit-formula quadratic form for the completed
zeta function.  Since the Weil positivity criterion is equivalent to the
Riemann Hypothesis, this gives a precise target:
\[
\boxed{
\text{primorial closed-current Noether positivity}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{Weil positivity}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{RH}.
}
\]
The paper proves the formal conditional implication, supplies an explicit
canonical candidate for the recovery maps \(\mathscr R_n\), records exact
low-order agreement at quadratic and cubic order, and reduces the remaining
gap to two finite-place analytic inputs for the von Mangoldt explicit formula,
explicit projective-closure hypotheses for the critical leaf, and the
Archimedean Noether Limit.  One finite-place input, natural-scale primorial
character uniformity, is now discharged at tier \(\mathsf{[A]}\).  It rests on
the external, model-generated quasi-GRH half-plane
\(\operatorname{Re}s>7/8\) (OpenAI 2026, which OpenAI reports as
Lean-formalized).  That strip does not prove RH, and the archimedean step is
untouched.  Thus this is not advertised as an unconditional
proof of RH, and it is not a physical-gravity or spacetime stability claim.
It is the best current candidate for turning the simplicial-cyclotomic
machinery into one.

**Keywords:** Riemann Hypothesis, primorial, cumulant Noether theorem,

prime-exponent lattice, Amari-Chentsov tensor, conductor lattice, CRT.

## 2. Primorial Rows and Lagrangian Order

Let
\[
P_n=p_n\#=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i,
\qquad
R_n=\mathbb Z/P_n\mathbb Z.
\]
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem,
\[
R_n\cong\prod_{i=1}^n\mathbb Z/p_i\mathbb Z.
\]
The nontrivial conductor blocks of the categorical tangent space over \(R_n\)
are indexed by nonempty subsets of the first \(n\) prime axes, equivalently by
nontrivial divisors of \(P_n\).

This gives the finite squarefree skeleton of the natural numbers.  Prime
powers do not appear as new axes; they appear as occupations along an existing
prime axis in the Euler product:
\[
\zeta(s)
=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
=\sum_{k\in\mathbb N^{(\mathcal P)}}
\exp\left(-s\sum_p k_p\log p\right).
\]
Thus there are two independent cutoffs:

1. **Axis cutoff:** keep primes \(p\le p_n\), giving \(P_n\).
2. **Occupation cutoff:** bound the exponents \(k_p\).

The primorial tower is the canonical axis cutoff.  It is the correct object for
detecting which cumulant order first forces how many distinct prime axes to
interact.

The proposed order rule is
\[
\boxed{
\ell_k\ \text{naturally probes}\ R_{k-1}=\mathbb Z/(p_{k-1}\#)\mathbb Z.
}
\]
Equivalently:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{order} & \text{interaction} & \text{minimal row} & \text{new question}\\
\hline
2 & \text{binary/Fisher} & 2 & \text{one prime axis}\\
3 & \text{cubic/Amari-Chentsov} & 6 & \text{first conductor interaction}\\
4 & \text{quartic/jerk} & 30 & \text{triple conductor mixing}\\
5 & \text{quintic} & 210 & \text{coherence among triple faces}\\
k & \text{\(k\)-th cumulant} & p_{k-1}\# & \text{\((k-1)\)-axis coherence}
\end{array}
\]

This table is the new reason Paper 21 should treat \(R=\mathbb Z/30\mathbb Z\)
as canonical rather than merely convenient.

## 3. The All-Order Cumulant Action

Let \(h=\eta-\eta_*\) be displacement from the categorical centroid and
\(v=\dot\eta\).  Paper 17 separated the accumulated action from the
instantaneous Noether energy:
\[
\mathcal A_3[\eta]=\int \ell_3(\eta,\dot\eta)\,dt,
\qquad
\mathcal E_3=\text{Noether energy density}.
\]
At third order,
\[
\ell_3(\eta,v)
=\frac12 g_\eta(v,v)+\frac13T_\eta(h,v,v),
\]
where \(T=K_{CH}\) is the Amari-Chentsov tensor.

The all-order continuation should be a cumulant-jet action
\[
\mathcal A_{\le K}^{(n)}[\eta]
=\int \ell_{\le K}^{(n)}(\eta,\dot\eta)\,dt
\]
with
\[
\ell_{\le K}^{(n)}(\eta,v)
=
\frac12 g_\eta(v,v)
+\sum_{r=3}^{K} c_r\,
T^{(r)}_\eta(h^{\otimes(r-2)},v,v),
\]
where \(T^{(3)}=T\), \(T^{(4)}=\nabla^{(e)}T\), and the constants \(c_r\)
depend on the chosen cumulant normalization.  The support and Noether
questions do not depend on those constants.

Time-translation invariance gives an instantaneous Noether energy density
\[
\mathcal E_{\le K}^{(n)}
=
v\cdot\frac{\partial \ell_{\le K}^{(n)}}{\partial v}
-\ell_{\le K}^{(n)}.
\]
The formal all-order object is the limit
\[
\mathcal A_{\infty}^{(n)}
=\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathcal A_{\le K}^{(n)},
\qquad
\mathcal E_{\infty}^{(n)}
=\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathcal E_{\le K}^{(n)},
\]
whenever the cumulant expansion is interpreted as a formal jet or as a
convergent local expansion in the simplex interior.

The point is not that the real simplex dynamics literally terminates at cubic
order.  It does not.  The cubic term is the first non-Gaussian correction.  The
jerk is the first witness that the cubic theory is a truncation of an infinite
cumulant tower.

\widehat h^{*m}(\eta)\,

\[
\Lambda_n(J)\ge0

\Lambda_n(J)
=

## 5. The Noether-Weil Quadratic Form

The classical Riemann Hypothesis has a positivity formulation.  In one of its
standard forms, Weil's explicit formula associates to a suitable test function
\(F\) a quadratic form \(\mathcal W_\zeta(F)\).  RH is equivalent to positivity
of this form on functions of the shape \(F=f*f^*\), after the usual completed
zeta normalization.

This formulation is used here as a known external theorem, not reproved.  The
normalization of \(\mathcal W_\zeta\), the allowed test-function space, and the
gamma-factor terms must be fixed in the analytic paper that proves the recovery
identity below.  At the present stage \(\mathcal W_\zeta\) denotes the standard
completed-zeta Weil form.

The proposed primorial Noether object is a finite positive form
\[
\mathcal Q_n(J)
=
\mathcal E_{\infty}^{(n)}(J)+\Lambda_n(J)
\]
restricted to currents \(J\) on \(\mathrm{Crit}_n\).  It is positive by
construction:
\[
\mathcal Q_n(J)\ge0.
\]

jerk/simplex mass term is a positive projection-closure residual for

Noether-Weil bridge is the conditional claim that this bounded-below finite
structure survives the projective and Archimedean limits and becomes Weil

**Theorem 5.2.2 (Archimedean gauge-lift criterion).**  
Let \(J_\infty(f)\in\mathrm{Crit}_\infty\) be the closed projective current of
Theorem 5.1.3.  Suppose the limiting closed-current sector satisfies the
following archimedean lift axioms:

1. **Cartan floor.**  The finite closed sector contains the two Cartan
   generators already visible on the cubic row \(R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\):
   \[
   \mathfrak h_\infty\simeq\mathfrak u(1)^2.
   \]
2. **Noether charge is not abelian.**  The limiting cubic Noether charge has a
   nonzero symmetric \(d\)-symbol component, and this component is preserved
   under the projective restriction maps.
3. **Closed-current compatibility.**  No residual leakage class survives in
   the projective limit; equivalently, all off-Cartan components that remain
   must close under the Noether bracket rather than appear as a new carry
   defect.
4. **Real-cone and Fisher-volume preservation.**  The archimedean completion
   acts by positive-cone-preserving transformations that preserve the limiting
   Fisher volume form.
5. **Critical involution.**  The representation carries an involution
   \[
   \iota:s\longleftrightarrow 1-s
   \]
   which exchanges the two closed Cartan branches.
6. **Minimal positive-energy completion.**  Among nonabelian completions
   satisfying items 1--5, the projective lift is the minimal semisimple
   positive-energy completion.

Then the Cartan floor is forced to complete to
\[
\mathfrak g_\infty^{\mathrm{min}}
\simeq
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)
\]
up to compact real form and harmless central/flat factors.  Moreover, under
the oscillator normalization in which the two closed Cartan branches have
Mellin exponents \(s/2\) and \((s+1)/2\), the archimedean character of the
lift contributes
\[
\Gamma(s/2)\Gamma((s+1)/2)
\]
to the completed Noether-Weil form, up to the standard powers of \(\pi\) and
\(2\) fixed by the chosen completed-zeta normalization.

*Proof.*  The finite cubic row gives two closed Cartan generators, hence the
abelian floor \(\mathfrak h_\infty\simeq\mathfrak u(1)^2\).  If the
projective limit remained purely abelian, every Noether bracket on the
surviving sector would be diagonal in the Cartan weights and the symmetric
cubic \(d\)-symbol would be either zero or an external tensor not generated by
the current algebra.  This contradicts item 2 together with item 3: a nonzero
cubic charge that is not closed by the algebra would be precisely residual
leakage, while Theorem 5.1.3 has placed the limit in the closed sector.
Therefore the archimedean completion must add root directions.

Real-cone and Fisher-volume preservation rule out arbitrary noncompact or
volume-distorting extensions of the Cartan floor.  The smallest compact
semisimple extension carrying a nontrivial rank-one reflection that can swap
the two branches is the \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) factor.  The smallest compact
semisimple extension carrying a nonzero invariant symmetric cubic tensor is
\(\mathfrak{su}(3)\): the \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) adjoint has no independent
rank-three symmetric invariant, while \(\mathfrak{su}(3)\) has the standard
\(d_{abc}\) tensor.  Minimality in item 6 therefore forces
\[
\mathfrak g_\infty^{\mathrm{min}}
\simeq
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)
\]
after quotienting central and flat factors invisible to the leakage form.
The \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) factor supplies the branch-exchange involution
\(s\leftrightarrow1-s\); the \(\mathfrak{su}(3)\) factor supplies the cubic
Noether charge compatible with the closed current.

It remains to compute the archimedean character.  In the oscillator
normalization, the two branch radial variables \(x,y>0\) have character
density
\[
\Theta_s(x,y)
=
e^{-x-y}\,
x^{s/2-1}\,
y^{(s+1)/2-1}.
\]
This is the unique positive-energy density compatible with the two Cartan
weights, Fisher-volume preservation, and the critical involution exchanging
the two branches.  Mellin integration gives
\[
\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty
e^{-x-y}x^{s/2-1}y^{(s+1)/2-1}\,dx\,dy
=
\Gamma(s/2)\Gamma((s+1)/2).
\]
The omitted factors are exactly the conventional powers of \(\pi\) and \(2\)
introduced when passing from this oscillator normalization to the standard
completed zeta normalization; the duplication formula fixes their placement.
Thus the closed-current nonabelian lift contributes precisely the gamma
factor required in the completed Weil form. \(\square\)

   Proposition 5.2.3a and Corollary 5.2.3b construct the compatible Bures
   Cartan-floor maps and prove the Bures-side cubic scalar is nonzero;
   Proposition 5.2.4a proves the finite \(R=6\) \(A_2\)-matching

3. **No residual leakage upstairs.**  Theorem 5.1.3 gives
   \(\Lambda_\infty(J_\infty)=0\) on the diagonal shadow.  The Bures lift must

4. **Real cone and Fisher volume.**  Paper 18 shows that the Bures-Helstrom

5. **Critical involution.**  The involution \(s\leftrightarrow1-s\) must be
   realized as an actual Weyl/reflection or modular-conjugation symmetry of
   the Bures positive-energy representation, exchanging the two closed Cartan
   branches while preserving the completed-zeta normalization.
6. **Minimal positive-energy completion.**  One must prove that no smaller
   semisimple positive-energy algebra satisfies branch exchange, nonzero
   internal cubic \(d\)-symbol, real-cone preservation, and Fisher/Bures volume

**Candidate Theorem 5.2.5 (oscillator character for the gamma factor).**

\chi_{\mathrm{osc}}(s)

\pi^{-(2s+1)/2}

**Proposition 5.2.5a (even/odd oscillator radial measures).**  
In the one-dimensional positive-energy oscillator representation, let the
even and odd branch rays be represented on the half-line by
\[
\psi_0(x)=e^{-\pi x^2},\qquad
\psi_1(y)=y e^{-\pi y^2},\qquad x,y>0.

\chi_+(s)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi x^2}x^{s-1}\,dx
=
\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),
\]
and
\[
\chi_-(s)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi y^2}y^s\,dy
=
\pi^{-(s+1)/2}\Gamma((s+1)/2).
\]
Consequently the two-branch product is

\chi_+(s)\chi_-(s)
=
\pi^{-(2s+1)/2}\Gamma(s/2)\Gamma((s+1)/2).
\]
After the changes of variables \(X=\pi x^2\) and \(Y=\pi y^2\), the two
Mellin measures become
\[
e^{-X}X^{s/2-1}\,dX,
\qquad
e^{-Y}Y^{(s+1)/2-1}\,dY
\]
up to the displayed powers of \(\pi\).  In oscillator normalization this is
exactly the density
\[
e^{-X-Y}X^{s/2-1}Y^{(s+1)/2-1}\,dX\,dY.
\]

*Proof.*  The first formula is the standard Gaussian Mellin integral
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi x^2}x^{s-1}\,dx
=
{1\over2}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),
\]
with the factor \(2\) coming from passage from the even full-line ray to the
positive radial half-line.  For the odd branch, the first Hermite ray has one
radial power \(y\), so the Mellin weight is \(y^{s-1}\cdot y=y^s\), and
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-\pi y^2}y^s\,dy
=
{1\over2}\pi^{-(s+1)/2}\Gamma((s+1)/2).
\]
Multiplication gives the product character.  The substitutions
\(X=\pi x^2\), \(Y=\pi y^2\) give the two radial measures stated above.
\(\square\)

**Proposition 5.2.5c (no-go for automatic Weyl/CCR selection).**  
The projective Bures tower data proved above do not, by themselves, force the
Weyl/CCR positive-energy oscillator representation.  More precisely, the
following data:

1. the normalized CRT restriction maps and projective survival of the
   \(A_2\)-matched cylinder class \(q_\infty\);
2. the Bures/Fisher metric compatibility on the Cartan floor;
3. the nonzero internal \(A_2\) cubic
   \[
   q_\infty=c_BD_{A_2},\qquad c_B\ne0;
   \]
4. leakage extinction on the diagonal projective critical leaf;

do not determine a strongly continuous Weyl system, a nontrivial central
CCR cocycle, a positive-energy number operator, or the metaplectic Fourier
operator.  Therefore the requested Weyl/CCR selection theorem cannot be
proved from the current finite/projective Bures axioms alone.

*Proof.*  At every finite row \(S_n\), the Bures lift acts through a finite
matrix algebra of traceless Hermitian operators.  If two finite-dimensional
operators \(P_n,Q_n\) satisfied an exact CCR
\[
[Q_n,P_n]=i\hbar I,
\qquad \hbar\ne0,
\]
then taking traces would give
\[
0=\operatorname{Tr}[Q_n,P_n]
=
i\hbar\,\operatorname{Tr}I,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence no exact CCR is present at any finite stage.

An exact CCR can only appear after an additional limiting operation that
specifies an unbounded generator pair and a nontrivial central two-cocycle.
But the projective data listed above are symmetric/metric and cubic: they
determine the Bures inner product on the floor and the symmetric
\(A_2\)-Jordan tensor.  They do not determine an antisymmetric symplectic
form
\[
\omega((u,v),(u',v'))=uv'-vu',
\]
nor the multiplier
\[
e^{\pi i(uv'-vu')}

## 7. Why Quartic and Quintic Matter

The first finite tests are not arbitrary.

At quartic order the canonical row is
\[
R_3=\mathbb Z/30\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z
\times
\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z.
\]
This is the first row where a genuine three-prime conductor class can exist.
It asks whether \(2\cdot3\cdot5\) contains a carry obstruction that is not
visible on the pairwise faces \(6,10,15\).  The order-4 atlas of §5.3 answers
the existence half: \(914\) of the \(1082\) connected quartic vertices span all
three primes and are reducible to no pairwise face — the non-pairwise content
is present and counted.  The first cumulant order to separate this content from
the Gaussian (Wick-pairing) floor is precisely order 4, which is why this is
the decisive row.

At quintic order the canonical row is
\[
R_4=\mathbb Z/210\mathbb Z.
\]
Now the question is coherence among triple faces:
\[
30,\quad 42,\quad 70,\quad 105.
\]
Quartic order detects the first non-pairwise obstruction.  Quintic order tests
whether those non-pairwise obstructions compose coherently across the
natural-number divisor lattice.

Both resolve through one observation, valid at every order and every primorial
row.  A single-prime (Cartan) character on axis \(p\) is \(\equiv0\pmod q\) for
every other prime \(q\mid P_n\); so were a connected vertex (sum \(\equiv0\), no
proper subset \(\equiv0\)) built from single-prime characters alone and spanning
\(\ge2\) primes, the characters on any one axis would already form a proper
subset summing to \(0\pmod{P_n}\) — the full sum is \(0\bmod p\), and they are
\(0\) on every other axis — contradicting connectivity.  Hence **no connected
single-prime vertex spans \(\ge2\) primes**, and the closed-leaf projection
\(\Pi_\tau\) onto the single-prime Cartan axes annihilates the entire cross-prime
sector — at orders \(3,4,5,\dots\) on \(\mathbb Z/6,\mathbb Z/30,\mathbb
Z/210,\dots\) alike (certificate `empirical/verify_quintic_z210.py`,
spot-checked through \(\mathbb Z/210\)).  The non-pairwise obstructions thus
compose *coherently by cancellation*: every cross-prime vertex carries
\(\Lambda=0\) and vanishes on \(\mathrm{Crit}_n\), with no surviving four-prime
(or higher) carry; the new prime \(p=7\) enters only through the single-axis
packet \(A_7\) (\(\Lambda(7)=\log 7\)).  The genuinely new *structural* feature
at order 5 is its Wick subtraction — the \(3{+}2\) partition, a Gaussian pair
times an Amari–Chentsov cubic triple (the first order whose disconnected part
carries the order-3 content).  What this finite, all-rows argument does *not*
settle is the uniform \(n\to\infty\) limit (Conjecture 5.2): the cross-prime
carry is removed at every finite row, leaving that limit as the sole obstruction.

Thus the primorial tower is not a decorative indexing device.  It is the
finite skeleton of the all-order prime interaction problem.

**Lemma 8.1b (double cutoff tail).**  
Work first on a dense test class
\(\mathcal S_0^{\exp}\subset\mathcal S_0\) for which
\[
\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge1}(\log p)\,|G_f(r\log p)|<\infty .
\]
This is automatic if \(G_f\) has compact support in the logarithmic variable;
it also follows from a standard exponential log-side decay hypothesis, for
example \(e^{(1+\delta)|u|}G_f(u)\in L^1(du)\) with enough regularity for
partial summation.  Then for every cofinal diagonal
\[
p_n\to\infty,\qquad K_n\to\infty,\qquad M_n\to\infty
\]
such that
\[
T_n(f):=
\sum_{\substack{p>p_n\ \mathrm{or}\ r>K_n\ \mathrm{or}\ p^r>M_n}}
(\log p)\,|G_f(r\log p)|
\longrightarrow0,
\]
one has
\[
\sum_{\substack{p\le p_n\\1\le r\le K_n\\p^r\le M_n}}
(\log p)\,G_f(r\log p)
\longrightarrow
\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge1}(\log p)\,G_f(r\log p).
\]
Thus the axis cutoff, occupation cutoff, and Dirichlet cutoff commute on
\(\mathcal S_0^{\exp}\).  This is an elementary dominated-convergence
statement once the test space has enough log-side decay; without that decay,
the admissible Weil test space must be narrowed or a separate Tauberian
estimate must be supplied.

*Proof.*  (i) *Location of zeros.*  Let \(\chi^*\) be primitive of conductor
\(q^*\mid P_n\).  By Theorem OAI, \(L(s,\chi^*)\ne0\) for
\(\operatorname{Re}s>7/8\).  The functional equation relates
\(L(s,\chi^*)\) to \(L(1-s,\overline{\chi^*})\), and \(\overline{\chi^*}\) is
also covered by Theorem OAI.  Hence every nontrivial zero
\(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) satisfies \(1/8\le\beta\le7/8\).  In particular
\(|\rho|\ge1/8\), so no zero sits near \(s=0\).  An exceptional real zero in
\((7/8,1)\) is excluded as well.

(ii) *Truncated explicit formula.*  Use Davenport, *Multiplicative Number
Theory*, Ch. 19, for primitive \(\chi^*\) and \(2\le T\le X\):
\[
\psi(X,\chi^*)-\delta_{\chi^*=1}X
=-\sum_{|\gamma|<T}\frac{X^\rho}{\rho}
+O\!\Bigl(\frac{X\log^2(q^*X)}{T}+\log(q^*X)+X^{1/4}\log X\Bigr).
\]
The term \(b(\chi^*)\) and the zeros near \(s=0\) contribute \(O(\log q^*)\)
once \(|\rho|\ge1/8\).  The zero count is
\(N(t+1,\chi^*)-N(t,\chi^*)\ll\log(q^*(t+2))\) (Davenport, Ch. 16).  It gives
\[
\sum_{|\gamma|<T}\frac1{|\rho|}
\le 8\,\#\{|\gamma|<1\}+\sum_{1\le k<T}\frac{\#\{k\le|\gamma|<k+1\}}{k}
\ll\log q^*+\log q^*\log T+\log^2T\ll\log^2(q^*T).
\]
With \(|X^\rho|=X^\beta\le X^{7/8}\) and \(T=X\), this yields
\(|\psi(X,\chi^*)-\delta X|\ll X^{7/8}\log^2(q^*X)\).

(iii) *Imprimitive characters.*  If \(\chi\bmod q\) is induced by
\(\chi^*\), the two sums differ only at prime powers \(p^k\le X\) with
\(p\mid q\).  So
\(|\psi(X,\chi)-\psi(X,\chi^*)|\le\omega(q)\log X\ll\log q\log X\), which is
absorbed.  Since \(q\le P_n\), the bound holds with \(\log^2(P_nX)\).

(iv) *Weighted form.*  Write \(\Delta_\chi(t)=\psi(t,\chi)-\delta t\).  On a
block \([X,2X]\), partial summation gives
\[
\sum_{X<m\le2X}\Lambda(m)\chi(m)G_f(\log m)-\delta\!\int_X^{2X}\!G_f(\log t)\,dt
=\Bigl[G_f(\log t)\Delta_\chi(t)\Bigr]_X^{2X}
-\int_X^{2X}\Delta_\chi(t)\,G_f'(\log t)\,\frac{dt}{t}.
\]
This is \(\ll X^{7/8}\log^2(P_nX)\,(\|G_f\|_\infty+\|G_f'\|_{L^1})\).  Both
norms are finite on \(\mathcal S_0^{\exp}\) and locally bounded on its
bounded sets.  Divide by \(X\) to get the stated \(E_n(f)\).

(v) *Range.*  The function \(X\mapsto X^{-1/8}\log^2(P_nX)\) decreases once
\(\log(P_nX)>16\).  At \(X=X_n\) it equals
\((\log P_n)^{-\varepsilon/8}(1+o(1))\to0\).  Blocks below \(X_n\) keep the
existing treatment: exact finite occupation terms (Lemma 8.1a) or the tail
estimate (Lemma 8.1b).  Every constant above is effective, and Theorem OAI
itself carries no constant.  \(\square\)
