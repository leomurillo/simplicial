# Claim contract: toward a defensible FEP advance

**October 10, 2026.** One-page research decision, not a breakthrough announcement. Existing papers remain untouched. Derivations and audit links are in the [standalone note](C:/Projects/simplicial/FEP_gaussian_observer_derivation_note.md).

## Proposed paper and main theorem

**Working title:** *When Can a Physical Blanket Support Inference? Operational Limits in Gaussian Networks*.

**Target theorem — not yet proved:** For a declared hidden target, generative model, observation history, posterior encoding, and finite Gaussian internal dynamics, characterize when the internal state can realize the posterior available from that history while satisfying an explicitly specified statistical blanket and fixed hidden port. When exact realization fails, derive a quantitative posterior deficit under stated noise, time, and actuation constraints.

This would give an operational test of a particular Bayesian-mechanics interpretation. It would not refute an unrestricted claim that some generative-model interpretation exists, and it would not establish that every physical observer is an agent.

## What is established now

- Exact initial-preparation visibility of the complete record and of a current physical memory, in the stated thermal models.
- Separation of record uncertainty, physical-memory loss, and declared decoder error. The projection/KL identities are classical.
- A scoped reversible quarter-transfer corollary of classical spectral-spread estimates.
- Reproduced feasible passive, gated, and active observer candidates; numerical optima remain uncertified.
- An exact attainable work/rate frontier for a separate two-register transfer stage, with deterministic control and reference-averaged work.
- A decisive scope check: the W/T=1 active witness has conditional correlation Corr(e,i|b) approximately 0.226788 at readout. It does not preserve the passive statistical blanket.

## Nearest prior result and required distinction

[Hartich, Barato and Seifert](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02111) already relate physical sensor states to information in their histories; Gaussian filtering supplies posterior realization machinery. [Heins and Da Costa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10190) explicitly analyze blanket constraints. [Dechant and Lutz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17831) already establish nonreciprocal sensing improvements.

The proposed advance must characterize **simultaneous physical compatibility** of posterior realization, a specified blanket, and the fixed-port experiment, with a nontrivial attainable bound or obstruction. “Blankets alone do not imply useful inference,” another powered-sensing example, or a post hoc posterior map would not meet the novelty target.

## The single gap and next-turn objective

**Gap:** no theorem currently joins the operational posterior requirement to a physically verified blanket under the same controlled dynamics and resource assumptions.

**Next turn:** audit the simultaneous constraints before constructing another model. Start with the existing three-coordinate family. State the Gaussian blanket condition in covariance/precision form and the full-record sufficiency condition as a conditional-covariance or innovations condition. Determine whether any admissible nonzero active flow can satisfy both while keeping the hidden port fixed. Prove a scoped obstruction if they conflict; otherwise construct and verify a matched witness. Only enlarge the model if a proved obstruction identifies the exact missing degree of freedom.

## Breakthrough gate and stop rule

Promote this to an FEP-facing central claim only if it supplies an explicit prediction or compatibility theorem that is not already a direct restatement of Gaussian filtering, data processing, or the known blanket conditions. Compare the exact statement against modern dynamical FEP, not only an older slogan.

If the simultaneous-constraint result is standard or trivial, stop the breakthrough framing and draft the valid restricted technical note. Do not compensate by adding more disconnected models. A successful next turn ends with one theorem or a clearly located obstruction, its closest prior result, and a revised publication decision.
