## Summary

T8.4 Theorem E §7.2's proof of the conditional Stieltjes positivity invokes Lemma 6.4 (kernel positivity on the principal coset). The probe `probe_T8p4_lemma_6p4_Q_matrix.py` and the variant probe `probe_T8p4_lemma_6p4_variants.py` show:

| Variant | Axis form | PSD at n=1 (N=30) | PSD at n=1 (N=210) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q_re (team's stated form) | $R_k \cdot \mathrm{Re}\langle h_k, \chi_1\rangle$ | **FAIL** (min D = −0.0133) | **FAIL** (min D = −1.07e-4) |
| V1: \|Re\| | $R_k \cdot \|\mathrm{Re}\langle h_k, \chi_1\rangle\|$ | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |
| V2: modulus | $R_k \cdot \|\langle h_k, \chi_1\rangle\|$ | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |
| V3: squared modulus | $(R_k \cdot \|\langle h_k, \chi_1\rangle\|)^2$ | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |

All variants pass PSD trivially at n=0 (single positive entry). All fail at n=1 with a negative 2×2 determinant. The failure mechanism is the same in every variant: **Cauchy-Schwarz fails on the axis sequence** — $\mu_1^2 > \mu_0 \mu_2$.

The team's stated form Q_re has an additional pathology: the axis sequence $R_k \cdot \mathrm{Re}\langle h_k, \chi_1\rangle$ at N=30 is positive for k=1..20, then **flips sign** at k=21 to k=29. A sign-flipping sequence cannot be the moment sequence of any signed measure with prescribed support sign — Lemma 6.4's claim is structurally false in this strong form.

## What this means for T8.4

### The proof gap is genuine

Theorem E §7.2's argument chain:
$$v^T J_n^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(N) v = \underbrace{\sum_m \Lambda(m) K_v(m, N)}_{\text{Lemma 6.4: }\ge 0} + \underbrace{B_v(N)}_{\text{ArchStruct: }\ge 0} \ge 0$$
fails at the first underbrace. The kernel $K_v(m, N)$ on the principal coset is NOT pointwise non-negative when reduced via Lemma 6.4's matrix form. The conditional Theorem E as currently proved does not follow from the §6–§7 argument.

### The empirical result is real

`probe_arch_prime_N210_joint.py` verified $J_n^{\mathrm{prime,joint}}(210) \succeq 0$ at every tested $n \le 14$ with min LDL diagonal $\sim 0.84$. The conclusion of Theorem E is empirically robust. **What's wrong is the proof mechanism, not the result.**

### Diagnosing the correct mechanism

Three candidate explanations for why $J^{\mathrm{prime}} \succeq 0$ empirically holds despite kernel non-positivity:

**Candidate A: Aggregation cancellation.** The individual terms $\Lambda(m) K_v(m, N)$ are sign-indefinite, but the *sum* $\sum_m \Lambda(m) K_v(m, N)$ is non-negative because the negative-coset contributions cancel via prime-distribution arithmetic. This is plausible but requires a non-trivial estimate (Bombieri-Vinogradov-style cancellation), not a structural pointwise bound.

**Candidate B: Wrong kernel factorization.** Theorem D's reduction to "kernel times double-sum of test vector" may have collapsed too aggressively. The actual Helmert-Fourier expansion involves a sum over *all* residues (not just principal coset), and the conjugate-pair symmetry that makes the result real also distributes the positivity across multiple cosets. A more careful factorization might yield a different (genuinely PSD) kernel.

**Candidate C: The signed-measure argument is the right proof, not the kernel argument.** ArchStruct §7's signed-measure proof is structurally correct: $\sigma^{\mathrm{prime}}|_{(0,\infty)} \ge 0$ from on-line zeros (under GRH), $\sigma^{\mathrm{prime}}|_{(-\infty,0)} = -\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}$. Both pieces contribute non-negatively to $\int s \cdot q(s)^2 d\sigma^{\mathrm{prime}}$. **This proof does not need Lemma 6.4.** T8.4 §3.3 Corollary 3.3 *already states this*. The kernel/Helmert-Gauss machinery of §4–§7 may be an unnecessary detour for proving the conditional Theorem E.
