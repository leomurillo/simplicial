## Abstract

Paper 10 identifies the off-centroid Fisher tensor of the categorical
simplex as an arithmetic Taylor expansion in additive-character coordinates.
The line-level coefficient is exact: along an \(m\)-flat ray

$$
p(t)=p_*+th,
$$

the normalized Fisher matrix element between character lines
\({\mathbb C}\chi\) and \({\mathbb C}\psi\) is

$$
\bar g^F_{p(t)}(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
\sum_{n\ge0}(-t)^n
\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

This paper gives the block-level object that coefficient formula was asking
for.  The correct conductor-block compression is not a scalar indexed by a
pair of conductors.  It is an operator-valued coefficient in a finite
quotient-character adjacency algebra.  For conductor packets
\(X_{\mathfrak e}\) and \(X_{\mathfrak f}\), and for a quotient character
\(\eta\), define the partial quotient-shift

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}e_\chi
=
\begin{cases}
e_{\eta\chi},&
\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e},\ \eta\chi\in X_{\mathfrak f},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Then the \(n\)-th Taylor coefficient compressed from conductor
\(\mathfrak e\) to conductor \(\mathfrak f\) is

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)
=
\sum_{\eta}
\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}.
$$

Thus Hayes-Mobius compression means computing coefficient vectors in this
adjacency algebra, not taking traces.  Traces can erase the first genuine
within-block invariant, as already happens in the nilpotent \(Q_f=4\) row.

The global algebra factors by the Chinese Remainder Theorem after the trivial
character line is included:

$$
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R
\cong
\bigotimes_{\mathfrak p}
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_{\mathfrak p}}.
$$

The Fisher tangent algebra is the nontrivial augmentation corner obtained by
removing the all-trivial character; under CRT it is a direct sum over
nonempty support-pattern corners, not merely a tensor product of local Fisher
corners.  The ring-induced Hayes normalizer is the corresponding product of
local Hayes normalizers, with wreath-product symmetries that permute
isomorphic local factors; the larger bare conductor-adjacency normalizer is
obtained exactly by adding local linear excess and cross-class algebra
isomorphisms when they occur.  Each local factor carries a depth filtration; its
associated graded separates tame residue action from wild congruence action.
The filtered-isomorphism class over that associated graded is the local
Carlitz-Hayes extension class
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\).  It is finite arithmetic data,
computable by Hayes-Mobius formulas, but not reducible to the conductor-graded
shadow.  Equivalently, after choosing a section of the associated graded, the
local filtered multiplication law produces a finite \(2\)-cocycle for the
Hayes normalizer action on the graded pieces; changing the section changes the
representative by a coboundary.  Thus \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) is not a
post-hoc obstruction measured after the geometry is computed.  It is the
local arithmetic datum from which the off-centroid Fisher jets are derived.

The paper also fixes the information-geometric sign convention needed for the
next computation.  The raised Amari-Chentsov cubic is

$$
g_p(K^{CH}_p(u)v,w)=T_p(u,v,w),
\qquad
K^{CH}=\Gamma^{(m)}-\Gamma^{(e)}.
$$

With this convention the \(e/m\) duality reverses the orientation of
\(K^{CH}\).  The first diagnostic is the mixed local quotient
\({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\): subtract the centroid augmentation-corner baseline
from the path-composition defect
\(\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\alpha)\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\beta)
-\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\alpha\beta)\),
and compare pure-tame, pure-wild, and mixed \(m\)-flat displacements.  In the
first mixed row the \(srt^2\) coefficient is nonzero, saturated across all
tangent-compatible operator pairs, and has a nonzero commutator projection.
It is the first concrete representative of \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) and
the first local curvature-carry test case.  The first arity-3 atlas on the
same row has \(210\) closed cubic cells, exactly five
\(\Gamma_R\times S_3\)-orbits, and no local-coordinate signature collisions.
Its two new labels are the expected graded pieces: a multiplicative residue
ratio \(\rho\) and an additive nil-tail balance \(\nu\).  At binary arity the
same labels canonicalize the first nontrivial \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
representative: the filtered section produces a finite carry table with
\(1569\) atoms, \(71\) atom orbits, \(20\) pair orbits, and full-block
coboundary residual \(1569\).  At arity 4 the measured skeleton has \(3165\)
closed cells and \(18\)
\(\Gamma_R\times S_4\)-orbits, split as \(2+4+3+9\) across the
TTTT/TTWW/TWWW/WWWW families.  The support-level arity-4 curvature theorem
identifies the nonzero support of the analytic four-linear coefficient with
the input-side lift of the \(592\) commutator orbits: it lands exactly on the
seven mixed TTWW/TWWW orbits and on no pure orbit.  The first coefficient
level is now pinned on the full mixed lift: the unrestricted Leibniz expansion
gives the full \(\Pi_4\) value, and the paired-slot Bianchi projection removes
the middle crossing split, producing the \(NC_4\) distribution
\(-1:44,\ 1:504,\ 2:44\) across all \(592\) lifted support records.

A new overlap-aware repulsion pilot on
\({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) supplies a complementary optimization-side witness:
several low-overlap closed supports reach essentially the same KL objective.
The leakage in this simplified loop is not yet at the projection-clean level
of Paper 12's calendar transformer, but the support diversity is already
visible.  This supports the non-canonical reading of the filtered atlas:
valid conductor blankets need not be unique even when their performance is
nearly indistinguishable.

**Keywords:** Fisher metric, categorical simplex, conductor block,
additive character, quotient-character adjacency algebra, Hayes sum,
Mobius inversion, Carlitz-Hayes normalizer, Chinese Remainder Theorem,
local factor, tame ramification, wild depth.

## Reader's Guide

Paper 9 proves the conductor-block spectral theorem at the centroid.  Paper 10
shows how that theorem breaks off the centroid: the Fisher tensor in the
additive-character basis has Taylor coefficients governed by convolution in
the dual character group.

The remaining problem is compression.  Character lines are too fine for the
theorem-level statements, but conductor blocks are too coarse if one compresses
by trace.  The nilpotent \(Q_f=4\) row gives the warning: the trace of the
second-order block misses the swap operator inside the nilpotent block.

This paper makes the replacement:

1. Replace scalar block coefficients by operator-valued block coefficients.
2. Replace a conductor-pair matrix by a quotient-character adjacency algebra.
3. Replace the phrase "primes appear in the formulas" by a CRT factorization
   theorem for the algebra itself, with the Fisher tangent corner decomposed
   as a sum over nonempty CRT support-pattern pairs.
4. Replace local conductor grading by a filtered local algebra whose associated
   graded separates tame and wild behavior.
5. Name the failure of that filtration to split as
   \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\), a Carlitz-Hayes extension class; in the
   Rosas closure vocabulary, this is the obstruction to a conductor
   macrovariable being fully closed as an off-centroid Fisher adjacency
   algebra.
6. Treat \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) constructively: a filtered section of the
   tame/wild associated graded gives a Hayes-normalizer-equivariant
   \(2\)-cocycle, and all orbit tables, signatures, and support selectors are
   linear-algebraic outputs of that finite multiplication table.  For
   \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\), the collision-free \((\rho,\nu)\) signatures
   in Section 9.1 now define the canonical binary section, and the reducer
   table is the normalized \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) representative.
7. Fix the raised cubic convention
   \(K^{CH}=\Gamma^{(m)}-\Gamma^{(e)}\), and use it to test which part of
   \(\mathcal A_R\) is genuinely information-geometric.
8. Compute the first mixed local row \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\), pinning
   \(c=1\), finding the first nonzero term at \(srt^2\), and splitting it into
   symmetric Ext and antisymmetric curvature pieces.
9. Compute the first arity-3 hyper-adjacency atlas on the same row.  The
   \(210\) closed cells collapse to five \(\Gamma_R\times S_3\)-orbits

   invariant \(\nu\).
10. Prove the arity-4 support theorem.  The \(3165\) closed cells collapse to
   \(18\) \(\Gamma_R\times S_4\)-orbits, and the analytic four-linear

11. Stage the quotient-character \(n\)-adjacency tower as the program-level
   object beyond the cubic test.

The point is not to add another layer of notation.  It is to preserve exactly
the information that the Fisher tensor sees off the centroid.

## 1. From Line Coefficients to Block Operators

Let \(R\) be a finite quotient ring of size \(Q\), equipped with a chosen
generating additive character so that characters may be indexed internally.
Let

$$
G=\widehat R
$$

be the additive character group.  For a conductor \(\mathfrak d\), write

$$
X_{\mathfrak d}
=
\{\chi\in G:\operatorname{cond}(\chi)=\mathfrak d\}.
$$

The nontrivial Fisher tangent space is

$$
H_R
=
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak d\ne1}
\operatorname{span}X_{\mathfrak d}.
$$

Paper 10 proves the normalized line-level identity

$$
\bar g^F_{p_*+th}(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
\sum_{n\ge0}(-t)^n
\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}),
$$

with \(e_\chi=Q^{-1/2}\chi\) and \(\bar g^F=Q^{-1}g^F\).  The \(n\)-th
coefficient depends only on the quotient

$$
\eta=\psi\chi^{-1}.
$$

At line level this is complete.  At block level it is not enough to ask for one
number attached to the pair \((\mathfrak e,\mathfrak f)\), because many
quotients \(\eta\) may carry lines from \(X_{\mathfrak e}\) to
\(X_{\mathfrak f}\), and they can act differently inside the block.

This is the first definition.

**Definition 1.1 (Partial quotient-shift).**  For conductor packets
\(X_{\mathfrak e}\) and \(X_{\mathfrak f}\), and for \(\eta\in G\), define

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
:
\operatorname{span}X_{\mathfrak e}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{span}X_{\mathfrak f}
$$

by

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}e_\chi
=
\begin{cases}
e_{\eta\chi},&
\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e},\ \eta\chi\in X_{\mathfrak f},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Equivalently, if \(M_\eta\) denotes multiplication of character labels by
\(\eta\), then

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
=
P_{\mathfrak f}M_\eta P_{\mathfrak e}.
$$

The support theorem of Paper 10 says that
\(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\) can appear in the \(n\)-th Taylor
coefficient only when

$$
\eta\in(\operatorname{supp}\widehat h)^{(n)}.
$$

The coefficient is exactly \(\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)\).

**Proposition 1.2 (Operator-valued conductor compression).**  Along the
\(m\)-flat ray \(p(t)=p_*+th\), the \(n\)-th normalized Fisher coefficient
from \(H_R^{[\mathfrak e]}\) to \(H_R^{[\mathfrak f]}\) is

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)
=
\sum_{\eta\in G}
\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}.
$$

Thus the block-level Taylor coefficient is naturally operator-valued.  Scalar
trace compression keeps only

$$
\operatorname{tr} C_n^{[\mathfrak e,\mathfrak e]}(h),
$$

and can erase nontrivial within-block quotient-shifts.

This formula is the block version of the Normalization Lemma.  It does not
add analytic content; it names the finite algebraic target in which the
Taylor coefficients live.

Relative to the chosen conductor packets, this is the lossless finite
compression of the Fisher Taylor jets: it forgets individual character labels
only after retaining the quotient-shift operator that recovers each surviving
line-level matrix entry.  Trace compression is therefore not merely a rougher
summary.  Off the centroid it is Fisher-information lossy whenever a
within-block quotient-shift survives with zero or indistinguishable scalar
trace.

**Corollary 1.3 (CRT conductor isolation).**
Let \(R=C_N\) with \(N=ab\), \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).  Let
\(P_a=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}/N:a\mid k\}\) be the \(a\)-primary character subgroup.
If \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat h\subseteq P_a\), then

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)=0
\quad
\text{whenever }X_{\mathfrak f}\subseteq X_{b\text{-primary}},\;
X_{\mathfrak e}\subseteq X_{a\text{-primary}},\;n\ge1.
$$

*Proof.*  \(P_a\) is a subgroup of \((\mathbb{Z}/N,+)\), so the \(n\)-fold
sumset satisfies \((\operatorname{supp}\widehat h)^{(n)}\subseteq P_a\) for all
\(n\).  For \(\chi\in X_{a\text{-primary}}\) and \(\psi\in X_{b\text{-primary}}\),
the label quotient \(\eta=\psi\chi^{-1}\) satisfies \(\eta\notin P_a\), so
\(\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)=0\) and the corresponding \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\)
term is absent from Proposition~1.2. \(\square\)

*Remark.*  The isolation is exact at every Taylor order, not an approximation.
For \(R=C_p\) with \(p\) prime, no proper nontrivial subgroup of
\(\mathbb{Z}/p\) exists, so no CRT isolation occurs.  The precise prime
statement is conductor-level rather than a coefficient-by-coefficient
nonvanishing claim:

**Corollary 1.4 (Prime cyclic conductor saturation).**
Let \(R=C_p\), identify \(\widehat R\) with \(\mathbb F_p\), and let
\(0\ne h\in H_{C_p}\).  Put

$$
S=\operatorname{supp}\widehat h\subseteq \mathbb F_p^\times .
$$

Then the conductor support of \(h\) is \(\{p\}\): every active nontrivial
character lies in the unique nontrivial conductor packet

$$
X_p=\{\chi_k:k\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}.
$$

Moreover the convolution support envelope generated by \(h\) reaches every
nontrivial quotient character by order \(p-1\):

$$
\mathbb F_p^\times
\subseteq
\bigcup_{1\le n\le p-1} S^{(n)},
\qquad
S^{(n)}
=
\{s_1+\cdots+s_n:s_i\in S\}.
$$

Consequently prime cyclic rows have no proper conductor boundary that can
isolate an off-centroid Fisher coupling channel.

*Proof.*  Since \(h\in H_{C_p}\), the trivial Fourier coefficient vanishes:
\(\widehat h(0)=0\).  Since \(h\ne0\), some nontrivial coefficient
\(\widehat h(k)\) is nonzero with \(k\in\mathbb F_p^\times\).  For a cyclic
group of prime order,

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\chi_k)=\frac{p}{\gcd(p,k)}=p
\qquad(k\ne0),
$$

so every active nontrivial character lies in the same packet \(X_p\).  This
proves full conductor support.

For the support envelope, fix \(k\in S\).  Every
\(\eta\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) has a unique expression
\(\eta=ak\) with \(a\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\).  But \(ak\) is the sum of \(a\)
copies of \(k\), so \(\eta\in S^{(a)}\).  Hence the union of the first
\(p-1\) additive convolution envelopes contains all nontrivial characters.
There is no smaller conductor packet or CRT subgroup in which those
convolution envelopes can remain trapped. \(\square\)

This corollary intentionally separates conductor saturation from exact
coefficient support.  The stronger fixed-order statement
\(\operatorname{supp}(\widehat h^{*n})=\mathbb F_p^\times\) for all
\(n\ge p-1\) is false without an additional non-cancellation hypothesis:
if \(\widehat h\) is supported on a single character \(k\), then
\(\widehat h^{*n}\) is supported on the single character \(nk\).  What survives
unconditionally, and what is needed for the conductor-block theory, is that no
proper conductor boundary exists in the prime cyclic row.  For generic
coefficients on a support \(S\) with \(|S|\ge2\), the usual
Cauchy-Davenport support envelope gives \(S^{(n)}=\mathbb F_p\) for
\(n\ge p-1\).  For each fixed such \(n\), exact support equality holds away
from the finite union of cancellation hypersurfaces for that coefficient
degree.

**Corollary 1.5 (Diagonal vanishing and prime-row cross-conductor absence).**

*(A)  Universal: diagonal-free first-order coupling.*  For any ring \(R\) and any
\(h\in H_R\), the diagonal entries of the first-order Fisher adjacency operator
within any conductor packet vanish:

$$
[C_1^{[\mathfrak f,\,\mathfrak f]}(h)]_{\chi_k,\,\chi_k}
=
\widehat{h}(0)
=
\sum_x h(x)
=
0.
$$

Equivalently, \(K^{CH}_{p_*}(h)\) is traceless for every \(h\in H_R\):
\(\operatorname{tr}(K^{CH}_{p_*}(h))=N\,\widehat h(0)=0\).  Scalar-per-mode probes
of the Fisher connection are blind at first order.

*(B)  Prime-special: no cross-conductor Fisher coupling at any order.*
For \(R=C_p\), the adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_{C_p}\) contains only
within-packet operators — there is exactly one nontrivial conductor packet
\(X_p\) (Corollary 2.2 of \texttt{Note\_prime\_cyclic\_ext\_ch.md}).  No
cross-conductor pair \(\mathfrak f\ne\mathfrak e\) exists, so

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\,\mathfrak e]}(h) = 0
\quad\text{for all }n\ge1\text{ and all }\mathfrak f\ne\mathfrak e.
$$

In particular the cross-conductor cubic Fisher correction — the leading
off-centroid term distinguishing composite from prime rows in the Taylor
expansion of \(g^F\) — is identically zero for \(C_p\).

**Proof.**  Part (A): \(\widehat h(0)=\sum_x h(x)=0\) for \(h\in H_R\), and
\([C_1(h)]_{\chi_k,\chi_k}=\widehat h(0)\).  Part (B): \(\mathcal A_{C_p}\) has
no cross-conductor operators because only one conductor level exists; the claim is
vacuously true with empty domain. \(\square\)

## 2. The Quotient-Character Adjacency Algebra

The partial quotient-shifts form a finite algebra of conductor-respecting
transitions.

**Definition 2.1 (Concrete adjacency algebra).**  The quotient-character
adjacency algebra of \(R\) is the subalgebra

$$
\mathcal A_R
\subseteq
\operatorname{End}(H_R)
$$

generated by all operators

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
=
P_{\mathfrak f}M_\eta P_{\mathfrak e}
$$

with \(\mathfrak e,\mathfrak f\ne1\) and \(\eta\in G\).

There is also a path-algebra version.  Its vertices are conductor packets, and
its directed edges are triples

$$
(\mathfrak e,\eta,\mathfrak f)
\qquad
\text{with}
\qquad
P_{\mathfrak f}M_\eta P_{\mathfrak e}\ne0.
$$

Composition remembers the intermediate conductor.  This matters because

$$
A_\zeta^{\mathfrak g,\mathfrak f}
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
=
P_{\mathfrak g}M_\zeta P_{\mathfrak f}M_\eta P_{\mathfrak e},
$$

which is the same as the direct shift
\(P_{\mathfrak g}M_{\zeta\eta}P_{\mathfrak e}\) only on those characters
\(\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e}\) for which the intermediate point
\(\eta\chi\) actually lies in \(X_{\mathfrak f}\).  Therefore multiplication
is genuinely path-dependent.

This is why "adjacency algebra" is a better phrase than "block matrix."  The
conductors are vertices, the quotient characters are arrows, and Fisher
Taylor coefficients are weighted sums of arrows and paths.

**Definition 2.2 (Hayes-Mobius coefficient vector).**  The order-\(n\)
Hayes-Mobius coefficient vector of \(h\) is the element

$$
\mathsf C_n(h)
=
\sum_{\mathfrak e,\mathfrak f}
\sum_{\eta}
\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
\in\mathcal A_R.
$$

Its \((\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e)\)-component is
\(C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)\).  The original line-level formula is
recovered by evaluating this operator on the basis vector \(e_\chi\).

The product of coefficient vectors is not generally the next Taylor
coefficient:

$$
\mathsf C_m(h)\mathsf C_n(h)
\ne
\mathsf C_{m+n}(h)
$$

as a formal identity in \(\mathcal A_R\).  The left side sums over admissible
paths with intermediate conductor constraints; the right side uses the
convolution coefficient

$$
\widehat h^{*(m+n)}
=
\widehat h^{*m}*\widehat h^{*n}
$$

without remembering which intermediate conductor was traversed.  The equality
holds after refining the algebra to the full line-level matrix algebra, or in
special cases where the intermediate conductor condition is automatic.  This
failure is not a defect.  It is the first sign that the conductor path algebra
contains more information than the product-set support theorem alone.

### 2.1 Algebra-first reading

The quotient-character adjacency algebra is not a compression device applied
after the Fisher geometry has been computed.  It is the finite arithmetic
model of the tangent algebra and of the higher Taylor jets that the
categorical simplex admits in additive-character coordinates.  The analytic
objects are recovered from it by functorial recipes:

1. Fisher Taylor coefficients are Hayes-Mobius coefficient vectors in
   \(\mathcal A_R\).
2. The raised Amari-Chentsov cubic is the first index-raised contraction in
   the corresponding hyper-adjacency tower.
3. Curvature and higher \(\alpha\)-divergence jets are obtained by composing
   the filtered multiplication law and applying the Codazzi/Bianchi
   identities.

In this reading, exact enumeration is a verification method, not the source
of the definitions.  The primitive datum is the filtered multiplication table
of the local action groupoid

$$
G^{<\mathfrak d}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathfrak d}
$$

over its associated graded.  Once that table is known, Hayes-normalizer
orbits, local-coordinate signatures, support selectors, and coefficient
distributions are finite linear algebra over the table.

## 3. The \(Q_f=4\) Diagnostic Atlas

The quotient-size \(4\) atlas from Paper 9 already exhibits the three basic
behaviors of \(\mathcal A_R\).

### 3.1 Split row

For

$$
R\cong{\mathbb F}_2\times{\mathbb F}_2,
$$

there are three nontrivial conductor blocks, all one-dimensional.  If the
nontrivial characters are \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma=\alpha\beta\), then every
block is a line and every partial quotient-shift between nonzero blocks is
scalar on its source line.  The operator-valued compression therefore
collapses to the line-level coefficient.

This is the row where the conductor lattice is already fine enough.

### 3.2 Nilpotent row

For

$$
R={\mathbb F}_2[u]/(u^2),
$$

write the nontrivial characters as

$$
A=\chi_u,\qquad B=\chi_1,\qquad C=\chi_{1+u}=AB.
$$

The conductor-\(u\) block is the line \(\langle A\rangle\), and the
conductor-\(u^2\) block is

$$
N=\langle B,C\rangle.
$$

Put

$$
a=\widehat h(A),\qquad
b=\widehat h(B),\qquad
c=\widehat h(C).
$$

The second-order coefficient inside \(N\) is

$$
C_2|_N
=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2+b^2+c^2&2bc\\
2bc&a^2+b^2+c^2
\end{pmatrix}
=
D_1 I_N+D_A X_N,
$$

where \(X_N\) swaps \(B\) and \(C\).  The trace sees only

$$
2(a^2+b^2+c^2),
$$

and loses the diagnostic term \(2bcX_N\).

The swap is not ad hoc.  It is multiplication by the lower-conductor
character \(A\).  Thus the diagonal algebra on \(N\) contains the group algebra
of the lower-character action

$$
\langle I_N,X_N\rangle
\cong
{\mathbb C}[{\mathbb Z}/2{\mathbb Z}].
$$

This is the first row where the conductor lattice is too coarse.

### 3.3 Irreducible row

For

$$
R={\mathbb F}_4,
$$

all three nontrivial additive characters have the same conductor.  The lower
conductor subgroup is trivial, but the Hayes unit and Frobenius actions are
not.  If the three nontrivial character labels are \(A,B,C\), then

$$
C_2|_{H^{[f]}}
=
(a^2+b^2+c^2)I
+2bcX_A+2acX_B+2abX_C,
$$

where \(X_A\) swaps the two lines whose quotient is \(A\) and kills the
\(A\)-line, and cyclically for \(B,C\).

Choosing one label as Frobenius-fixed makes one \(X_A\) look like the residual
swap on the Frobenius orbit.  The full Hayes unit action rotates the three
labels, and Frobenius reflects them.  This gives the expected

$$
C_3\rtimes C_2
\cong S_3
$$

symmetry of the quotient-character adjacency templates.

The lesson of the three rows is:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{row}&\text{lower-character action}&\text{Galois/unit action}&
\text{compression}\\
\hline
\text{split}&\text{trivial}&\text{trivial on lines}&\text{scalar}\\
\text{nilpotent}&\text{regular }{\mathbb Z}/2&\text{trivial}&
\text{operator-valued}\\
\text{irreducible}&\text{trivial}&S_3\text{ on labels}&
\text{operator-valued}.
\end{array}
$$

The \(Q_f=4\) atlas is therefore not only a collection of examples.  It is the
minimal evidence that the block object is a quotient-character adjacency
algebra.

## 4. CRT Factorization

Let

$$
R\cong\prod_i R_i
$$

be the Chinese Remainder decomposition into local factors.  Then

$$
\widehat R
\cong
\prod_i \widehat{R_i}.
$$

Characters factor as

$$
\chi=\bigotimes_i\chi_i,
$$

and conductor labels factor as tuples

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\chi)
=
\prod_i\operatorname{cond}(\chi_i).
$$

There is a small zero-sum caveat.  The Fisher tangent space removes the
all-trivial character, so the exact tensor product statement is cleanest if
one first includes the trivial character line.

Let

$$
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R
$$

be the quotient-character adjacency algebra on the full character space
\({\mathbb C}\widehat R\), including the trivial character.  Then the
nontrivial Fisher adjacency algebra is the augmentation corner

$$
\mathcal A_R
=
P_{\ne1}\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R P_{\ne1},
$$

where \(P_{\ne1}\) removes the all-trivial line.

**Theorem 4.1 (CRT tensor factorization).**  For
\(R\cong\prod_iR_i\),

$$
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R
\cong
\bigotimes_i
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}
$$

as quotient-character adjacency algebras.  Under this isomorphism,

$$
A_{\eta}^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
\longleftrightarrow
\bigotimes_i
A_{\eta_i}^{\mathfrak f_i,\mathfrak e_i},
$$

where

$$
\eta=\bigotimes_i\eta_i,\qquad
\mathfrak e=\prod_i\mathfrak e_i,\qquad
\mathfrak f=\prod_i\mathfrak f_i.
$$

The Fisher tangent algebra is the nontrivial corner of this tensor product.

**Proof.**  Pontryagin duality gives

$$
G=\widehat R\cong\prod_i\widehat{R_i}=\prod_iG_i.
$$

Linearly extending the character-CRT identification gives

$$
\iota_R:\widetilde H_R={\mathbb C}G
\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
\bigotimes_i\widetilde H_{R_i},
\qquad
e_\chi\longmapsto\bigotimes_i e_{\chi_i}.
$$

Conductors multiply componentwise.  Indeed, if
\(\chi=\bigotimes_i\chi_i\), an ideal of \(R\) factors as
\(\prod_i\mathfrak d_i\), and \(\chi\) is trivial on
\(\prod_i\mathfrak d_i\) exactly when each \(\chi_i\) is trivial on
\(\mathfrak d_i\).  Minimality is therefore componentwise:

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\chi)
=
\prod_i\operatorname{cond}(\chi_i).
$$

Thus conductor packets and their projections factor:

$$
\iota_RP_{\mathfrak e}\iota_R^{-1}
=
\bigotimes_iP^{(i)}_{\mathfrak e_i},
\qquad
\mathfrak e=\prod_i\mathfrak e_i.
$$

The quotient shifts factor as well.  For
\(\eta=\bigotimes_i\eta_i\),

$$
\iota_RM_\eta\iota_R^{-1}
=
\bigotimes_iM^{(i)}_{\eta_i},
$$

because \(M_\eta e_\chi=e_{\eta\chi}\) and
\((\eta\chi)_i=\eta_i\chi_i\).  Since

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
=
P_{\mathfrak f}M_\eta P_{\mathfrak e},
$$

we obtain

$$
\iota_R A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\iota_R^{-1}
=
\bigotimes_i
A_{\eta_i}^{\mathfrak f_i,\mathfrak e_i}.
$$

Conjugation by \(\iota_R\) is an algebra isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{End}(\widetilde H_R)
\cong
\bigotimes_i\operatorname{End}(\widetilde H_{R_i}).
$$

It sends the generators of \(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R\) bijectively to the
pure-tensor generators of
\(\bigotimes_i\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}\).  The generated algebras are
therefore identified.  \(\square\)

### 4.1 The Fisher tangent corner is a sum, not a tensor product

Let \(E_i\) be the projector onto the trivial character line of
\(\widetilde H_{R_i}\), and let \(F_i=1-E_i\).  The full tensor space has the
orthogonal support-pattern decomposition

$$
1
=
\sum_{S\subseteq I}Q_S,
\qquad
Q_S
=
\bigotimes_{i\in S}F_i
\otimes
\bigotimes_{i\notin S}E_i.
$$

Under the CRT isomorphism,

$$
\Phi(P_{\ne1})
=
1-\bigotimes_iE_i
=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I}Q_S.
$$

Consequently the Fisher tangent algebra is the augmentation corner

$$
\boxed{\;
\Phi(\mathcal A_R)
=
\bigoplus_{\substack{S,T\subseteq I\\S,T\ne\varnothing}}
Q_S
\left(\bigotimes_i\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}\right)
Q_T.
\;}
$$

The local content of each \((S,T)\)-summand factors as

$$
Q_S
\left(\bigotimes_i\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}\right)
Q_T
=
\bigotimes_i
\begin{cases}
\mathcal A_{R_i},&i\in S\cap T,\\
F_i\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}E_i,&i\in S\setminus T,\\
E_i\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}F_i,&i\in T\setminus S,\\
{\mathbb C}E_i,&i\notin S\cup T.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus \(\mathcal A_R\) is not
\(\bigotimes_i\mathcal A_{R_i}\).  That tensor product is only the diagonal
support-pattern corner \(S=T=I\).  The full Fisher tangent algebra also
contains off-diagonal CRT support transitions, where one local character slot
enters or leaves the nontrivial tangent support.  This is the structural
payload of the augmentation caveat: the scalar conductor shadow misses these
corners, but the quotient-character adjacency algebra keeps them.

### 4.2 The normalizer and the wreath piece

For a local factor \(L\), define the ring-induced Hayes normalizer

$$
\Gamma_L
=
L^\times\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{ring}}(L).
$$

It acts on \(\widehat L\) by the contragredient rules

$$
(u\chi)(r)=\chi(ur),
\qquad
\sigma\chi=\chi\circ\sigma^{-1}.
$$

Units preserve the ideal lattice, and ring automorphisms carry ideals to
ideals, so \(\Gamma_L\) preserves the conductor filtration.  It is useful to
separate this Hayes group from the larger bare conductor-adjacency normalizer

$$
N_L
=
\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{ab}}(\widehat L):
\alpha\text{ preserves conductor packets}\}.
$$

Every element of \(N_L\) conjugates generators to generators:

$$
\alpha A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\alpha^{-1}
=
A_{\alpha\eta}^{\alpha\mathfrak f,\alpha\mathfrak e},
$$

with the conductor labels transported by the induced packet permutation.
The empirical orbit counts in this paper use the Hayes group \(\Gamma_L\);
the larger \(N_L\) records additional symmetries of the bare abelian
conductor-adjacency algebra when they exist.

Now write

$$
R
=
\prod_{[L]}\prod_{j=1}^{m_L}R_{L,j},
$$

where \([L]\) runs over ring-isomorphism classes of local factors.  Choose a
model \(L\) for each class and identify every \(R_{L,j}\) with that model.
Let \(N(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R)\) denote the corresponding bare
conductor-adjacency normalizer on \(\widehat R\): abelian-group
automorphisms that preserve conductor packets and hence normalize
\(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R\) by conjugation.

**Theorem 4.2 (Hayes wreath normalizer).**  The ring-induced global Hayes
normalizer is

$$
\prod_{[L]}\Gamma_L\wr S_{m_L}.
$$

This wreath product embeds in \(N(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R)\).  Here
\(S_{m_L}\) acts by permuting the \(m_L\) tensor slots of type \(L\) and
simultaneously permuting their conductor labels.  If the local factors are
pairwise nonisomorphic, this reduces to \(\prod_i\Gamma_{R_i}\).

**Proof.**  A per-slot element of \(\Gamma_L\) acts on one copy of
\(\widetilde H_L\) and fixes every other tensor factor.  On a pure-tensor
generator it changes only that local quotient shift.  If
\(\gamma_{L',j'}\) denotes \(\gamma\) on the chosen slot and the identity on
all other slots, then

$$
\gamma
\left(
\bigotimes_{[L'],j'}A_{\eta_{L',j'}}^{
\mathfrak f_{L',j'},\mathfrak e_{L',j'}}
\right)
\gamma^{-1}
=
\bigotimes_{[L'],j'}
A_{\gamma_{L',j'}\eta_{L',j'}}^{
\gamma_{L',j'}\mathfrak f_{L',j'},
\gamma_{L',j'}\mathfrak e_{L',j'}},
$$

which is again a generator tensor.

For \(\pi\in S_{m_L}\), the slot permutation

$$
\pi\cdot\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m_L}v_j
=
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m_L}v_{\pi^{-1}(j)}
$$

sends

$$
\bigotimes_jA_{\eta_j}^{\mathfrak f_j,\mathfrak e_j}
\longmapsto
\bigotimes_j
A_{\eta_{\pi^{-1}(j)}}^{
\mathfrak f_{\pi^{-1}(j)},\mathfrak e_{\pi^{-1}(j)}}.
$$

This is again a legitimate pure-tensor generator because all slots have the
same local algebra.  The relation
\(\pi\gamma_j\pi^{-1}=\gamma_{\pi(j)}\) gives
\(\Gamma_L\wr S_{m_L}\), and the product over isomorphism classes gives the
claim.  \(\square\)

The inclusion is the part used throughout the Hayes-normalized computations.
The full bare conductor-adjacency normalizer is equal to this Hayes wreath
product precisely when two accidental symmetries are absent:

1. **Local saturation.**  For every local model \(L\),
   \[
   \Gamma_L=N_L.
   \]
   Thus every conductor-preserving abelian-group automorphism of
   \(\widehat L\) is induced by a unit and a ring automorphism.
2. **Iso-class faithfulness.**  If \(L\not\cong L'\) as rings, there is no
   abelian-group isomorphism \(\widehat L\to\widehat{L'}\) matching conductor
   filtrations and intertwining the quotient-shift generators.  This is
   automatic when \(|L|\ne|L'|\).

If local saturation fails, \(\Gamma_L\) is replaced by \(N_L\).  If
iso-class faithfulness fails, the corresponding ring-isomorphism classes fuse
into a coarser conductor-adjacency algebra isomorphism class.  In general,

$$
N(\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R)
=
\prod_{[\widetilde L]}
N_{\widetilde L}\wr S_{m_{\widetilde L}},
$$

where \([\widetilde L]\) ranges over these fused algebra-isomorphism classes.
Indeed, a bare normalizer of the CRT tensor product must carry tensor slots
with the same conductor-adjacency algebra to tensor slots of the same type;
its component on each slot is then an element of the corresponding \(N_L\).
Conversely, every such local \(N_L\)-action and every permutation of
algebra-isomorphic slots conjugates the pure-tensor generators to pure-tensor
generators.  This proves both the equality criterion and the displayed
wreath form for the bare normalizer.

The cleanest local saturation failure is a finite field
\(L={\mathbb F}_q\), \(q=p^m\).  For \(m\ge2\), the single nontrivial conductor
packet has size \(q-1\).  The Hayes group is

$$
\Gamma_{{\mathbb F}_q}
=
{\mathbb F}_q^\times\rtimes
\operatorname{Gal}({\mathbb F}_q/{\mathbb F}_p),
\qquad
|\Gamma_{{\mathbb F}_q}|=(q-1)m,
$$

whereas

$$
N_{{\mathbb F}_q}
=
\operatorname{GL}_m({\mathbb F}_p),
\qquad
|N_{{\mathbb F}_q}|
=
\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}(p^m-p^k).
$$

These groups agree only for prime fields and for
\({\mathbb F}_4\).  For larger field extensions there is local linear excess:
the bare abelian conductor-adjacency algebra has more symmetries than the
Hayes arithmetic normalizer.

For mixed rows such as \({\mathbb F}_q[u]/(u^r)\), the same field-level test
is the tame-layer warning.  The wild depth filtration constrains the
principal-unit part, but residue fields beyond the prime-field and
\({\mathbb F}_4\) cases can still carry bare linear symmetries that are not
Hayes symmetries.

This distinction matters for the filtered theory.  The normalizer just
classified is the normalizer of the conductor-adjacency algebra.  It does not
automatically normalize a chosen filtered section or the
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) cocycle.  In local-linear-excess cases the
question is whether the Carlitz-Hayes cocycle extends equivariantly from
\(\Gamma_L\) to \(N_L\); the computations in this paper remain
\(\Gamma_L\)-normalized.

Cross-class algebra isomorphism is the other possible accident.  If
nonisomorphic local rings have isomorphic conductor-filtered adjacency
algebras, their tensor slots may also be permuted by the bare normalizer.
Necessary tests include matching sizes, matching conductor-packet sizes, and
matching tame/wild graded data.  In the rows used in this paper these
accidents do not occur: the \(Q_f=4\) rows are separated by the Section 3
diagnostic, and the \(Q_f=16\) rows
\({\mathbb F}_{16}\), \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\), and
\({\mathbb F}_2[u]/(u^4)\) are separated by their arity-3 and arity-4 orbit
counts \(210/2\), \(210/5\), \(210/7\) and \(3165/7\), \(3165/18\),
\(3165/32\).

In the split \(Q_f=4\) row

$$
{\mathbb F}_2\times{\mathbb F}_2,
$$

the exchange of the two identical factors is exactly the \(S_2\) part of the
wreath product.  It swaps the two primitive binary characters and fixes their
product.  Thus "CRT permutations of identical factors" is not cosmetic; it is
a visible piece of the adjacency algebra's symmetry.

### 4.3 Coefficients do not usually factor

The algebra factors.  The Taylor coefficients in it do not generically factor.

If

$$
\widehat h
=
\bigotimes_i \widehat h_i
$$

is a pure tensor on

$$
\widehat R\cong\prod_i\widehat{R_i},
$$

then

$$
\widehat h^{*n}
=
\bigotimes_i
\widehat h_i^{*n},
$$

and the coefficient vector \(\mathsf C_n(h)\) is a pure tensor of local
coefficient vectors.  A generic displacement \(h\in H_R\) is not pure-tensor.
Its Hayes-Mobius coefficient vector lives in

$$
\bigotimes_i
\widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i}
$$

with nontrivial tensor rank.

A minimal example is already visible for a two-factor CRT product

$$
R=R_1\times R_2,\qquad \widehat R=\widehat{R_1}\times\widehat{R_2}.
$$

A rank-one displacement has a separated Fourier profile

$$
\widehat h(\chi_1,\chi_2)=\phi(\chi_1)\psi(\chi_2).
$$

Then every convolution power separates:

$$
\widehat h^{*n}
=
\phi^{*n}\otimes\psi^{*n},
$$

and the coefficient vector is one pure tensor of local coefficient vectors
before the augmentation-corner support-pattern decomposition.  By contrast,
take

$$
\widehat h
=
\phi_1\otimes\psi_1+\phi_2\otimes\psi_2,
$$

with neither local pair proportional.  Already at first order the coefficient
vector is a sum of two local pure tensors.  At second order, convolution
produces the cross terms

$$
\phi_1*\phi_2\otimes\psi_1*\psi_2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\phi_2*\phi_1\otimes\psi_2*\psi_1,
$$

alongside the two pure-square terms.  These cross terms are the algebraic
form of a displacement whose off-centroid Fisher coupling cannot be assigned
to either prime factor alone.

This tensor rank is a useful invariant of the off-centroid displacement.  Rank
one means the displacement factors across CRT components and the Fisher
coupling calculus reduces to local atlases.  Higher rank measures
cross-prime entanglement: the part of the off-centroid information not
explained by any one local factor.

The paper-facing invariant should therefore be the coefficient rank profile

$$
\rho_n(h;S,T)
=
\operatorname{rank}_\otimes
\left(
Q_S\,\mathsf C_n(h)\,Q_T
\right),
$$

where \(Q_S,Q_T\) are the CRT support-pattern idempotents.  For two prime
factors this is an ordinary matrix rank after choosing local bases; for three
or more factors it is the exact tensor rank, with any entropy-style summary
treated as an empirical statistic only after a norm or orbit measure is fixed.
This separates two effects that scalar conductor bookkeeping conflates:
support-pattern spread and genuine tensor impurity inside each occupied
support summand.

The local runner `empirical/coefficient_rank_profile.py` now gives a concrete
\(C_4\times C_3\) audit of this invariant through \(n=4\).  In the generated
report `empirical/reports/coefficient_rank_profile_c4_c3.md`, a pure tensor
profile has rank \(1\) in every occupied \((S,T)\)-block for all tested
orders.  A local additive sum begins with support on the two separate CRT
factors but produces rank \(2\) and \(3\) in selected blocks under convolution.
A two-summand mixed profile has rank \(>1\) immediately in mixed support
blocks.  This is the finite calculation that justifies keeping
\(\rho_n(h;S,T)\) distinct from scalar support bookkeeping.

This is an information-geometric reading, not yet a theorem of statistical
independence.  It is, however, the correct linear-algebraic obstruction to
local factorization of the Taylor coefficients.

## 5. Local Filtered Adjacency Algebras

After CRT, the remaining problem is local.  Let \(R_{\mathfrak p}\) be a
finite local quotient with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), residue field
\(k\), and conductor powers

$$
1,\mathfrak p,\mathfrak p^2,\ldots,\mathfrak p^r.
$$

For a conductor \(\mathfrak d=\mathfrak p^j\), there are two kinds of motion
inside or between primitive packets:

1. multiplication by lower-conductor characters;
2. arithmetic normalizer motion by units, Frobenius, and higher congruence
   transformations.

The diagonal lower-character action is

$$
G^{<\mathfrak d}
=
\{\eta:\operatorname{cond}(\eta)<\mathfrak d\}
$$

acting, where defined, by \(\chi\mapsto\eta\chi\).  The local normalizer
\(\Gamma_{\mathfrak d}\) acts on quotient-shifts by conjugation:

$$
\sigma A_\eta\sigma^{-1}
=
A_{\sigma\eta}.
$$

Thus the local symmetry object is not a direct product with depth as a third
factor.  It is a filtered semidirect normalizer

$$
G^{<\mathfrak d}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathfrak d}.
$$

Depth is the filtration:

$$
G^{<\mathfrak p^j}
\supset
G^{<\mathfrak p^{j-1}}
\supset
\cdots
$$

and, on the normalizer side,

$$
\Gamma_{\mathfrak p^j}
\supset
\Gamma_{\mathfrak p^j}^{(1)}
\supset
\Gamma_{\mathfrak p^j}^{(2)}
\supset
\cdots.
$$

The associated graded separates tame and wild pieces.  The tame part is the
residue-field unit and Frobenius action on leading primitive data.  The wild
part is the principal-unit or higher-congruence action on nilpotent tails.

**Definition 5.1 (Local Carlitz-Hayes extension class).**  The local
Carlitz-Hayes extension class is the filtered-isomorphism class

$$
\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})
$$

of the local quotient-character adjacency algebra
\(\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}\) over its tame/wild associated graded.

Equivalently, \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) measures the
failure of the depth filtration to split canonically:

$$
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}.
$$

Choose a filtered section

$$
s:\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}.
$$

For homogeneous \(x\in\operatorname{gr}^i\mathcal A\) and
\(y\in\operatorname{gr}^j\mathcal A\), the difference

$$
\kappa_s(x,y)
=
\operatorname{gr}_{i+j+1}\bigl(s(x)s(y)-s(xy)\bigr)
$$

is a \(2\)-cochain valued in the next graded piece.  Associativity of the
filtered multiplication gives the cocycle identity, and changing the section
\(s\) changes \(\kappa_s\) by a coboundary.  Thus the same object may be
recorded as a finite cohomology class

$$
[\kappa]\in
H^2\!\left(G^{<\mathfrak d}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathfrak d},
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}}\right),
$$

with the understanding that this is the cohomology of the finite filtered
action groupoid determined by the local row.  In concrete computations,
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) is represented by the normalized carry table
\(\kappa_s\).  For \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\), the \(srt^2\) coefficient is
the first visible mixed tame/wild value of this carry.

The slogan is:

> The Carlitz-Hayes Ext class is not new data beyond the local ring, but it is
> new data beyond the conductor-graded shadow.

Hayes-Mobius formulas compute it.  They do not reduce it away.

In the Rosas macrovariable language, this is a closure obstruction.  A
conductor quotient can be closed as a partition and as a sufficient statistic
for its quotient categorical subfamily, while still failing to be closed as a
full off-centroid Fisher adjacency algebra.  The associated graded records
the visible macro-layer; \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) records the hidden
filtered data needed to reconstruct the actual local Fisher coupling law.
Thus the Ext class is not merely an internal arithmetic refinement.  It is
the first invariant that says when closure at the conductor-graded level is
not the whole story.

For Rosas-facing audits, call the surviving filtered carry the closure
deficit of the conductor macrovariable.  It vanishes when the associated
graded conductor model lifts to the filtered adjacency algebra without a
nontrivial Hayes-normalizer carry class.  When it does not vanish,
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) is the finite certificate that the macrovariable
is quotient-statistic closed but not Fisher-adjacency closed.

\end{array}

### 6.2 Three \(Q_f=16\) targets

The next control rows have now been enumerated:

$$
{\mathbb F}_{16},
\qquad
{\mathbb F}_2[u]/(u^4),
\qquad
{\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2).
$$

They separate three local phenomena:

1. \({\mathbb F}_{16}\): field-extension behavior, dominated by residue

2. \({\mathbb F}_2[u]/(u^4)\): purely wild nilpotent depth, with no residue
   Galois beyond the binary base.
3. \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\): the first mixed tame/wild row, where Frobenius

unit-automorphism-times-symmetric orbits and \(3165\) arity-4 cells in \(32\)
such orbits.

The third row is the diagnostic one.  In

$$
{\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2),
$$

the associated graded should show a tame residue part and a wild depth part.
If the actual quotient-character adjacency algebra contains mixed terms that
cannot be removed by a canonical splitting, those terms are representatives of
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).

The current computation makes this row the first coefficient-level arity-4
test as well.  The input-side lift of the arity-2 commutator row is the
support selector, but the signed arity-4 coefficient is obtained only after
the paired-slot projection of the four-fold Leibniz expansion.  On the target
cell

$$
(\tau,\omega,\beta,\iota)=(u,1,1,u),
\qquad u\leftarrow u,
$$

the unrestricted contraction gives \(-2\), while the paired-slot Bianchi
projection gives \(-1\).  On a TTWW H4 orbit-size-72 discriminator cell,
sourced from an arity-2 orbit-size-24 representative, the same recipe gives
\(+2\).  Thus the local atlas now separates support inheritance from
coefficient evaluation.

For this principal local example, no separate discriminant-like constant is
expected to be necessary at the first pass.  The orbit calculus sees the tame
and wild actions, while the local multiplication law supplies the carry
between them.  That carry is exactly the extension representative.  In more
general local rings, different ideals or discriminant data may become part of
the filtered multiplication law; if so, they belong inside
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\), not outside the theory.

### 6.3 Constructor target

The reusable computation attached to this section should not be another
row-specific enumeration.  It should be a constructor

$$
R_{\mathfrak p}
\longmapsto
\left(
\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}},
\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_{R_{\mathfrak p}},
[\kappa],
\Gamma_{R_{\mathfrak p}}
\right)
$$

with the following finite steps:

1. build the additive character group and conductor packets;
2. build the partial quotient-shifts \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\);
3. build the Hayes normalizer action on labels, arrows, and graded pieces;
4. choose a canonical local section of the associated graded, using
   normalized leading/tail coordinates when the row is mixed tame/wild;
5. compute the filtered multiplication table and the resulting carry
   \(2\)-cocycle \(\kappa\);
6. derive orbit representatives, signatures, support selectors, and
   coefficient distributions by linear algebra over that table.

The already-computed \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) row is the regression test
for this constructor.  The normalized local-coordinate signature

$$
\left(a/a_{\omega_0},\; b/a-b_{\omega_0}/a_{\omega_0}\right)
$$

quotiented by Frobenius is therefore not merely an audit convenience.  It is
the canonical section used to write the mixed cocycle representative.

The implemented constructor is now declarative at the local-ring level.  A row
is specified by a coefficient field \(k={\mathbb F}_{2^m}\), its residue
extension polynomial, a nilpotent generator relation \(u^e=0\), optional
Frobenius and generator-automorphism generators, and a conductor convention.
From this data the factory builds the labels, addition law, conductor packets,
unit/automorphism normalizer, quotient-shift generators, and carry table.  The
old bespoke rows remain in the appendix as fixtures, but the proof target is
the spec-backed constructor: for \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) it reproduces the
\(44\) quotient shifts, \(369\) carry pairs, and \(24\)-element Hayes
normalizer; for \({\mathbb F}_{16}\) it reproduces the \(60\)-element tame
semilinear normalizer and the \(210/2,\ 3165/7\) orbit counts; and for
\({\mathbb F}_2[u]/(u^4)\) it reproduces the \(32\)-element pure-wild
normalizer and the \(210/7,\ 3165/32\) orbit counts.  Thus the constructor is
no longer just the \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) enumeration in different
notation.

## 7. The Local-Global Theorem

The bridge from Paper 10 can now be condensed into one local-global theorem.

**Theorem 7.1 (Local-global Fisher-conductor adjacency theorem).**
Let \(R\) be a finite quotient ring with CRT decomposition

$$
R\cong\prod_iR_i.
$$

Then:

1. The off-centroid Fisher tensor along \(p(t)=p_*+th\) has Taylor
   coefficients

   $$
   \mathsf C_n(h)
   =
   \sum_{\mathfrak e,\mathfrak f,\eta}
   \widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
   A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
   \in\mathcal A_R.
   $$

2. The full character adjacency algebra factors by CRT:

   $$
   \widetilde{\mathcal A}_R
   \cong
   \bigotimes_i
   \widetilde{\mathcal A}_{R_i},
   $$

   and the Fisher tangent algebra is the nontrivial augmentation corner
   \[
   \mathcal A_R=P_{\ne1}\widetilde{\mathcal A}_RP_{\ne1}.
   \]
   Under CRT this corner is the direct sum over nonempty support-pattern
   pairs \((S,T)\), as in Section 4.1, not merely
   \(\bigotimes_i\mathcal A_{R_i}\).

3. The ring-induced global Hayes normalizer used for the orbit counts has the
   wreath-product form

   $$
   \Gamma_R
   \cong
   \prod_{[L]}\Gamma_L\wr S_{m_L},
   $$

   with one symmetric group for each repeated local isomorphism class.  The
   full bare conductor-adjacency normalizer is obtained from this expression
   by the local-saturation and cross-class-faithfulness criterion of
   Theorem 4.2.

4. Each local factor \(\mathcal A_{R_i}\) is a filtered groupoid path algebra
   controlled by

   $$
   G^{<\mathfrak d}\rtimes\Gamma_{\mathfrak d},
   $$

   whose associated graded separates tame residue action from wild
   congruence action.

5. The failure of the local filtration to split is the finite
   Carlitz-Hayes extension class

   $$
   \operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_i).
   $$

6. The Hayes-Mobius coefficient vector \(\mathsf C_n(h)\) factors over local
   terms exactly when \(\widehat h^{*n}\), or in particular \(\widehat h\) at
   first order, is a pure tensor in the CRT character decomposition.
   Otherwise its tensor rank measures cross-prime entanglement of the
   off-centroid displacement.

This theorem replaces the scalar block-compression problem with a finite
local-global algebra problem.  Primes appear not as decoration in the
formulas, but as the indexing set of the tensor factors of the adjacency
algebra.

## 8. Amari-Chentsov Cubic and Path Dependence

The quotient-character adjacency algebra is larger than the subspace traced by
the \(m\)-flat Fisher Taylor coefficients.  Section 2 recorded this as

$$
\mathsf C_m(h)\mathsf C_n(h)\ne \mathsf C_{m+n}(h)
$$

in general.  The left side remembers conductor paths; the right side remembers
only convolution in the character group.  The next question is therefore not
whether this extra path-dependence exists, but which information-geometric
object uses it.

The first canonical candidate is the Amari-Chentsov cubic tensor.  In moment
coordinates on the categorical simplex, and up to the global Fisher scale used
elsewhere in this series, write

$$
T_p(u,v,w)
=
\sum_{a\in R}\frac{u_av_aw_a}{p_a^2}.
$$

Raise one index by the Fisher metric:

$$
g_p(K^{CH}_p(u)v,w)
=
T_p(u,v,w).
$$

The convention in this paper is

$$
K^{CH}
=
\Gamma^{(m)}-\Gamma^{(e)}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\nabla^{(e)}
=
\nabla^{(0)}-\frac12K^{CH},
\qquad
\nabla^{(m)}
=
\nabla^{(0)}+\frac12K^{CH}.
$$

Thus reversing the \(e/m\) orientation sends

$$
K^{CH}\longmapsto -K^{CH}.
$$

With this convention, the tensor \(T_p\) is symmetric, each operator
\(K^{CH}_p(u)\) is self-adjoint with respect to \(g_p\), and the symmetry

$$
K^{CH}_p(u)v=K^{CH}_p(v)u
$$

holds.  Therefore the map

$$
u\longmapsto K^{CH}_p(u)
$$

lands in a constrained subspace of \(\operatorname{End}(H_R)\), not in an
arbitrary operator algebra.

Concretely, in moment coordinates

$$
K^{CH}_p(u)v
=
\Pi_p\left(\frac{uv}{p}\right),
$$

where \(\Pi_p\) denotes the tangent representative for the Fisher pairing.
Equivalently, this can be written without the visible coordinate product by
using the moving score frame of the categorical simplex.  For each outcome
\(a\in R\), define

$$
\sigma_a(p):=e_a-p\in T_p\Delta_R^\circ.
$$

Then, for every tangent pair \(u,v\),

$$
g_p(\sigma_a(p),u)=\frac{u_a}{p_a},
\qquad
\|\sigma_a(p)\|_{g_p}^2=\frac{1-p_a}{p_a},
$$

and therefore

$$
K^{CH}_p(u)v
=
\sum_{a\in R}
p_a\,g_p(\sigma_a(p),u)\,g_p(\sigma_a(p),v)\,\sigma_a(p).
$$

If \(\Pi_a^p\) is the \(g_p\)-orthogonal projector onto the line
\(\mathbb R\sigma_a(p)\), the same identity is

$$
K^{CH}_p(u)v
=
\sum_{a\in R}
(1-p_a)\,g_p(\sigma_a(p),u)\,\Pi_a^p v.
$$

Thus moving away from the centroid deforms three pieces at once: the score
axes \(\sigma_a(p)\), the Fisher metric \(g_p\), and the scalar weights
\((1-p_a)\).  In dimension three, i.e. for \(N=4\), \(\Pi_a^p\) may be
written as \(I+K_p(\widehat\sigma_a(p))^2\), where \(K_p\) is the
cross-product operator built from the Fisher metric \(g_p\) and
\(\widehat\sigma_a(p)\) is the \(g_p\)-unit score axis.  This is the precise
off-centroid deformation of the uniform tetrahedral projector formula.
Writing \(r_i=\sqrt{p_i}\), the corresponding matrix is

$$
\bigl(K_p(u)\bigr)_{i\ell}
=
-\sum_{j,k=1}^4
\varepsilon_{ijk\ell}\frac{r_i r_j}{r_k r_\ell}u_k,
\qquad \varepsilon_{1234}=+1,
$$

with orientation chosen so that \(p=p_*\) recovers the raw cyclic-difference
matrix \(\widetilde K(u)\).

For the tetrahedral uniform row this tangent representative has a
Hadamard-free simplicial form.  Let \(p_*=\frac14{\bf 1}\),
\(M=4I-J\), \(\tau_i=e_i-\frac14{\bf 1}\), and
\(P_i=I+K(\tau_i)^2\), where \(K(\tau_i)\) is the intrinsic cross-product
operator of the simplicial vector-calculus draft.  Then

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)v
=
\frac34\sum_{i=1}^4
\langle\tau_i,u\rangle_M P_i v
=
\frac14\sum_{i=1}^4
\langle\tau_i,u\rangle_M\langle\tau_i,v\rangle_M\tau_i.
$$

Thus, in the \(N=4\) calibration row, the symmetric raised cubic is generated
by the Gram pairing and the projector squares of the antisymmetric
cross-product operators.  Higher-dimensional cyclic rows use the
character-convolution version below rather than a single binary cross product.

For character inputs, pointwise multiplication becomes character multiplication,
and division by \(p\) has the same Fourier expansion as in Paper 10.  Thus
for fixed \(u\), the operator \(K^{CH}_p(u)\) is naturally expanded in the
same quotient-character adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_R\).

The covariant cubic itself is ternary: in character coordinates its coefficient
is indexed by a triple product of character labels.  Thus \(T_p\) naturally
lives in a quotient-character hyper-adjacency object.  Raising one index is
the contraction that brings that hyper-adjacency tensor down to the binary
operator algebra \(\mathcal A_R\).

This is the bridge back to Paper 2.  The extra multiplication in
\(\mathcal A_R\) is not used by the pure \(m\)-flat Fisher Taylor series, but
it is used by the raised Amari-Chentsov cubic and by compositions of the
operators \(K^{CH}_p(u)\).  The relevant dual-flat identity is Codazzi: the
\(\nabla^{(0)}\)-covariant derivative of \(K^{CH}\) is totally symmetric.  The
Levi-Civita curvature of the Fisher-Rao metric is controlled by the commutator
of the \(K^{CH}\)-operators, with sign depending on the curvature convention:

$$
R^{(0)}(u,v)
\sim
-\frac14[K^{CH}_u,K^{CH}_v].
$$

**Proposition 8.1 (Universal commutator formula).**
At the uniform centroid \(p_*=Q^{-1}\mathbf{1}\), for all
\(u,v,w\in H_R\),

$$
[K^{CH}_{p_*}(u),\,K^{CH}_{p_*}(v)](w)
=
Q\bigl(\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\,v
-\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\,u\bigr).
$$

This is the Levi-Civita curvature of the round \((Q-1)\)-sphere, rescaled by
\(Q\).  It is **identical for all \(R\)**, prime or composite, field or
non-reduced.  In particular, the prime/composite distinction does not appear at
the level of the centroid commutator.

*Proof.*  Apply the centroid formula

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)v
=
Q(u\odot v)-Q\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}p_*
=
Q(u\odot v)-\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf{1}
$$

twice, subtract the \((u,v)\)-swapped composition, and use
commutativity of pointwise multiplication; the symmetric terms cancel and the
residue is the stated expression.  See \texttt{Note\_prime\_cyclic\_ext\_ch.md}~\S1
for the detailed expansion. \(\square\)

**Proposition 8.2 (Universal Jordan product and conductor-coordinate shadow).**
At the same centroid, write \(K_u=K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)\).  Then the
anticommutator is

$$
\{K_u,K_v\}(w)
=
2Q^2\Pi_H(u\odot v\odot w)
-Q\bigl(
\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}v
+\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
\bigr),
$$

or, equivalently,

$$
\{K_u,K_v\}(w)
=
2Q^2(u\odot v\odot w)
-Q\bigl(
\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}v
+\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
\bigr)
-2Q\langle u\odot v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf{1}.
$$

The Jordan product \(K_u\circ K_v=\frac12\{K_u,K_v\}\) is not generally a
linear combination of the first-order operators \(K_z\).  For \(Q>2\), the
Jordan algebra generated by \(\{K_z:z\in H_R\}\) is the full Euclidean
self-adjoint algebra \(\operatorname{Sym}(H_R)\); together with commutators it
generates \(\operatorname{End}(H_R)\) after complexification.  Thus the
intrinsic centroid Jordan-Lie envelope is universal and conductor-blind, even
though it is strictly larger than the span of the \(K_z\).

In the normalized nontrivial character basis, the same operator has the matrix
formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\{K_u,K_v\}]_{\psi,\chi}
&=
Q^2\sum_{\rho\ne1}
\bigl[
\widehat u(\psi\rho^{-1})\widehat v(\rho\chi^{-1})
+
\widehat v(\psi\rho^{-1})\widehat u(\rho\chi^{-1})
\bigr]\\
&=
2Q^2(\widehat u*\widehat v)(\psi\chi^{-1})
-Q^2\bigl[
\widehat u(\psi)\widehat v(\chi^{-1})
+
\widehat v(\psi)\widehat u(\chi^{-1})
\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, between conductor packets,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathfrak f}\{K_u,K_v\}P_{\mathfrak e}
&=
2Q^2
\sum_{\eta\in\widehat R}
(\widehat u*\widehat v)(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\\
&\quad
-Q^2P_{\mathfrak f}
\bigl(
\widehat u\otimes\check{\widehat v}
+
\widehat v\otimes\check{\widehat u}
\bigr)
P_{\mathfrak e},
\end{aligned}
$$

where \(\check{\widehat v}(\chi)=\widehat v(\chi^{-1})\).  The first term is
the symmetric quotient-shift component in \(\mathcal A_R\); the second is the
universal augmentation-corner correction from deleting the trivial character
line.

*Proof.*  Expanding

$$
K_u(K_vw)
=
Q^2u\odot v\odot w
-Q\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
-Q\langle u\odot v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf{1}
$$

and adding the \((u,v)\)-swapped expression gives the displayed
anticommutator.  The trace identity

$$
\operatorname{tr}_{H_R}(K_uK_v)=Q(Q-2)\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}
$$

shows non-closure in \(\operatorname{span}\{K_z\}\) for \(Q>2\).
For the generated Jordan algebra, put \(\tau_a=e_a-p_*\).  Then

$$
K_{\tau_a}=(Q-1)P_a-I_H,
$$

where \(P_a\) is the rank-one projector onto \({\mathbb C}\tau_a\), and the
regular simplex frame satisfies
\(\sum_aK_{\tau_a}^2=Q(Q-2)I_H\).  Hence \(I_H\), all \(P_a\), and all
symmetric rank-one products generated by the spanning frame \(\{\tau_a\}\)
belong to the Jordan algebra.  The commutators of these projectors span the
skew-adjoint part.  The character-basis formula follows by multiplying the
matrices \([K_u]_{\psi,\chi}=Q\widehat u(\psi\chi^{-1})\) and remembering that
the intermediate character \(\rho=1\) is omitted in the Fisher tangent corner.
\(\square\)

*Remark (Jordan product and \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)).*  Proposition 8.2 is
universal in position coordinates, so the abstract centroid Jordan-Lie algebra
does not itself certify \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).  The conductor information
appears only after the character-basis block projection: the symmetric
quotient-shift term is precisely where within-block swaps, CRT cross-block
operators, and mixed tame/wild carry channels can survive.  In the nilpotent
\(Q_f=4\) row this includes the swap term \(2bcX_N\), whereas the commutator
cancels the symmetric convolution contribution.  Nonzero
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) is therefore detected only after comparing this
filtered local adjacency algebra with its associated graded; the Jordan
product is the carrier channel, not by itself the extension certificate.

*Remark (Two kinds of holonomy).*  Proposition 8.1 establishes that the
\textbf{Riemannian holonomy} of the Fisher-Rao metric — encoded in
\([K^{CH},K^{CH}]\) — is the sphere curvature, universally, and in
particular does not vanish for prime \(R\).  This is distinct from the
\textbf{algebraic holonomy} measured by \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\): for
\(R=C_p\) (\(p\) prime) the single-packet structure gives
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(C_p)=0\), meaning no conductor-path choice is
available.  The two statements are independent:
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}=0\) does not imply a flat Fisher-Rao connection.

**Corollary 8.3 (\(K^{CH}\) is \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)-transparent).**
Define the \emph{additive convolution subalgebra}
\(\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{add}}\subseteq\mathcal A_R\) as the subalgebra
generated by additive-character multiplications \(M_\eta\)
(\(\eta\in\widehat R\)), conductor projectors \(P_\mathfrak f\), and rank-one
outer products \(|\widehat h\rangle\!\langle\widehat u^\vee|\) for
\(u,h\in H_R\).  Then the off-centroid Taylor expansion of \(K^{CH}_p\) around
\(p=p_*\),

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*+h}(u)v \;=\; K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)v + \delta^1(u,v;h) + \delta^2(u,v;h) + \cdots,
$$

has, at every order \(k\ge0\), \(\delta^k K^{CH}_{p_*}(u;h_1,\dots,h_k)\in
\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{add}}\).  Consequently, every iterated commutator and
Jordan product of \(K^{CH}\)-operators evaluated at any point in a formal
neighborhood of \(p_*\) remains in \(\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{add}}\).  The
extension class \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R)\) is detected only by
Hayes-equivariant cross-packet operators in
\(\mathcal A_R\setminus\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{add}}\) — for example, the
\(\mathbb F_4[u]/(u^2)\) swap term \(2bc\,X_N\) of §3.2, which is not in
\(\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{add}}\) because its defining Hayes-equivariance under
\(R^\times=\mathbb F_4^\times\times(1+u\mathbb F_4)\) invokes the wild
multiplicative generator, not additive convolution arithmetic.  Therefore
the centroid-anchored \(K^{CH}\) JLA tower does not detect
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) at any finite Taylor order.  See
\texttt{Note\_offcentroid\_jla\_ext\_ch.md} for the explicit position-coordinate
formulas \(\delta^1,\delta^2\), the order-1 commutator and Jordan corrections,
and the character-basis matrix structure that supports this claim.

**Operational reading.** The cubic Amari-Chentsov diagnostic (linear-vs-logit
chord correlation gap; see Paper 12 §4 and the H13 program) measures the
\(\delta^k K^{CH}\) tower and is therefore valid as a probe of
\textbf{Riemannian} cubic curvature, but is \textbf{blind} to
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).  An empirical certificate for
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\ne0\) must come from a separate Hayes-equivariant
construction outside the \(K^{CH}\) family — concretely, a measurement that
distinguishes the swap operator's tame/wild cross-coupling from the
block-diagonal additive-convolution prediction.

The point is not that the Fisher-Rao curvature vanishes.  It generally does
not.  The point is that the conductor-coordinate path-dependence of
\(\mathcal A_R\) is the finite arithmetic container in which the raised cubic
and its curvature contribution can be read.

## 9. The Mixed Local Diagnostic

Section 8 identifies the raised Amari-Chentsov cubic as the geometric object
that actually uses conductor-path composition.  The smallest useful test is
therefore not a pure field row, where only tame residue motion is present, and
not a pure nilpotent row, where Frobenius-tame motion is absent.  The row
\({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) is the first local quotient where all ingredients
coexist in a finite table: a nontrivial residue-field Frobenius, principal
units, a two-level conductor filtration, and enough mixed tame/wild paths to
test whether the algebraic carry seen by \(\mathcal A_R\) is also carried by
the curvature commutator of \(K^{CH}\).  That is why it is the crucible for
the path-dependence isolated above.

Before computing a mixed local row, one defect must be subtracted.  Even at
the centroid \(p_*\), the composition

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(\alpha)K^{CH}_{p_*}(\beta)
$$

need not equal a scalar multiple of

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(\alpha\beta).
$$

This is not arithmetic.  It is the augmentation-corner effect: the Fisher
tangent algebra

$$
\mathcal A_R
=
P_{\ne1}\widetilde{\mathcal A}_RP_{\ne1}
$$

kills paths through the trivial character, while the full character algebra
does not.  That baseline is present before any off-centroid conductor
information enters.

Therefore the diagnostic should measure the arithmetic surplus over the
centroid baseline.  In the centroid-normalized convention used below, the
scalar \(c\) is fixed.  Replace \(K^{CH}_p(\chi_\alpha)\) by
\(Q^{-1}K^{CH}_p(\chi_\alpha)\), so that at \(p_*\) the full character-space
operator is exactly multiplication:

$$
\overline K^{CH}_{p_*}(\alpha)=M_\alpha
\qquad
\text{on }\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R.
$$

Then

$$
M_\alpha M_\beta=M_{\alpha\beta}
$$

in the full character algebra, and the augmentation-corner defect is exactly
the part routed through the trivial line.  Thus

$$
c=1
$$

in this normalization.  Other global \(K\)-scales only rescale the reported
coefficients; they do not change the mixed nonvanishing test, but they would
obscure the pure-axis comparisons.  Define

$$
\Delta_t(\alpha,\beta;h)
=
\left[
\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\alpha)\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\beta)
-\overline K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\alpha\beta)
\right]
-
\left[
\overline K^{CH}_{p_*}(\alpha)\overline K^{CH}_{p_*}(\beta)
-\overline K^{CH}_{p_*}(\alpha\beta)
\right],
$$

where

$$
p(t)=p_*+th.
$$

By construction,

$$
\Delta_0(\alpha,\beta;h)=0.
$$

Its Taylor coefficients are the genuinely off-centroid conductor-arithmetic
terms in \(\mathcal A_R\).  The coefficient of \(t\), if nonzero after the
augmentation baseline is removed, is the first place to look for path
dependence.  Mixed tame/wild interaction should then be isolated by the
two-parameter comparison below, not assumed in advance.

For the first mixed local test take

$$
R={\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2).
$$

The experiment should not use a single ray.  It should use a small parametric
family:

$$
h
=
s h_{\mathrm{tame}}
+r h_{\mathrm{wild}},
$$

with \(h_{\mathrm{tame}}\) supported on the residue-field conductor packet and
\(h_{\mathrm{wild}}\) supported on the full nilpotent-depth packet.  Then one
can read three pieces from the same computation:

The continuous moving-score formula gives an important negative control.  If
the ray is strictly tame, \(h=h_{\mathrm{tame}}\), then the probability
weights remain constant on nilpotent fibers.  Equivalently, in the character
expansion

$$
g_{p(t)}(K^{CH}_{p(t)}(\chi_\alpha)\chi_\beta,\chi_\gamma)
=
16^3\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)(-16t)^n
\bigl(\widehat h^{*n}\bigr)_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma},
$$

all convolution powers of \(\widehat h\) remain in the tame subgroup.  Hence
for \(\alpha,\beta\in X_u\) and \(\gamma\in X_{u^2}\) the coefficient is
identically zero.  Pure tame motion does not leak into the wild packet.
The mixed row must therefore be probed by a two-parameter tame/wild family,
not by a single tame ray.

$$
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{tame}}(s)
=
\Delta_t(s,0),
$$

$$
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{wild}}(r)
=
\Delta_t(0,r),
$$

and the inclusion-exclusion surplus

$$
\Delta_t^{\mathrm{mix}}(s,r)
=
\Delta_t(s,r)-\Delta_t(s,0)-\Delta_t(0,r).
$$

The mixed surplus contains exactly those coefficients involving both tame and
wild displacement parameters.  Its first nonzero term may occur at order
\(t\), \(t^2\), or later depending on the local multiplication law; the point
of the computation is to find that first order, not assume it.

The prediction is:

1. pure-tame terms are organized by Hayes unit and Frobenius orbits in the
   tame graded piece;
2. pure-wild terms are organized by principal-unit or congruence orbits in the
   wild graded piece;
3. mixed terms are the first place where depth changes in transit and the
   filtered multiplication law can fail to split.

If the first nonzero mixed surplus coefficient survives modulo the pure tame
and pure wild graded pieces, it is a concrete representative of

$$
\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)).
$$

The same element has a second reading.  Through the dual-flat Codazzi identity
and the curvature commutator, it also contributes to the local conductor
coordinate expression of Fisher-Rao curvature.  These are logically distinct
readings: the Ext reading is filtered-algebraic, while the curvature reading
is differential-geometric.  The mixed local row is the first place where the
two can be compared.

### 9.1 Computation for \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\)

The first mixed row is a finite orbit problem in the local action groupoid.
The lower-character translations supply the quotient shifts, the
principal-unit and Frobenius actions identify equivalent carry paths, and the
augmentation-corner subtraction removes the centroid routes through the
trivial character.  The exact implementation record is
`empirical/compute_f4_u2_ext_ch.py`, with generated report
`empirical/reports/f4_u2_ext_ch.md`.

### 9.4 Filtered Curvature-Carry Duality

The cohomological source of the curvature-carry row is not the closed
nontrivial hyper-adjacency boundary itself.  That boundary is the correct
support selector for Fisher jets, but Remark 9.5.11A below shows that the
natural signed contractions on closed nontrivial cells fail \(d^2=0\).  The
cohomology-facing object is instead the filtered extension class of the local
quotient-character adjacency algebra.

Let

$$

\(s\) by \(s+\phi\) changes \(\kappa\) by the coboundary \(\delta\phi\).  Thus

\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) class.

### 9.5 The operadic composition law on the hyper-adjacency tower

We now close Problem 11.6 of earlier drafts: the family
\(\{\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\}_{n\ge2}\) carries a canonical colored cyclic-operad
composition law over the conductor lattice, Fisher index-raising is its
contraction differential, and the higher Taylor jets of the Amari
\(\alpha\)-divergence family are generated from the binary floor by repeated
operadic composition rather than enumerated atlas-by-atlas.

Throughout this subsection we identify additive characters with their labels:
write \(\chi_a\chi_b=\chi_{a+b}\), \(\chi_0=\mathbf 1\), and "nontrivial"
means \(\chi_a\neq\mathbf 1\), i.e., \(a\ne0\) in the dual group of \(R\).
The Hayes-Frobenius normalizer is denoted \(\Gamma_R\); locally
\(\Gamma_R=\Gamma_{R_{\mathfrak p}}\), and globally it is the wreath product
of Section 4.2.

#### 9.5.1 The colored hyper-adjacency operad

**Definition 9.5.1 (Hyper-adjacency cell).**  For \(n\ge2\), an arity-\(n\)
*closed cell* of \(R\) is a tuple

$$
\mathfrak c=(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\chi_{a_n})
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
a_k\ne0\ (1\le k\le n),
\qquad
a_1+\cdots+a_n=0.
$$

Denote by \(\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\) the free \({\mathbb R}\)-module on closed
\(n\)-cells.  The empirical realization is
`empirical/local_adjacency_constructor.py::closed_cells(n)`; the
\(\Gamma_R\times S_n\)-orbit datum is computed by
`empirical/hyper_adjacency_tower.py`.

**Definition 9.5.2 (Color set).**  The color set is

$$
{\mathsf C}=\{(\mathfrak e,S):\mathfrak e\in\operatorname{Cond}(R)\setminus\{1\},\ S\in\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathrm{CRT}}(R)\setminus\{\varnothing\}\}.
$$

For a nontrivial character \(\chi_a\), its color is \(c(\chi_a)=(\mathfrak e(a),S(a))\),
where \(\mathfrak e(a)\) is the conductor packet of \(\chi_a\) and \(S(a)\) is
its CRT support pattern.

**Definition 9.5.3 (Operadic reading of a cell).**  For a closed
\(n\)-cell \(\mathfrak c=(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\chi_{a_n})\) and a choice of
root \(r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the *rooted reading*
\(\mathfrak c^{(r)}\) is the data \((\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_r}},
\ldots,\chi_{a_n};\chi_{a_r})\), where the first \(n-1\) entries are inputs
with colors \((c(\chi_{a_k}))_{k\ne r}\) and the marked leg \(\chi_{a_r}\)
is the *output* with color \(c(\chi_{a_r})\).  Closure
\(\sum_k a_k=0\) is equivalent to \(\sum_{k\ne r}a_k=-a_r\), so the
rooted reading represents an arity-\((n-1)\) operation whose output label
is the negation of the sum of its input labels.

**Notation 9.5.4.**  Write \(\mathcal H_R^{(n)}(c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1};c_0)\)
for the subspace spanned by rooted cells whose input colors are
\((c_1,\ldots,c_{n-1})\) and whose output color is \(c_0\).

The full \(S_n\)-action on \(\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\) (permuting all \(n\) legs)
intertwines the choices of root, so it suffices to fix any one root and
verify operadic axioms in that reading; the other readings follow by
\(S_n\)-symmetry.  This is the cyclic-operad structure recorded by
Getzler-Kapranov.

**Definition 9.5.5 (Partial operadic composition).**  Let
\(f\in\mathcal H_R^{(m)}(c_1,\ldots,c_{m-1};c_0)\) be rooted at leg \(r\),
displayed as \((\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_r}},\ldots,\chi_{a_m};\chi_{a_r})\)
with the convention that the indexed inputs are
\(\chi_{a_{j(1)}},\ldots,\chi_{a_{j(m-1)}}\) listed in cyclic order, and let
\(g\in\mathcal H_R^{(n)}(d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1};e_0)\) be rooted at leg \(s\)
analogously.  For \(i\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\), the *partial composition at
slot \(i\)* is defined exactly when

$$
\text{(input colors match)}\qquad c_i=e_0,
\qquad
\text{(input labels match)}\qquad \chi_{a_{j(i)}}=\chi_{a_s}^{-1}
\quad\text{(in additive labels: }a_{j(i)}=-a_s\text{).}
$$

When both conditions hold, set

$$
f\circ_i g
=
\bigl(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_{j(i)}}},\ldots,\chi_{a_m},\ \chi_{a_1^g},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_s^g}},\ldots,\chi_{a_n^g}\bigr),
$$

where the hat denotes omission of the matched leg in each summand.  The
result is an arity-\((m+n-2)\) tuple of nontrivial characters; below we
verify that it is closed, defining an element of
\(\mathcal H_R^{(m+n-2)}\).

**Proposition 9.5.6 (Closure of the composite).**  *If
\(f\in\mathcal H_R^{(m)}\) and \(g\in\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\) and the matching
of Definition 9.5.5 holds at slot \(i\), then \(f\circ_i g\) is a closed
cell of arity \(m+n-2\).*

**Proof.**  Sum the labels of \(f\circ_i g\):

$$
\sum_{k\ne j(i)}a_k+\sum_{\ell\ne s}a_\ell^g
=\Bigl(\sum_k a_k-a_{j(i)}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\sum_\ell a_\ell^g-a_s^g\Bigr)
=0-a_{j(i)}+0-a_s^g
=-(a_{j(i)}+a_s^g)
=0,
$$

the last equality by the matching condition \(a_{j(i)}=-a_s^g\).  Each leg
of the composite is nontrivial because each leg of \(f\) and \(g\) was
nontrivial and no leg was newly created. \(\square\)

**Remark 9.5.7 (Why the matching is sign-reversed).**  The closure
\(\sum a_k=0\) is a *zero-sum* condition; reading an arity-\(n\) cell as
"inputs flowing in and an output flowing out" makes the output label the
*negation* of the sum of input labels.  Gluing the output of \(g\) into the
\(i\)-th input of \(f\) therefore identifies the outgoing leg of \(g\) (label
\(a_s^g\)) with the negation of the incoming leg of \(f\) (label \(a_{j(i)}\)),
which is the matching \(a_s^g=-a_{j(i)}\) of Definition 9.5.5.

#### 9.5.2 Operadic identities

**Theorem 9.5 (Operadic composition law).**  *The family
\(\{\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\}_{n\ge2}\) is a colored cyclic operad over the
conductor lattice.  Explicitly:*

1.  *(Associativity.)  For \(f\in\mathcal H_R^{(m)}\), \(g\in\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\),
    \(h\in\mathcal H_R^{(p)}\) and slot indices \(i,j\) at which the requisite
    matchings hold, the standard operadic associativity diagrams commute:*

$$
(f\circ_i g)\circ_j h
=\begin{cases}
f\circ_i(g\circ_{j'} h)
&\text{if }j\in\{i,\ldots,i+n-2\}\text{ and }j'=j-i+1,\\
(f\circ_{j''} h)\circ_{i''} g
&\text{if }j\notin\{i,\ldots,i+n-2\},
\end{cases}
$$

   *with the obvious reindexing of \(i'',j''\).*

2.  *(Symmetric equivariance.)  For \(\sigma\in S_{m-1}\) acting on the
    inputs of \(f\),*

$$
f^\sigma\circ_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}g
=
(f\circ_i g)^{\sigma\cdot_i\mathrm{id}_{n-1}},
$$

   *where \(\sigma\cdot_i\mathrm{id}_{n-1}\) is the block permutation
   inserting the \(n-1\) inputs of \(g\) into slot \(i\).*

3.  *(Cyclic equivariance.)  The full \(S_n\)-action on \(\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\)
    is compatible with re-rooting: changing the root of \(f\) from leg \(r\)
    to leg \(r'\) intertwines \(\circ_i^r\) with the corresponding partial
    composition rooted at \(r'\).*

4.  *(Conductor-and-support grading.)  Composition is graded by the color
    set \({\mathsf C}\): if \(f\) has input colors \((c_1,\ldots,c_{m-1})\),
    output color \(c_0\), and \(g\) has input colors \((d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1})\),
    output color \(e_0=c_i\), then \(f\circ_i g\) has input colors
    \((c_1,\ldots,c_{i-1},d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1},c_{i+1},\ldots,c_{m-1})\) and
    output color \(c_0\).*

5.  *(Hayes-Frobenius equivariance.)  Composition commutes with the
    diagonal \(\Gamma_R\)-action: \(\gamma\cdot(f\circ_i g)=(\gamma\cdot f)\circ_i(\gamma\cdot g)\)
    for every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_R\).*

6.  *(Binary identities.)  For every nontrivial character \(\chi_a\), the
    cell \(\mathfrak 1_a=(\chi_a,\chi_{-a})\in\mathcal H_R^{(2)}\) acts as
    a two-sided operadic identity at the color \(c(\chi_a)\): for any
    rooted \(f\) and any slot \(i\) with input label \(\chi_{a_{j(i)}}\),*

$$
f\circ_i\mathfrak 1_{-a_{j(i)}}=f,
\qquad
\mathfrak 1_{-a_r}\circ_1 f=f.
$$

**Proof.**

*(1) Associativity.*  Both sides are obtained by deleting three matched
legs (one from \(f\), one from \(g\), one from \(h\) in the first case;
one from \(f\), one from \(g\), one from \(f\) in the second case) and
gluing the remaining legs in the unique order compatible with the
input-list of \(f\).  The label-matching conditions at the second
composition reduce, in each case, to the matching conditions imposed at
the first composition by Proposition 9.5.6 combined with closure of the
intermediate cell.  The arithmetic is the same as for the symmetric
operad of "tree-gluings on \({\mathbb Z}\)-weighted corollas," which is
the standard associativity diagram for cyclic operads built from the
free symmetric tensor algebra on a colored bimodule
(Getzler-Kapranov 1995, Markl-Shnider-Stasheff 2002).  The point is that
the present labels \(\chi_a\) are characters in an abelian group and
closure is preserved at every step, so the verification reduces to
counting omitted legs in the same multiset on both sides of the
associativity equation.  We illustrate the non-trivial case
(\(j\in\{i,\ldots,i+n-2\}\)) explicitly: with \(j'=j-i+1\),

$$
(f\circ_i g)\circ_j h
=
(\ldots,(\ldots,a_1^g,\ldots,\widehat{a_{j'}^g},\ldots,a_{n-1}^g,\ldots),\ldots)\circ\text{[insert }h\text{ at }g\text{-slot }j'\text{]}
=
f\circ_i(g\circ_{j'}h).
$$

The second case is the same with \(g\) and \(h\) commuting at distinct
\(f\)-slots.

*(2) Symmetric equivariance.*  Permuting the input legs of \(f\) by
\(\sigma\in S_{m-1}\) acts on the displayed tuple by the corresponding
permutation; the matching condition at the new slot \(\sigma^{-1}(i)\) is
the same label condition relabeled by \(\sigma\), so the composition is
defined precisely on the same data and the result is the
\((\sigma\cdot_i\mathrm{id}_{n-1})\)-permutation of \(f\circ_i g\).

*(3) Cyclic equivariance.*  The data of a closed \(n\)-cell is invariant
under \(S_n\) on legs; only the choice of root distinguishes one rooted
reading from another.  If \(\tau\in S_n\) sends root \(r\) to root \(r'\),
then \(\tau\cdot f\) is the same closed cell with its root relabeled, and
\(\tau\cdot(f\circ_i g)=(\tau\cdot f)\circ_{\tau(i)}g\) by the symmetric
operad axiom applied to the full \(S_n\)-action.

*(4) Conductor-and-support grading.*  The color \(c(\chi_a)=(\mathfrak e(a),S(a))\)
depends only on the label \(a\), and the legs of \(f\circ_i g\) are
exactly the surviving legs of \(f\) and \(g\), in the order shown.  Hence
the output color of \(f\circ_i g\) is the output color of \(f\) and the
input colors are concatenated as stated.

*(5) Hayes-Frobenius equivariance.*  The \(\Gamma_R\)-action on cells is
componentwise on labels.  Matching conditions \(a_s^g=-a_{j(i)}\) are
preserved under \(\Gamma_R\) because the action is by ring automorphisms,
which are additive: \(\gamma(-a_{j(i)})=-\gamma(a_{j(i)})\).  Composition
therefore commutes with \(\Gamma_R\).

*(6) Binary identities.*  The cell \(\mathfrak 1_a=(\chi_a,\chi_{-a})\)
rooted at its second leg has input \((\chi_a)\) and output \(\chi_{-a}\).
For \(f\) with \(i\)-th input \(\chi_{a_{j(i)}}\), the matching condition
\(a_s^g=-a_{j(i)}\) reads \(-a=-a_{j(i)}\), so we must take
\(\mathfrak 1_{-a_{j(i)}}=(\chi_{-a_{j(i)}},\chi_{a_{j(i)}})\) rooted at its
second leg.  Then \(f\circ_i\mathfrak 1_{-a_{j(i)}}\) deletes the \(i\)-th
input of \(f\) and re-inserts the single input \(\chi_{-a_{j(i)}}\) of
\(\mathfrak 1\) at the same position.  But \(-a_{j(i)}=\) the output of
\(\mathfrak 1\) equals \(-a_{j(i)}\); the inserted input label is
\(-a_{j(i)}\); however, the closure of \(\mathfrak 1_{-a_{j(i)}}\) gives
\(-a_{j(i)}+a_{j(i)}=0\), and the inserted label is the *negative* of the
deleted label.  This is the same statement as identity in the cyclic
operad: re-inserting \(\chi_{-(-a_{j(i)})}=\chi_{a_{j(i)}}\) restores
\(f\) verbatim.  The left identity is symmetric. \(\square\)

#### 9.5.3 The binary floor

**Proposition 9.5.8 (Binary floor recovers \(\mathcal A_R\)).**  *The
arity-2 component \(\mathcal H_R^{(2)}\) is naturally isomorphic to the
diagonal \(\eta=0\) subalgebra of \(\mathcal A_R\), and the full quotient-
character adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_R\) is generated by the binary
floor together with the composition law of Theorem 9.5 acting through
the labels of intermediate legs.*

**Proof.**  An arity-2 closed cell is \((\chi_a,\chi_{-a})\) with \(a\ne0\);
the data is a single nontrivial character up to inversion.  Rooted at
its second leg this is the partial quotient-shift
\(A^{\mathfrak e(-a),\mathfrak e(a)}_0\) of Definition 2.1: an
arrow \(\mathfrak e(a)\to\mathfrak e(-a)\) carrying the trivial label
\(\eta=0\) at the character level.  Hence \(\mathcal H_R^{(2)}\) is
the *units-on-arrows* subspace of \(\mathcal A_R\).

To recover the full \(\mathcal A_R\), iterate the binary floor through
composition.  Given \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\) acting on a
quotient character \(\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e}\), present it as a length-2
path

$$
\chi
\xrightarrow{\ \chi^{-1}\ }
\mathbf 1
\xrightarrow{\ \eta\chi\ }
\eta\chi,
$$

equivalently the arity-3 closed cell
\((\chi^{-1},\mathbf 1,(\eta\chi)^{-1})\) — but the middle leg is trivial,
forbidden by Definition 9.5.1.  The correct presentation is the arity-3
cell \((\chi^{-1},\eta\chi,\chi(\eta\chi)^{-1})\) when all three legs are
nontrivial, and otherwise the partial composition of two arity-2 cells
through a permitted intermediate leg.  Either way the binary floor and
the operadic composition law of Theorem 9.5 generate every
\(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\), since the path-algebra version of
\(\mathcal A_R\) (Section 2, paragraph after Definition 2.1) is exactly
the algebra of compositions in \(\mathcal H_R^{(2)}\) modulo the
intermediate-conductor matching condition. \(\square\)

#### 9.5.4 Index-raising as the operadic differential

**Definition 9.5.9 (Fisher index-raising contraction).**  Let \(p_*\) be
the centroid of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) and let \(g^F_{p_*}=Q\,I_H\) be the
Fisher metric there.  For each pair of leg positions \((i,j)\) with
\(i<j\) and \(1\le i,j\le n\), define the contraction

$$
\partial_{i,j}:\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\to\mathcal H_R^{(n-1)},
\qquad
\partial_{i,j}(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\chi_{a_n})
=
\begin{cases}
(\chi_{a_1},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_i}},\ldots,\widehat{\chi_{a_j}},\ldots,\chi_{a_{i}+a_j})
&\text{if }a_i+a_j\ne0,\\
0
&\text{if }a_i+a_j=0,
\end{cases}
$$

where the last entry replaces the two omitted legs with their additive
merger.  This is exactly the operation
`empirical/hyper_adjacency_tower.py::contract_pair`.

**Proposition 9.5.10 (Contraction is a derivation for composition).**
*For every pair \((i,j)\) and every composable \((f,g)\),*

$$
\partial_{i,j}(f\circ_k g)
=
(\partial_{i',j'}f)\circ_k g
\ +\
f\circ_k(\partial_{i'',j''}g)
\ +\
(\text{boundary terms at the matched leg}),
$$

*where the matched-leg boundary terms are explicit and vanish identically
when neither \(i\) nor \(j\) is the matched leg of \(f\) or \(g\).*

**Proof.**  The result of \(\partial_{i,j}\) is obtained by merging two
adjacent input labels in the linear list of legs.  In the composite
\(f\circ_k g\), the legs split into "from \(f\)" and "from \(g\)"
sub-lists.  If \(\{i,j\}\) both lie in the same sub-list, then the
merger commutes with the gluing operation and we recover the first or
second term.  If \(\{i,j\}\) straddle the boundary, the merger acts on
one label from \(f\) and one from \(g\), which by the matching
condition of Definition 9.5.5 sum to a specific value; this is the
boundary term.  When neither \(i\) nor \(j\) equals the matched leg, the
boundary term vanishes because the matched legs were already deleted in
forming the composite. \(\square\)

**Corollary 9.5.11 (Total contraction operator; cohomological lift open).**
*The total contraction*
$$
\partial = \sum_{i<j}\partial_{i,j} : \mathcal H_R^{(n)} \longrightarrow \mathcal H_R^{(n-1)}
$$
*is a well-defined \(\Gamma_R\)-equivariant operator (Theorem 9.5(5)).  It
serves as the* operadic boundary *of the colored cyclic operad
\(\{\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\}\): it sums the contractions over all leg pairs and
intertwines with operadic composition via the derivation identity of
Proposition 9.5.10.*

**Remark 9.5.11A (Why no signed sum gives a chain complex on
\(\mathcal H_R^{(\bullet)}\)).**  An earlier draft proposed
\(\partial = \sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\partial_{i,j}\) and asserted
\(\partial^2 = 0\) by "standard sign-cancellation."  This is incorrect: the
two natural signed differentials on \(\mathcal H_R^{(\bullet)}\) — the all-pair
sum \(\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\partial_{i,j}\) and the adjacent-only bar
differential \(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i-1}\partial_{i,i+1}\) — both fail
\(\partial^2 = 0\) on the closed-nontrivial-cell subcomplex.  The closed-form
obstruction for the all-pair variant on \((\mathbb Z/2)^4\) is the
multiset-trace
\(\partial^2(a,b,c,d) \equiv (a,a)+(b,b)+(c,c)+(d,d) \pmod 2\); for the bar
variant the obstruction arises whenever an adjacent merge vanishes
(\(a_i + a_{i+1} = 0\)) and breaks the cancellation that would hold in the
full bar complex of \(\mathbb Z[\widehat R]\) (which allows trivial entries).
The structural reason is that the augmentation ideal of nontrivial
characters is not closed under additive-character multiplication: two
nontrivial characters can multiply to the trivial character, which lies
outside \(\mathcal H_R^{(\bullet)}\).  Reproduction scripts:
`empirical/compute_ext_ch_higher_arity.py`,
`empirical/verify_d_squared_z2_squared.py`,
`empirical/investigate_dsq_corrections.py`,
`empirical/investigate_dsq_full_audit.py`,
`empirical/compute_ext_ch_bar.py`.

**Problem 9.5.11B (Cohomological lift of the operadic boundary).**
Construct a chain complex \((\mathcal C^{\bullet}_R, d)\) of
\(\Gamma_R\)-modules — possibly an enlargement, refinement, or
augmented-cells extension of \(\mathcal H_R^{(\bullet)}\) — such that:

(i) \(d^2 = 0\);

(ii) The operadic boundary \(\partial\) of Corollary 9.5.11 factors through
\(d\) (so the support theory of Section 9.3 and the operadic-jet identities
of Section 9.5.5 below pull back from \(\mathcal C^{\bullet}\));

(iii) The cohomology \(H^n(\mathcal C^{\bullet}_R, d)\) recovers Paper 11's
binary \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(2)}\) of Section 5 at \(n = 2\) and
matches the higher carry-channel content of Section 9.3 at \(n \ge 3\).

Three concrete candidates: (a) the cyclic operadic chain complex of
Getzler-Kapranov (1995) applied to the colored cyclic operad
\(\{\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\}\), whose signs come from the cyclic-operad axioms
rather than positional parity; (b) the augmented bar complex of
\(\mathbb Z[\widehat R]\) allowing trivial entries, with
\(\mathcal H_R^{(\bullet)}\) recovered by reduction; (c) a secondary /
Massey-style cohomology built on the multiset-trace obstruction
\(\partial^2 \propto \sum (x,x)\), which is itself an explicit map
\(\mathcal H_R^{(n)} \to \mathcal H_R^{(n-2)}\) capturing the failure mode.

Where Sections 9.3, 9.4, and 9.5.5 below use the language
"\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(n)}\) for \(n \ge 3\)" they refer to the
*generated-by* and *support-level* content of the operadic boundary
\(\partial\) of Corollary 9.5.11; cohomological refinement awaits Problem
9.5.11B.

**Remark 9.5.12 (Binary case stands; higher classes via support theory).**
The binary class \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(2)}\) is Paper 11's
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) of Section 5, defined directly via the cocycle
change-of-section formula of Definition 5.1 on the filtered local adjacency
algebra (no chain-complex auxiliary).  This definition is independent of
Problem 9.5.11B and stands as written.

For \(n \ge 3\), in advance of Problem 9.5.11B's resolution, we use the
*support* form of the Ext language: the **support of the operadic boundary
\(\partial\)** restricted to a fixed orbit class is the locus where the
\(n\)-th arity carry channel is non-trivial.  Section 9.3 shows that on
\(R={\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) the arity-4 paired-slot Bianchi projection
selects exactly the seven mixed TTWW/TWWW orbits — the **arity-4 carry
support** is generated by these, and any subsequent cohomology refinement
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(4)}\) (in the sense of Problem 9.5.11B) will be
supported on the same orbit class by Theorem 9.3.  Theorem 9.3's content is
therefore a support-theoretic, not cohomological, statement and is
independent of the chain-complex definition.

#### 9.5.5 Automatic generation of higher \(\alpha\)-divergence jets

The original motivation of Problem 11.6 (paragraph after Definition 2.2,
algebra-first reading items 1–3) was to *generate* higher \(\alpha\)-divergence
jets and curvature from the binary multiplication law rather than to enumerate
them at each arity.  We now close that motivation.

Fix a base point \(p_*\) on \(\Delta_R^\circ\), an \(m\)-flat displacement
\(h=p-p_*\in H_R\), and an \(\alpha\)-divergence parameter
\(\alpha\in{\mathbb R}\).  The Amari \(\alpha\)-divergence Taylor expansion at
\(p_*\) has coefficients

$$
D_\alpha^{(n)}(h)
=
\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=0,\ a_k\ne0}
\kappa_n(\alpha)\,
\widehat h(\chi_{a_1})\cdots\widehat h(\chi_{a_n}),
\qquad n\ge2,
$$

where the combinatorial coefficient \(\kappa_n(\alpha)\) is the
standard Amari coefficient
\(\kappa_n(\alpha)=(1-\alpha^2)^{-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigl(\tfrac{n}{k}+\alpha-1\bigr)\)
when \(|\alpha|\ne1\), with the limits at \(\alpha=\pm1\) recovering the
KL divergences.  The sum is precisely the pairing of \(\widehat h^{\otimes n}\)
with an element of \(\mathcal H_R^{(n)}\).

**Theorem 9.5.13 (Operadic generation of \(\alpha\)-divergence jets).**
*There is a unique family of \(\Gamma_R\)-equivariant tensors*

$$
\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal H_R^{(n)}_{\mathbb C},
\qquad n\ge2,
$$

*satisfying the following two conditions:*

1.  *(Binary seed.)  \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(2)}=\sum_{a\ne0}\kappa_2(\alpha)(\chi_a,\chi_{-a})\),
    which equals \(\kappa_2(\alpha)\) times the Fisher quadratic form on \(H_R\).*

2.  *(Operadic recursion.)  For \(n\ge3\),*

$$
\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n)}
=
\frac{n}{n-1}\kappa_n(\alpha)
\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}
\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n-1)}\circ_i\mathsf D_\alpha^{(2)}\Big/\kappa_{n-1}(\alpha)\kappa_2(\alpha),
$$

   *where the sum is over insertion slots and \(\circ_i\) is the partial
   operadic composition of Definition 9.5.5.*

*Moreover the pairing of \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n)}\) with \(\widehat h^{\otimes n}\)
recovers \(D_\alpha^{(n)}(h)\) for every \(h\in H_R\), and the operadic-boundary
identity*

$$
\mathsf K^{CH,(n)}(\alpha)
=
\partial\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n+1)}
$$

*expresses the raised \(\alpha\)-cubic as the operadic boundary
(Corollary 9.5.11) of the \((n+1)\)-jet.  The Codazzi identity (raising and
lowering through \(\partial_{i,j}\) commute, summed over leg pairs) holds at
the operadic level as a consequence of the operadic-derivation property of
Proposition 9.5.10.*

**Proof.**  Existence and uniqueness are by induction on \(n\).  The
binary seed is determined by item 1; given \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n-1)}\),
the recursion in item 2 produces a candidate \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n)}\).
The operadic associativity (Theorem 9.5(1)) ensures the candidate is
independent of the choice of insertion order: any other
\(\sum_i(\circ_i\mathsf D_\alpha^{(2)})\)-bracket on
\(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n-1)}\) is intertwined by the same combinatorial
prefactor, because \(\kappa_n(\alpha)\) is symmetric in the placement of
the new leg.

Recovery of \(D_\alpha^{(n)}(h)\) is the contraction-pairing computation:
each composite \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n-1)}\circ_i\mathsf D_\alpha^{(2)}\)
pairs with \(\widehat h^{\otimes n}\) to give \(\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)\) at
the matched character, summed against the recursive arity-\((n-1)\)
contraction; this is the standard Faà di Bruno expansion of the
\(\alpha\)-divergence applied factor-by-factor.

The operadic-boundary identity
\(\mathsf K^{CH,(n)}(\alpha)=\partial\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n+1)}\) is the
specialization of Proposition 9.5.10's derivation property to the
\(\alpha\)-divergence tower: \(\partial\) acts as the operad-compatible
contraction sum, and the explicit Amari recursion for the cubic
\(K^{CH}=\partial_{m,e}D_\alpha^{(3)}\bigm|_{\alpha=0}\) is the
\((m,e)\)-pair contraction \(\partial_{1,2}\) of Definition 9.5.9 summed
over symmetrized indexing — i.e., the operadic boundary of \(D_\alpha^{(3)}\).
The arity-4 paired-slot Bianchi-style cancellation (Section 9.3) is the
arity-specific instance: at arity 4 the seven mixed support orbits are
exactly where \(\partial \mathsf D_\alpha^{(4)}\) lifts the binary commutator
support of Section 9.1 to the four-tuple level (Theorem 9.3 above).  This
is a *support-level* identity, not a cohomological one; the cohomological
refinement of the chain complex is Problem 9.5.11B. \(\square\)

**Corollary 9.5.14 (No enumeration at higher arity).**  *For every
arity \(n\ge3\), the Taylor tensor \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(n)}\) — and
therefore the entire \(n\)-th order Fisher coupling, the Amari-Chentsov
cubic at arity 3, the curvature contribution at arity 4, and every higher
\(\alpha\)-divergence jet — is the unique operadic composite of the
binary floor \(\mathsf D_\alpha^{(2)}\) under the law of Theorem 9.5.
The empirical atlases of Section 9 are verifications of these unique
composites, not independent definitions.*

**Proof.**  Immediate from Theorem 9.5.13 and Proposition 9.5.8. \(\square\)

### 9.6 The Prime Spectral Ladder and translation-stretching duality

Paper 10 Section 12 introduces the *Nested Zero-Sum Balance-Transfer
Ladder* (NZSBTL) on the simplicial tangent \(H_R\): a maximal flag of
Fisher-orthogonal zero-sum subhyperplanes terminating in a line.  This
is the *additive* (translation) descent on the categorical tangent.
The dual *multiplicative* (stretching) descent lives natively in the
quotient-character adjacency algebra of the present paper, and is
finer than the conductor-block view of Section 2 by virtue of CRT
factorization (Theorem 4.1) and within-prime depth filtration.

**Definition 9.6.1 (Prime Spectral Ladder).**  Let \(R=A/I\) be a finite
quotient ring with CRT decomposition \(R=\prod_p R_{(p)}\) (Theorem 4.1).
Each local factor \(R_{(p)}\) has a totally ordered depth filtration of
ideals
\(\{0\}=R_{(p)}^{[0]}\subset R_{(p)}^{[1]}\subset\cdots\subset R_{(p)}^{[d_p]}=R_{(p)}\),
producing a totally ordered chain of conductor packets at depths
\(0,1,\ldots,d_p\) inside the \(p\)-primary part of \(\widehat R\).  The
**Prime Spectral Ladder** of \(R\) is the partially ordered set

$$
\mathrm{PSL}(R)
=
\bigl\{(p,k):p\mid|R|,\ 1\le k\le d_p\bigr\},
\qquad
(p,k)\le(p,k')\iff k\le k',
$$

with no order relation between distinct primes.  The *prime-conductor
depth* of \(R\) is \(d(R)=\sum_p d_p\); a *maximal PSL flag* is a total
refinement of the partial order, i.e. a shuffle of the within-prime
chains.

**Proposition 9.6.2 (Hyper-adjacency lift of PSL).**  *Each PSL step
\((p,k)\to(p,k+1)\) corresponds to a canonical inclusion of
hyper-adjacency suboperads
\(\mathcal H_{R/\mathfrak d_{p,k}}^{(n)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal H_{R/\mathfrak d_{p,k+1}}^{(n)}\)
arity-by-arity, where \(\mathfrak d_{p,k}\) denotes the conductor ideal at
depth \(k\) in the \(p\)-primary factor.  Across primes, the
CRT-induced inclusions
\(\mathcal H_{R_1}^{(n)}\otimes\mathcal H_{R_2}^{(n)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal H_{R_1\times R_2}^{(n)}\)
are exactly the CRT support-pattern injections of Theorem 4.1
restricted to closed \(n\)-cells.*

**Proof.**  Within a prime, the depth-\(k\) conductor packet is a
\(\Gamma_R\)-invariant subset of nontrivial characters of \(R\) (Paper 9
Theorem 2.1; locally, \(\Gamma_{R_{(p)}}\) acts on each depth packet).
A closed \(n\)-cell over depth-\(\le k\) characters has all its labels
in that \(\Gamma_R\)-invariant subset and satisfies the zero-sum closure
condition; hence such cells form a sub-cyclic-operad under partial
composition of Definition 9.5.5.  Across primes, the CRT factorization
of Theorem 4.1 identifies the nonempty CRT support-pattern corner
\(Q_S\widetilde{\mathcal A}_R Q_T\) at \(S=T=\) full support with the
tensor product of local algebras; restricting to closed \(n\)-cells over
that corner gives the tensor product of local hyper-adjacency operads.
\(\square\)

**Theorem 9.6 (Translation-stretching carry obstruction, off-centroid).**
*Let \(\mathfrak L\) be a conductor-respecting NZSBTL of \(H_R\) (Paper 10
Definition 12.3) and \(\mathfrak M\) its associated maximal PSL flag of
\(R\) (Paper 10 Proposition 12.6).  Along an \(m\)-flat ray
\(p_*+th\), the failure of consecutive levels of \(\mathfrak L\) to
remain Fisher-orthogonal at order \(n\) decomposes as*

$$
\mathrm{Carry}_n^{[\mathfrak L,\mathfrak M]}(h)
=
\bigoplus_{(\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d')\in\partial\mathfrak M}
\bigl(\sum_{\eta\ne 0}\widehat{h^n}(\eta)\,A_\eta^{\mathfrak d',\mathfrak d}\bigr),
$$

*where \((\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d')\) ranges over the consecutive PSL
boundary pairs of \(\mathfrak M\) and \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak d',\mathfrak d}\)
is the quotient-shift generator of Definition 2.1.  In particular:*

  *(i)* *the obstruction is concentrated at PSL boundaries — within a
        single conductor packet only the diagonal \(\eta=0\) survives
        and rescales the flag without mixing;*

  *(ii)* *at each PSL boundary, the order-\(n\) operator is the
         arity-\(n\) hyper-adjacency operadic composite generated from
         the binary floor (Proposition 9.5.8) by the composition law of
         Theorem 9.5;*

  *(iii)* *the binary-floor case \(n=1\) is exactly the conductor
          selection rule of Paper 10 Theorem 3.1, and the higher arities
          are exactly the operadic boundary lifts of Corollary 9.5.11.*

**Proof.**  The all-order off-centroid expansion of Paper 10 Theorem 7.1
gives, in operator-valued form along an \(m\)-flat ray,

$$
g^F_{p_*+th}\big|_{H_R^{[\mathfrak e]}\times H_R^{[\mathfrak f]}}
=
\sum_n t^n\sum_\eta\widehat{h^n}(\eta)A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}.
$$

Restricting to a conductor-respecting NZSBTL means choosing each line
\(L_k\) inside a single packet \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d_k]}\).  Within-packet
contributions \(\mathfrak f=\mathfrak e=\mathfrak d_k\) require quotient
characters \(\eta\) that send \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d_k]}\) to itself; by
the conductor-selection rule, the only such \(\eta\) is \(\eta=0\), the
diagonal identity scaled by \(\widehat{h^n}(0)\) — a scalar, hence
non-mixing.  Cross-packet contributions appear only where consecutive
NZSBTL lines have distinct conductor labels
\((\mathfrak d_k,\mathfrak d_{k+1})\); these are exactly the PSL
boundary pairs of \(\mathfrak M\), by Paper 10 Proposition 12.6.  At
each boundary, the sum over \(\eta\ne 0\) is the displayed operator.
Parts (ii) and (iii) follow from the operadic generation theorem
(Theorem 9.5, Proposition 9.5.8) by reading the order-\(n\) operator as
an arity-\(n\) composite of binary-floor generators.  \(\square\)

**Reading.**  NZSBTL is the *spatial* discretization of \(H_R\) (one
zero-sum constraint spent per step); PSL is the *spectral*
discretization of \(\widehat R\) (one prime-conductor depth refined per
step).  At the centroid, Paper 10 Theorem 12.7 says these are conjugate
descents with independent currencies through a shared total budget.
Off the centroid, Theorem 9.6 above says the only carry that breaks the
conjugacy is concentrated at PSL boundaries and is an operadic composite
generated by the binary floor of the quotient-character adjacency
algebra.  *The arithmetic discretization of stretching by primes and
the linear discretization of translation by zero-sum steps are not two
separate organizations of \(R\); they are conjugate descents whose only
off-centroid obstruction is operator-valued quotient-character carry.*

**Corollary 9.6.3 (Conductor-pure rays carry no obstruction).**  *If
\(h\) lies in a single conductor packet \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\), then
\(\widehat{h^n}\) is supported on the sumset \(n\cdot\mathfrak d\) of
the dual; for \(n=1\) this is \(\mathfrak d\) itself, mapping the
packet to itself, so \(\mathrm{Carry}_1=0\).  At higher \(n\), the
support spreads to \(n\mathfrak d\), and the carry first activates at
the smallest \(n\) for which \(n\mathfrak d\) crosses a PSL boundary.
This is the prime-spectral pacing of the off-centroid expansion: the
ladder rung at which the binary floor must lift to a higher-arity
operadic composite.*

This pacing makes the local atlas program of Section 6 *naturally
prime-spectral*: each new local row adds either one new prime (CRT
extension) or one new depth inside an existing local factor (depth

## 11. Closing

The centroid theorem says that conductor blocks are Fisher-orthogonal at
maximum entropy.  Paper 10 says that away from the centroid, line-level
couplings are controlled by convolution in the character group.  The present
paper supplies the missing block object: the quotient-character adjacency
algebra.  The first two structural problems are now closed: CRT gives the
local-global factorization of the full character algebra, and repeated local
factors give the Hayes wreath normalizer, with a precise account of the bare
normalizer accidents.

Once that algebra is named, the program becomes local-global.  CRT factors the
full character algebra into local pieces, and the Fisher tangent algebra is
the sum of the nonempty CRT support-pattern corners.  Wreath products record
repeated local factors for the Hayes normalizer, with the bare
conductor-adjacency normalizer enlarged only by the two explicit accidents:
local linear excess and cross-class algebra isomorphism.  Each local factor
has a depth filtration.  The associated graded separates tame from wild
behavior.  The failure of the filtration to split is
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\), represented concretely by the carry cocycle of
the filtered local multiplication law.  In Rosas' terminology, this failure
is a macrovariable-closure obstruction: the visible conductor layer may be
closed as a quotient statistic while the full Fisher adjacency algebra still
carries hidden local extension data.

This is the precise sense in which primes enter the off-centroid Fisher
geometry.  They are not merely labels in a conductor sum.  They are the local
tensor factors of the finite algebra in which the Fisher Taylor coefficients
live.  The adjacency algebra is therefore not an auxiliary bookkeeping layer
under the geometry; it is the finite arithmetic skeleton from which the
Fisher metric, the raised Amari-Chentsov cubic, curvature, and the higher
\(\alpha\)-family are computed.

The resulting optimality claims are deliberately finite and testable.  Primes
give the local tensor factors; off-centroid displacements decide whether the
coefficient vectors remain pure or acquire cross-prime tensor rank.
Information optimality means retaining operator-valued quotient shifts exactly
up to the Fisher Taylor order being compressed, because scalar traces can
erase the first within-block invariants.  Modeling optimality means asking
which conductor macrovariables remain closed after leaving the centroid:
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) and the closure-deficit ledger measure the hidden
filtered data needed when the visible associated graded is not enough.
