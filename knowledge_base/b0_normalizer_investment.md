# B3 Normalizer-Saturated Information Investment

Created: 2026-05-14

B3 separates two kinds of information investment:

- **B2 minimal prediction investment:** buy the subgroup generated by the active
  reciprocal spectrum.
- **B3 symmetry-complete investment:** buy the subgroup generated by the
  normalizer orbit of the active reciprocal spectrum.

## Theorem

Let \(G\) be a finite character group with normalizer action
\(\Gamma\le \operatorname{Aut}(G)\).  For a nonempty reciprocal support
\(S\subset G\setminus\{0\}\), the minimum \(\Gamma\)-invariant B0 support is

```text
D_G^Gamma(S) = < Gamma . S > \ {0}.
```

It is the unique minimum-resource support that:

1. contains \(S\);
2. is additively closed;
3. is \(\Gamma\)-invariant.

## Symmetry Premium

The difference

```text
premium(S) = |D_G^Gamma(S)| - |D_G(S)|
```

is the extra information cost of symmetry completion.  In cyclic rows this
premium is zero because every subgroup is characteristic under the unit
normalizer.  In local rows, it can be positive: a one-dimensional generated
line may become a full conductor orbit under units/Frobenius.

## A0 Reading

B3 is the first policy knob that looks like real model design:

> buy minimal closure when prediction efficiency matters; buy normalizer
> saturation when equivariance, symmetry, or transfer across equivalent tasks
> matters.

This makes A0 less monolithic.  "Optimal discretization" is not always the
smallest support and not always the most symmetric support.  It is the support
whose structural costs and symmetry premiums are justified by the task and
resource budget.
