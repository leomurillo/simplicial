# FC1 Natural Macrovariable Theorem

Created: 2026-05-15

This note sharpens FC1, the macrovariable naturality target in the fundamental
conjecture program.  The goal is not to classify every smooth
Fisher-volume-preserving vector field on a simplex.  Paper 13 already separates
the exact decomposable Nambu currents from the larger closed-current calculus.

The FC1 target is narrower and stronger:

> under the finite arithmetic symmetries and a relative finite-sufficiency
> principle, the conductor macrovariables used in the simplectic papers are
> forced natural invariants, not convenient Hamiltonian choices.

## Refined Theorem Target

Let \(R\) be either:

1. a finite quotient ring with additive character group \(\widehat R\), CRT
   factorization, conductor filtration, and Hayes/Galois normalizer
   \(\Gamma_R\); or
2. a finite abelian output algebra with a specified normalizer action
   \(\Gamma\le \operatorname{Aut}(\widehat R)\).

Write \(\Delta_R^\circ\) for the positive categorical simplex,
\(p_*=|R|^{-1}{\bf 1}\), and

```text
H_R = {h in C^R : sum_a h_a = 0}.
```

At \(p_*\), the additive-character lines decompose

```text
H_R = direct sum_{chi != 1} C chi.
```

For quotient rings, group these lines by primitive conductor packets
\(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\).  For a general finite abelian output algebra, replace
"conductor packet" by the \(\Gamma\)-orbit packet of nontrivial characters.

Define the **natural finite macrovariable algebra** \(\mathcal N_R\) to be the
smallest algebra of local observables whose Taylor jets are generated by:

1. quotient sufficient-statistic layers \(R\to R/\mathfrak d\), equivalently
   cumulative spaces \(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak e\mid\mathfrak d}H_R^{[\mathfrak e]}\);
2. primitive packet energies
   \[
   E_{\mathfrak d}(p)=\frac12\|\Pi_{\mathfrak d}(p-p_*)\|_{F,*}^2;
   \]
3. packetwise Fisher-orthogonal refinements, treated as internal flag freedom;
4. quotient-character adjacency orbit data in \(\mathcal A_R\) when the
   observable is asked to be stable off the centroid.

The theorem target has three parts.

**FC1-A. Centroid invariant classification.**  At \(p_*\), the
\(\Gamma_R\)-equivariant, finite-sufficient quadratic Fisher macrovariables
are exactly functions of the packet energies \(E_{\mathfrak d}\), with
packetwise orthogonal refinements allowed inside each packet.  Any genuine
cross-packet quadratic term is ruled out by additive-translation invariance,
normalizer equivariance, or relative finite sufficiency.  This part is now
proved in `knowledge_base/fc1_centroid_classification_theorem.md`.

**FC1-B. Noether naturality for exact decomposable currents.**  Let
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{|R|-2})\) be simplectic Hamiltonian data on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\), and assume each \(C_i\) is finite-sufficient and
\(\Gamma_R\)-equivariant in the above sense.  Then

```text
i_{X_C} Omega_R^F = dC_1 wedge ... wedge dC_{|R|-2}
```

is generated by \(\mathcal N_R\), up to smooth functional dependence among the
\(C_i\) and packetwise orthogonal flag freedom.  Conversely, every exact
decomposable Fisher-volume-preserving current with Hamiltonian differentials
in the finite-sufficient \(\Gamma_R\)-invariant jet module is represented this
way.  The exact finite-jet differential-module version is now proved in
`knowledge_base/fc1_exact_differential_module_theorem.md`.

**FC1-C. Off-centroid stability and first obstruction.**  Along an
\(m\)-flat ray \(p_*+th\), the natural macrovariable algebra remains closed
through order \(k\) exactly when the operator-valued Fisher coefficients

```text
C_n(h) = sum_eta \hat h^{*n}(eta) A_eta
```

preserve the declared packet algebra for every \(n\le k\).  The first failure
is the earliest quotient-character shift \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak d',\mathfrak d}\)
that crosses a PSL/conductor boundary.  In local filtered rows, the
section-extension class \([\kappa]\) of FC2 is the filtered source of that
failure, and \(K^{CH}\), H4, and higher Fisher jets are its visible shadows
modulo the Fisher-jet kernel.  Thus the first obstruction is quotient-character
adjacency carry, not a new arbitrary Hamiltonian term.  This finite-jet
version is now proved in
`knowledge_base/fc1_off_centroid_closure_theorem.md`.

## Assumption Ledger

**Finite output structure.**  The quotient-ring case is the clean theorem
setting because conductor, CRT, Hayes normalizer, and local depth are intrinsic.
For a plain finite abelian output algebra, the theorem should be stated after
choosing a normalizer action and its packet system.  Without such a packet
system, "conductor" should be replaced by "normalizer orbit of characters."

**Centroid versus nonuniform.**  The invariant classification is a centroid
statement.  Away from \(p_*\), fixed conductor packets are generally not
Fisher-orthogonal.  The exact nonuniform locus is controlled by the Fourier
support of the reciprocal density \(m_p=1/p\): only reciprocal modes that
preserve conductor labels may remain active.

**Equivariance group.**  For quotient rings, the intended group is generated
by CRT factorization, local Hayes unit/principal-unit actions, Galois/Frobenius
actions, and wreath permutations of isomorphic local factors.  The full
outcome-permutation symmetry is used only to fix the categorical Fisher scale,
not to define the arithmetic macrovariable algebra.

**Finite sufficiency.**  Sufficiency is relative.  A quotient map
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}:R\to R/\mathfrak d\) is not sufficient for the full
microscopic \(Q\)-outcome family unless it is injective.  It is sufficient for
the \(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak d}\)-measurable categorical subfamily.  FC1 should
therefore say "finite sufficient relative to the quotient subfamily" rather
than "globally sufficient."

**Current class.**  FC1-B concerns exact decomposable Nambu-Fisher currents.
General Fisher-volume-preserving fields are closed-current objects
\(d(\iota_X\Omega_R^F)=0\) and may have nondecomposable filtered residuals.
Those residuals belong to the Paper 13 closed-current completion, not to the
classification of named macro-Hamiltonian tuples.

## Proof Skeleton

### Lemma 1: Invariant Classification

Let \(B\) be the quadratic jet of a candidate macrovariable at \(p_*\).  If
\(B\) is invariant under additive translations, Schur's lemma for the finite
abelian regular representation makes \(B\) diagonal in the additive-character
basis.  Thus no cross-character terms survive translation invariance.

If \(B\) is also equivariant under the Hayes/Galois/normalizer action, the
line scalars collapse along the corresponding character orbits.  In the
quotient-ring case these orbits are the conductor packets, up to the local
packet refinements already recorded in the Hayes normalizer.  Hence the
remaining quadratic invariants are packet energies and packetwise internal
refinements.

Finite sufficiency excludes arbitrary diagonal weights that are not functions
of quotient layers: those weights distinguish microscopic character labels
that the quotient statistic has deliberately forgotten.  The remaining freedom
is exactly the within-packet Fisher-orthogonal flag freedom already present in
NZSBTL.

### Lemma 2: Noether Naturality

For any tuple \(\mathbf C\),

```text
i_{X_C} Omega_R^F = dC_1 wedge ... wedge dC_{Q-2}
```

defines a unique vector field preserving Fisher volume and the \(C_i\).  If a
symmetry \(\phi\) normalizes \(\mathcal A_R\) and rescales
\(\Omega_R^F\) and \(P_{\mathbf C}=dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}\) by
constant characters, Paper 13's adjacency-Noether theorem gives
\(\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}=(\sigma_\phi/\tau_\phi)X_{\mathbf C}\).

Therefore the finite Noether census reduces to normalizer orbits of
Hamiltonian tuples, determinant characters, stabilizers, and closure-defect
operators.  Applying Lemma 1 to the Hamiltonian differentials gives the FC1-B
classification up to functional dependence and packetwise flag freedom.

### Lemma 3: Off-Centroid Stability and Obstruction

Paper 10 gives the line-level expansion along \(p_*+th\):

```text
g_{p_*+th}(chi, psi)
  = sum_{n >= 0} (-t)^n \hat h^{*n}(psi chi^{-1})
```

in normalized character coordinates.  Paper 11 packages the block coefficient
as

```text
C_n^{[f,e]}(h) = sum_eta \hat h^{*n}(eta) A_eta^{f,e}.
```

Hence closure through order \(k\) is the finite vanishing problem:
no active \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\) may leave the declared packet
algebra for \(n\le k\).  When one first appears, it is exactly the
quotient-character adjacency carry of Paper 11.  At PSL boundaries this is
the translation-stretching carry obstruction; in local filtered rows its
cohomological source is the FC2 section class \([\kappa]\).

## Failure Modes

1. A nontrivial cross-packet quadratic macrovariable is found that is
   additive-translation invariant, Hayes/Galois/CRT equivariant, and relative
   finite-sufficient.
2. A finite-sufficient quotient statistic produces a natural decomposable
   current whose first nonzero jet is not generated by packet energies,
   quotient layers, or quotient-character adjacency orbit data.
3. An off-centroid current leaves the natural packet algebra before any
   quotient-character shift appears in the Paper 10/Paper 11 expansion.
4. A full filtered residual is mistaken for zero because support projection
   or \(K^{CH}\)-visible projection erased it.  This would not refute FC1-C;
   it would refute an overcompressed version of FC1-C.
5. In a non-quotient-ring or nonabelian output problem, the chosen normalizer
   packets fail to play the role that conductors play in quotient rings.

## Proof Status

The centroid quadratic classification is proved in
`knowledge_base/fc1_centroid_classification_theorem.md`: additive translation
diagonalizes the quadratic jet in the character basis, normalizer equivariance
collapses weights to packets, and relative finite sufficiency restricts which
quotient layers may appear.  The same note proves the first-current corollary:
if macro-Hamiltonians have those quadratic leading jets, then the first
nonzero decomposable current jet is the wedge of packet-energy covectors and
transforms by the Paper 13 determinant character.

The exact finite-sufficient differential module is proved in
`knowledge_base/fc1_exact_differential_module_theorem.md`: at finite
adjacency/Fisher-jet order \(k\), finite-sufficient macrovariables are
pullbacks through a finite observation map \(\Phi_{R,k}\); their differentials
span a finite-rank module; exact decomposable Nambu currents are exterior
products in that module; and the Noether determinant character is computed in
the finite atlas.  The first finite coordinate seed is emitted by
`empirical/fc1_noether_atlas_seed.py` into
`empirical/reports/fc1_noether_atlas_seed.md`.

The first orbit-skeleton census certificate is emitted by
`empirical/fc1_noether_atlas_census.py` into
`empirical/reports/fc1_noether_atlas_census_f4_u2.md`.  It verifies the
finite orbit/stabilizer base table and residual visibility ledger for
`F4[u]/(u^2)`.  It does not yet enumerate all 14-Hamiltonian tuple supports
in the 637-coordinate seed; tuple stabilizers and determinant characters
remain the next census layer.

The first small tuple-support baseline is emitted by
`empirical/fc1_noether_tuple_census_f4_u2.py` into
`empirical/reports/fc1_noether_tuple_census_f4_u2_small.md`.  It enumerates

Because these are already normalizer orbit-buckets, every support has full

The off-centroid finite-jet closure theorem is proved in
`knowledge_base/fc1_off_centroid_closure_theorem.md`: closure through order
\(k\) is equivalent to the vanishing of the finite defects \(D_m^S\); if
closure first fails at order \(m_0\), the surviving term is a
quotient-character adjacency carry crossing the declared packet/PSL boundary;
and in local filtered rows the section-independent source is the FC2 class
\([\kappa]\), with \(K^{CH}\), H4, contraction shadows, and higher Fisher jets
only visible projections modulo their kernels.

What remains after the finite-jet theorem is the Noether atlas census, not a
new naturality axiom:

1. choose the finite observation coordinates \(\Phi_{R,k}\), polynomial jet
   order \(r\), and support/coefficient skeleton for each row;
2. enumerate Hamiltonian tuple orbits, stabilizers, determinant characters,
   and closure defects on that finite tuple set;
3. attach \(D_m^S\) and \([\kappa]\) labels while preserving the
   full/support/Fisher-shadow distinctions;
4. avoid importing Bures, transformer, FEP, or primorial material as proof
   input.
