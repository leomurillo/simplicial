# B0 Finite-Abelian Subgroup Descent Theorem

Created: 2026-05-14

This is the B2 generalization of the cyclic B1 theorem.

## Theorem

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian character group and let
\(S\subset G\setminus\{0\}\) be a nonempty reciprocal Fourier support.  The
B0 balance-transfer descent closure is

```text
D_G(S) = <S> \ {0},
```

the nonzero part of the subgroup generated by \(S\).

This support is the unique minimum-resource support with zero structural B0
cost: zero reciprocal projection, zero Fisher projection, zero additive closure
leakage, and zero descent-balance cost.

## Proof Sketch

Fourier supports inside a subgroup \(H\le G\) form a finite function algebra:
they are closed under pointwise multiplication, and inversion of a nowhere-zero
function stays in the same algebra.  Therefore, if \(m_p=1/p\) has support in
S, the probability \(p\propto 1/m_p\) and tangent \(p-p_*\) have support inside
the subgroup generated by \(S\).

Any support with zero reciprocal projection for generic coefficients on \(S\)
must contain \(S\).  Any additively closed support containing \(S\) must contain
the subgroup \(\langle S\rangle\).  Therefore \(D_G(S)\) is the smallest
support with zero projection, zero closure leakage, and zero descent-balance
cost.

## Why This Is Stronger Than B1

In cyclic groups, subgroups are indexed by divisors, so the generated subgroup
is equivalently

```text
{ k != 0 : cond(k) divides lcm(cond(S)) }.
```

In noncyclic groups, lcm/order data is not enough.  Example:
in \(C_2\times C_2\), a single nonzero mode generates a one-dimensional
line, while order-lcm saturation buys all three nonzero modes.  The B2
invariant is subgroup generation, not conductor order alone.

For local additive rows, conductor saturation can also overbuy.  It may be the
right policy when normalizer symmetry is required, but it is not the minimal
B0 information investment.

## A0 Reading

B2 is the sharper information-investment rule:

> buy the subgroup generated by the active reciprocal spectrum; buy conductor
> or normalizer saturation only when the task requires that extra symmetry.

This separates minimal prediction investment from symmetry-complete modeling.
That distinction is central for A0 because efficiency, latency, and compression
depend on not buying symmetry one does not need.
