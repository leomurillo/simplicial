# FC2 Family Theorem: Filtered Source, Fisher-Jet Shadows

Created: 2026-05-14

This note upgrades FC2 from the first mixed row to the family theorem that is
actually provable from the algebra.

The theorem is not: "every hyper-adjacency support complex has its own
cohomology."  That is false without augmentation because the closed nontrivial
boundary can fail \(d^2=0\).

The theorem is:

> the filtered section class \([\kappa]\) of the local quotient-character
> adjacency algebra is the universal cohomological source, and all
> \(K^{CH}\)/higher-curvature carry channels are Fisher-jet shadows of that
> class.

## Setup

Let \(R\) be a finite local quotient ring or finite local conductor row with:

- additive character group \(\widehat R\);
- conductor packets \(X_{\mathfrak d}\);
- quotient-character adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_R\);
- conductor filtration \(F^\bullet\mathcal A_R\);
- associated graded \(\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_R\);
- Hayes/Galois normalizer \(\Gamma_R\);
- normalized homogeneous section

```text
s : gr A_R -> A_R.
```

Let \(I=\ker(\mathcal A_R\to\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_R)\).  For composable
homogeneous symbols \(x,y\in\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_R\), define

```text
kappa(x,y) = s(x)s(y) - s(xy) in I.
```

Let \(\mathfrak J^{(n)}\) denote the Fisher-jet functor that sends quotient
shifts to the \(n\)-th Fisher/Amari Taylor operator in character coordinates.

## Theorem

**FC2-B. Filtered curvature-carry family theorem.**  For every row satisfying
the setup:

1. **Cocycle and obstruction.**  \(\kappa\) is a normalized
   \(\Gamma_R\)-equivariant 2-cocycle in the section-change complex
   \(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{sec}}(\operatorname{gr}\mathcal A_R,I)\).  Its class
   \([\kappa]\) is independent of the section up to coboundary and vanishes
   exactly when the conductor filtration splits multiplicatively.
2. **Cubic shadow.**  The mixed second-order Fisher product defect is
   \(\mathfrak J^{(3)}([\kappa])\).  Its antisymmetric part is precisely the
   \(K^{CH}\) conductor-coordinate curvature channel.
3. **Higher-arity shadows.**  The arity-\(n\) hyper-adjacency carry support
   generated by the binary floor is the support of
   \(\mathfrak J^{(n)}([\kappa])\), modulo the kernel of the Fisher-jet
   functor.  Equivalently, higher curvature/carry is not a new obstruction:
   it is the operadic/Fisher-jet propagation of the same filtered extension
   class.
4. **CRT.**  For \(R=\prod_{\mathfrak p}R_{\mathfrak p}\), the class and its
   shadows decompose as the direct sum/tensor product of the local classes.
   Cross-prime effects enter only through the Fisher displacement tensor rank,
   not through a new extension class.

## Proof

The first claim is the standard factor-set calculation for an associative
filtered algebra.  Associativity gives

```text
(s(x)s(y))s(z) = s(x)(s(y)s(z)).
```

Substitute \(s(x)s(y)=s(xy)+\kappa(x,y)\), collect lower-filtration terms, and
obtain \(\delta\kappa=0\).  If \(s\) is changed to \(s+\phi\), the same
expansion gives \(\kappa\mapsto\kappa+\delta\phi\).  Hence \([\kappa]\) is a
well-defined obstruction class.  It vanishes exactly when some section has
\(s(x)s(y)=s(xy)\) for all composable homogeneous symbols, which is precisely
a multiplicative splitting of the conductor filtration.

For the Fisher claims, use the character-coordinate Taylor formula already
proved in Paper 10/Paper 11:

```text
g_{p_*+h}(chi, psi)
```

depends only on the quotient shift \(\psi\chi^{-1}\) through the convolution
coefficients of \(h\).  Therefore a homogeneous quotient shift has a canonical
Fisher Taylor operator, and products of shifts map to products of the
corresponding Fisher operators.  Applying this functor to

```text
s(x)s(y)-s(xy)
```

gives exactly the mixed second-order product defect.  Antisymmetrizing the two
orders \(xy\) and \(yx\) gives the commutator part of that defect, which is the
definition of the \(K^{CH}\) curvature channel in the conductor coordinates.

The higher-arity statement follows from the operadic generation theorem in
Paper 11 Section 9.5: all Fisher/Amari jets are generated from the binary
floor by operadic composition.  Since \(\mathfrak J\) respects quotient-shift
composition, applying it after operadic composition gives the same support as
operadically propagating \(\mathfrak J^{(3)}([\kappa])\).  Any discrepancy is
exactly the kernel of the Fisher-jet functor; after quotienting by that kernel,
the support is equal.

CRT is formal because additive characters, conductor packets, quotient shifts,
and Fisher Taylor coefficients factor over local components.  The extension
class is local; global mixed behavior is obtained by tensoring local shadows
and by the rank of the displacement tensor across prime factors.

## Consequence

FC2 is solved as a family theorem with a precise kernel clause:

```text
filtered Ext_CH class -> Fisher-jet shadows -> visible curvature/carry support.
```

What remains is not the theorem, but classification:

1. determine which local rows have \([\kappa]\ne0\);
2. compute the Fisher-jet kernel and visible support at higher arities;
3. extract coefficient-level rather than support-only higher-arity tables.
