# Note H8: Jordan-Lie Algebra of \(K^{CH}_{p_*}\)

**Status:** Centroid computation and manuscript insertion note.

This note computes the symmetric half of the operator algebra generated by
the raised Amari-Chentsov cubic at the uniform categorical centroid.  It
complements Paper 11 Proposition 8.1, where the commutator was shown to be
universal round-sphere curvature.

Throughout, \(R\) is a finite label algebra of size \(N\),
\(p_*=N^{-1}\mathbf 1\), and

$$
H_R=\{h\in {\mathbb C}^R:\sum_x h_x=0\}.
$$

The all-ones vector is \(\mathbf 1\), and
\(\Pi_H z=z-\langle z,\mathbf 1\rangle N^{-1}\mathbf 1\) is the Euclidean
projection onto \(H_R\).  The centroid operator is

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)v
=
N\Pi_H(u\odot v)
=
N(u\odot v)-\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf 1
=
N(u\odot v)-N\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}p_*.
$$

This last display is the convention used below.

---

## 1. The Explicit Anticommutator

Let \(K_u=K^{CH}_{p_*}(u)\).  For \(u,v,w\in H_R\),

$$
K_u(K_vw)
=
N^2(u\odot v\odot w)
-N\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
-N\langle u\odot v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf 1.
$$

Swapping \(u\) and \(v\) and adding gives:

$$
\boxed{
\{K_u,K_v\}(w)
=
2N^2(u\odot v\odot w)
-N\bigl(
\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}v
+\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
\bigr)
-2N\langle u\odot v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}\mathbf 1.
}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\boxed{
\{K_u,K_v\}(w)
=
2N^2\Pi_H(u\odot v\odot w)
-N\bigl(
\langle u,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}v
+\langle v,w\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}u
\bigr).
}
$$

The Jordan product is half of this:

$$
K_u\circ K_v={1\over2}\{K_u,K_v\}.
$$

The commutator cancels the \(N^2u\odot v\odot w\) and
\(\langle u\odot v,w\rangle\mathbf 1\) terms.  The anticommutator keeps them.
This is the whole reason the symmetric product is the correct place to look
for quotient-shift data.

---

## 2. The Span of the \(K_u\) Does Not Close

The map \(u\mapsto K_u\) is not closed under Jordan product, except in the
degenerate \(N=2\) case where \(K_u=0\) on the one-dimensional tangent line.
For \(N>2\), \(K_u^2\) has positive trace whenever \(u\ne0\), while every
\(K_z\) is traceless.

More explicitly, as operators on \(H_R\),

$$
\operatorname{tr}(K_uK_v)
=
N(N-2)\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Euc}}.
$$

Thus

$$
\operatorname{tr}\{K_u,K_u\}
=
2N(N-2)\|u\|^2
$$

for \(N>2\), so \(\{K_u,K_u\}\) cannot lie in
\(\operatorname{span}\{K_z:z\in H_R\}\).

The failure of closure is universal.  It is not a prime/composite or
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) phenomenon.

---

## 3. The Universal Jordan-Lie Envelope

Although the first-order span does not close, the algebra generated by the
\(K_u\) has a clean universal description.

For \(i\in R\), put

$$
\tau_i=e_i-p_*.
$$

Then, for \(w\in H_R\),

$$
K_{\tau_i}w
=
Nw_i\tau_i-w
=
(N-1)P_iw-w,
$$

where \(P_i\) is the Euclidean rank-one projector onto the line
\({\mathbb C}\tau_i\).  The simplex frame is tight:

$$
\sum_i P_i={N\over N-1}I_H.
$$

Therefore

$$
\sum_i K_{\tau_i}^2=N(N-2)I_H.
$$

For \(N>2\), the identity belongs to the Jordan algebra generated by the
\(K_u\), and hence each \(P_i=(K_{\tau_i}+I_H)/(N-1)\) belongs to it.  The
Jordan products \(P_i\circ P_j\) span all Euclidean self-adjoint endomorphisms
of \(H_R\), because the vectors \(\tau_i\) span \(H_R\).  The commutators
\([P_i,P_j]\) span the skew-adjoint endomorphisms.

**Theorem (universal centroid JLA envelope).**  For \(N>2\), the Jordan
algebra generated by \(\{K^{CH}_{p_*}(u):u\in H_R\}\) is

$$
\operatorname{Sym}(H_R),
$$

the Euclidean self-adjoint endomorphisms of \(H_R\).  Together with the Lie
bracket it generates

$$
\operatorname{End}(H_R)
$$

after complexification, with Lie part \(\mathfrak{so}(H_R)\) over the real
tangent space.  This statement depends only on \(N\) and the centroid
Euclidean structure.

Thus the intrinsic centroid JLA is conductor-blind.  It is larger than the
first-order span of the \(K_u\), but it is still universal.

## 4. Character-Basis Matrix Formula

The conductor data enters only after a character basis and conductor packet
decomposition are chosen.

Let \(G=\widehat R\), write the trivial character as \(1\), and use the
normalized nontrivial character basis of \(H_R\).  For
\(\psi,\chi\ne1\),

$$
[K_u]_{\psi,\chi}
=
N\widehat u(\psi\chi^{-1})
$$

because \(\widehat u(1)=0\).  Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\{K_u,K_v\}]_{\psi,\chi}
&=
N^2\sum_{\rho\ne1}
\bigl[
\widehat u(\psi\rho^{-1})\widehat v(\rho\chi^{-1})
+
\widehat v(\psi\rho^{-1})\widehat u(\rho\chi^{-1})
\bigr] \\
&=
2N^2(\widehat u*\widehat v)(\psi\chi^{-1})
-N^2\bigl[
\widehat u(\psi)\widehat v(\chi^{-1})
+
\widehat v(\psi)\widehat u(\chi^{-1})
\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first term is the symmetric quotient-shift term.  The second term is the
universal augmentation-corner correction caused by deleting the trivial
character line.

For conductor packets \(X_{\mathfrak e}\) and \(X_{\mathfrak f}\), this says

$$
\boxed{
P_{\mathfrak f}\{K_u,K_v\}P_{\mathfrak e}
=
2N^2
\sum_{\eta\in G}
(\widehat u*\widehat v)(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}
-N^2
P_{\mathfrak f}
\bigl(
\widehat u\otimes\check{\widehat v}
+
\widehat v\otimes\check{\widehat u}
\bigr)
P_{\mathfrak e},
}
$$

where \(\check{\widehat v}(\chi)=\widehat v(\chi^{-1})\).  This is the
lossless conductor-coordinate form of the Jordan product.

The commutator cancels the convolution term and keeps only the antisymmetric
rank correction, which is why Paper 11 Proposition 8.1 sees only sphere
curvature.  The Jordan product keeps the convolution term, so any
nontrivial quotient-shift inside \(\mathcal A_R\), including the nilpotent
swap term in the \(Q_f=4\) row, can survive in the symmetric product.

## 5. What the Jordan Product Sees

The answer has two levels.

**Intrinsic level.**  The centroid JLA is universal.  It does not intrinsically
detect conductor packets, the prime/composite distinction, CRT splitting, or
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).  It also does not close inside
\(\operatorname{span}\{K_u\}\); for \(N>2\) its Jordan envelope is the full
self-adjoint operator algebra.

**Conductor-coordinate level.**  Once the character basis and conductor
packets are fixed, the Jordan product has a visible symmetric
quotient-shift component

$$
2N^2\sum_\eta(\widehat u*\widehat v)(\eta)A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}.
$$

This component detects the available quotient-shift channels of
\(\mathcal A_R\): one-packet behavior in \(C_p\), cross-conductor CRT
channels in \(C_{p^2}\) and \(C_{12}\), and the mixed tame/wild swap/carry
channels in \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\).  However, the label
"\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)" is not produced by the centroid Jordan formula
alone.  It is produced by comparing the filtered local adjacency algebra with
its associated graded, i.e. by the carry cocycle of the local filtered
multiplication law.

Thus the prime row's commutative single-packet quotient algebra removes
cross-conductor channels, but it does not make the Jordan product close inside
\(\operatorname{span}\{K_u\}\) or collapse the universal self-adjoint envelope.

So H8 supports the following refinement of outcome (a):

> The intrinsic centroid Jordan-Lie algebra is universal and
> conductor-blind, but the character-basis Jordan matrix is the symmetric
> carrier through which quotient-character adjacency data enters.  It can
> display the \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) swap/carry term, but certifying
> nonzero \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) requires the filtered comparison beyond
> the bare centroid JLA.

This explains why the first nontrivial
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) representative in Paper 11 appears in the mixed
path-composition defect after subtracting the universal centroid baseline,
not in the abstract commutator or abstract Jordan envelope alone.
