## Abstract

This paper formulates the three-body problem as the first nontrivial rung of
an N-ary mechanics program.  The Newtonian three-body problem is a problem
inside a pairwise force theory: each force is decomposed into binary
action-reaction channels, and the total motion is obtained by summing those
channels.  The replacement problem asks what a closed mechanics would look like
if the primitive interaction were irreducibly ternary, so that the force on
each body is generated by the geometry of the ordered triplet and cannot be
written as a sum of antisymmetric pair forces.

The ideal target is not a declaration that "everything is ternary."  It is a
projection-closure ladder.  One body teaches gauge dependence: absolute
position, absolute velocity, and even mass have no internal dynamical bite
without a declared comparison or probe.  Two bodies give the first relational
closure: relative position, relative momentum, and central pair laws can form
a valid dyadic shadow.  Three bodies are the first place where that dyadic
shadow can be asked to confess its limits.  Pairwise mechanics is therefore a
closure-valid sector, ternary mechanics is the first triangular test beyond it,
and higher irreducible constraints require their own arity, cumulant, lift,
and residual budgets.

Papers 29, 30, and 31 sharpen this lesson from inside the simplicial sequence.
Paper 29 says identity is whole-relative participation.  Paper 30 says
projection failure is typed residual overflow.  Paper 31 names the general
rule: a projection is legitimate only when its required participation survives
and the residuals it creates are typed, bounded, stable, and falsifiable for
the declared task and horizon.  Paper 28 is therefore the dynamical prototype
of projection-closure theory.  It closes the classical three-body problem only
in this programmatic sense: the classical model remains valid inside its
pairwise assumptions, but it becomes a benchmark quotient rather than the
ontological destination.

The construction is anchored internally by the third-order information-geometric
mechanics of Papers 13--18 and 24, and externally by Nambu mechanics,
information geometry, nonadditive three-body forces, ternary algebraic
structures, and structure-preserving discretization.  On a categorical
simplex, the first nonlinear momentum-gap term is the raised Amari-Chentsov
third-order tensor \(K^{CH}\), the natural Hamiltonian structure is
Nambu-Fisher rather than symplectic, and the autonomous 3-order action carries
a conserved Noether energy.  The organizing reading in this revision is that
the log-partition cumulant tower is the program's categorical effective
action: the first cumulant gives centroid or position data, the second
cumulant gives the Fisher metric and hence both ruler and inertia, the third
cumulant gives the Amari-Chentsov force/source response, and fourth and higher
terms measure typed projection defects.  We define an admissible physical-space projection of this third-order
tensor to a ternary force law
\[
  \dot r_i^a = {p_i^a\over m_i},\qquad
  \dot p_i^a =
  F_i^a(r,p)
  =
  \sum_{j,k=1}^3
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\,(v_j\cdot v_k),
\]
with \(v_i=p_i/m_i\).  Irreducibility is not the bare exclusion of arbitrary
antisymmetric pair decompositions, since those are pointwise vacuous under
momentum balance.  It is the nonvanishing connected off-target tensor relative
to a declared pair-locality class.  Physical admissibility requires global
momentum balance, global torque balance, and an autonomous variational or
Nambu-Fisher 3-order energy
\[
  {\mathcal E}_3
  =
  {1\over2}\sum_i m_i |v_i|^2
  +
  {1\over3}\sum_{i,j,k,a}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\,h_i^a\,(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]

Under these hypotheses total momentum, total angular momentum, 3-order energy,
and Nambu phase volume are conserved, with momentum and torque conditional on
the stated balance constraints and 3-order energy conditional on autonomy and
regularity.  This is the conservation-laws crescendo: conservation is not
pairwise action-reaction; it is closure of a declared whole under its
admissible symmetries.  Pairwise antisymmetry is one route to conservation,
not the ontology of conservation.

The jerk is not an additional force law: it is the chain-rule derivative of
the ternary force and, in the underlying information geometry, the projected

## 1. Scope and Claim Status

This paper does **not** solve the Newtonian gravitational three-body problem.
The Newtonian problem keeps the pairwise inverse-square law and asks for the
resulting trajectories.  Inside that model, chaotic sensitivity remains a real
and well-tested fact.

The present paper formulates a replacement problem:

\[
  \text{pairwise Newtonian three-body dynamics}
  \quad\leadsto\quad
  \text{irreducible ternary three-body dynamics}.
\]

The replacement is not a numerical trick.  It changes the primitive
interaction.  Instead of assuming that the three-body force is assembled from
three binary conversations, it asks for a force law whose primitive vertex is
the whole triplet.

The paper is therefore theorem-facing but not triumphalist.  Its ambition is to

This is the precise sense in which the classical three-body program can be

### 1.1 Nine defensible claims kept on the table

The paper should not leave the following safe claims implicit.

1. **One-body mechanics is a gauge lesson before it is a force lesson.**
   Absolute position, absolute velocity, and mass do not become internally
   meaningful until a frame, comparison, coupling, or measuring context is
   declared.  Paper 28 begins after that lesson, not before it.
2. **Two-body mechanics is the first relational closure.**  The dyadic shadow
   is not an enemy.  It is the first nontrivial closure-valid projection and
   the boundary condition any higher-arity theory must recover in the low
   residual regime.
3. **Pairwise action-reaction is sufficient but not necessary for
   conservation.**  Newton's third-law pair channels are one route to total
   momentum and angular-momentum conservation.  They are not the ontology of
   conservation.  Global balance can be imposed directly on a closed ternary
   tensor.
4. **Richer pairwise features are not equivalent to higher arity.**  Adding
   more edge features can improve approximation, but it does not by itself
   certify that the missing whole has been carried.  The empirical audit treats
   pairwise radial enrichments as controls, not replacements for the arity
   question.
5. **The classical model is a residual-zero sector, not the neutral default.**
   In the language of Paper 30, pairwise Newtonian mechanics is the sector in
   which 3-ary overflow is declared zero or negligible under the chosen task
   horizon.
6. **Jerk-only and snap-only models are controls, not destinations.**  Jerk is
   residual force drift after admitted closure; snap is residual force
   concavity after force and drift have already been admitted or bounded.
   Neither terminates the higher-cumulant tower.
7. **The projection map must be selected, not fitted.**  The finite \(Z_6\)
   prototype now uses a non-arbitrary bridge: minimal CRT row, \(e\)-pure dyadic
   base, scale-free Heron copula gate, and \(\Pi_{\rm EL}\).  The reduced E5
   gate preserves a coherent near-equilateral sign, while the Cartesian
   ephemeris gate says the finite row is not a parameter-fixed gravitational
   force.  The open problem is whether a continuum/Bures or mass-participating
   lift can derive the extra suppression and body-removal behavior a real force
   requires.
8. **The three-body problem becomes an arity-detection problem.**  The mature
   question is not only whether one can integrate trajectories, but which arity
   is required after lower-arity shadows and typed residual controls have been
   exhausted.
9. **The physics bridge is a cumulant-tower statement.**  In this sequence,
   "space" and "mass" are not introduced as unrelated primitives: both are
   readings of the Fisher metric, the second cumulant.  Likewise time,
   energy, and gravity-adjacent residuals are kept on one Noether line:
   time is the autonomous flow parameter, energy is its conserved charge when
   the projection closes, and the gravity-adjacent object is the typed
   closure defect when that charge is transported through a non-closing
   projection.  This is a structural analogy to physics, not a claim that
   Paper 28 has derived gravitation.

### 1.2 Terminology debt: cubic, square, and arity

This revision deliberately reduces the word "cubic" where it was doing
conceptual work that is better carried by "third-order," "3-order,"
"3-ary," "trilateral," or "triangular."  The inherited term remains only where
it names a standard object or internal dependency: the Amari-Chentsov cubic
tensor, the Cubic Noether theorem, and earlier paper titles.

There is a similar debt around the word "square."  The paper still uses
standard phrases such as inverse-square law, square-root Fisher map, and
squarefree support because those are entrenched mathematical names.  But in
the N-ary ladder, "square" should not silently signal ontology.  It often
marks a dyadic or second-order projection convention, not the full structure.
Future drafts should keep separating literal algebraic degree from arity,
geometry, and residual type.

## 2. Dependency Spine

The construction sits on a specific internal chain.

Paper 13 introduces the central move: replace the symplectic 2-form of binary
Hamiltonian mechanics by the top-degree Fisher volume form

\[
  \iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
  =
  dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
\]
The resulting dynamics preserve the Fisher volume and the chosen
macro-Hamiltonians.  This is the mechanical source of the "post-binary"
language used here.

Papers 10 and 11 identify the finite arithmetic coordinates in which the

### 2.9 The cumulant-effective-action bridge

The physics-facing bridge can now be stated without changing the formal
claims.  In ordinary mechanics and field theory, position, mass, force,
stress-energy, and gravitational curvature are usually introduced in a
pre-existing spacetime language.  In this program the manifold is not a
neutral container supplied before the state.  It is built from the same
convex potential whose derivatives produce the mechanics.  Thus the
log-partition potential \(\Phi=\log Z\) plays the role of a categorical
effective action.

The local dictionary is:

| cumulant rung | simplicial object | physics-facing reading |
|---|---|---|
| first | centroid, mean coordinate, \(e\)-affine position | where the state is read |
| second | Fisher metric \(g=\nabla^2\Phi\) | metric, ruler, and inertia/mass matrix |
| third | Amari-Chentsov tensor \(T=K^{CH}\) | force/source response, the variation of \(g\) |

This table is not an additional physical law.  It is a bookkeeping correction
for the direction of projection.  The effective geometry should be recovered
from the cumulant tower, beginning with \(g\), before one asks whether a
finite physical-space shadow such as \(Q_3\) carries the required invariants.
The old temptation was to look for gravity by projecting \(K^{CH}\) down to an
already understood physical target.  The sharper task is to ask whether the
second and third rungs already determine an effective metric, connection, and

The Noether side gives the paired dictionary.  Time is the autonomous flow

gravity-adjacent residual, if it exists in this program, is not a new force

time-translation charge under a projection that does not close.  This is the
precise sense in which Paper 28 should talk about gravity-adjacent behavior:
as a closure certificate to be computed, bounded, and falsified.

**Nonadditive three-body forces.**  Fujita and Miyazawa's pion-exchange
three-nucleon force, modern chiral effective field theory three-nucleon
forces, and the Axilrod-Teller-Muto triple-dipole dispersion term show that
real physical modeling sometimes requires nonadditive three-body
contributions.  These are important precedents against naive pairwise
reduction.  They are not the same as the projected Amari-Chentsov tensor
defined here.

**Ternary algebras in high-energy theory.**  Filippov \(n\)-Lie algebras and
the Bagger-Lambert-Gustavsson/Gustavsson multiple-M2-brane literature show
that physically motivated field theories can use ternary brackets as native
algebraic structure.  Paper 28 cites this as a structural analogy only.  It
does not claim a derivation of M-theory, supergravity, or spacetime
gravitation.

**Discrete exterior calculus and variational numerics.**  Hirani's discrete
exterior calculus and the Desbrun-Hirani-Leok-Marsden DEC program support the
idea that geometry-preserving discretizations should preserve forms and
invariants rather than merely approximate coordinates.  This strengthens the
numerical agenda for Nambu-style integrators, while leaving the actual
discrete Nambu-Fisher integrator as a separate construction task.

**N-ary identity and multi-element constraints.**  The N-ary identity and
multi-element constraint language in Mlynarski's work and in the foundations

dyadic identity or pairwise causality as ontology.  In this paper it is not a
theorem source.  Its role is to motivate the formal question: when is a
constraint genuinely irreducible of arity \(n\), and what residual appears

**Higher-derivative cautions.**  Ostrogradsky's instability theorem and its
modern degenerate/constraint-based qualifications are relevant because Paper

a conditional, falsifiable ternary mechanics template and its place in a

## 3. The Pairwise Three-Body Problem and the Replacement Problem

Projection-closure theory forces this paper to begin one step earlier than the
ordinary three-body story.

For one free body, there is no internal relational geometry.  Translation of
the origin,
\[
  r\mapsto r+a,
\]
and change of inertial frame,
\[
  v\mapsto v+b,
\]
are gauge choices.  Even mass is not internally measured by a one-body world
unless a coupling, comparison, collision, gravitational field, or measuring
apparatus has been declared.  The one-body lesson is therefore not a force
law.  It is:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{state variables become meaningful only relative to a declared
  transfer, probe, or comparison context.}
  }
\]

Two bodies give the first relational closure.  The relative coordinate
\[
  r_{12}=r_2-r_1
\]
survives translations, and relative velocity survives boosts.  A central
two-body law can then close total momentum, angular momentum, and energy.  In
this sense the dyadic shadow is not an enemy.  It is the first nontrivial
closure-valid projection, and any higher-arity or gravity-adjacent theory must
recover it in the low residual regime.

In Newtonian mechanics, the force on body \(i\) is written
\[
  F_i
  =
  \sum_{j\ne i} F_{ij},
  \qquad
  F_{ij}=-F_{ji}.
\]
For gravity,
\[
  F_{ij}
  =
  G\,{m_i m_j\over |r_j-r_i|^3}(r_j-r_i).
\]
The three-body problem asks for the trajectories generated by this pairwise
inverse-square law.  The difficulty is not that the equations are unknown.
The difficulty is that the resulting nonlinear flow is generally nonintegrable
and can be chaotic.

The replacement problem changes the primitive:

\[
  \boxed{
  \text{Do not decompose the interaction into pairwise channels.}
  }
\]

Instead, require a force law generated by the whole triplet.  The force on a
single body is not "body 2 plus body 3"; it is a component of one ternary
interaction vertex.

Equivalently, write an admitted arity expansion schematically as
\[
  F_i
  =
  F_i^{(1)}
  +
  \sum_{j\ne i}F_{ij}^{(2)}
  +
  \sum_{j<k,\;j,k\ne i}F_{ijk}^{(3)}
  +
  \cdots.
\]
Here \(F^{(1)}\) is body-local or external, \(F^{(2)}\) is edge-level, and
\(F^{(3)}\) is triangular.  In a closed one-body universe \(F^{(1)}\) is zero
or gauge/context data.  In the Newtonian two-body benchmark, \(F^{(2)}\)
closes.  Paper 28 asks what happens when \(F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}\) is not a
closure-valid projection of a three-body whole.

The admitted residual after arity \(n\) is
\[
  R_{\le n}
  =
  F_{\mathrm{truth}}
  -
  \sum_{k\le n}F^{(k)}.
\]
The replacement is meaningful only if it passes three tests:

1. **Irreducibility.**  The force cannot be rewritten as pairwise
   action-reaction bookkeeping.
2. **Conservation closure.**  Total momentum, total angular momentum, and a
   time translation energy are preserved by the admitted whole, not merely by
   pairwise bookkeeping.
3. **Predictive closure.**  Given the tensor, initial data, and a tolerance,
   the state evolves by a closed ODE/Nambu system whose invariants can be
   monitored.

The rest of the paper formulates exactly these tests.

## 4. Physical-Space Projection of the Third-Order Tensor

Let
\[
  Q_3
  =
  \left\{
    (r_1,r_2,r_3)\in(\mathbb R^3)^3:
    \sum_i m_i r_i=0
  \right\}
\]
be the center-of-mass configuration space, away from collision strata.

The arrow of this section is important.  \(Q_3\) is the declared physical
benchmark shadow used to compare with ordinary three-body mechanics; it is not
the source from which the simplex geometry receives its metric.  The intrinsic
metric and inertia come from the second cumulant \(g\) on the categorical
state space.  The third cumulant \(K^{CH}\) records how that metric varies and
therefore supplies the first force/source response.  A physical projection is
valid only if it transports this tower into \(Q_3\) with the required closure
certificates.  When it fails, the failure is not automatically a force; it is
a typed residual to be measured.

An **Amari-Chentsov ternary realization** consists of:

1. a finite or limiting categorical state space \(\Delta_R^\circ\);
2. a smooth embedding or observation map
   \[
     \eta:Q_3\to \Delta_R^\circ;
   \]
3. a velocity lift \(D\eta_r:T_rQ_3\to T_{\eta(r)}\Delta_R^\circ\);
4. a physical projection
   \[
     \Pi_r:T_{\eta(r)}\Delta_R^\circ\to T_rQ_3;
   \]
5. a tensor \(T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\) obtained by projecting the raised third-order response
   \(K^{CH}\) back to physical components.

The word "body" therefore has two layers in this paper.  The bodies
\((r_i,p_i)\) are physical variables in \(Q_3\).  The simplex coordinates are
not bodies; they are categorical/information coordinates in which the third-order
tensor is native.  The map \(\eta\) and projection \(\Pi\) are the bridge
between those layers.  Constructing a natural bridge is the main new
mathematical problem introduced here.

Formally, if \(e_{j,b}\) denotes the physical velocity basis direction for
body \(j\) and coordinate \(b\), then a projected coefficient has the schematic
form
\[
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)
  =
  \sum_{b,c}
  \Pi_{i}^{a}
  \left[
    K^{CH}_{\eta(r)}
    \bigl(D\eta_r(e_{j,b}),D\eta_r(e_{k,c})\bigr)
  \right]
  M^{bc}_{jk}(r),
\]
where \(M^{bc}_{jk}\) is the contraction rule that converts physical velocity
components into the scalar products \(v_j\cdot v_k\).  In the simplest
isotropic version this contraction is absorbed into the displayed force law:
\[
  F_i^a(r,p)
  =
  \sum_{j,k=1}^3
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\,(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]

This definition is intentionally modular.  The present paper does not pretend
that the correct \(\eta\) and \(\Pi\) for gravitation have already been found.
Indeed, the projection is not supposed to manufacture gravity by pushing a
tensor onto \(Q_3\).  It states the admissibility conditions a shadow must
satisfy if the Fisher metric, Amari-Chentsov response, and Noether energy are
to close on the declared physical variables.

If one wants a fully tensorial version before imposing the isotropic
contraction, the projected raised third-order response may be written schematically as
\[
  \widehat K^{a}_{i\,jb\,kc}(r)
  =
  \Pi_i^a(r)\,
  K^{CH}_{\eta(r)}
  \bigl(D\eta_r(e_{j,b}),D\eta_r(e_{k,c})\bigr),
\]
so that
\[
  F_i^a
  =
  \sum_{j,k,b,c}
  \widehat K^{a}_{i\,jb\,kc}(r)\,v_j^b v_k^c.
\]
The reduced tensor \(T^a_{i\,jk}\) used below is the rotationally contracted
version of this object.  This distinction matters: if \(\Pi\) or \(D\eta\)
varies along the trajectory, the jerk contains their covariant time
derivatives.

## 5. Definition of the Ternary Three-Body Model

Let \(m_i>0\) be constant.  Let
\[
  v_i={p_i\over m_i}.
\]
Work in the center-of-mass frame:
\[
  \sum_i m_i r_i=0,
  \qquad
  \sum_i p_i=0.
\]
Thus the regular configuration space is
\[
  Q_3^{\rm reg}
  =
  \left\{
    (r_1,r_2,r_3)\in({\mathbb R}^3)^3:
    \sum_i m_i r_i=0,\ \text{no collision, not collinear}
  \right\}.
\]
On \(Q_3^{\rm reg}\), labelled non-collinear triples have trivial rotational
stabilizer, so
\[
  \pi:Q_3^{\rm reg}\longrightarrow S:=Q_3^{\rm reg}/SO(3)
\]
is a principal \(SO(3)\)-bundle.  Since \(\dim Q_3=6\) and the rotational
orbit has dimension \(3\), the triangle shape space \(S\) has dimension \(3\).
Its tangent space is the rotation-reduced internal space
\[
  T_{\pi(r)}S\cong T_rQ_3/(\mathfrak{so}(3)\cdot r).
\]
The collision and collinear strata are removed rather than ignored; this is
also the domain on which the converse problem for the mixed-partial certificate
can carry topological holonomy.

The ternary dynamics are
\[
  \dot r_i^a={p_i^a\over m_i},
\]
\[
  \dot p_i^a
  =
  F_i^a(r,p)
  =
  \sum_{j,k=1}^3
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\,(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]

Here \(T^a_{i\,jk}\) is a vector-valued ternary force tensor:

- \(i\) is the target body receiving the force;
- \(j,k\) are the two velocity legs being coupled;
- \(a\) is the physical spatial component.

The tensor may be symmetric in the velocity legs \(j,k\), since
\((v_j\cdot v_k)=(v_k\cdot v_j)\).  Full symmetry over all simplex indices
belongs to the underlying Amari-Chentsov tensor before projection; the
physical projection can break manifest symmetry by choosing the target body
and spatial component.

### 5.1 Pointwise pair decomposition and the locality correction

The bare statement "there do not exist antisymmetric pair fields
\(f_{ij}=-f_{ji}\) with \(F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij}\)" is not strong enough.
It is false as an irreducibility axiom unless the pair fields are restricted
to a declared locality class.

**Theorem 5.1 (pointwise vacuity of the unrestricted axiom).**  Let
\(N\ge2\) bodies have force vectors \(F_i\in{\mathbb R}^d\) with
\(\sum_iF_i=0\).  Define
\[
  \Phi:\bigoplus_{\{i,j\}}{\mathbb R}^d
  \longrightarrow
  \{(F_1,\ldots,F_N):\sum_iF_i=0\},
  \qquad
  F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij},\quad f_{ji}=-f_{ij}.
\]
Then \(\Phi\) is surjective.  Its kernel has dimension
\[
  d\left({N\choose2}-N+1\right),
\]
the \(d\)-fold cycle space of the complete graph \(K_N\).

**Proof.**  Read \(f\) as an \({\mathbb R}^d\)-valued \(1\)-cochain on the
oriented edges of \(K_N\).  The assembled force \(F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij}\)
is the graph divergence.  Since \(K_N\) is connected, every zero-sum vertex
cochain lies in the image of this divergence map.  Rank-nullity gives
\(\dim\ker\Phi=d{N\choose2}-d(N-1)\).  For \(N=3\), the kernel is the single
triangle cycle, with dimension \(d\).  \(\square\)

Theorem 5.1 is a useful embarrassment: momentum balance alone can always be
explained by some unrestricted antisymmetric pair assignment at a fixed state.
The irreducible content therefore cannot be the force value by itself.  It is
the failure of pair decomposition relative to a declared locality class.

**Theorem 5.2 (invariance forces rank at most three).**  Let
\(\eta:Q_3^{\rm reg}\to\Delta_R^\circ\) be smooth and strictly
\(SO(3)\)-invariant:
\[
  \eta(g\cdot r)=\eta(r)
  \qquad(g\in SO(3)).
\]
Then, for every \(r\in Q_3^{\rm reg}\):

1. \(\eta=\bar\eta\circ\pi\) for a unique smooth map
   \(\bar\eta:S\to\Delta_R^\circ\);
2. \(\operatorname{rank}D\eta_r\le3\), and
   \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\cdot r\subseteq\ker D\eta_r\);
3. for any symmetric form \(\mathsf G\) on
   \(T_{\eta(r)}\Delta_R^\circ\), the pulled-back form
   \[
     (D\eta_r)^*\mathsf G(D\eta_r)
   \]
   has kernel of dimension at least \(3\) on the six-dimensional space
   \(T_rQ_3\).

In particular, the pulled-back velocity Hessian
\[
  H_r=(D\eta_r)^*
  \left[g_\eta+{2\over3}T_\eta(h,\cdot,\cdot)\right]
  (D\eta_r)
\]
is degenerate on \(T_rQ_3\), regardless of the number of categorical
channels, whenever the physical projection is strictly rotation-invariant.

**Proof.**  Invariance makes \(\eta\) constant on \(SO(3)\)-orbits, hence it
descends to a unique smooth map \(\bar\eta\) on the quotient because \(\pi\) is
a submersion.  The chain rule gives
\[
  D\eta_r=D\bar\eta_{\pi(r)}\circ D\pi_r.
\]
Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}D\eta_r\le\operatorname{rank}D\pi_r=\dim S=3\),
and \(\ker D\pi_r=\mathfrak{so}(3)\cdot r\) lies in \(\ker D\eta_r\).  Pulling
back a quadratic form through a linear map of rank at most \(3\) on a
six-dimensional domain leaves a kernel of dimension at least \(3\), containing
the rotational orbit tangent.  \(\square\)

**Remark 5.2.1 (why equivariance does not rescue categorical simplices).**
One might try to avoid Theorem 5.2 by letting \(SO(3)\) act nontrivially on the
categorical simplex and asking \(\eta\) to be equivariant rather than
invariant.  But continuous affine automorphisms of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) are only
the finite permutation group of its vertices, and every continuous homomorphism
from connected \(SO(3)\) to a finite group is trivial.  Thus the categorical
simplex supplies no nontrivial continuous rotational representation in this
setting.  The degeneracy is structural, not an artefact of too few channels.

A **locality class** \({\mathcal L}\) is a functional constraint on admissible
pair fields.  The default class in this paper is strict pair-locality:
\[
  {\mathcal L}_{\rm pair}:\qquad
  f_{ij}=f_{ij}(z_i,z_j),\qquad z_i=(r_i,v_i),
\]
with no dependence on any third body.  Other classes, such as retarded,
radially enriched, or frame-covariant pair fields, must be named explicitly.
The phrase "irreducibly ternary" is meaningless until \({\mathcal L}\) has
been declared.

### 5.2 Computable irreducibility certificate

Fix a target \(i\), and let \(j,k\) be the other two bodies.  If
\[
  F_i=f_{ij}(z_i,z_j)+f_{ik}(z_i,z_k)
\]
is strict pair-local on an open set, then
\[
  {\partial^2F_i\over\partial z_j\,\partial z_k}\equiv0.
\]
Thus a nonzero mixed derivative with respect to the two non-target bodies is
a certificate of \({\mathcal L}_{\rm pair}\)-irreducibility.  The converse is
not claimed here: turning vanishing mixed partials plus momentum balance into
a global pair decomposition requires an integration theorem on the regular
configuration space, with collision strata and possible holonomy controlled.

For the force law in this paper,
\[
  F_i^a=\sum_{j,k}T^a_{i\,jk}(r)(v_j\cdot v_k),
\]
the velocity-sector certificate is exact:
\[
  {\partial^2 F_i^a\over\partial v_j^b\,\partial v_k^c}
  =
  \left(T^a_{i\,jk}(r)+T^a_{i\,kj}(r)\right)\delta^{bc},
\]
for \(j,k\ne i\), \(j\ne k\).  Hence the connected off-target component of
\(T\) is the operational ternary certificate.  The old triplet-vanishing
language should be read through this test: when a body is removed, sent to a
decoupling boundary, or assigned zero coupling in \(\eta\), this connected
component must vanish for the irreducible sector.  Lower-order pair forces may
exist in a larger physical theory, but they are controls, not the irreducible
tensor studied here.

### 5.3 Momentum condition

Total momentum conservation is imposed at the tensor level:
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^3 T^a_{i\,jk}(r)=0
  \qquad
  \text{for all }a,j,k,r.
\]

### 5.4 Torque condition

Total angular momentum conservation is imposed by
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^3
  \sum_{b,c}
  \epsilon_{abc}r_i^b T^c_{i\,jk}(r)=0
  \qquad
  \text{for all }a,j,k,r.
\]

This condition is stronger than zero total force.  It says that the global
force balance does not inject external torque.

Equivalently, the physical projection must be compatible with the Euclidean
\(SE(3)\) moment-map data.  The Nambu-Fisher bracket supplies conservation of
its own macro-Hamiltonians on the simplex; it does not by itself know what
Euclidean translations and rotations are.  Those symmetries enter through the
projection constraints above.

### 5.5 3-order energy condition

Let
\[
  h_i=r_i-R_c
\]
be the displacement from the chosen categorical/physical centroid.  In the
center-of-mass frame one may take \(R_c=0\) after fixing conventions, but it is
useful to keep \(h_i\) visible.

Define
\[
  {\mathcal E}_3
  =
  {1\over2}\sum_i m_i |v_i|^2
  +
  {1\over3}
  \sum_{i,j,k,a}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)h_i^a(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]

An admissible conservative ternary realization must pay for this quantity
variationally or Hamiltonianly.  In the variational route, the pulled-back
3-order information Lagrangian has the form
\[
  \ell_3(\eta,v)
  =
  {1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)+{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v),
  \qquad h=\eta-\eta_\star,
\]
with \(T\) symmetric in its three slots.  The projected realization must then
satisfy two named hypotheses:

1. **Autonomy.**  The spacetime pullback \((\eta,\Pi)^*\ell_3\) has no
   explicit time dependence.
2. **Reduced regularity.**  The velocity Hessian
   \[
     {\partial^2\ell_3\over\partial v\,\partial v}
     =
     g_\eta+{2\over3}T_\eta(h,\cdot,\cdot)
   \]
   is nondegenerate on the rotation-reduced internal space
   \[
     T_{\pi(r)}S\cong T_rQ_3/(\mathfrak{so}(3)\cdot r),
     \qquad \dim S=3.
   \]
   The rotational sector is not required to be regular inside the internal
   categorical Hessian; its conservation is carried by the \(SO(3)\) moment
   map, equivalently by the total angular momentum \(L\).

These are not fine print.  Autonomy is the physical projection problem in
conservation-law form.  Full-space regularity on \(T_rQ_3\) is impossible for
strictly \(SE(3)\)-invariant categorical projections by Theorem 5.2, so
regularity is required only transverse to rotational gauge.  Reduced regularity
can still fail where the cubic correction cancels the Fisher metric on shape
space; that failure locus is a disconfirmer, not a nuisance term.  In a
Nambu-Fisher presentation, \({\mathcal E}_3\) may instead be supplied as one of
the macro-Hamiltonians, but then the remaining macro-Hamiltonians and
regularity of the decomposable Nambu flow must also be supplied.

### 5.6 Force notation versus Noether boundary data

This paper reserves \(F_n\) for admitted \(n\)-ary force laws.  Thus
\[
  F_1=\text{body-local, external, or inertial force data},
  \qquad
  F_2=\text{pairwise force data},
\]
and \(F_3\) is the triangular force law studied here.

The Noether boundary or gauge term is not denoted by \(F\).  Write it as
\(B_n\).  If an \(n\)-ary Lagrangian or action density is invariant only up to
a total derivative, the theorem-facing condition is
\[
  \delta L_n={dB_n\over dt}.
\]
The conserved Noether quantity is then corrected by \(B_n\).  This distinction
matters because the residual ladder studies \(D_tF_n\), \(D_t^2F_n\), and
higher force derivatives.  Confusing force \(F_n\) with the Noether boundary
term \(B_n\) would collapse dynamics and gauge bookkeeping into one symbol.

## 6. Conservation Theorems

The next two results are conditional balance lemmas.  They are useful, but
they do not prove that the balance conditions are natural; they show what
follows after an admissible physical projection has already supplied them.

### Theorem 6.1: Total momentum balance

Assume the ternary force law and the momentum condition
\[
  \sum_i T^a_{i\,jk}=0.
\]
Then
\[
  P=\sum_i p_i
\]
is conserved.

**Proof.**  In components,
\[
  {d\over dt}\sum_i p_i^a
  =
  \sum_i F_i^a
  =
  \sum_{i,j,k}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]
Rearranging the finite sums gives
\[
  {d\over dt}\sum_i p_i^a
  =
  \sum_{j,k}
  \left(\sum_i T^a_{i\,jk}(r)\right)(v_j\cdot v_k)
  =
  0.
\]
Thus \(dP/dt=0\).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 6.2: Total angular momentum balance

Assume the ternary force law and the torque condition
\[
  \sum_{i,b,c}
  \epsilon_{abc}r_i^bT^c_{i\,jk}=0.
\]
Then
\[
  L=\sum_i r_i\times p_i
\]
is conserved.

**Proof.**  Differentiate:
\[
  {dL\over dt}
  =
  \sum_i \dot r_i\times p_i
  +
  \sum_i r_i\times \dot p_i.
\]
Since \(\dot r_i=p_i/m_i\), the first term vanishes:
\[
  \dot r_i\times p_i={p_i\over m_i}\times p_i=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
  {dL\over dt}
  =
  \sum_i r_i\times F_i.
\]
In components,
\[
  {dL_a\over dt}
  =
  \sum_{i,b,c}\epsilon_{abc}r_i^bF_i^c.
\]
Substitute the ternary force:
\[
  {dL_a\over dt}
  =
  \sum_{j,k}
  \left(
    \sum_{i,b,c}\epsilon_{abc}r_i^bT^c_{i\,jk}
  \right)
  (v_j\cdot v_k).
\]
The torque condition makes each parenthesized coefficient vanish.  Hence
\[
  {dL\over dt}=0.
\]
\(\square\)

### Theorem 6.3: Reduced 3-order Noether conservation

Assume the projected model is generated by the pulled-back autonomous
first-order 3-order action
\[
  \ell_3(\eta,v)
  =
  {1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)+{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v),
  \qquad h=\eta-\eta_\star,
\]
with \(T\) slot-symmetric and \(\eta\) strictly \(SO(3)\)-invariant.  Assume
autonomy and reduced regularity on \(T_{\pi(r)}S\).  Then:

1. The Jacobi energy
   \[
     {\mathcal E}_3
     =
     \langle v,\partial_v\ell_3\rangle-\ell_3
     =
     {1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)+{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v)
   \]
   is conserved on Euler-Lagrange solutions.
2. \(SO(3)\)-invariance makes total angular momentum \(L\) a conserved
   Noether charge.  In the invariant case the torque condition of
   Section 5.4 is derived rather than imposed.
3. The action descends, after quotienting rotational gauge and fixing the
   rotational moment map, to an autonomous reduced Lagrangian
   \(\ell_3^{\rm red}\) on \(TS\).  Reduced regularity makes its Legendre
   transform a local diffeomorphism, so the shape dynamics are a well-posed
   first-order flow.
4. The reduced Jacobi energy
   \[
     {\mathcal E}_3^{\rm red}
     =
     \langle\dot\sigma,\partial_{\dot\sigma}\ell_3^{\rm red}\rangle
     -
     \ell_3^{\rm red}
   \]
   is conserved.

**Proof.**  Slot symmetry gives
\[
  \partial_v\ell_3
  =
  g_\eta v+{2\over3}T_\eta(h,v,\cdot).
\]
Substituting into
\(\langle v,\partial_v\ell_3\rangle-\ell_3\) gives the displayed
\({\mathcal E}_3\).  On Euler-Lagrange solutions the standard time-translation
identity gives
\[
  {d\over dt}\left(\langle v,\partial_v\ell_3\rangle-\ell_3\right)
  =
  -{\partial\ell_3\over\partial t}=0,
\]
because the pulled-back Lagrangian is autonomous.

For \(\xi\in\mathfrak{so}(3)\), invariance implies
\(\delta_\xi\ell_3=0\).  Noether's theorem therefore conserves
\[
  J_\xi=\langle\partial_v\ell_3,\xi_{Q_3}(r)\rangle.
\]
As \(\xi\) ranges over a basis of \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\), these charges are the
components of \(L\), and their vanishing variation is exactly the
infinitesimal torque-balance identity.  Since \(\eta\) is invariant, the
Lagrangian is constant on rotational orbits and descends to the shape quotient
after the cyclic rotational variables are eliminated at fixed \(L\).  Reduced
regularity is precisely nondegeneracy of

reduced Legendre transform is locally invertible.  Autonomy descends to
\(\ell_3^{\rm red}\), and the same time-translation identity conserves

### Corollary 6.6: Energy decomposition and the composition gap

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 6.3, \({\mathcal E}_3\), \(L\), and
\({\mathcal E}_3^{\rm red}\) are conserved.  Hence the rotational remainder

then the remainder has the standard locked-inertia form

  {\mathcal E}_3
  =
  {\mathcal E}_3^{\rm red}
  +
  {1\over2}L^\top{\mathbb I}(\sigma)^{-1}L.
\]
The horizontality condition is a separate falsifiable structural condition.
Its failure breaks the closed locked-inertia formula, not the conservation

### Proposition 6.4: No Ostrogradsky ghost at 3-order

The Lagrangian \(\ell_3\) is first-order in \(v=\dot\eta\).  Ostrogradsky
instability requires nondegenerate dependence on \(\ddot\eta\) or higher
derivatives.  Thus the 3-order action used here is Ostrogradsky-safe.  The
jerk \(D_tF_3\), or geometrically \(\nabla^{(e)}T=T^{(4)}\), is a diagnostic
of the solution and projection channel, not a variable in the action.

**Proof.**  The first condition differentiates to \(\sum_i p_i=0\).  The

### Theorem 7.1: Conditional existence of a natural connected projection

The literal no-go disconfirmer for T*1 is false under a mild active-packet
assumption.  Let \(R\) be a CRT row whose simplex has a tangent vector
\(s\in T_{p_0}\Delta_R^\circ\) at some positive point \(p_0\), with
\[
  K^{CH}_{p_0}(s,s)\ne0.
\]
This holds on any nontrivial row with at least three active outcomes; in
moment coordinates
\[
  K^{CH}_{p_0}(u)v=\Pi_{p_0}\left({uv\over p_0}\right),
\]
so choosing \(s\) whose squared coordinate profile is not constant gives a
nonzero tangent projection.

Let \(q:Q_3\to{\mathbb R}\) be any smooth, nonconstant, translation- and
rotation-invariant triangle scalar, for example
\[
  q(r)=\log(s_{12}s_{23}s_{31}),
  \qquad
  s_{ij}=\sqrt{\|r_i-r_j\|^2+\varepsilon^2}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), define
\[
  \eta(r)=p_0+\epsilon\,\psi(q(r))s
\]
with \(\psi'\ne0\) on the region under study, so that \(\eta(r)\) stays inside
\(\Delta_R^\circ\).  Let \(\ell\) be any linear functional on
\(T_{p_0}\Delta_R^\circ\) satisfying
\[
  \ell\!\left(K^{CH}_{p_0}(s,s)\right)=1,
\]
using the standard affine identification of all simplex tangent spaces with
the zero-sum subspace.  Equivalently, choose \(s\) and \(\ell\) inside a
declared CRT conductor packet or packet combination; no canonical packet
selection is claimed here.
and define the physical projection on the active cubic channel by
\[
  \Pi_r(Y)=\ell(Y)\,\nabla q(r)\in T_rQ_3.
\]
Then the projected cubic force
\[
  F(r,v)
  =
  \Pi_r\!\left(K^{CH}_{\eta(r)}(D\eta_r v,D\eta_r v)\right)
\]
has, to leading nonzero order in \(\epsilon\),
\[
  F_i(r,v)
  =
  \epsilon^2(\psi'(q))^2\,
  \nabla_{r_i}q(r)\,
  \left(\sum_{\ell=1}^3\nabla_{r_\ell}q(r)\cdot v_\ell\right)^2
  +O(\epsilon^3).
\]
It is autonomous and \(SE(3)\)-natural.  Since \(q\) is translation invariant,
\[
  \sum_i\nabla_{r_i}q=0,
\]
so total force balances.  Since \(q\) is rotation invariant,
\[
  \sum_i r_i\times\nabla_{r_i}q=0,
\]
so total torque balances.  Moreover, for distinct non-target bodies \(j,k\),
\[
  {\partial^2F_i^a\over\partial v_j^b\,\partial v_k^c}
  =
  2\epsilon^2(\psi'(q))^2\,
  (\nabla_{r_i}q)^a
  (\nabla_{r_j}q)^b
  (\nabla_{r_k}q)^c
  +O(\epsilon^3),
\]
which is nonzero on a generic open set of nondegenerate triangles.  Hence the
induced force has a nonzero connected ternary component in the sense of
Section 5.2.

**Proof.**  \(SE(3)\)-naturality follows because \(q\) is invariant and
\(\nabla q\) is equivariant: translating all \(r_i\) changes nothing, while a
rotation sends each gradient vector to its rotated copy.  Differentiating
\(\eta\) gives
\[
  D\eta_r(v)=\epsilon\,\psi'(q)\,dq_r(v)\,s,
  \qquad
  dq_r(v)=\sum_\ell\nabla_{r_\ell}q\cdot v_\ell.
\]
Applying the cubic tensor and then \(\Pi_r\) gives the displayed leading force.
The force and torque identities are the infinitesimal translation and rotation
Noether identities for the invariant scalar \(q\).  The mixed velocity
derivative is displayed above and is generically nonzero.  \(\square\)

**What this proves, and what it does not.**  The theorem kills the strongest
no-go: \(SE(3)\)-naturality does not force the connected ternary tensor to
vanish.  It does **not** prove that the displayed \(q\), active CRT packet
\(s\), or channel functional \(\ell\) is the physically canonical projection
for gravity.  It discharges T*1 as an existence theorem for the abstract
projection class and replaces the old projection problem by the sharper
selection problem: which \(q\), packet \(s\), and closure certificate are
forced by independent physics?

Theorem 7.1 is deliberately not the final witness: its image is a curve, so
Theorem 5.2 predicts that its unreduced Hessian is degenerate and its reduced
rank is only one.  The next result repairs exactly that gap by using a full
coframe on the three-dimensional shape space.

### Theorem 7.2: Regular rotation-reduced connected witness

On the nondegenerate scalene stratum of \(Q_3^{\rm reg}\), choose a fixed
side regularizer \(\delta>0\) and write
\[
  s_{ij}^2=\|r_i-r_j\|^2+\delta^2.
\]
Define three \(SE(3)\)-invariant shape functions
\[
  q_1=\log(s_{12}+s_{23}+s_{31}),
  \qquad
  q_2={1\over2}\log(16A_\delta^2),
  \qquad
  q_3=\log(s_{12}s_{23}s_{31}),
\]
where
\[
  16A_\delta^2
  =
  2s_{12}^2s_{23}^2+2s_{23}^2s_{31}^2+2s_{31}^2s_{12}^2
  -s_{12}^4-s_{23}^4-s_{31}^4.
\]
Each \(q_\alpha\) depends on all three sides and therefore on all three
bodies.  On the open stratum where the Jacobian of
\((s_{12},s_{23},s_{31})\mapsto(q_1,q_2,q_3)\) is invertible, the
\(q_\alpha\) form local coordinates on \(S\).

Choose a CRT row with \(\dim T_{p_0}\Delta_R^\circ\ge3\), and choose
Fisher-orthonormal tangent directions
\[
  g_{p_0}(s^{(\alpha)},s^{(\beta)})=\delta^{\alpha\beta},
  \qquad \alpha,\beta=1,2,3.
\]
For small \(\epsilon\), set
\[
  \eta(r)=p_0+\epsilon\sum_{\alpha=1}^3
  \psi_\alpha(q_\alpha(r))s^{(\alpha)},
  \qquad \psi_\alpha'\ne0
\]
on the region studied, and choose linear functionals \(\ell_\alpha\) on the
zero-sum tangent space so that, for some \(\alpha,\beta\),
\[
  C^\alpha_{\beta\beta}
  :=
  \ell_\alpha\!\left(K^{CH}_{p_0}(s^{(\beta)},s^{(\beta)})\right)
  \ne0.
\]
This is possible whenever the active row has a nontrivial cubic channel.  Let
\[
  \Pi_r(Y)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^3\ell_\alpha(Y)\nabla q_\alpha(r).
\]
Then \((\eta,\Pi)\) is autonomous, \(SE(3)\)-natural, reduced-regular, and has
a nonzero connected off-target velocity mixed partial on an open dense subset.
Consequently, it satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 6.3 and closes the
internal composition gap: irreducibility, naturality, autonomy, reduced
regularity, energy conservation, and angular-momentum conservation hold on one
explicit object.

**Proof.**  The differential is
\[
  D\eta_r(v)
  =
  \epsilon\sum_{\beta=1}^3
  \psi_\beta'(q_\beta)\,\dot q_\beta\,s^{(\beta)},
  \qquad
  \dot q_\beta=dq_\beta(v).
\]
Since \(K^{CH}\) is symmetric bilinear in its two tangent slots and
\(K^{CH}_{\eta(r)}=K^{CH}_{p_0}+O(\epsilon)\), the projected force has leading
term
\[
  F_i^a(r,v)
  =
  \epsilon^2
  \sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
  C^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}
  \psi_\beta'\psi_\gamma'
  (\nabla_{r_i}q_\alpha)^a
  \dot q_\beta\dot q_\gamma
  +O(\epsilon^3).
\]
All ingredients are time-independent, giving autonomy.  Translation invariance
of each \(q_\alpha\) gives \(\sum_i\nabla_{r_i}q_\alpha=0\), hence total force
balance.  Rotation invariance gives
\[
  \sum_i r_i\times\nabla_{r_i}q_\alpha=0,
\]
hence torque balance.  Thus the Euclidean moment-map constraints are automatic
for this witness.

In local shape coordinates \(\sigma^\mu\), define
\[
  B_{\beta\mu}
  =
  \psi_\beta'(q_\beta){\partial q_\beta\over\partial\sigma^\mu}.
\]
Fisher-orthonormality gives the leading reduced kinetic Hessian
\[
  H^{\rm red}
  =
  \epsilon^2 B^\top B+O(\epsilon^3).
\]
The Jacobian \((\partial q_\beta/\partial\sigma^\mu)\) is invertible on the
chosen stratum and each \(\psi_\beta'\) is nonzero, so \(B\) is invertible and
\(B^\top B\) is positive definite.  For sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), the
\(O(\epsilon^3)\) cubic correction does not destroy definiteness.  This is
compatible with Theorem 5.2: the unreduced Hessian on \(T_rQ_3\) still has the
rotational kernel; only the quotient Hessian is nondegenerate.

For distinct non-target bodies \(j,k\ne i\),
\[
  {\partial^2F_i^a\over\partial v_j^b\,\partial v_k^c}
  =
  2\epsilon^2
  \sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
  C^\alpha_{(\beta\gamma)}
  \psi_\beta'\psi_\gamma'
  (\nabla_{r_i}q_\alpha)^a
  (\nabla_{r_j}q_\beta)^b
  (\nabla_{r_k}q_\gamma)^c
  +O(\epsilon^3).
\]
Because each \(q_\alpha\) is genuinely ternary, these body-gradients are
generically nonzero.  A diagonal coefficient
\(C^\alpha_{\beta\beta}\ne0\) supplies a nonzero rank-one contribution at a
generic base triangle.  The field is real analytic on the regular stratum, so
nonvanishing at one point implies nonvanishing on an open dense subset.
\(\square\)

**Design rule: avoid the single-pair trap.**  The channels in Theorem 7.2 are
not the three individual side scalars \(\log s_{12},\log s_{23},\log s_{31}\).

## 14. A Contest-Style Formulation

The following problem is included because it isolates the model's formal core.
It is not a standard physics olympiad problem about Newtonian gravity.  It is
an assessment problem for the replacement theory.

### Problem statement

Consider a hypothetical replacement for Newton's pairwise three-body dynamics.
Three point masses form one irreducible ternary system: the force on each body
is produced by the whole ordered triplet and has nonzero connected off-target
component relative to the declared strict pair-locality class.

Let the state be
\[
  (r_i,p_i),\qquad i=1,2,3,
\]
with constant masses \(m_i\), velocities \(v_i=p_i/m_i\), and center-of-mass
frame
\[
  \sum_i m_i r_i=0,\qquad \sum_i p_i=0.
\]
The dynamics are
\[
  \dot r_i={p_i\over m_i},
\]
\[
  \dot p_i^a
  =
  F_i^a
  =
  \sum_{j,k=1}^3
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)(v_j\cdot v_k),
\]
where \(T^a_{i\,jk}\) is an irreducible ternary tensor obtained by projecting
the Amari-Chentsov third-order tensor from an information-geometric simplex to
physical space.

Assume
\[
  \sum_i T^a_{i\,jk}=0,
\]
\[
  \sum_i\epsilon_{abc}r_i^bT^c_{i\,jk}=0,
\]
and that the flow is generated by an autonomous 3-order action, with
nondegenerate velocity Hessian, whose Noether energy is
\[
  {\mathcal E}_3
  =
  {1\over2}\sum_i m_i|v_i|^2
  +
  {1\over3}
  \sum_{i,j,k,a}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)h_i^a(v_j\cdot v_k),
\]
where \(h_i=r_i-R_c\).

Explain why this model is a locality-relative irreducible ternary replacement
for Newton's three-body problem, and show that total momentum, total angular
momentum, and 3-order energy are conserved under the stated hypotheses.

### Official solution

The force law is not Newtonian pairwise gravity.  In Newtonian mechanics one
writes
\[
  F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}F_{ij},
  \qquad
  F_{ij}=-F_{ji}.
\]
Here the relevant decomposition is not merely forbidden by decree.  The force
is generated by one ternary
tensor vertex:
\[
  F_i^a
  =
  \sum_{j,k}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]
Unrestricted antisymmetric pair fields are too permissive: under momentum
balance they can always be solved for pointwise.  Irreducibility here means
that the connected off-target velocity component is nonzero relative to
strict pair-locality:
\[
  {\partial^2F_i^a\over\partial v_j^b\,\partial v_k^c}
  =
  \left(T^a_{i\,jk}+T^a_{i\,kj}\right)\delta^{bc}\ne0
\]
for some \(i\) and distinct non-target bodies \(j,k\).  Thus the three bodies
are treated as one coupled object by a checkable certificate, not by rhetoric.

Total momentum is conserved because
\[
  {d\over dt}\sum_i p_i^a
  =
  \sum_iF_i^a
  =
  \sum_{i,j,k}
  T^a_{i\,jk}(r)(v_j\cdot v_k).
\]
Using
\[
  \sum_iT^a_{i\,jk}=0,
\]
we get
\[
  {d\over dt}\sum_i p_i=0.
\]

Angular momentum is
\[
  L=\sum_i r_i\times p_i.
\]
Since \(\dot r_i\) is parallel to \(p_i\), the kinetic term gives no torque
contribution:
\[
  {dL\over dt}
  =
  \sum_i r_i\times F_i.
\]
In components,
\[
  {dL_a\over dt}
  =
  \sum_{i,b,c}\epsilon_{abc}r_i^bF_i^c.
\]
Substituting the ternary force law gives
\[
  {dL_a\over dt}
  =
  \sum_{j,k}
  \left(
    \sum_{i,b,c}
    \epsilon_{abc}r_i^bT^c_{i\,jk}
  \right)
  (v_j\cdot v_k).
\]
By the torque condition, the parenthesized term vanishes for each \(j,k\).
Hence
\[
  {dL\over dt}=0.
\]

The 3-order energy is conserved because the action is autonomous and regular.
For
\[
  \ell_3={1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)+{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v),
\]
the Noether charge for time translation is
\[
  \langle v,\partial_v\ell_3\rangle-\ell_3
  =
  {1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)+{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v)
  =
  {\mathcal E}_3.
\]
Autonomy gives
\[
  {d{\mathcal E}_3\over dt}=0.
\]

Finally, the jerk is not an extra force law.  It is the chain-rule derivative
of the ternary force:
\[
  \dot F_i^a
  =
  \sum_{j,b}
  {\partial F_i^a\over\partial r_j^b}\dot r_j^b
  +
  \sum_{j,b}
  {\partial F_i^a\over\partial p_j^b}\dot p_j^b.
\]
Thus the model is predictive once \(T\), the initial data, and a tolerance are
specified: the state evolves by a closed ODE system while preserving total
momentum, angular momentum, 3-order energy, and Nambu phase volume.

## 15. Research Program

The paper leaves several deliberate doors open.  These are not weaknesses to
hide.  They are the point of making the replacement problem explicit.

### 15.1 Projection problem

Find explicit physically meaningful maps
\[
  \eta:Q_3\to\Delta_R^\circ,
  \qquad
  \Pi:T\Delta_R^\circ\to TQ_3
\]
such that the projected \(K^{CH}\) tensor satisfies the momentum and torque
conditions.

This is the main mathematical gate, but the arrow is now constrained.  The
projection should not be treated as a search for a force downstairs on a
pre-given container.  It must first respect the cumulant tower: \(g\) as
metric/inertia, \(K^{CH}\) as variation/source response, and
\({\mathcal E}_3\) as the time-translation Noether charge.  Without
\(\eta\), \(\Pi\), and their closure certificate, the force tensor is an
admissible abstract object, not a physical law.

### 15.2 Irreducibility certificates

Develop practical tests proving that a given tensor field cannot be decomposed
inside its declared locality class, for example strict pair-locality:
\[
  F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij},
  \qquad f_{ij}=-f_{ji}.
\]
Unrestricted full-state \(f_{ij}\) are excluded from this research target,
because Theorem 5.1 makes them pointwise vacuous.  Possible certificates
include mixed-partial tests, rank conditions on locality-constrained force
maps, nonvanishing ternary cohomology classes, or hyper-adjacency support
conditions in the sense of Paper 11.

### 15.3 Nambu integrators

Construct discrete integrators that preserve:

1. total momentum;
2. total angular momentum;
3. 3-order energy;
4. Nambu phase volume;
5. admissibility constraints on \(T\).

The numerical problem should be posed as invariant-preserving integration,
not generic ODE stepping.

Discrete exterior calculus and variational integrator methods provide the
nearest external numerical template: discretize the geometric object being
preserved rather than only the coordinate equation.  For the present program,
the corresponding object is not a symplectic 2-form but the Fisher/Nambu
volume form together with the declared macro-Hamiltonians and physical
momentum-map constraints.

### 15.4 Comparison with Newtonian data

If the model is to touch celestial mechanics, it must recover known pairwise
limits or explain precisely why it applies only to a different interaction
regime.  The comparison problem is:
\[
  \text{When does the ternary tensor reduce to an effective pairwise theory?}
\]
and
\[
  \text{What residual remains when pairwise channels are forced?}
\]

### 15.5 N-ary tensor hierarchy and residual budgets

Extend the 3-order model to fourth and higher cumulants without collapsing the
tower to jerk.  Paper 21 identifies the next natural step, while Paper 30
supplies the residual notation:
\[
  R_{\mathrm{hc}}^{\ge4}
  =
  (R^{(4)},R^{(5)},R^{(6)},\ldots).
\]
The key questions are:

1. whether higher-order mechanics inherits the same Cartan obstruction or
   develops new carry phenomena;
2. whether each claimed \(n\)-ary rung has an irreducibility certificate;
3. whether the corresponding residual is bounded under a declared finite-jet
   and prediction-horizon budget;
4. whether a lower-arity quotient shadow explains the data just as well.
5. whether the time-translation closure certificate continues to hold, and if
   not, whether the residual is a controlled \(R^{(4)}\), \(R^{(\ge4)}\)
   overflow rather than an untyped anomaly.

### 15.6 Large-system scaling

For \(N\gg3\), decide whether the correct object is:

- a sum of local ternary vertices;
- a full \(k\)-uniform hypergraph of irreducible interactions;
- a conductor-resolved tensor network;
- or a projective finite-to-Archimedean limit of Noether currents.

Paper 27 supplies the first rigorous language for the last option.

## 16. Falsifiers and Failure Modes

A useful theory must know how it can fail.

### F1. Pair decomposition exists

If the proposed tensor admits a decomposition inside the declared locality
class \({\mathcal L}\), then the model is not irreducibly ternary relative to
that class.  Unrestricted pair decompositions do not count as a falsifier,
because Theorem 5.1 says they are always available pointwise under momentum
balance.

### F2. Momentum or torque constraints fail

If
\[
  \sum_iT^a_{i\,jk}\ne0
\]
or
\[
  \sum_{i,b,c}\epsilon_{abc}r_i^bT^c_{i\,jk}\ne0,
\]
then the model injects external force or torque unless an additional field is
introduced to carry the missing balance.

### F3. 3-order energy is not conserved

If no autonomous Nambu-Fisher or variational structure produces
\({\mathcal E}_3\) as a conserved quantity, or if the variational Hessian is
degenerate throughout the target physical regime, then the proposed force law
is not closed as a conservative ternary mechanics.

### F4. Projection is arbitrary

If the maps \(\eta\) and \(\Pi\) are chosen only to fit desired trajectories,
the model loses explanatory content.  They must be constrained by geometry,
symmetry, and independent measurements.

### F5. Known limits are violated

Any gravitational application must survive precision two-body and weak-field
tests.  Failure there does not invalidate abstract ternary mechanics, but it
does invalidate that physical application.

### F6. Large-\(N\) scaling has the wrong sign or magnitude

If the effective large-system residual scales incorrectly, the dark-matter-
adjacent motivation fails.

### F7. Nambu closure does not eliminate chaos

Nambu structure preserves volume and macro-Hamiltonians.  It does not, by
itself, guarantee absence of chaotic behavior in every dimension.  Non-chaotic
classification requires enough independent invariants to reduce regular leaves
to low-dimensional curves, as in Paper 19.  Any higher-dimensional extension
must prove the corresponding leaf structure.

### F8. Jerk becomes a catch-all explanation

If every projection error, numerical derivative, missing lift, and
higher-order mismatch is simply renamed "jerk," then the theory has lost its
residual discipline.  Jerk must be measured as \(R^{(4)}\) under a declared
connection and projection convention, and higher residuals must remain visible
as separate unknowns.

### F9. The N-ary ladder collapses to lower-arity shadows

If a claimed \(n\)-ary interaction can be reconstructed from lower-arity
marginals, lower-arity constraints, or pair/ternary quotient shadows, then it
is not irreducibly \(n\)-ary.  The ladder survives only where each rung has a
certificate that it is not reducible to the rungs below.

### F10. The controlled residual audit fails its null or planted tests

The empirical scaffold must reject ternary structure under a pure pairwise
null and recover it under a planted nonadditive triplet channel.  If future
versions hallucinate \(\alpha\ne0\) for \(\alpha=0\), or fail to recover a
known planted \(\alpha\) under held-out initial conditions, then the audit is
not a valid comprobation tool for Paper 28.

### F11. The Bures Schur kernel is not a conservative force generator

The \(Z_{30}\) projected-resolvent signal is not allowed to substitute for the
conservative ternary dynamics.  If the non-self-adjoint generator
\[
  \mathcal L=i[H,\cdot]+D_{\rm dephase}+D_{\rm classical}
\]
cannot be derived from a time-symmetric Bures/Nambu variational structure, or
cannot be reduced to an effective conservative potential/force on the physical
quotient, then it is a separate Bures-to-Laplacian model.  In that case it may

## 17. Technical Gap Ledger

The purpose of this paper is partly to expose the missing work.  The main
gaps are now explicit.

### G1. The body map is canonical only as a finite-row projection

The physical bodies \((r_i,p_i)\) live in a Euclidean center-of-mass phase
space.  The Amari-Chentsov tensor lives on a categorical simplex or its
density-matrix completion.  A physical theory requires a principled map
\[
  \eta:Q_3\to\Delta_R^\circ
\]
and projection
\[
  \Pi:T\Delta_R^\circ\to TQ_3.
\]
The \(Z_6\) bridge now supplies such a pair for the finite-row prototype:
\(\eta\) is the \(e\)-pure dyadic product base plus scale-free Heron copula
gate, and \(\Pi=\Pi_{\rm EL}\) is the Euler-Lagrange projection of the
pulled-back cubic Fisher action.  This clears arbitrary channel choice in the
prototype.  It does not yet turn the model into a law of celestial motion,
because the finite-row metric residual and any gravitational parameter
selection remain typed, falsifiable overflow.

### G2. Irreducibility needs certificates

The old axiom "no pair decomposition exists" has been replaced by a
locality-relative certificate.  Section 5.2 supplies a strict pair-local
velocity-sector test, but the mature program still needs broader certificates.
Candidate routes include:

1. a nonzero third cumulant after all pairwise marginal cumulants vanish;
2. a nonzero component in the Fisher-orthogonal complement of all pairwise
   subspaces;
3. a nontrivial hyper-adjacency support class in the Paper 11 operadic tower;
4. a cohomological obstruction to writing \(F_i=\sum_{j\ne i}f_{ij}\) inside a
   declared locality sheaf, not among arbitrary full-state functions.

The present paper provides a useful certificate for the displayed force law.
It does not yet provide a universal certificate for every admissible locality
class.

### G3. Euclidean symmetries are external to the simplex

Nambu-Fisher mechanics preserves its macro-Hamiltonians and Fisher volume.
Ordinary linear and angular momentum are Euclidean moment-map quantities.  A
physical realization must prove that the projection intertwines the simplex
flow with the relevant translation and rotation symmetries, or else impose the
momentum and torque constraints directly as this paper does.

### G4. The 3-order energy must be part of the Hamiltonian data

Paper 17 supplies the 3-order Noether energy on the simplex.  In a projected
physical system, one must decide whether \({\mathcal E}_3\) is:

1. one of the Nambu macro-Hamiltonians;
2. the Noether energy of a projected autonomous action;
3. or an approximate invariant with a controlled projection error.

These are different claims.

The strongest gravity-adjacent reading requires the third option to be
measured rather than hidden.  If \({\mathcal E}_3\) fails to close after
projection, the defect is a time-translation closure residual.  It must be
typed, bounded, assigned to a connection convention, and tested against the
Filippov/fundamental-identity bookkeeping before it is compared with
stress-energy or effective attraction.

### G5. Projection-dependent jerk terms must be measured

If the projection \(\Pi\) varies, the jerk contains \(D_t\Pi\) terms.  Those
terms may be physically meaningful leakage, gauge artifacts, or evidence that
the chosen projection is wrong.  They cannot be silently folded into
\(T^{(4)}\) without a connection convention.

### G6. Higher-order non-chaos requires a leaf-dimension proof

Paper 19 gives a clean long-time classification for the finite Nambu-Fisher
setting it studies.  A larger \(N\)-body or higher-cumulant system may have
regular leaves of higher dimension.  Volume preservation and conserved
macro-Hamiltonians do not alone imply non-chaotic motion.  Any claim of
non-chaotic long-time behavior must prove the relevant leaf classification.

### G7. Astrophysics is a separate model

The dark-matter-adjacent question is valuable because it is sharp:
can projected higher-order geometry produce an effective residual that scales
like missing binding?  But answering it requires a spacetime model, data
comparison, lensing predictions, and weak-field limits.  None of those are
supplied here.

The residual to test is specifically the closure defect of a projected
Noether-current and cumulant tower, not a free parameter named "missing mass."
This keeps the question falsifiable: if the defect does not close in the
weak-field/two-body limit, has the wrong sign, or cannot be separated from a
standard mass model, the gravity-adjacent interpretation is rejected.

### G8. N-ary arity certificates are missing

Paper 28 gives a ternary irreducibility condition.  A mature N-ary ladder needs
certificates for \(n>3\): tests showing that an \(n\)-tuple constraint cannot
be reconstructed from lower-arity shadows.  Candidate tools include
hyper-adjacency support, higher cumulant vanishing tests for lower marginals,
filtered carry classes, and packet-resolved residual comparisons.

### G9. Higher-cumulant residuals need typed budgets

The jerk term is only \(R^{(4)}\).  A higher-order extension must define
\[
  R^{(5)},R^{(6)},\ldots
\]
as typed residuals rather than unnamed derivatives.  Each term needs a frame,
connection, norm or support ledger, boundedness claim, and falsifier.  Without
that bookkeeping, the N-ary ladder becomes rhetoric instead of mechanics.

### G10. From planted residuals to discovered residuals

The current three-body audit is planted: the ternary feature is known, and the
test is whether the recovery machinery finds it while rejecting it under the
null.  The next empirical gate is discovery: given trajectory data generated
by an unknown force law, can the audit distinguish pairwise closure, planted
triplet residuals, shortcut pairwise bases, and genuinely new residual
features without being told the correct triplet potential in advance?

### G11. The four-body gate must separate edges, faces, and tetrahedra

The expanded audit now discharges the planted version of this gate.  It is not
a generic \(N\)-body simulation; it is a four-body arity-resolution test.
Pairwise edge models, triplet face models, and a genuine tetrahedral residual
are separated by quotient recovery, held-out trajectory replay, opposite-edge
and vertex-star C4 minimal-nonface derivatives, planar-degeneration controls,
and cluster-separation controls.  The next gap is no longer planted
separability; it is discovery under unknown force laws and noise.

### G12. The five-body gate must decide whether snap is stateful

Five bodies should be used to test connected residuals after all edge, face,
and tetrahedral sub-wholes have been removed.  The arity certificate is
\({\mathsf C}_5\), not snap.  Snap becomes mandatory only if the connected
five-body residual has time-concavity large enough to violate the declared
prediction-horizon budget:
\[
  {H^4\|M^{-1}D_t^2R_{\le4}\|\over24}>\varepsilon_r,
  \qquad
  R_{\le4}=F_{\mathrm{truth}}-F_{\le4}
\]
or the corresponding velocity/acceleration inequalities fail.  If those
inequalities remain below tolerance, \(R^{(5)}\) remains a bounded remainder.
If they fail, snap must be promoted to a tracked residual state.

## 19. Conclusion

The classical three-body problem is usually introduced as the moment where
prediction breaks.  This paper proposes a different use for it: as the minimal
stage on which to ask whether pairwise force decomposition is the correct
primitive at all.

That gives Paper 28 a defensible closure claim.  The classical three-body
program is not closed as mathematics, astronomy, or computation.  It is closed
as the central foundation problem of this simplicial sequence.  Its new role is
to serve as the canonical dyadic benchmark quotient: the baseline that proves
what pairwise mechanics can carry, and therefore exposes what it cannot carry
without typed residuals.

Starting from the third-order information-geometric mechanics developed in Papers
13--18 and 24, we formulated an irreducible ternary replacement model.  The
force is generated by a projected Amari-Chentsov third-order tensor, not by a sum of
binary action-reaction channels.  The first hard correction is that this
cannot mean "no arbitrary antisymmetric pair decomposition," because such
decompositions are pointwise available whenever total momentum balances.
Irreducibility must be locality-relative, and the connected off-target tensor
is the certificate used here.  Conservation is global rather than pairwise:
zero total force and zero total torque are balance conditions on the ternary
tensor, while 3-order energy is conserved only after the projected action pays
the autonomy and reduced-regularity costs stated in Theorem 6.3.  The second
hard correction is Theorem 5.2: strict \(SE(3)\)-invariance makes full
regularity on \(T_rQ_3\) impossible, because rotational gauge directions lie
in the kernel of \(D\eta\).  The repair is not to add more channels
indiscriminately, but to require regularity only on the three-dimensional
shape space and to use exactly three genuinely ternary shape channels.

The resulting theory is predictive in the precise mathematical sense: once the
tensor, initial data, and tolerance are supplied, the state evolves by a closed
ODE/Nambu system with explicit invariants.  The jerk is not a mysterious source
of unpredictability; it is the derivative of the force tensor and, in the
underlying simplex theory, the first fourth-cumulant leakage pointer.

The larger interpretation is the cumulant-effective-action bridge.  The
second cumulant \(g\) is both metric and inertia, so the program does not need
separate primitive notions of space and mass at the categorical level.  The
third cumulant \(K^{CH}\) is the first source/force response, and fourth and
higher cumulants measure the projection defects that appear when a shadow
cannot carry the whole tower.  Time, energy, and any gravity-adjacent residual
therefore sit on one Noether line: flow parameter, conserved charge, and
closure anomaly.  Paper 28 does not derive gravity from this line; it turns
that possibility into a closure certificate with explicit failure modes.

The major missing piece has been sharpened rather than wished away.  Theorem
7.2 constructs a regular \(SE(3)\)-natural connected witness, so the internal
existence gap is closed.  The \(Z_6\) canonical bridge certificate now selects a
finite-row projection from the admissible class: \(e\)-pure dyadic marginals,
the scale-free Heron copula gate, and \(\Pi_{\rm EL}\).  That clears T*1' in the
finite prototype sense.  It does not clear the physical force gate.  The first
E5 result is two-sided and is reported as such: a parameter-fixed reduced-shape
gate passes sign-definitely at
\(\lambda_{\rm E5}\approx9.93\times10^{-9}\), while the Cartesian ephemeris
gate fixes \(\lambda_{\rm E5}\approx7.48\times10^{-11}\) and fails by a
controlled \(10^{-9}\)-scale position/velocity margin with a below-floor
near-equilateral signal.  In the language of Paper 30 this is typed projection
overflow, not a refuted theory and not a validated one; in the language of
Paper 31 it is a closure certificate with one passed reduced gate, one named
bounded overflow, and an explicit continuum/Bures continuation target.

That continuation target is now more precise, and also more dangerous.  The
inverse-square law is a Green-kernel quotient condition, not a parameter to
insert by hand.  The reversible leg-local CRT/Bures route has been finitely
killed as a local Laplace quotient: it can isotropize the second symbol only by
mean-field coherences whose fourth moment does not decay.  But the coherent
route is not killed by that result.  The diagonal off-diagonal audit is only a
carre-du-champ identity check on commuting coordinate observables.  The first
full projected non-reversible resolvent audit on \(Z_{30}\) shows that
off-diagonal coherences can change the physical Green kernel through the Schur
correction \(L_{PQ}L_{QQ}^{-1}L_{QP}\): with the diagonal Gamma shadow still
mean-field and nonlocal, the projected kernel reaches a finite
\(1/r\)-fit \(R^2=0.8797\), and the Schur reconstruction matches the full
resolvent at machine precision.

This is not yet gravity recovery, and it is not load-bearing for the
conservative ternary force law as presently written.  The Schur audit uses a
non-self-adjoint generator with dephasing and dissipation; the conservation
results of this paper use autonomous variational/Nambu dynamics.  Unless a
bridge derives that non-symmetric effective operator from the conservative
Bures lift, or shows how its projected resolvent reduces to a conservative
potential on the physical quotient, the \(Z_{30}\) signal belongs to a
neighboring Bures-to-Laplacian program rather than to Paper 28's proof spine.
The immediate task is therefore not merely \(Z_{210}\) refinement.  It is the
conservative Schur-bridge problem.

The later participation, residual, and projection-closure papers sharpen the
meaning of that door.  Paper 29 says that identity is whole-relative
participation, not an absolute pairwise score.  Paper 30 says that projection
failure is typed overflow, not an excuse for arbitrary explanation.  Paper 31
says that a projection is legitimate only when its required participation
survives and its residuals remain typed, bounded, stable, and falsifiable.
Paper 28 is the dynamical version of the same lesson: if a pairwise shadow
cannot carry the triplet, the residual should be measured, typed, and tested
before it is promoted to ontology.  The controlled audit now included in
`empirical/three_body_ternary_residual_audit.py` supplies the first local
comprobation scaffold.  Its status is conditional: it rejects a ternary
coefficient under the pairwise null, recovers a planted \(\alpha=0.035\)
triplet channel with test \(R^2=1.0\), and shows the pairwise replay diverging
while the recovered ternary replay remains at numerical floor.  Its falsifier
is just as important: a degree-of-freedom matched pairwise model that performs
indistinguishably on held-out trajectories would make empirical irreducibility
vacuous in that regime.

But ternary mechanics must not become a new stopping point.  The slogan "jerk
is all we need" would repeat the same low-order mistake at the next rung.
Jerk is \(R^{(4)}\), the first named member of the higher-cumulant residual
tower, not the end of it.  A mature N-ary mechanics must climb with
discipline: arity certificate, conservation law, projection map, residual
budget, stability gate, and falsifier.

The immediate next chamber is the four-body gate.  Four bodies contain six
edges, four triangular faces, and one tetrahedral whole, so they are the first
place where the program can test whether lower-arity shadows and a genuine
4-ary residual can be separated under the same controls.  That is the
mathematical-physics reason to move attention from the classical three-body
foundation room to the N-ary ladder room.  The new four-body audit supplies
the first local test: lower pairwise-plus-face controls explain only
\(R^2=0.35115\) of the planted tetrahedral residual on held-out force samples,
whereas the admitted tetrahedral feature recovers \(\beta=0.08\) with
\(R^2=1.0\).  Its expanded C4 detector also passes on 18 held-out asymmetric
tetrahedra, with minimum tetra/face C4 ratio about \(2.09\times10^4\), and the
planar and cluster-separation controls suppress the tetrahedral channel.  The
reported gate status is **PASS_ARITY_RESOLUTION_GATE**.

The five-body analysis adds the next discipline.  Five bodies do not
automatically mean snap.  They force a connected-residual test after edge,
face, and tetrahedral shadows have been removed.  Snap becomes compulsory only
when the residual force concavity \(M^{-1}D_t^2R\) exceeds the declared
prediction-horizon budget after force and force drift have already been
admitted or bounded.  Otherwise \(R^{(5)}\) remains a bounded remainder rather
than a new state variable.

That is the research door.  If it opens, the three-body problem stops being
only a symbol of chaos.  It becomes the first test of a post-binary mechanics
and the first invitation to the N-ary ladder: not three pairwise
conversations, but one triangular interaction, followed by the higher
constraints that the triangle itself cannot yet carry.

In the language of Paper 31, this whole paper can be compressed to one
closure question:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{What can the pairwise shadow carry, what overflows, and does the
  overflow close?}
  }
\]
