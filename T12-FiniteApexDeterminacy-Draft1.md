## Abstract

**Situation.** T0 named the Fourier-Mellin apex
\[
\mathcal M_x(\beta,c)=\frac1N\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z/N}e^{\beta y_s}\overline{\chi_c(s)}
\]
as the two-axis generator of the lamed-series. T2 read Taylor derivatives of the log-apex as a cumulant effective action. T3 and T11 proved the character-conservation vertex rules that govern the Fourier channels. T10 placed the integer gauge samples into a power-closure hierarchy. These papers make the tower visible.

**Gap.** Visibility is not yet determinacy. The apex has infinitely many Taylor coefficients, infinitely many scalar moments, infinitely many cumulants, and, in the structured setting, one such tower in every character channel. It is tempting to treat high order as a reservoir of endlessly new information. That is wrong in the finite simplex case. Since the centered ledger \(y\) assumes only finitely many values, the entire tower must be generated by a finite spectral measure.

**Action.** We prove six structure theorems. First, the **Finite Spectral Support Theorem**: the scalar moment tower depends only on the distinct ledger levels and their aggregate weights. Second, the **Hankel Rank Closure Theorem**: the moment Hankel matrices stabilize at the number of distinct ledger levels, and all higher moments obey the recurrence produced by the minimal closure polynomial. Third, the **Newton-Hankel Reconstruction Theorem**: a finite prefix of the tower reconstructs the closure polynomial, the ledger levels, and the weights. Fourth, the **Channel-Resolved Closure Theorem**: every Fourier channel obeys the same closure polynomial. Fifth, the **Cumulant Closure Theorem**: higher cumulants are generated by the same finite spectral data. Sixth, the **Finite Apex Determinacy Theorem**: the apex tower is the quotient algebra \(\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)\) seen through scalar and channel linear functionals.

**Result.** The main theorem is the finite-algebraic compression
\[
\boxed{\text{apex tower of }x\quad \cong\quad \mathbb R[t]/(P_x)}
\]
where
\[
P_x(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t-\lambda_j)
\]
and \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) are the distinct values of the centered ledger. Moments, cumulants, channel moments, and apex generating functions are not independent infinite objects. They are finite spectral consequences.

**Harvest.** T11 gives finite Feynman rules along the character axis. T12 gives finite determinacy along the tensor/gauge-order axis. Together they say: character labels fuse by conductor arithmetic, while orders close by a Hankel/Newton annihilating polynomial. The next natural step is exact finite renormalization: when a projected sector leaks through the T11 boundary, T12 supplies the finite algebra in which all induced corrections must live.

**Keywords:** apex generator, moment tower, cumulant tower, finite moment problem, Hankel rank, Newton identities, Prony reconstruction, closure polynomial, spectral measure, quotient algebra, Fourier channels, finite determinacy, effective action.

## Notation

Let \(\Omega\) be a finite support set. In the structured case,
\[
\Omega=G=\mathbb Z/N.
\]
Let \(x:\Omega\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) be an interior positive ledger, and let
\[
y_s=\log x_s-\sum_{t\in\Omega}w_t\log x_t
\]
be the centered log-ledger with respect to fixed positive weights
\[
w_s>0,\qquad \sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s=1.
\]
The unweighted case is recovered by \(w_s=1/|\Omega|\).

Assume \(y\) takes exactly \(r\) distinct values
\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r.
\]
Let
\[
\Omega_j=\{s\in\Omega:y_s=\lambda_j\},
\qquad
W_j=\sum_{s\in\Omega_j}w_s.
\]
Since the weights are positive and every \(\lambda_j\) is assumed attained,
\[
W_j>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^rW_j=1.
\]

The scalar moments are
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s y_s^n,\qquad n\ge0.
\]
The scalar moment-generating apex and cumulant-generating apex are
\[
M(\beta)=\sum_{n\ge0}\mu_n\frac{\beta^n}{n!}
=\sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s e^{\beta y_s},
\]
and
\[
K(\beta)=\log M(\beta),\qquad \kappa_n=K^{(n)}(0).
\]

The closure polynomial is
\[
P_x(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t-\lambda_j)
=t^r+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0.
\]

The Hankel matrices are
\[
H_k=(\mu_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le k}.
\]

In the structured Fourier case, let \(\chi_c\) be a character on \(G\), and let \(\alpha_c(s)\) be a channel coefficient, for example
\[
\alpha_c(s)=\frac1N\overline{\chi_c(s)}
\]
or, in a weighted version,
\[
\alpha_c(s)=w_s\overline{\chi_c(s)}.
\]
The channel moment tower is
\[
M_c^{(n)}=\sum_{s\in\Omega}\alpha_c(s)y_s^n.
\]

## 1. Why finite determinacy is needed

T11 proves that the apex has finite vertex rules:
\[
\frac{d^n}{d\beta^n}\mathcal M_x(0,c)
=
\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=c}
\widehat y_{a_1}\cdots\widehat y_{a_n}.
\]
This is the right answer along the character axis. It says how Fourier labels fuse and how conductor packets leak.

But it leaves a different question open:

\[
\text{How many independent orders does the apex really contain?}
\]

At first sight, the tower looks infinite:
\[
\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,
\qquad
\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3,\ldots,
\qquad
M_c^{(0)},M_c^{(1)},M_c^{(2)},\ldots.
\]
T12 answers that the infinity is not algebraic. In the finite simplex case, powers of the ledger
\[
1,y,y^2,y^3,\ldots
\]
live inside the finite-dimensional algebra generated by the finitely many values of \(y\). Once \(y\) has \(r\) distinct values, every power \(y^n\) reduces modulo one degree-\(r\) polynomial.

Thus T11 and T12 close two different axes:

| Axis | T11 | T12 |
|---|---|---|
| Character/channel axis | finite vertex conservation and conductor fusion | channel towers share one closure polynomial |
| Tensor/order axis | \(n\)-leg diagrams | Hankel/Newton finite recurrence |
| Projection failures | conductor-fusion boundary | induced corrections still lie in finite apex algebra |

The slogan of T12 is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{higher order is real, but not freely new.}}
\]

## 2. Finite spectral support

### Theorem 1. Finite Spectral Support Theorem

Let \(y:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) take exactly \(r\) distinct values
\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r.
\]
Then the scalar moment tower
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s y_s^n
\]
is the moment tower of the finite atomic measure
\[
\nu_x=\sum_{j=1}^r W_j\delta_{\lambda_j},
\qquad
W_j=\sum_{s:y_s=\lambda_j}w_s.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mu_n=\int t^n\,d\nu_x(t)
=\sum_{j=1}^r W_j\lambda_j^n
\]
for every \(n\ge0\). Thus the scalar tower depends only on the distinct ledger levels and their aggregate weights, not on the individual labels in \(\Omega\).

**Proof.** Partition \(\Omega\) into level sets
\[
\Omega_j=\{s\in\Omega:y_s=\lambda_j\}.
\]
These sets are disjoint and cover \(\Omega\). Hence, for every \(n\ge0\),
\[
\mu_n
=\sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s y_s^n
=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{s\in\Omega_j}w_s y_s^n.
\]
On \(\Omega_j\), \(y_s=\lambda_j\), so
\[
\mu_n
=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{s\in\Omega_j}w_s\lambda_j^n
=\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j^n\sum_{s\in\Omega_j}w_s
=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n.
\]
This is exactly
\[
\int t^n\,d\left(\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\delta_{\lambda_j}\right).
\]
Therefore the scalar moments are generated by \(\nu_x\). \(\square\)

The first theorem is elementary, but it is the turn in the road. The simplex may have \(N\) labels, while the ledger may have only \(r\le N\) distinct levels. The scalar tower sees the level spectrum, not the raw labels.

## 3. Hankel rank closure

### Theorem 2. Hankel Rank Closure Theorem

Assume
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n
\]
with distinct \(\lambda_j\) and positive \(W_j\). Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_k=\min(k+1,r).
\]
In particular, the Hankel rank stabilizes at \(r\).

Moreover, if
\[
P_x(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t-\lambda_j)
=t^r+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0,
\]
then every moment satisfies
\[
\mu_{n+r}+a_{r-1}\mu_{n+r-1}+\cdots+a_0\mu_n=0
\]
for all \(n\ge0\). This \(P_x\) is the unique monic recurrence polynomial of minimal degree.

**Proof.** For \(0\le i,j\le k\),
\[
(H_k)_{ij}
=\mu_{i+j}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^rW_\ell\lambda_\ell^{i+j}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^rW_\ell\lambda_\ell^i\lambda_\ell^j.
\]
Let
\[
V_k=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&\cdots&1\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_2&\cdots&\lambda_r\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\lambda_1^k&\lambda_2^k&\cdots&\lambda_r^k
\end{pmatrix}
\]
and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(W_1,\ldots,W_r)\). Then
\[
H_k=V_kDV_k^T.
\]
Since all \(W_j>0\), \(D\) is invertible and positive definite. The distinctness of the \(\lambda_j\) implies that every square Vandermonde minor of size at most \(r\) has nonzero determinant. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}V_k=\min(k+1,r),
\]
and hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_k=\min(k+1,r).
\]

Now \(P_x(\lambda_j)=0\) for every \(j\). Therefore, for every \(n\ge0\),
\[
0=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^nP_x(\lambda_j).
\]
Expanding \(P_x\) gives
\[
0
=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^{n+r}
+a_{r-1}\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^{n+r-1}
+\cdots
+a_0\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n,
\]
which is exactly
\[
\mu_{n+r}+a_{r-1}\mu_{n+r-1}+\cdots+a_0\mu_n=0.
\]

It remains to prove minimality. Suppose a monic polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(q<r\) gives a recurrence for all \(n\ge0\). Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^nQ(\lambda_j)=0
\]
for \(n=0,\ldots,r-1\). Set \(A_j=W_jQ(\lambda_j)\). Then
\[
\sum_{j=1}^rA_j\lambda_j^n=0,
\qquad n=0,\ldots,r-1.
\]
This is a Vandermonde system with invertible matrix, so \(A_j=0\) for all \(j\). Since \(W_j>0\), \(Q(\lambda_j)=0\) for all \(j\). But a nonzero polynomial of degree \(q<r\) cannot vanish at \(r\) distinct points. Contradiction. Hence \(P_x\) is the minimal monic recurrence polynomial. \(\square\)

The closure polynomial is the apex analog of an annihilating polynomial. Once it is known, no scalar moment above order \(r-1\) is algebraically free.

## 4. Newton-Hankel reconstruction

### Theorem 3. Newton-Hankel Reconstruction Theorem

Assume \(r\) is known and
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n
\]
with distinct \(\lambda_j\) and positive \(W_j\). Then the finite prefix
\[
\mu_0,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{2r-1}
\]
uniquely determines:

1. the closure polynomial \(P_x(t)\);
2. the levels \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\), up to permutation;
3. the weights \(W_1,\ldots,W_r\), with the same permutation.

**Proof.** By Theorem 2, the moments obey
\[
\mu_{n+r}+a_{r-1}\mu_{n+r-1}+\cdots+a_0\mu_n=0.
\]
For \(n=0,\ldots,r-1\), this gives the linear system
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_0&\mu_1&\cdots&\mu_{r-1}\\
\mu_1&\mu_2&\cdots&\mu_r\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mu_{r-1}&\mu_r&\cdots&\mu_{2r-2}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
a_0\\a_1\\\vdots\\a_{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}
=-
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_r\\\mu_{r+1}\\\vdots\\\mu_{2r-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix on the left is \(H_{r-1}\). By Theorem 2,
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_{r-1}=r,
\]
so \(H_{r-1}\) is invertible. Thus the coefficients \(a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1}\) are uniquely determined by the displayed moment prefix.

The polynomial
\[
P_x(t)=t^r+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0
\]
is therefore reconstructed. Its roots are exactly \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\), so the levels are determined up to permutation.

Finally, use the first \(r\) moments:
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n,
\qquad n=0,\ldots,r-1.
\]
This is the Vandermonde system
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&\cdots&1\\
\lambda_1&\lambda_2&\cdots&\lambda_r\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\lambda_1^{r-1}&\lambda_2^{r-1}&\cdots&\lambda_r^{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
W_1\\W_2\\\vdots\\W_r
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_0\\\mu_1\\\vdots\\\mu_{r-1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Vandermonde determinant
\[
\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_j-\lambda_i)
\]
is nonzero, so the weights are uniquely determined. \(\square\)

### 4.2 Rank-detected form

If \(r\) is not known in advance, exact rank detection supplies it. In the positive finite atomic case,
\[
\det H_{r-1}\ne0,\qquad \det H_r=0.
\]
Thus \(r\) is the first stabilized Hankel rank, equivalently the first size at which the next Hankel determinant vanishes after positive definiteness at the previous size. Certifying this rank drop requires the moment \(\mu_{2r}\); reconstructing \(P_x\) and the atoms then uses the prefix through \(\mu_{2r-1}\).

This is the finite moment-problem meaning of the apex tower: the tower carries its own stopping rule.

## 5. Channel-resolved closure

### Theorem 4. Channel-Resolved Closure Theorem

Let \(M_c^{(n)}\) be any channel tower of the form
\[
M_c^{(n)}=\sum_{s\in\Omega}\alpha_c(s)y_s^n,
\]
where \(\alpha_c\) is any fixed channel coefficient. Then every channel obeys the same closure recurrence
\[
M_c^{(n+r)}
+a_{r-1}M_c^{(n+r-1)}
+\cdots
+a_0M_c^{(n)}
=0
\]
for all \(n\ge0\), where
\[
P_x(t)=t^r+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0
\]
is the scalar closure polynomial.

Equivalently, if
\[
A_{c,j}=\sum_{s:y_s=\lambda_j}\alpha_c(s),
\]
then
\[
M_c^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^r A_{c,j}\lambda_j^n.
\]

**Proof.** Since the distinct values of \(y\) are the roots of \(P_x\),
\[
P_x(y_s)=0
\]
for every \(s\in\Omega\). Hence for every \(n\ge0\),
\[
y_s^nP_x(y_s)=0.
\]
Expanding,
\[
y_s^{n+r}
+a_{r-1}y_s^{n+r-1}
+\cdots
+a_0y_s^n=0.
\]
Multiply by \(\alpha_c(s)\) and sum over \(s\):
\[
\sum_s\alpha_c(s)y_s^{n+r}
+a_{r-1}\sum_s\alpha_c(s)y_s^{n+r-1}
+\cdots
+a_0\sum_s\alpha_c(s)y_s^n
=0.
\]
By definition of \(M_c^{(m)}\), this is the claimed recurrence.

For the equivalent spectral form, group the sum by level sets:
\[
M_c^{(n)}
=\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{s:y_s=\lambda_j}\alpha_c(s)\lambda_j^n
=\sum_{j=1}^rA_{c,j}\lambda_j^n.
\]
\(\square\)

A channel may have smaller rank if some amplitudes \(A_{c,j}\) vanish. But no channel can escape the common closure polynomial produced by the ledger spectrum.

## 6. Cumulant closure

### Theorem 5. Cumulant Closure Theorem

Let
\[
M(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j e^{\beta\lambda_j}
\]
and
\[
K(\beta)=\log M(\beta).
\]
Then the full cumulant tower
\[
\kappa_n=K^{(n)}(0)
\]
is determined by the same finite spectral data
\[
\{(\lambda_j,W_j)\}_{j=1}^r,
\]
equivalently by the finite Newton-Hankel reconstruction data of Theorem 3. No higher cumulant contains independent data beyond the finite spectral measure \(\nu_x\).

**Proof.** Since \(W_j>0\) and \(\sum_jW_j=1\),
\[
M(0)=1.
\]
Therefore \(M(\beta)\) is nonzero in some neighborhood of \(\beta=0\), and
\[
K(\beta)=\log M(\beta)
\]
is analytic near \(0\). Thus every cumulant
\[
\kappa_n=K^{(n)}(0)
\]
is uniquely determined by \(M\).

But
\[
M(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j e^{\beta\lambda_j}
\]
is completely determined by the finite spectral data. Hence all cumulants are determined by that same data.

Equivalently, the moment-cumulant relation gives
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}\prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa_{|B|}
\]
and Mobius inversion gives
\[
\kappa_n=
\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}
(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
\prod_{B\in\pi}\mu_{|B|}.
\]
Thus each \(\kappa_n\) is a universal polynomial in \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\). By Theorem 2, every moment \(\mu_m\) with \(m\ge r\) is generated recursively from \(P_x\) and the first \(r\) moments. By Theorem 3, \(P_x\) and the finite spectral measure are reconstructed from a finite prefix. Therefore the entire cumulant tower is finite spectral data in logarithmic connected coordinates. \(\square\)

The cumulants continue forever as numbers, but not as independent degrees of freedom. They are connected shadows of one finite atomic spectrum.

## 7. The finite apex algebra

### Theorem 6. The Apex Tower Is a Finite Spectral Object

Let \(y:\Omega\to\mathbb R\) take exactly \(r\) distinct values
\[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r,
\]
and let
\[
P_x(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(t-\lambda_j).
\]
Then the scalar moment tower, cumulant tower, apex generating function, closure recurrence, and every channel-resolved tower are finite consequences of the quotient algebra
\[
\mathbb R[t]/(P_x).
\]
In particular, the apex tower is infinite only as an indexed sequence. Algebraically it is the finite spectral algebra generated by the ledger.

**Proof.** Let \(A_y\) be the algebra generated by the ledger observable \(y\) inside the algebra of functions on \(\Omega\):
\[
A_y=\{q(y):q\in\mathbb R[t]\}.
\]
Define the evaluation homomorphism
\[
\Phi:\mathbb R[t]\to A_y,
\qquad
\Phi(q)=q(y).
\]
Since \(y\) takes exactly the values \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\),
\[
q(y)=0
\]
if and only if
\[
q(\lambda_j)=0
\]
for every \(j=1,\ldots,r\). Because the \(\lambda_j\) are distinct, this occurs if and only if \(P_x\) divides \(q\). Hence
\[
\ker\Phi=(P_x).
\]
By the first isomorphism theorem,
\[
A_y\cong\mathbb R[t]/(P_x).
\]

Thus every power \(y^n\) has a unique reduction modulo \(P_x\) to a polynomial in \(y\) of degree \(<r\). This is the algebraic source of the closure recurrence.

Define the scalar linear functional
\[
L(q)=\sum_{s\in\Omega}w_s q(y_s).
\]
Then
\[
\mu_n=L(t^n).
\]
Since \(t^n\) is seen only through its residue class in \(\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)\), the scalar moment tower is the finite quotient algebra viewed through \(L\). In level form,
\[
L(q)=\sum_{j=1}^rW_jq(\lambda_j),
\]
so
\[
\mu_n=\sum_{j=1}^rW_j\lambda_j^n.
\]

Inside \(\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)\),
\[
t^r+a_{r-1}t^{r-1}+\cdots+a_0=0.
\]
Multiplying by \(t^n\) and applying \(L\) gives
\[
\mu_{n+r}+a_{r-1}\mu_{n+r-1}+\cdots+a_0\mu_n=0,
\]
which is the Hankel closure recurrence.

The moment-generating apex is
\[
M(\beta)=L(e^{\beta t})
=\sum_{j=1}^rW_je^{\beta\lambda_j}.
\]
Hence \(M\) is a finite exponential polynomial. Since \(M(0)=1\), the cumulant apex
\[
K(\beta)=\log M(\beta)
\]
is analytic near zero, and every cumulant \(K^{(n)}(0)\) is determined by the same finite data.

For a channel \(c\), define
\[
L_c(q)=\sum_{s\in\Omega}\alpha_c(s)q(y_s).
\]
Then
\[
M_c^{(n)}=L_c(t^n).
\]
Again \(t^n\) is seen only modulo \(P_x\). Grouping by level sets gives
\[
M_c^{(n)}=\sum_{j=1}^rA_{c,j}\lambda_j^n,
\qquad
A_{c,j}=\sum_{s:y_s=\lambda_j}\alpha_c(s).
\]
Therefore every channel is a linear functional on the same finite apex algebra.

Finally, Theorem 3 reconstructs \(P_x\), the \(\lambda_j\), and the \(W_j\) from a finite moment prefix. Thus the scalar moments, cumulants, apex function, closure recurrence, and channel recurrences are all finite spectral consequences of \(\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)\). \(\square\)

The main theorem should be read as an equivalence of viewpoints:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{finite ledger spectrum}\\
\text{minimal closure polynomial}\\
\text{Hankel rank}\\
\text{finite moment reconstruction}\\
\text{cumulant tower}\\
\text{channel tower}
\end{array}
\quad
\text{are one object seen through different probes.}
}
\]

## 8. Worked example: three ledger levels

Let \(\Omega=\mathbb Z/6\) with uniform weights, and suppose the centered ledger has three levels, each appearing twice:
\[
y=(a,a,b,b,c,c),
\qquad
a+b+c=0,
\qquad
a,b,c\text{ distinct}.
\]
Then
\[
r=3,
\qquad
W_a=W_b=W_c=\frac13,
\]
and
\[
\nu_x=\frac13\delta_a+\frac13\delta_b+\frac13\delta_c.
\]
The closure polynomial is
\[
P_x(t)=(t-a)(t-b)(t-c).
\]
Since \(a+b+c=0\),
\[
P_x(t)=t^3+(ab+ac+bc)t-abc.
\]
Therefore every scalar moment obeys
\[
\mu_{n+3}+(ab+ac+bc)\mu_{n+1}-abc\,\mu_n=0.
\]

For instance,
\[
\mu_4=-(ab+ac+bc)\mu_2+abc\,\mu_1.
\]
Because the ledger is centered, \(\mu_1=0\), so
\[
\mu_4=-(ab+ac+bc)\mu_2.
\]
The fourth moment is not freely new. It is already forced by the degree-three closure polynomial.

For a character channel \(c_0\), define
\[
\alpha_{c_0}(s)=\frac16\overline{\chi_{c_0}(s)}.
\]
Then
\[
M_{c_0}^{(n)}
=A_{c_0,a}a^n+A_{c_0,b}b^n+A_{c_0,c}c^n,
\]
where
\[
A_{c_0,a}=\sum_{s:y_s=a}\frac16\overline{\chi_{c_0}(s)}
\]
and similarly for \(b,c\). Every such channel obeys the same recurrence
\[
M_{c_0}^{(n+3)}
+(ab+ac+bc)M_{c_0}^{(n+1)}
-abc\,M_{c_0}^{(n)}
=0.
\]

This example separates the two closures:

1. T11 determines which character channels can appear when products are expanded.
2. T12 determines which orders are independent once the channel is fixed.

The smallest cross-prime example \(N=6\) remains the natural laboratory, but T12 adds a new invariant: the number \(r\) of distinct ledger levels.

## 9. Relation to diagrams and effective actions

T11 writes the \(n\)-th channel coefficient as a finite vertex sum:
\[
\frac{d^n}{d\beta^n}\mathcal M_x(0,c)
=
\sum_{a_1+\cdots+a_n=c}
\widehat y_{a_1}\cdots\widehat y_{a_n}.
\]
T12 says these coefficients, as \(n\) varies, satisfy
\[
M_c^{(n+r)}
+a_{r-1}M_c^{(n+r-1)}
+\cdots
+a_0M_c^{(n)}=0.
\]
Thus diagrams of order \(n+r\) are not independent of lower-order diagrams once the ledger spectrum is fixed.

This does not make higher diagrams meaningless. It makes them constrained. Higher vertices still expose different connected/disconnected decompositions, different cumulant combinations, and different projection residuals. But the scalar/channel data they produce must satisfy the finite recurrence. This gives a test for proposed higher-order phenomena:

\[
\boxed{
\text{if it violates }P_x\text{-closure, it is not an apex effect of the given finite ledger.}
}
\]

For effective actions, this is sharp. T2 says cumulants are connected coefficients of the log apex. T12 says those connected coefficients are generated by the same finite spectral measure. Thus high-order effective-action terms can be necessary without being independent.

## 10. What T12 does not claim

1. **It does not claim that the ambient simplex has dimension \(r-1\).** The labels in \(\Omega\) still matter for Fourier channels, conductor packets, Helmert coordinates, and projections. T12 says the scalar ledger powers factor through \(r\) distinct values.

2. **It does not claim every channel has minimal rank \(r\).** A channel may vanish on some level aggregates, so its minimal recurrence may be a divisor of \(P_x\). The common statement is that \(P_x\) annihilates every channel tower.

3. **It does not make cumulants trivial.** Cumulants remain the connected coordinates of the theory. The theorem says they are finitely determined, not conceptually disposable.

4. **It does not solve numerical stability.** Exact reconstruction by Hankel and Vandermonde systems is algebraically valid. Numerical conditioning is a separate analytic question, especially when ledger levels nearly collide.

5. **It does not yet treat noncommutative observables.** The algebra here is the commutative algebra generated by one scalar ledger. Matrix-valued ledgers, density matrices, and noncommutative cumulants belong to a later paper.

6. **It does not replace T11.** T11 closes the character-conductor axis. T12 closes the order-spectrum axis. The full lamed-series needs both.

## 11. Forward doors

### 11.1 Exact simplicial renormalization

T11 identifies the boundary into which a projected packet sector leaks. T12 says all induced correction towers must close inside \(\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)\). The next paper can combine the two:
\[
\text{projection boundary}+\text{finite apex algebra}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{exact finite effective action}.
\]

### 11.2 Rank strata and discriminants

The rank \(r\) changes when ledger levels collide. The discriminant
\[
\Delta(P_x)=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^2
\]
marks the singular strata where the apex algebra loses dimension. These strata are natural candidates for phase-boundary geometry in the simplex.

### 11.3 Multivariate closure

T12 treats one scalar ledger generator. A multiledger theory would replace
\[
\mathbb R[t]/(P_x)
\]
by a finite coordinate algebra
\[
\mathbb R[t_1,\ldots,t_m]/I
\]
for the joint image of several observables. This is the natural route from one apex observable to finite algebraic statistics.

### 11.4 Multiplicative spectral closure

T8 suggests a Dirichlet-character analog. The finite spectral support theorem should have a multiplicative version in which the atoms are values of multiplicative ledgers over conductor classes, with Dirichlet channels acting as probes.

### 11.5 Noncommutative apex algebra

The physics-facing future is the noncommutative replacement of \(P_x\)-closure: density matrices, spectral projectors, matrix cumulants, and finite operator algebras. In that setting the slogan becomes:
\[
\text{the apex tower is a finite observable algebra}.
\]

## 12. Coda

T11 says that every apex coefficient is computed by finite conservation vertices. T12 says that the sequence of those coefficients cannot wander freely forever. It is governed by the closure polynomial of the ledger spectrum.

The key compression is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{moments, cumulants, channels, and apex functions}
\quad
=
\quad
\text{linear and logarithmic probes of }\mathbb R[t]/(P_x).
}
\]

The finite simplex does not hide an infinite continuum of higher-order information. It hides a finite spectral algebra in plain sight.
