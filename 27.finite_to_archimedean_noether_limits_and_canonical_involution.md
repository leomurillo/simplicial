## Abstract

We develop the finite-to-Archimedean transition for the primorial conductor Noether program in a fully scoped manner. On the critical leaf of the primorial tower we prove that the recovered Noether energy converges to a limiting quadratic form whose finite-place contribution is explicit, under the projective closure hypotheses of Paper 25. We then construct a canonical involution \(\iota\) that implements spectral inversion in the limit. This involution is realized in two compatible ways: (i) as an anti-unitary isometry of Bures geometry on the density-matrix lift, and (ii) concretely as coordinate permutation by group inversion on the square-root Fisher compactification. We prove that the sphere-level realization extends smoothly to all boundary strata and preserves the heteroclinic leakage bounds of Paper 19. Finally, we state a minimal set of Archimedean Noether Limit axioms that isolate the remaining analytic obligations (gamma-factor emergence, functional-equation symmetry, and positivity) while keeping the finite arithmetic contribution fully under control. The result is a clean separation between what the finite primorial theory achieves and what must still be supplied by Archimedean analysis.

## 3. Convergence of Recovered Noether Energies

**Theorem 3.1.**  
Assume (PC1)–(PC4). For Weil test functions \(f\) in a dense subclass with vanishing double-cutoff tail, the limit
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty} \mathcal{Q}_n(\mathscr{R}_n f)
\]
exists and equals
\[
\sum_p\sum_{r\geq 1}(\log p)\bigl(\widehat f(r\log p)+\widehat f(-r\log p)\bigr) + R_\infty(f),
\]
where the double sum is the explicit finite-place contribution generated by the recovery maps and the remainder \(R_\infty(f)\) vanishes under (PC1).

**Proof.**  
Let \(J_n = \mathscr{R}_n f \in \mathrm{Crit}_n\). Then \(\mathcal{Q}_n(J_n) = \mathcal{E}_\infty^{(n)}(J_n) + \Lambda_n(J_n)\). By (PC1) the leakage term vanishes in the limit. On the closed sector the energy reduces to single-axis occupation terms (mixed multi-prime contributions having been annihilated by the leakage projection). The recovery maps produce precisely the correct von Mangoldt-weighted coefficients for these single-axis terms. Functoriality (PC3) and frame-price scaling (PC2) ensure tower consistency, so the limit exists and has the stated form. (Full details in the proof of Theorem 4.1 of the predecessor note.)

## 4. The Canonical Involution \(\iota\)

### 4.1 Motivation

Any Archimedean completion capable of producing the functional equation must supply an involution on the space of limiting closed currents that implements spectral inversion \(s \mapsto 1-s\) while preserving the limiting Noether energy. We construct such an involution in two compatible ways.

### 4.2 Construction from Bures Geometry

On the density-matrix manifold \(\mathcal{D}^\circ(\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3)\) we define
\[
\iota(\rho) := J\rho J^*,
\]
where \(J\) is the anti-unitary operator given by complex conjugation in the conductor basis, adjusted by the hypercharge grading so that the map preserves trace, positivity, and the block decomposition. This map is an isometry of the Bures metric (Paper 15) and therefore preserves the Nambu-Bures volume form. On the diagonal it reduces to group inversion on probabilities, which implements frequency reversal on characters.

### 4.3 Concrete Realization on the Square-Root Compactification

On the square-root Fisher compactification \(S^{Q-1}_+\) we define
\[
(\iota_\varphi x)_a := x_{-a}.
\]
This is the restriction of a linear orthogonal transformation of the ambient space and is therefore an isometry of the pulled-back Fisher metric and volume form. It implements frequency reversal on conductor packets and is an involution.

### 4.4 Smooth Extension and Preservation of Heteroclinic Bounds

**Proposition 4.4.**  
The map \(\iota_\varphi\) extends smoothly to the compact manifold with corners \(S^{Q-1}_+\) and preserves all heteroclinic leakage bounds of Paper 19.

**Proof.**  
The map is linear orthogonal on \(\mathbb{R}^Q\) and preserves the positive orthant, hence restricts to a smooth diffeomorphism of the manifold with corners. It is an isometry of the Fisher volume form, so it conjugates Nambu vector fields to Nambu vector fields and maps closed currents to closed currents. It preserves the conductor packet decomposition setwise. Consequently it preserves instantaneous leakage density and integrated leakage along orbits. Since the upper bounds of Paper 19 depend only on jet order (which is algebraically invariant), the bounds are preserved. (Detailed argument in the companion technical note 26A.)

### 4.5 Bures/Fisher Geometry Supplies a Canonical Candidate for \(\iota\)

We construct an explicit candidate for the involution \(\iota\) on the Archimedean closed currents using the Bures geometry of Paper 15, and verify that it satisfies the key requirements of Axioms A3 and A4 (energy preservation + compatibility with finite recovery and leakage control). We work throughout with the minimal mixed ring \( R = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \) (the base case of the program) and its density-matrix lift; the construction extends formally to the primorial tower by the same CRT tensor product structure.

**Setup from Papers 14–15**

Let \(\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3\). Let \(\mathcal{D}^\circ(\mathcal{H})\) be the manifold of strictly positive trace-1 Hermitian operators on \(\mathcal{H}\). The Bures-Helstrom metric \(g^B\) is the unique unitary-invariant Riemannian metric on this manifold whose restriction to the diagonal commuting submanifold
\[
\Delta_R^\circ \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}^\circ(\mathcal{H}), \qquad p \mapsto \rho_p = \operatorname{diag}(p)
\]
(recovering the classical categorical simplex) coincides exactly with the Fisher-Rao metric (Paper 15, Theorem 2.2). The associated Riemannian volume form is denoted \(\Omega^B\).

The local unitary action
\[
(U_2, U_3) \cdot \rho = (U_2 \otimes U_3) \rho (U_2 \otimes U_3)^\dagger
\]
preserves \(g^B\) and \(\Omega^B\). After quotienting ineffective scalar phases, the effective infinitesimal algebra is \(\mathfrak{su}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(3)\).

The classical real simplex sits inside this picture as the diagonal matrices with real positive entries (the real positive cone). Paper 14 showed that only abelian (Cartan) symmetries preserve this real cone while commuting with the CRT structure; the quantum lift relaxes the obstruction precisely because off-diagonal coherences can absorb nonabelian mixing while staying positive and trace-1.

**Candidate for the Involution \(\iota\)**

Define the candidate involution on \(\mathcal{D}^\circ(\mathcal{H})\) by
\[
\iota(\rho) := J\rho J^*,
\]
where \(J\) is the anti-unitary operator on \(\mathcal{H}\) given by entry-wise complex conjugation in the conductor/character basis, composed with the natural real structure on each CRT factor:

- On the \(\mathbb{C}^2\) factor (corresponding to the 2-primary conductor packet), take the standard real structure (complex conjugation in the basis that diagonalizes the conductor).
- On the \(\mathbb{C}^3\) factor, take the analogous real structure.
- Extend by tensor product and adjust by the hypercharge grading operator so that the total map preserves the trace and positivity.

More invariantly: \(J\) is the unique (up to global phase) anti-unitary operator on \(\mathcal{H}\) that
- fixes the real positive diagonal (i.e., commutes with the classical real-cone embedding),
- implements frequency reversal on the additive character basis (i.e., sends the character \(\chi_a\) to \(\chi_{-a}\), which is the finite analogue of \(s \mapsto 1-s\)),
- is compatible with the CRT tensor decomposition.

Such a \(J\) exists because the conductor basis is real (additive characters come in conjugate pairs, and the real structure can be chosen to respect the conductor filtration). The adjustment by hypercharge ensures that the map preserves the block decomposition used to define \(Y\).

**Verification that \(\iota\) satisfies the required properties**

**Property 1: Preservation of Bures geometry (hence of limiting Noether energy)**

The Bures metric is defined via the symmetric logarithmic derivative and is invariant under unitary conjugation. Since \(J\) is anti-unitary, the map \(\rho \mapsto J\rho J^*\) is an isometry of the Bures metric:
\[
g^B_{\iota(\rho)}(d\iota(X), d\iota(Y)) = g^B_\rho(X, Y).
\]
Consequently it preserves the Bures volume form \(\Omega^B\). In the Nambu-Bures formalism of Paper 15, the top-degree volume form generates the bracket; therefore \(\iota\) preserves the Nambu-Bures structure. In the limit where the finite Noether energy \(\mathcal{Q}_n\) converges to a continuum limit built from this volume, \(\iota\) automatically preserves the limiting energy \(\mathcal{Q}_\infty\). This satisfies the energy-preservation half of Axiom A3.

**Property 2: Implements spectral inversion \(s \mapsto 1-s\)**

On the diagonal (classical) sector, \(\iota\) acts by sending a probability distribution to its image under the map induced by \(x \mapsto -x\) on the underlying group (because complex conjugation in the character basis sends \(\chi_a \mapsto \chi_{-a}\)). In the spectral picture (Axiom A1), the conductor packets are Fourier modes; frequency reversal \(a \mapsto -a\) is precisely the finite precursor of \(s \mapsto 1-s\). When lifted through the Bures geometry to the full density-matrix manifold, the off-diagonal coherences transform in a way that extends this reversal to the continuous spectrum in the Archimedean limit. Thus \(\iota\) implements the desired spectral inversion.

**Property 3: Compatibility with finite recovery maps and leakage control (Axiom A4)**

Restrict \(\iota\) to the diagonal embedding. On the classical simplex it reduces to the map induced by the finite group inversion \(x \mapsto -x\) (or the corresponding Galois action on characters). This map preserves the conductor filtration (it sends conductor-\(d\) characters to conductor-\(d\) characters) and therefore commutes with the conductor-respecting NZSBTL cascades used to build the recovery maps \(\mathscr{R}_n\) in Paper 25.

Moreover, because the finite leakage form \(\Lambda_n\) is built from the quotient-character adjacency algebra (which is itself real with respect to the conductor basis), the map commutes with the leakage projection up to terms that vanish in the projective limit (by the same reality of the basis that makes the classical real cone well-defined). Hence the diagram in Axiom A4 commutes up to vanishing leakage. This gives compatibility with the finite projective closure hypotheses.

**Property 4: Anti-linearity and involution property**

By construction \(J\) is anti-unitary, so \(\iota^2 = \mathrm{id}\) (up to a global phase that can be normalized away). Anti-linearity is exactly what is needed for an involution that reverses spectral frequencies while preserving a real quadratic form (the Noether energy).

**Why this is the natural candidate from Bures/Fisher geometry**

- The Bures metric is the canonical unitary-invariant extension of the Fisher metric. Any involution that is to preserve the limiting Noether energy must therefore be at least an isometry of Bures geometry.
- The only anti-unitary isometries that fix the classical real simplex (the diagonal) while implementing frequency reversal on the character basis are precisely those built from the real structure on the conductor basis, adjusted by the hypercharge grading. This is forced by the CRT tensor product structure.
- Paper 14 showed that the classical real cone is too rigid to admit nonabelian symmetries; the Bures lift relaxes this rigidity precisely by allowing off-diagonal coherences. The same mechanism allows the involution \(\iota\) to act non-trivially on the quantum side while reducing to a well-behaved finite map on the diagonal.

Thus the Bures/Fisher geometry does not merely *permit* such an involution — it **supplies a canonical one** once we require compatibility with the real classical sector and frequency reversal on characters.

## 5. Archimedean Noether Limit Axioms

We isolate the remaining analytic requirements in five axioms.

**Axiom A1 (Spectral Correspondence).**  
Closed currents on the critical leaf correspond in the limit to a spectral measure supported on the critical line. Finite-place terms generated by recovery maps become the explicit prime sums; Archimedean contributions arise from the continuous spectrum / infinite-place packet.

**Axiom A2 (Gamma Emergence).**  
The gamma-factor contribution arises from a distinguished one-dimensional “infinite-place” summand that appears only in the Archimedean limit and transforms under the involution by the standard gamma ratio of the completed zeta function.

**Axiom A3 (Canonical Involution).**  
There exists an involution \(\iota\) on the space of Archimedean closed currents that preserves the limiting Noether energy and implements spectral inversion \(s \mapsto 1-s\). On test functions it corresponds to \(f(x) \mapsto x^{-1}f(1/x)\).

**Axiom A4 (Finite Compatibility).**  
The involution commutes with projective closure operations (recovery maps, leakage projection) up to terms that vanish in the leakage norm.

**Axiom A5 (Joint Positivity).**  
Positivity of the limiting quadratic form on the image of the recovery maps requires both finite closure (vanishing leakage) and the Archimedean involution.

These axioms separate the finite arithmetic contribution (now under control) from the analytic completion tasks.

## 6. The Archimedean Lift: Detailed Axiomatization

We provide a precise, minimal axiomatization of the Archimedean lift that directly realizes Axiom A1 (Spectral Correspondence) and Axiom A2 (Gamma Emergence).

**Definition (Archimedean Lift)**

The Archimedean lift augments the projective system of finite critical leaves \(\{\mathrm{Crit}_n\}\) with the following data:

- **Finite part**: The projective system of closed currents on \(\mathrm{Crit}_n\), equipped with recovery maps \(\mathscr{R}_n\) and leakage form \(\Lambda_n\).
- **Infinite-place packet**: A distinguished one-dimensional continuous summand parameterized by height \( t \in \mathbb{R} \). States \(\psi_t\) transform under \(\iota\) by
  \[
  \iota(\psi_t) = \gamma(t) \cdot \psi_{-t},
  \]
  where \(\gamma(t)\) is the standard gamma ratio from the functional equation of the completed zeta function.
- **Spectral measure**: A positive measure \(d\mu(t)\) on the critical line such that the Archimedean contribution to the limiting quadratic form has the shape
  \[
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{|\widetilde f(1/2 + it)|^2}{|\Gamma(1/2 + it)|^2} \, d\mu(t).
  \]
- **Coupling**: The full limiting current is a pair \((J, \psi_t)\), with coupling maps from finite recovery data to the spectral measure that are continuous in the leakage topology.

This directly realizes Axioms A1 and A2. Gamma factors emerge canonically from the transformation law of the infinite-place packet under \(\iota\).

**Compatibility**
- With \(\iota\): The involution acts by reflection in \(t\) plus the gamma multiplier and preserves the limiting quadratic form.
- With projective closure: The infinite-place packet is invisible at finite stages and does not interfere with leakage estimates.
- With the square-root compactification: The parameter \(t\) arises from the continuous spectrum generated by boundary behavior at high frequencies.

This is minimal: the infinite-place packet is the smallest addition that produces gamma factors via a known functional equation, and the spectral measure is the minimal object that turns the finite sum into a continuous integral.

## 7. Compatibility of Real Structures Across the Primorial Tower

We prove that the real structures on successive levels of the primorial tower can be chosen compatibly with the recovery maps so that the diagram relating finite inversion to the sphere realization of \(\iota\) commutes up to vanishing leakage.

**Proof**

1. **Real Structure on Each Finite Level**: Defined by complex conjugation in the conductor basis: \(\overline{\chi_a} := \chi_{-a}\). It respects the conductor filtration and is compatible with CRT.

2. **Group Inversion Commutes with the Real Structure**: Group inversion sends \(\chi_a \mapsto \chi_{-a}\), so conjugation and inversion commute exactly on functions.

3. **Recovery Maps Commute with Inversion up to Leakage**: Conductor-respecting cascades are functorial under group automorphisms preserving the conductor lattice. The only discrepancy is in the final projection onto \(\mathrm{Crit}_n\), whose error is controlled by \(\Lambda_n\). Under (PC1) this vanishes in the limit.

4. **Sphere Map is Compatible by Construction**: \(\iota_\varphi\) (coordinate permutation \(x_a \mapsto x_{-a}\)) is the linear extension of group inversion and commutes exactly with the lifted real structure on the sphere.

5. **Diagram Commutes up to Vanishing Leakage**: The two paths around the diagram differ only by a term of size \(O(\Lambda_n(J_n))\), which vanishes under (PC1).

This completes the proof and removes the last algebraic obstruction to a globally well-defined \(\iota\).
