# Regular simplices and prime-indexed spectral spikes in the geometry of consecutive integers

Research draft, 17 October 2026. The proofs below are a new derivation in this conversation, using classical arithmetic moment estimates. They have not received independent mathematical review. Priority is not established. This is not a Collatz or RH proof.

## Proposed abstract

Represent an integer by its prime-exponent vector and use the regular-simplex metric on the valuation quotient. We study the centered Gram matrix of a uniformly located window of consecutive integers. Windows of length o(log log X) become regular simplices in normalized operator norm. At the scale m~c log log X, the largest eigenvalues instead approach 1+c p/(p-1)^2, indexed by the primes in increasing order, while the smallest eigenvalue approaches 1. The spectral deviations are generated by shared small-prime valuations. The proof separates those periodic columns from a diagonal background and controls the remaining correlations by divisibility of the index differences. This establishes a transition in spectral shape, rather than merely an average-distance identity. As a consequence, the actual mean turn of the integer walk tends to 120 degrees.

## 1. Definitions and statements

Let X tend to infinity, choose n uniformly from {1,...,X}, and put L=log log X. Let m=m(X)>=2, with m=O(L). Work in any frame N>=pi(X+m). Write

\[
v_i=e(n+i),\quad 0\le i<m,
\qquad Q(a)=\sum_p v_p(a)^2,\quad\Omega(a)=\sum_pv_p(a).
\]

Let E be the ordinary Euclidean Gram matrix of the exponent vectors, and G the prime-crystal Gram matrix:

\[
E_{ij}=\sum_pv_p(n+i)v_p(n+j),\qquad
G=\frac{N}{N-1}E-\frac{\boldsymbol\Omega\boldsymbol\Omega^T}{N-1}.
\]

Set H=I_m-m^{-1}11^T. The centered, normalized shape matrix is

\[
K_X=\frac1L H G H,
\]

viewed on 1-perp. The identity on that space is the shape of a regular simplex with squared edge length 2 after normalization. All limits below are in probability with respect to the choice of n; they do not assert a uniform statement about every starting integer.

**Theorem A (subcritical windows).** If m=o(L), then

\[
\|K_X-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0.
\]

**Theorem B (critical spectral law).** If m/L->c in (0,infinity), list the m-1 eigenvalues on 1-perp in decreasing order. For every fixed j>=1,

\[
\boxed{\lambda_j(K_X)\longrightarrow
1+c\frac{p_j}{(p_j-1)^2}.}
\]

The smallest eigenvalue tends to 1. In particular,

\[
\|K_X-H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow2c.
\]

The first shifts above the regular-simplex baseline are

\[
2c,\quad\frac34c,\quad\frac5{16}c,\quad\frac7{36}c,\ldots.
\]

Thus m of order log log X is the onset scale for a nonvanishing spectral distortion. This is not a claim that individual pairwise distances cease to be approximately equal at that scale: operator norm detects a collective deformation that entrywise comparisons can miss.

## 2. Arithmetic moment input

Uniformly for 0<=i<=m with m=O(L),

\[
\mathbb E|Q(n+i)-L|^2=O(L),\qquad
\mathbb E|Q(n+i)-L|^4=O(L^2),\qquad
\mathbb E\Omega(n+i)^2=O(L^2). \tag{1}
\]

These are standard additive-function moment estimates; they can also be obtained directly as follows. For a uniform integer up to Y, truncate omega, the number of distinct prime factors, to primes at most Y^(1/8). The omitted number of prime factors is bounded by 8. Expand the first four centered moments of the truncated sum. The Chinese remainder theorem replaces the divisibility indicators by independent Bernoulli variables of means 1/p, with total error O(Y^(-1/2)): there are O(Y^(1/2)) ordered four-tuples and each residue-count error is O(1/Y), with a fixed number of terms per tuple. Repeated primes are grouped before counting. Mertens' estimate gives mean log log Y+O(1), and the independent fourth centered moment is O((log log Y)^2).

To pass from omega to Q, write

\[
Q(a)-\omega(a)=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}(2k-1)1_{p^k\mid a}.
\]

This nonnegative remainder has a uniformly bounded fourth moment. On expanding its fourth power, the average of a product of divisibility indicators is at most the reciprocal of the least common multiple of the relevant prime powers. This is exactly the corresponding expectation in a model of independent geometric valuations. The contribution at each prime to each of its first four moments is O(p^(-2)), and those sums converge. The same argument applies to Omega-omega. For shifted intervals, bound their nonnegative moment sums by the full interval up to X+m and use log log(X+m)=L+o(1).

For background on these classical methods see [Kowalski, *An introduction to probabilistic number theory*](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/probabilistic-number-theory.pdf). The geometric conclusions below, not these moment inputs, are the proposed contribution.

## 3. A summable bound for off-diagonal arithmetic correlations

Put C_h(n)=sum_p v_p(n)v_p(n+h). Only primes dividing h contribute. Uniformly for shifts with n+i<=2X,

\[
\mathbb E v_p(n+i)^4\le\frac{C}{p}.
\]

Indeed, count multiples of p^a in the full interval up to 2X and sum the fourth-moment tail. Since ab<=(a^2+b^2)/2, Minkowski's inequality gives

\[
\|C_h\|_2\le C\sum_{p\mid h}p^{-1/2}.
\]

The crucial point is to average over the gap h, rather than maximize over it:

\[
\sum_{h<m}\left(\sum_{p\mid h}p^{-1/2}\right)^2
\le m\left(\sum_p p^{-2}+\sum_{p\ne q}(pq)^{-3/2}\right)
\ll m. \tag{2}
\]

For the diagonal terms use floor(m/p)<=m/p; for distinct primes use floor(m/(pq))<=m/(pq). Consequently,

\[
\sum_{i\ne j}\mathbb E E_{ij}^2\ll m^2. \tag{3}
\]

More precisely, if E^(>y) retains only primes greater than y, its off-diagonal part O^(>y) satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\|O^{(>y)}\|_F^2
\le C m^2\eta(y),\qquad
\eta(y)=\sum_{p>y}p^{-2}+
\left(\sum_{p>y}p^{-3/2}\right)^2\longrightarrow0. \tag{4}
\]

No unproved assertion about primes or about independent consecutive integers enters this bound.

## 4. Proof of Theorem A

By (1) and (3),

\[
\mathbb E\|E/L-I_m\|_F^2
\ll\frac mL+\frac{m^2}{L^2}.
\]

This tends to zero when m=o(L). Centering cannot increase operator norm. The prime-crystal metric correction is negligible: its mass term has expected normalized operator norm

\[
\mathbb E\frac{\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|_2^2}{(N-1)L}
=O(mL/N)=o(1),
\]

and the factor N/(N-1) tends to one. Here N>=pi(X+m), with the classical lower bound pi(X) asymptotic to X/log X more than sufficient. This proves Theorem A. ∎

## 5. Finite-prime covariance within a long window

Fix y and let V_y be the m-by-pi(y) matrix with entries v_p(n+i), p<=y. If m tends to infinity, then

\[
\frac1m V_y^T H V_y
\longrightarrow \operatorname{diag}_{p\le y}\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^2}\right). \tag{5}
\]

To prove this, first truncate each valuation at a fixed depth a. Its values, squares, and products at distinct primes are periodic modulo a fixed product of prime powers. Averages over any consecutive window of length m therefore approach their period averages as m tends to infinity, uniformly in its starting point. CRT makes the truncated valuations at different primes independent in the period average. Remove the truncation afterward: the expected squared truncation tail is bounded uniformly in the window location distribution by a convergent geometric tail. Cauchy–Schwarz handles cross-products.

The geometric valuation law has

\[
\mathbb E v_p=\frac1{p-1},\qquad
\mathbb E v_p^2=\frac{p+1}{(p-1)^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(v_p)=\frac{p}{(p-1)^2}.
\]

These variances, not prime gaps or zeta zeros, produce the spectral constants in Theorem B.

## 6. Proof of Theorem B

Fix y. Split

\[
E=V_yV_y^T+\operatorname{diag}(Q_{>y}(n+i))+O^{(>y)}.
\]

For each fixed y, the fourth-moment argument in (1) also gives

\[
\mathbb E|Q_{>y}(n+i)-L|^4=O_y(L^2).
\]

A union bound over m=O(L) indices shows

\[
\max_{i<m}|Q_{>y}(n+i)/L-1|\longrightarrow0.
\]

The centered metric correction is negligible as before. Thus

\[
K_X=H+L^{-1}HV_yV_y^TH+R_{X,y},
\]

where for each epsilon>0,

\[
\limsup_{X\to\infty}
\mathbb P(\|R_{X,y}\|_{\mathrm{op}}>\epsilon)
\le C_c\eta(y)/\epsilon^2. \tag{6}
\]

By (5), the nonzero eigenvalues of L^(-1)HV_yV_y^TH tend to c p/(p-1)^2 for p<=y. The other eigenvalues on 1-perp are zero. The function p/(p-1)^2 decreases strictly with p. Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and (6), first with X tending to infinity and then y tending to infinity, prove the claimed limit for each fixed ordered eigenvalue. The same comparison proves that the minimum eigenvalue tends to 1: each finite-rank comparison has minimum exactly 1 once m-1>pi(y). The top eigenvalue therefore tends to 1+2c and the largest absolute deviation from 1 tends to 2c. ∎

## 7. Exact structure and the walk's mean turn

For any finite window of length m, primes greater than m cannot divide two different window entries. Hence the exact identity

\[
E=V_{\le m}V_{\le m}^T+
\operatorname{diag}(Q_{>m}(n),...,Q_{>m}(n+m-1))
\]

separates shared small-prime structure from mutually disjoint large-prime coordinates. This is the geometric reason the analysis works.

For a fixed triple n,n+1,n+2, Theorem A gives all three squared distances asymptotic to 2L in probability. The angle between the two directed consecutive steps tends in probability to 2pi/3. Since angles lie in [0,pi], their actual arithmetic mean tends to 2pi/3 as well. This supplies the missing normalization argument in Corollary 6.4 of the main manuscript: a ratio of averaged inner products alone did not establish the mean-angle conclusion. No first-order correction coefficient or sharp mean-angle rate is claimed here.

Two further statistics are promising, but should be kept separate from the proved spectral theorem until their precise joint Erdős–Kac inputs are cited and checked:

* A three-shift Gaussian input gives sqrt(8L)(theta-2pi/3) converging to a standard normal. Linearization gives the fluctuation -(Z_0-2Z_1+Z_2)/(4 sqrt(3L)), of variance 1/(8L).
* A two-shift Gaussian input gives a limiting prime-birth stretching fraction P(|Z|>1)=approximately 0.3173105, because stretching is exactly (Omega(n+1)-Omega(n))^2>Q(n)+Q(n+1).

These are candidate corollaries, not prerequisites for Theorems A and B. The spectral proofs above need moment estimates, not a multivariate central limit theorem.

## 8. Finite diagnostics and publication limits

`empirical_01g/geometric_window_probe.py` checked the exact small-prime-plus-diagonal decomposition in 1,200 windows and checked a parity Rayleigh lower bound. Spectral calculations use floating point; integer decompositions are checked separately. The adjacent JSON records the results.

The diagnostics on n in [X,2X) show mean turns of about 131.04, 130.37, 129.89, and 129.52 degrees for X=10^3,10^4,10^5,10^6. The corresponding stretching fractions are 0.1700, 0.1930, 0.20467, and 0.213885. These ranges are far from the proposed asymptotic stretching fraction and the limiting turn angle. They illustrate slow convergence and do not certify the asymptotic spectral law.

The claimed advance to review is the growing-window spectral theorem, particularly the summable tail estimate (4), the fixed-prime covariance limit (5), and their combination at m~c log log X. The classical valuation representation and Erdős–Kac philosophy must not be advertised as newly discovered. The theorem has a complete proof argument here, but needs an independent referee-level audit and a broader literature search before any priority or publicity claim.
