## Theorem-by-Theorem Capture

### 1. Galois-Simplex Realization

**Assessment:** Correct but classical.

The normalizer of an `N`-cycle in `S_N` is the affine group

```text
C_N semidirect (Z/NZ)^x
```

and the quotient by the cyclic subgroup is the automorphism group of the cyclic group, hence the cyclotomic Galois group.  Permutation matrices preserve the Euclidean metric and the zero-sum hyperplane.  Fourier/circulant diagonalization already explains why index multiplication produces Galois conjugation on spectral blocks.

**Draft action:**

- Rename or qualify the result as a "realization" or "coordinate form" rather than a novel theorem.
- Add explicit prior-art language: holomorph of a cyclic group, affine normalizer, circulant diagonalization, real DFT.
- Keep the proof because it is useful and short.

### 2. Janus Scope and Crystallographic Restriction

**Assessment:** Correct, philosophically useful, but classical.

Complex conjugation is the multiplier `a=-1`.  On a real cyclotomic plane it is an orientation-reversing reflection.  The "single orientation bit" picture occurs exactly when the relevant cyclotomic block is two-dimensional, i.e. when `phi(d)=2`.  This mirrors the classical crystallographic restriction: lattice rotations force integer traces, hence low-degree cyclotomic possibilities.

**Draft action:**

- Add one explicit bridge paragraph to the crystallographic restriction theorem.
- Avoid presenting the totient condition as new.
- Preserve the Janus interpretation as a coordinate-language synthesis.

### 3. Cyclotomic Units and Arc-Stars

**Assessment:** Algebra standard, geometry valuable.

The identity

```text
(1 - zeta_p^a)/(1 - zeta_p) = 1 + zeta_p + ... + zeta_p^(a-1)
```

is elementary, and Sinnott's theorem supplies the finite index of circular units in the full real unit group.  The new value is the spatial representation: units become consecutive arcs, and real units become palindromic stars in the regular simplex frame.

**Draft action:**

- Keep the Kummer-Sinnott caveat in the proposition.
- Strengthen the language around the open question: the class-number obstruction might be read as a volume/index deficiency of the arc-star lattice after the logarithmic embedding.
- Do not claim a new formula for the regulator.

### 4. Parity-Dual Simplices

**Assessment:** Strongest discrete-geometric contribution.

For even `N`, the alternating vector spans the `Phi_2` block.  The Householder reflection

```text
rho_2 = I - (2/N)ss^T
```

maps the regular simplex to another regular simplex.  The cross-Gram matrix is explicit:

```text
<v_i,w_j> = delta_ij - 1/N - (2/N)s_i s_j.
```

The `N=4` case yields the complementary tetrahedron of the stella octangula.  For `N>=6`, the dual is not the antipodal simplex.

**Important correction to keep in the drafts:** The union `V_N union W_N` is a compound of two regular simplex ETFs.  It should not be described as a single ETF unless the full union is being projectivized or otherwise reinterpreted and the angle condition is verified.  The even-`N` census shows that the full union generally has multiple absolute inner products.

**Draft action:**

- Add a "compound, not single ETF" warning.
- Add the empirical census reference.
- Move from `N=6`-only evidence to an even-`N` stabilizer pattern.

### 5. Fisher-Legendre and Aitchison Symmetries

**Assessment:** Correct, but classical.

The global Fisher-Rao isometry group of the categorical simplex is discrete at the outcome-label level; the Fisher metric is Markov invariant in the Chentsov sense.  Aitchison geometry is flat in centered log-ratio coordinates, so orthogonal spectral reflections are global Aitchison isometries but not global Fisher-Rao symmetries.

**Draft action:**

- Paper 2 already states "no new theorem in information geometry proper."
- Add one short positioning paragraph saying the paper's novelty is expository/coordinate-theoretic, while the parity-dual and Sinnott-deficiency work lives in the companion discrete-geometric/arithmetic papers.
