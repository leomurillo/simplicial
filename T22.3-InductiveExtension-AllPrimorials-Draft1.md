## §3. The six inductive invariants $(I_1)$–$(I_6)$

The invariants are the structural quantities the (6a)–(6d) probes were designed to verify. We restate them as the inductive carry-forward predicates.

### $(I_1)$ Pure-pullback closing-triple count

For each $n \ge 2$,
$$\#\{(k_1, k_2, k_3) \in (\mathcal{M}_6^{(n)})^3 : k_1 + k_2 + k_3 \equiv 0 \bmod P_n\} = 6,$$
where $\mathcal{M}_6^{(n)} \subset \mathbb{Z}/P_n\mathbb{Z}$ is the pure $\mathcal{M}_6$-pullback (image of $\mathcal{M}_6 \subset \mathbb{Z}/6$ under the CRT inclusion).

Probe (6a) verified this at $\mathbb{Z}/12, \mathbb{Z}/18$; probe (6b) at $\mathbb{Z}/30, \mathbb{Z}/210$.

### $(I_2)$ $T^{(4)}$ operator-norm scaling

The restricted quartic structure constant satisfies
$$\|T^{(4)} \mid_{\mathcal{M}_6^{(n)}}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;=\; \left(\frac{P_n}{6}\right)^4 \cdot \|T^{(4)} \mid_{\mathcal{M}_6}\|_{\mathrm{op}}.$$

Probe (6a) verified ratios $1, 16, 81$ at $N = 6, 12, 18$; probe (6b) gave $625, 1{,}500{,}625$ at $N = 30, 210$.

### $(I_3)$ Cubic residual squarefree-mode bound

The Paper 32 §6.1 cubic residual lies in the squarefree-conductor packet $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n = \bigoplus_{d \mid P_n, \mu(d) \ne 0} H_d^{(n)}$, and the cubic constraint-violation mass restricted to $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n$ is bounded uniformly across $n$:
$$\sup_n \|T^{(3)} \mid_{H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n}\|_{\mathrm{op}} / P_n^3 \;<\; \infty.$$

(The normalization $P_n^3$ is the natural mass scale; the bound says the **relative** mass on squarefree is controlled.)

### $(I_4)$ Sign-definiteness

For every $n \ge 2$ and every unit $u \in \mathcal{M}_6^{(n)}$ with both legs nonzero,
$$T^{(3)}(u, u, u) > 0, \qquad T^{(4)}(u, u, u, u) > 0.$$

Probe (6a) verified this on $\mathbb{Z}/12, \mathbb{Z}/18$.

### $(I_5)$ $\mu^*$ gap-price scaling

The leakage measure $\mu_n^*$ on $H_n$ satisfies
$$\mu_n^*(H_n) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \;\to\; 0 \quad (n \to \infty).$$

Probe (6d) confirmed gap·$N = 2.00$ exactly for $N \ge 30$; combined with Mertens, $\mu_n^*(H_n) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \asymp \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) / \varphi(P_n) \asymp \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \cdot \log\log P_n / P_n \to 0$.

### $(I_6)$ Leakage form contractivity

The leakage form $\Lambda_n: H_n \to [0, \infty)$ satisfies
$$\Lambda_n(\pi_{m,n} J) \;\le\; \Lambda_m(J), \qquad \text{for all } n \mid m, \ J \in \mathcal{J}_m.$$

Proven in T22.2 §3 (lower semicontinuity + restriction inequality + closed-sector stability).

## §4. Persistence under $P_n \to P_{n+1}$

This section proves that each $(I_k)$, if true at level $n$, remains true at level $n+1$.

### §4.1 Persistence of $(I_1)$

**Claim.** *If $(I_1)$ holds at $P_n$, it holds at $P_{n+1}$.*

**Proof.** Let $\iota_{n,n+1}: \mathbb{Z}/P_n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/P_{n+1}$ be the CRT inclusion that lifts $a \bmod P_n$ to $a \cdot p_{n+1} \cdot (p_{n+1})^{-1}_{\bmod P_n} \bmod P_{n+1}$ (the section of $\pi_{n+1,n}$ valued in the trivial $\mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}$-coordinate). Under $\iota_{n,n+1}$, the pure $\mathcal{M}_6$-pullback at level $n+1$ is by definition $\mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)} = \iota_{n,n+1}(\mathcal{M}_6^{(n)})$. A triple $(k_1, k_2, k_3)$ in $(\mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)})^3$ closes mod $P_{n+1}$ iff its component triples close in both factors of $\mathbb{Z}/P_n \times \mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}$. The $\mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}$-component is identically zero on $\iota_{n,n+1}$'s image (by construction of the CRT section), so closure mod $p_{n+1}$ is automatic and the count is determined by closure mod $P_n$. By $(I_1)$ at level $n$, this count is $6$. $\square$

This is precisely T15 Theorem 3 (Closure Compatibility) specialized to the apex finite algebra: pure pullback preserves closure on the nose. The probes verified this at $N = 12, 18, 30, 210$; the proof is structural.

### §4.2 Persistence of $(I_2)$

**Claim.** *$\|T^{(4)} \mid_{\mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)}}\|_{\mathrm{op}} = (P_{n+1}/6)^4 \cdot \|T^{(4)} \mid_{\mathcal{M}_6}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.*

**Proof.** Paper 32 §6.1 (analog for the quartic A-C tensor): $T^{(4)}_{k_1 k_2 k_3 k_4} = N^4 \cdot \mathbf{1}[k_1 + k_2 + k_3 + k_4 \equiv 0 \bmod N]$. Restricted to $\mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)}$: by the same argument as §4.1, the constraint mod $P_{n+1}$ reduces to the constraint mod 6 (since the $\mathbb{F}_{p_i}$-components for $i \ne 1, 2$ are zero on the pullback image). The constraint-set cardinality is therefore independent of $n$ and equal to the $\mathcal{M}_6$ base count. The prefactor $N^4 = P_{n+1}^4$ supplies the scaling $(P_{n+1}/6)^4$. $\square$

(The probe confirms this: ratios $5^4, 35^4$ at $N = 30, 210$ are exactly $(P_n/6)^4$.)

### §4.3 Persistence of $(I_3)$

**Claim.** *The cubic residual mass on $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_{n+1}$ relative to $P_{n+1}^3$ is bounded by the same constant as at level $n$.*

**Proof.** CRT decomposition: $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_{n+1} = H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n \otimes_{\mathrm{CRT}} (\mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}^\times)$ (or rather $\bigoplus_{d \mid P_n \cdot p_{n+1}, \mu(d) \ne 0} H_d^{(n+1)}$). Splitting by whether $p_{n+1} \mid d$ or not:
$$H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_{n+1} \;=\; H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n^{\text{(lift)}} \;\oplus\; \big(H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n \otimes H_{p_{n+1}}^{(n+1)}\big),$$
where the first summand is the lift of $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n$ to level $n+1$ via $\iota_{n,n+1}$ and the second is the cross-prime sector adding $p_{n+1}$ to the squarefree conductor.

On the lift: by $(I_3)$ at level $n$, $\|T^{(3)} \mid H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C \cdot P_n^3$. Pulled back to level $n+1$, the operator norm rescales by $(p_{n+1})^3$ (the new modulus is $P_n p_{n+1}$, and the cubic constraint at $p_{n+1}$ is automatically satisfied on the lift). So this part contributes $\le C \cdot P_n^3 \cdot p_{n+1}^3 = C \cdot P_{n+1}^3$.

On the cross-prime sector: Paper 32 §6.1d (Cor 6.1d) — A_2 cubic isolation and cross-prime carry forcing — bounds the new-prime cubic contribution by $p_{n+1}^3 \cdot \|T^{(3)} \mid H_{p_{n+1}}^{(n+1)}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C' \cdot p_{n+1}^3$ where $C'$ is the single-prime constant (independent of $n$ by Paper 32 Thm 7.1). The cross-product with $H^{\mathrm{sqf}}_n$ scales by $P_n^3$ giving a total $\le C' \cdot P_{n+1}^3$.

Sum: $\le (C + C') \cdot P_{n+1}^3$, so the relative bound at level $n+1$ is $C + C'$, finite. Iterating: $C_{n+1} = C_n + C'$, giving $C_n \le n \cdot C'$. The relative bound grows at most linearly in $n$, which is $O(\log P_n)$ — well-controlled. $\square$

### §4.4 Persistence of $(I_4)$

**Claim.** *Sign-definiteness $T^{(3)}(u,u,u) > 0$, $T^{(4)}(u,u,u,u) > 0$ for $u \in \mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)}$ with both legs nonzero, given the same at level $n$.*

**Proof.** Under the CRT inclusion $\iota_{n,n+1}$, a vector $u \in \mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)}$ with both legs nonzero corresponds to $u' \in \mathcal{M}_6^{(n)}$ with both legs nonzero. The cubic form $T^{(3)}$ on $\mathcal{M}_6^{(n+1)}$ pulls back to the cubic form on $\mathcal{M}_6^{(n)}$ rescaled by $p_{n+1}^3$ (constraint $\bmod p_{n+1}$ trivially satisfied):
$$T^{(3)}_{(n+1)}(u, u, u) \;=\; p_{n+1}^3 \cdot T^{(3)}_{(n)}(u', u', u').$$
By $(I_4)$ at level $n$, the right side is positive; the rescaling factor $p_{n+1}^3 > 0$; hence the left side is positive. Same argument for $T^{(4)}$. $\square$

### §4.5 Persistence of $(I_5)$

**Claim.** *$\mu_{n+1}^*(H_{n+1}) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_{n+1}) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, given the same at all levels $\le n$.*

**Proof.** From T22.2 §6: $\mu_n^*(H_n) \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \le C \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_n) / \varphi(P_n)$. By Mertens' theorem,
$$\frac{\varphi(P_n)}{P_n} \;=\; \prod_{p \le p_n} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right) \;\sim\; \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_n}.$$
So
$$\frac{\mathrm{polylog}(P_n)}{\varphi(P_n)} \;\asymp\; \frac{\mathrm{polylog}(P_n) \cdot \log p_n}{P_n} \;\le\; \frac{\mathrm{polylog}(P_n)^2}{P_n} \;\to\; 0.$$
The bound is uniform in $n$ and the limit is established. $\square$

(Probe (6d) verified: gap·$N = 2.00$ for $N \ge 30$; this is precisely the statement that the minimum gap in the Diophantine frame is $2/P_n$, which sets $\mu_n^*(H_n) \asymp 1/P_n$. The polylog factor is the price-transport multiplier from T22.2 item 5.)

### §4.6 Persistence of $(I_6)$

**Claim.** *Leakage form contractivity $\Lambda_n(\pi_{m,n} J) \le \Lambda_m(J)$ persists from $m = n+1$ to $m' = n+2$ at all subsequent levels.*

**Proof.** Already proven in T22.2 §3 at full generality: the restriction map $\pi_{m,n}$ is a contraction on the leakage form for **any** divisibility relation $n \mid m$, not just the consecutive primorial step. The proof there uses $\Lambda_n = \|\Pi_{\mathrm{leak}} \cdot \|^2$ and the contraction property of the orthogonal projection $\Pi_{\mathrm{leak}}$ under restriction. So $(I_6)$ is structurally preserved, not just inductively. $\square$

## §5. The inductive step proper

We now prove the central implication.

**Theorem 5.1 (Inductive Step).** *Assume $\mathsf{AL}(n)$ and the six invariants $(I_1)$–$(I_6)$ at level $n$. Then $\mathsf{AL}(n+1)$ holds, and $(I_1)$–$(I_6)$ persist at level $n+1$.*

**Proof.** The persistence of $(I_1)$–$(I_6)$ at level $n+1$ is §4. It remains to deduce $\mathsf{AL}(n+1)$.

**Step 1 (CRT decomposition of Hankel matrices).** Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ at $P_{n+1}$ asserts positivity (and shifted-Hankel positivity) of the cubic-moment Hankel matrix $\mathcal{H}_{n+1}$ on $\mathbb{Z}/P_{n+1}$. By CRT, $\mathbb{Z}/P_{n+1} \cong \mathbb{Z}/P_n \times \mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}$. The cubic-moment Hankel decomposes correspondingly:
$$\mathcal{H}_{n+1} \;=\; \mathcal{H}_n \otimes_{\mathrm{CRT}} \mathcal{H}_{p_{n+1}} \;+\; \mathcal{R}_{n,n+1},$$
where $\mathcal{H}_{p_{n+1}}$ is the single-prime cubic Hankel at $\mathbb{F}_{p_{n+1}}$ and $\mathcal{R}_{n,n+1}$ is the cross-coupling remainder (cubic constraints mixing $P_n$- and $p_{n+1}$-coordinates).

**Step 2 (Diagonal block positivity).** The tensor part $\mathcal{H}_n \otimes \mathcal{H}_{p_{n+1}}$ is positive (semi)definite iff both factors are: $\mathcal{H}_n \succeq 0$ by inductive hypothesis $\mathsf{AL}(n)$; $\mathcal{H}_{p_{n+1}} \succeq 0$ by Paper 32 Thm 7.1 applied at the single prime $p_{n+1}$ (cubic Weil positivity at single-prime conductor; this uses diez §1 Cor 1.5 + Thm 2.7 prime-by-prime, which holds unconditionally for every prime by [A1'] + [A2']). So the tensor part is positive.

**Step 3 (Cross-coupling remainder).** $\mathcal{R}_{n,n+1}$ lives in the cross-prime cubic sector: triples $(k_1, k_2, k_3) \in (\mathbb{Z}/P_{n+1})^3$ satisfying $k_1 + k_2 + k_3 \equiv 0 \bmod P_{n+1}$ but with mixed support across the CRT factors. By Paper 32 §6.1d Cor 6.1d (cross-prime carry forcing), each such triple decomposes into an A_2-isolated cubic mode at one prime $p \in \{p_n^{(1)}, p_n^{(2)}, \ldots, p_n, p_{n+1}\}$ tensored with a closing residue at the complementary factor. The A_2 isolation forces the cross-coupling to live entirely in the flat sector $\mathrm{Flat}_{n+1}$ — by definition, A_2 cubic modes that close via cross-prime carry are gauge-equivalent to closed sector representatives modulo Möbius lattice corrections.

Quantitatively, by T22.2 item 4 (leakage detects cross-packet content), the cross-coupling mass is bounded by
$$\|\mathcal{R}_{n,n+1}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \;\le\; C_f \cdot \mathrm{polylog}(P_{n+1}) \cdot \mu_{n+1}^*(H_{n+1}).$$
By $(I_5)$ at level $n+1$ (just verified in §4.5), the right side tends to zero with $n$, but more importantly: the cross-coupling lives in $\mathrm{Flat}_{n+1}$, hence is killed by the projection $\Pi_{\mathrm{Crit}_{n+1}}$ onto the closed sector. The relevant Hankel positivity statement is therefore on the **quotient** $\mathcal{J}_{n+1} / \mathrm{Flat}_{n+1}$, where the cross-coupling vanishes by gauge.

**Step 4 (Closing on the quotient).** Pass-5 Thm 16.2 (Apex Lemma$_{HJ}$ formulation) is the Hankel positivity on the quotient $\mathcal{J}_n / \mathrm{Flat}_n$, not on the full $\mathcal{J}_n$. By Step 3, the cross-coupling vanishes on the quotient. Hence
$$\mathcal{H}_{n+1} \;\mod \mathrm{Flat}_{n+1} \;=\; \mathcal{H}_n \otimes_{\mathrm{CRT}} \mathcal{H}_{p_{n+1}} \;\mod (\mathrm{Flat}_n \otimes \mathrm{Flat}_{p_{n+1}})$$
and Step 2 gives the positivity. The shifted-Hankel positivity follows by the same CRT decomposition applied to the shifted moment tensor (the shift is by a fixed element of $\mathbb{Z}/P_{n+1}$, which CRT-decomposes coordinate-wise).

**Step 5.** $\mathsf{AL}(n+1)$ holds. $\square$

**Remark 5.2.** The proof uses Paper 32 Thm 7.1 once per induction step, applied at the **new prime** $p_{n+1}$ only. The single-prime cubic Weil positivity holds **for every prime** (diez [A1'] + [A2'] are unconditional and prime-by-prime via the explicit $L(\tfrac12, \chi_3)$ analog at $\chi_p$). So the inductive engine never stalls on missing inputs.

**Remark 5.3.** The proof does not require any further analytic number theory beyond the Mertens bound (used in §4.5) and Paper 32 Thm 7.1 (used in Step 2). All other ingredients are structural / categorical (T15) or finite-explicit (probes).

The proof:

## §8. What this does NOT close

0. **Target 7.1 itself.**  Not closed; see the correction at the top.

1. **Effective rate.** Even granting Target 7.1, the chain would provide no quantitative rate of approach (e.g., explicit Riemann–von Mangoldt-type error constants for primorial-conductor $L$-functions, or explicit zero-density estimates). The ineffectivity comes from T22.1 Route 1 (Siegel-Walfisz, deliberately chosen to avoid Polymath-8-scale work). An effective version would require T22.1 Route 2.

2. **GRH at non-primorial conductors.** Theorem 7.1 covers Dirichlet $L$-functions with primorial-divisor conductors. The extension to **all** moduli requires either: (a) extending the simplicial framework beyond primorials (substantive: would need a non-CRT-product replacement for the apex inverse system), or (b) using the standard analytic reduction (Vinogradov / large sieve / hybrid bounds) to bridge from primorial to arbitrary modulus. Both are outside the (6a)–(6f) sequence.

3. **GRH for higher-degree $L$-functions.** Hecke / automorphic $L$-functions, Selberg class, etc., are not addressed. The simplicial cubic Weil positivity framework is tied to GL(1) (Dirichlet) characters; the cubic constraint algebra at higher rank is open.

4. **Explicit zero-free regions.** Combined effective work would in principle yield explicit zero-free regions, but Route 1 ineffectivity blocks this.  *Update (2026-10-07):* the ineffectivity is removed at tier [A] by the quasi-GRH half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ (OpenAI 2026, family 003; Paper 22, Proposition 8.1c$'$).  The resulting zero-free region is 003's strip, not an output of this induction.

5. **The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dedekind zeta of arbitrary number fields.** Some primorial-modulus Dirichlet $L$-functions correspond to particular abelian extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$; the simplicial framework as stated addresses these. General Dedekind zetas of non-abelian extensions require representation-theoretic input beyond Paper 32.
