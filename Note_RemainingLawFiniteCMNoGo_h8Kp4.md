# Remaining Law: Finite CM No-Go

Random suffix: `h8Kp4`

Mission: close the remaining non-circular law
\[
P_{\rm CM}=P_B\succeq0
\]
as a positive Gram kernel of \(K\)-covariant Cayley-Menger/Bures edge states.

Verdict: the finite six-edge Cayley-Menger state space cannot close this law.
The required Gram space is necessarily infinite-dimensional and divisor/canonical
system-like.  Therefore the remaining law is not a missing finite \(Z/6\)
identity; it is exactly the infinite positive spectral measure/canonical-system
step equivalent to RH.

## 1. The tempting finite law

The strongest finite-dimensional hope is:
\[
P_B(z,w)
=
\langle R_z,R_w\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm edge}},
\]
where \(R_z\) is built functorially from the six completed Bures-Cayley edge
coordinates
\[
F_c(z)\in\mathbb C^6
\]
and \(\mathcal H_{\rm edge}\) is a positive Hilbert space of finite dimension
coming from the \(K\)-covariant Cayley-Menger shape metric.

At the regular tetrahedron, \(-D^2\Delta_{\rm CM}\) is positive on the
scale-quotiented shape space, so this hope is geometrically natural.

But finite-dimensional positivity has an unavoidable rank consequence.

## 2. Rank obstruction

If \(\dim\mathcal H_{\rm edge}=d<\infty\), then for every finite set
\[
z_1,\ldots,z_N\in\mathbb C_+
\]
the Gram matrix
\[
\left[\langle R_{z_i},R_{z_j}\rangle\right]_{i,j=1}^N
\]
has rank at most \(d\).

Therefore any equality
\[
P_B(z,w)=\langle R_z,R_w\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm edge}}
\]
with a six-edge state space would force
\[
\operatorname{rank}\left[P_B(z_i,z_j)\right]\le 6
\]
for every finite sample.

This is false.

For points near the first ten critical-line zeros,
\[
z_n=\gamma_n+0.5i,\qquad n=1,\ldots,10,
\]
the numerical Pick matrix has ten strictly positive eigenvalues:
\[
1.74139276,\;2.11366282,\;2.50035121,\;2.97843705,\;3.65800326,
\]
\[
4.10181175,\;5.01024891,\;5.79895904,\;6.81438349,\;8.17364549.
\]

Thus
\[
\operatorname{rank}[P_B(z_i,z_j)]_{i,j=1}^{10}=10>6.
\]

So no six-dimensional Bures-Cayley edge-state Gram law can equal \(P_B\).

## 3. Formal infinite-rank theorem

Assume for contradiction that \(P_B\) is a positive finite-rank kernel on
\(\mathbb C_+\).

Then \(m_B=-\Xi'/\Xi\) is Herglotz, because
\[
P_B(z,z)=
{\operatorname{Im}m_B(z)\over\operatorname{Im}z}\ge0.
\]

By the Herglotz representation, \(m_B\) has a positive representing measure
\(\mu\) on the real line.  Its atoms are exactly the real poles of \(m_B\), i.e.
the real zeros of \(\Xi\), counted with multiplicity:
\[
\mu\supset\sum_{\Xi(\gamma)=0}m_\gamma\delta_\gamma.
\]

Hardy's theorem gives infinitely many zeros of \(\zeta(1/2+it)\), hence
\(\Xi\) has infinitely many real zeros \(\gamma\).  The Cauchy functions
\[
R_z(\gamma)={1\over\gamma-z}
\]
over infinitely many atoms generate an infinite-rank Cauchy Gram kernel.

Therefore \(P_B\), if positive, is necessarily infinite-rank.

Consequently:
\[
\boxed{
P_B\text{ cannot be the Gram kernel of a finite six-edge CM/Bures state space.}
}
\]

## 4. What the finite Cayley-Menger geometry really supplies

The finite \(Z/6\) tetrahedral geometry supplies:

1. the exact six-edge carrier;
2. the \(K:r\mapsto-r\) edge reflection;
3. the Cayley-Menger cubic curvature;
4. the divisor factorization
   \[
   \Delta_{\rm CM}(F_c)=\Xi\,{\mathcal A}_B;
   \]
5. the normalized log-derivative identity
   \[
   -\partial\log\Delta_{\rm CM}(F_c)+\partial\log{\mathcal A}_B
   =
   -{\Xi'\over\Xi}.
   \]

This is enough to identify the correct divisor.  It is not enough to provide
the infinite positive spectral measure of that divisor.

## 5. The only possible non-circular closure

The remaining law must be infinite-dimensional:
\[
P_B(z,w)
=
\langle {\mathcal R}_z,{\mathcal R}_w\rangle_{\mathcal H_{\rm CM}^{\infty}},
\]
where \(\mathcal H_{\rm CM}^{\infty}\) is not the finite six-edge shape space
but an infinite Bures-Cayley divisor space or a canonical-system/de Branges
space generated by the normalized CM transfer.

Equivalently, one must construct a positive Hamiltonian
\[
H_{\rm CM}(t)\succeq0
\]
such that its Weyl function is
\[
m_{\rm CM}(z)=m_B(z)=-{\Xi'(z)\over\Xi(z)}.
\]

That construction would prove \(P_B\succeq0\), hence RH.  Without it, the
finite \(Z/6\) geometry has already delivered all it can deliver.

## 6. Closure

The remaining non-circular law is closed negatively in finite dimension:
\[
\boxed{
\text{No finite six-edge Cayley-Menger/Bures Gram law can equal }P_B.
}
\]

The true law, if it exists, is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Infinite CM divisor/canonical-system positivity for }
\Delta_{\rm CM}(F_c)/{\mathcal A}_B=\Xi.
}
\]

This is not a retreat.  It is the exact boundary of the reduction.  The finite
tetrahedral \(Z/6\) carrier identifies the right divisor; the RH proof requires
building its infinite positive spectral Hilbert space without assuming boundary
support.
