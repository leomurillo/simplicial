## Abstract

We develop the first-order differential operators of three-dimensional vector calculus — gradient, divergence, curl, and the Laplacian — intrinsically in the overcomplete simplicial (Quadray) coordinate system, on the zero-sum hyperplane $H\cong\mathbb{R}^3$ of the $N=4$ regular-simplex frame. The construction is *intrinsic to the frame data* in the sense of Paper 01: once the simplicial Gram matrix $G$ and the orientation class are fixed, the operators require no ambient Cartesian frame.

Four facts organize the theory. **(i)** Gauge invariance of a scalar field is not a constraint to be imposed but a property to be exploited: $f(c+k\mathbf{1})=f(c)$ forces the coordinate differential $df=(\partial_1 f,\dots,\partial_4 f)$ to lie in $H$ automatically, so the gradient is zero-sum by construction. **(ii)** The curl *is* the Paper 01 cross-product operator on the gradient axis,
$$\operatorname{curl}F=K(\nabla)F,$$
obtained by substituting the partial-derivative operators $\partial_j$ for the axis coordinates $u_j$ in the cyclic-difference matrix $K(u)$ of Definition 01.3.1; it lands in $H$ because $K$ has vanishing row sums. **(iii)** The second-order identities of vector calculus are operator promotions of Paper 01's algebra: $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$ and $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$ are the operator forms of $K(u)u=0$ and the column-sum identity, requiring exactly one new differential input — equality of mixed partials ($d^2=0$); and the curl–curl identity $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\Delta$ is the operator promotion of Corollary 01.3.6, $K(u)^2=P_\parallel-I$. **(iv)** The differential complex carries a **gauge–Hodge (Helmholtz) decomposition** in which the gauge direction $\mathbf{1}$ is the distinguished harmonic class; in the algebra of Paper 01 the gauge direction is inert ($K\mathbf{1}=0$, $R\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$), and it is precisely the differential layer that makes it dynamical.

To show the calculus carries weight, §8 works **five canonical doors** intrinsically in the frame: the **electromagnetic wave equation** (where the single load-bearing step — $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}E-\Delta E$ — is Corollary 01.3.6 promoted, so the speed $c=1/\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}$ emerges from the same cubic $K^3=-K$ that powered the Rodrigues exponential); the **heat equation** with its simplicial dispersion $\omega=\tfrac14\|k\|^2$; **incompressible fluids**, where vorticity $\omega=\operatorname{curl}u$ is exactly the antisymmetric/current half of the velocity Jacobian; the **Poisson equation**, whose solvability obstruction is precisely the gauge line; and **rotational dynamics**, where the rigid-rotation field has constant vorticity $2\Omega$ and Paper 01's finite rotation $R=\exp(\theta\widetilde K(\Omega))$ is recovered as its flow. All five are exact-certified.

The scope is the *continuum* differential calculus of a single simplex's tangent hyperplane, expressed in overcomplete coordinates with explicit gauge bookkeeping. This is complementary to, and distinct from, discrete exterior calculus (DEC) and finite element exterior calculus (FEEC), which build $d$, $\star$, $\wedge$ on simplicial *complexes* with edge/face degrees of freedom. The relationship to those frameworks, and to the $\alpha$-connections of information geometry on the probability simplex, is made precise in §10.

**Keywords:** simplicial coordinates, Quadray coordinates, gradient, divergence, curl, Laplacian, Helmholtz decomposition, Hodge decomposition, gauge direction, zero-sum hyperplane, cross product, curl–curl identity, Maxwell equations, electromagnetic wave equation, heat equation, vorticity, Poisson equation, rigid-body rotation, equiangular tight frame, discrete exterior calculus, finite element exterior calculus.

**MSC 2020:** 53A45, 35Q61 (Maxwell equations), 58A10 (differential forms), 15A72, 65N30 (finite element methods), 94A12 (signal theory, frames).

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The forward door

Paper 01 closes its abstract with the sentence "Differential operators ($\nabla$, $\mathrm{div}$, $\mathrm{curl}$) are not treated here." The version-2 changelog records that the paper was *retitled* from "vector calculus" to "vector algebra" precisely to honor this deferral. The deferral was prudent — the algebraic layer is self-contained and the differential layer raises a genuine new question, namely how the differential operators interact with the one-dimensional gauge redundancy along $\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)$. But the deferral was never examined as an *opportunity*. This paper opens that door.

The central observation is that Paper 01 already did most of the differential work without naming it. In ordinary three-dimensional vector calculus the curl is the cross product with the gradient operator, $\operatorname{curl}F=\nabla\times F$, and the second-order identities
$$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0,\qquad \operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\Delta$$
are operator shadows of the algebraic identities
$$a\times a=0,\qquad a\cdot(a\times b)=0,\qquad [a]_\times^2=aa^\top-|a|^2 I .$$
Paper 01 proved the simplicial-coordinate forms of exactly these algebraic identities — $K(u)u=0$ (Appendix 01.B, Step 1), the vanishing column sums of $K(u)$ (Cor. 01.3.7), and $K(u)^2=P_\parallel-I$ (Cor. 01.3.6) — for the cyclic-difference operator $K(u)$ that represents $v\mapsto u\times v$ on $H$. To obtain the differential calculus one substitutes the gradient operator for the constant axis, $u\mapsto\nabla$, and supplies a single new ingredient that algebra cannot provide: the equality of mixed partial derivatives, $\partial_i\partial_j=\partial_j\partial_i$, i.e. $d^2=0$.

### 1.2 Contributions

1. **Gradient (§3).** Scalar fields in simplicial coordinates are gauge-invariant functions $f(c+k\mathbf{1})=f(c)$. We show this forces the coordinate differential $df$ into the zero-sum hyperplane $H$, so that the gradient is automatically in the canonical gauge — no gauge-fixing of the derivative is required. The gradient vector is $\operatorname{grad}f=M_H^{-1}\,df=\tfrac14\,df\in H$ (working $M$-normalization; $\tfrac34\,df$ under the ETF metric $G$).

2. **Divergence and Laplacian (§4).** We define the divergence intrinsically as the negative adjoint of the gradient under the $L^2$ pairing on $H$, give its coordinate form, and obtain the simplicial Laplace–Beltrami operator $\Delta=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}$, equal up to the metric constant to $\sum_i\partial_i^2$ on gauge-invariant fields.

3. **Curl as $K(\nabla)$ (§5).** We prove $\operatorname{curl}F=K(\nabla)F$, the differential promotion of Definition 01.3.1, and the differential analog of Proposition 01.3.3: under the synthesis isometry $S_H$, the simplicial curl is conjugate to the ordinary Cartesian curl.

4. **Second-order identities (§6).** We prove $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$, $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$, and the curl–curl identity $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\Delta$ as promotions of Paper 01's algebraic identities, isolating $d^2=0$ as the unique new differential input.

5. **Gauge–Hodge decomposition and the curvature/current split (§7).** We establish the Helmholtz decomposition $F=\operatorname{grad}f+\operatorname{curl}A+h$ on $H$-valued fields, identify the gauge direction $\mathbf{1}$ as the distinguished harmonic/cohomology sector, and connect it to the one-erasure parity check of the simplex ETF. At the operator level (Proposition 7.3) the field Jacobian splits as $S+A$ with $\operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{div}F$ and $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$: the Helmholtz split *is* the symmetric/antisymmetric curvature-vs-current decomposition of the reversibility line.

6. **Five worked doors (§8).** We carry five canonical problems into the $N=4$ frame: the electromagnetic wave equation (load-bearing step = the curl–curl identity = Corollary 01.3.6 promoted); the heat equation with dispersion $\omega=\tfrac14\|k\|^2$; incompressible fluids, with vorticity as the antisymmetric/current half of the velocity Jacobian; the Poisson equation, whose Fredholm obstruction is exactly the gauge line; and rotational dynamics, recovering Paper 01's finite rotation as the flow of the rigid-rotation field (constant vorticity $2\Omega$). Each is exact-certified.

7. **Discrete bridge and positioning (§§9–10).** We resolve Paper 01 §1.3 (Theorem 10.1): on the tetrahedron's $1$-skeleton $K_4$, the cross-product generator is the opposite-edge Hodge dual of the discrete gradient, $\widetilde K(u)=\star_E(d_0u)$; the integer metric $M$ is the $K_4$ graph Laplacian; and the gauge direction is $H^0(K_4)$. This welds the continuum operators to DEC/FEEC; the gauge-covariant derivative $D=\nabla+A$ resolves (abelian-trivial, discrete-$\mathbb{Z}/2$ via $w_1$) in the structural companion paper.

### 1.3 What is genuinely new, and what is organization

We are explicit about the novelty boundary, because much of the material is a re-presentation of classical facts in new coordinates.

- **Standard, re-presented.** That the curl is the cross product with $\nabla$ is classical and is the organizing principle of geometric calculus [Hestenes, Macdonald]; an index-free matrix representation valid in all dimensions appears in [Hayes]. That gradients, divergences, and curls discretize on simplicial complexes is the content of DEC [Hirani, Desbrun–Hirani–Leok–Marsden] and FEEC [Arnold–Falk–Winther], with edge elements for Maxwell going back to [Bossavit]. That the probability simplex carries Fisher metric and $\alpha$-connections is classical information geometry [Amari, Chentsov, Nielsen]. None of these are claimed as new.

- **New here.** (a) The *continuum* differential operators written intrinsically in the **overcomplete $N$-frame coordinate with the gauge direction carried explicitly**, on a single simplex rather than a complex — a presentation that does not appear in the Quadray/synergetics literature [Urner, Ace, Thomson] nor in the DEC/FEEC literature, which uses edge/face DOFs and circumcentric duals rather than vertex-frame coordinates. (b) The identification of Paper 01's algebraic identities ($K^2$, $K^3$, $K u=0$, column sums) as the **symbols of the vector-calculus second-order identities** in these coordinates, with $d^2=0$ isolated as the unique new input. (c) The **gauge–Hodge decomposition** with $\mathbf{1}$ as the distinguished class, and the gauge-covariant derivative $D=\nabla+A$ that upgrades the static analogy of §01.2.2.1 to a differential object.

The honest one-line summary: the *operators* are organization, the *gauge-differential structure* is new, and the *unification* — one cyclic-difference object $K$ serving simultaneously as Lie algebra, curl, and the seed of the wave operator — is the conceptual payoff.

### 1.4 Notation and standing assumptions

Throughout, $N=4$ and $H=\{c\in\mathbb{R}^4:\sum_i c_i=0\}$ carries the simplicial inner product $\langle a,b\rangle_s=a^\top G b$ with $G=\tfrac43 I-\tfrac13 J$ (Paper 01 §2.4). On $H$, $G$ acts as the scalar $\tfrac43 I_H$, since $Gv=\tfrac43 v$ for $v\in H$. The all-ones vector is $\mathbf{1}$; $J=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top$. We write $\partial_i=\partial/\partial c_i$. A *scalar field* is a smooth gauge-invariant function $f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$ on an open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^4$ with $f(c+k\mathbf{1})=f(c)$; a *vector field* is a smooth map $F:\Omega\to H$. $S=(\mathbf v_1\,\mathbf v_2\,\mathbf v_3\,\mathbf v_4)$ is the $3\times4$ synthesis matrix and $S_H:H\to\mathbb{R}^3$ its restriction, with $S_H^{-1}=\tfrac34 S^\top$ and $SS^\top=\tfrac43 I_3$ (Paper 01 §2.5).

**Working normalization.** All coordinate formulas below are stated in the integer normalization $M=3G=4I-J$ of Paper 01 §7.5, in which $M|_H=4I_H$, the operators are fully rational (no $\sqrt3$), and the synthesis isometry is the integer height map $\widehat S=\sqrt3\,S$ with $\widehat S\widehat S^\top=4I_3$ and $\widehat S_H^{-1}=\tfrac14\widehat S^\top$. The ETF metric $G$ rescales these by harmless constants; we note the $G$-form where it clarifies. The exact-arithmetic certificate of Appendix A confirms every operator identity below in this normalization with residual identically zero, and fixes the curl conjugacy constant at $1$ (equivalently $\kappa=\tfrac14$ for the raw cyclic-difference form).

## 2. Setup: fields on the zero-sum hyperplane

We recall only what is needed. The geometric arena is the three-dimensional hyperplane $H\subset\mathbb{R}^4$, identified with the quotient $\mathbb{R}^4/\langle\mathbf 1\rangle$ via the zero-sum section $c\mapsto c-\bar c\,\mathbf 1$ (Paper 01 §2.3). A point of physical space is a gauge class $[c]$; the canonical representative is the zero-sum tuple. Because every operator below must descend to gauge classes, the guiding principle is the differential analog of Theorem 01.4.1: *scalars are gauge-invariant; vector operators preserve $H$ and annihilate or fix $\mathbf 1$.*

The cross-product operator $K(u)$ of Definition 01.3.1 is the cyclic-difference matrix
$$K(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix}
0 & u_3-u_4 & u_4-u_2 & u_2-u_3\\
u_4-u_3 & 0 & u_1-u_4 & u_3-u_1\\
u_2-u_4 & u_4-u_1 & 0 & u_1-u_2\\
u_3-u_2 & u_1-u_3 & u_2-u_1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},$$
with vanishing row and column sums, $K(u)\mathbf1=0$, $K(u)^\top=-K(u)$, $K(u)u=0$, and (for $G$-unit zero-sum $u$) $K(u)^3=-K(u)$, $K(u)^2=P_\parallel-I$ on $H$ where $P_\parallel$ is the $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_s$-orthogonal projector onto $\mathbb{R}u$.

## 3. The Gradient

### 3.1 Gauge invariance forces the zero-sum gradient

**Proposition 3.1 (The differential of a scalar field is zero-sum).** *Let $f$ be a scalar field. Then at every point the coordinate differential $df:=(\partial_1 f,\dots,\partial_4 f)$ satisfies $\sum_i\partial_i f=0$; that is, $df\in H$.*

*Proof.* Gauge invariance $f(c+k\mathbf 1)=f(c)$ for all $k$ means the directional derivative of $f$ along $\mathbf 1$ vanishes:
$$0=\frac{d}{dk}\Big|_{k=0} f(c+k\mathbf 1)=\sum_{i=1}^4 \partial_i f(c)\cdot 1=\mathbf 1^\top df.$$
Hence $df\perp\mathbf 1$ under the standard inner product, i.e. $df\in H$. $\square$

This is the differential counterpart of the algebraic fact that $K$ annihilates $\mathbf 1$: in the algebra, gauge data is killed on the *output* side; in the calculus, gauge invariance of the input field kills it on the *derivative* side. No gauge-fixing step is needed — the canonical gauge is produced automatically by differentiation.

**Definition 3.2 (Gradient).** The *simplicial gradient* of a scalar field $f$ is the metric dual of the coordinate differential $df$ under the simplicial inner product:
$$\operatorname{grad}f:=M_H^{-1}\,df=\tfrac14\,df\in H,$$
in the working $M$-normalization ($M|_H=4I_H$). Under the ETF metric $G$ the constant is instead $\tfrac34$ ($\operatorname{grad}_G f=\tfrac34\,df$); the two differ only by the overall metric scale $M=3G$ and define the same direction in $H$.

**Proposition 3.3 (Conjugacy with the Cartesian gradient).** *Let $\tilde f=f\circ \widehat S_H^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}$ be the field read in Cartesian height coordinates $x=\widehat S c$. Then $\widehat S_H\,\operatorname{grad}f=\nabla_x\tilde f$; the simplicial gradient is carried to the Cartesian gradient by the synthesis isometry.*

*Proof.* By the chain rule $df=\widehat S^\top\,d\tilde f$ as covectors (where $d\tilde f=\nabla_x\tilde f$). Then $\widehat S\operatorname{grad}f=\tfrac14 \widehat S\widehat S^\top\,\nabla_x\tilde f=\tfrac14\cdot 4\,\nabla_x\tilde f=\nabla_x\tilde f$, using $\widehat S\widehat S^\top=4I_3$. $\square$

## 4. Divergence and the Laplacian

### 4.1 Divergence as the gradient adjoint

The divergence is fixed by requiring the integration-by-parts identity to hold on $H$-valued fields with compact support in $\Omega$.

**Definition 4.1 (Divergence).** The *simplicial divergence* $\operatorname{div}F$ of a vector field $F:\Omega\to H$ is the unique scalar field such that
$$\int_\Omega \langle \operatorname{grad}f,\,F\rangle_s\,dV=-\int_\Omega f\,\operatorname{div}F\,dV$$
for every compactly supported scalar field $f$, where $dV$ is the Riemannian volume of $(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_s)$.

**Proposition 4.2 (Coordinate form).** *In zero-sum coordinates, $\operatorname{div}F=\sum_{i=1}^4 \partial_i F_i$, where $F=(F_1,\dots,F_4)$ with $\sum_i F_i=0$. This expression is gauge-invariant as a scalar and independent of the chosen gauge representative of the differentiation directions.*

*Proof sketch.* Pairing $\langle\operatorname{grad}f,F\rangle_M=(\operatorname{grad}f)^\top M F=\tfrac14\,df^\top(4F)=df^\top F=\sum_i(\partial_i f)F_i$ (using $MF=4F$ for $F\in H$). Integrating by parts in each coordinate and discarding boundary terms gives $-\int f\sum_i\partial_i F_i$. Gauge invariance follows because $F\in H$ makes the $\mathbf 1$-direction inactive; the same cancellation shows the value is independent of the gauge representative. $\square$

**Definition 4.3 (Laplacian).** $\Delta:=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}$. On gauge-invariant scalar fields, $\Delta f=\tfrac14\sum_{i=1}^4\partial_i^2 f$ in the $M$-normalization (and $\tfrac34\sum_i\partial_i^2 f$ under $G$), the Laplace–Beltrami operator of the flat metric on $H$; equivalently, the ambient $\mathbb{R}^4$ Laplacian restricted to gauge-invariant fields, rescaled by the metric constant. The vector Laplacian $\Delta F$ acts componentwise and preserves $H$.

## 5. The Curl is $K(\nabla)$

### 5.1 Definition and zero-sum closure

**Definition 5.1 (Curl).** The *simplicial curl* of a vector field $F:\Omega\to H$ is the Paper 01 cross-product operator evaluated on the gradient axis — schematically "$\nabla\times$" written in the frame. In the working $M$-normalization the precise operator is
$$\operatorname{curl}F:=\widetilde K(\operatorname{grad})\,F=\tfrac14\,\widetilde K(d)\,F,$$
where $\operatorname{grad}=\tfrac14\,d$ is the gradient operator of Definition 3.2 and $\widetilde K=\sqrt3\,K$ is the *raw* cyclic-difference matrix of Paper 01 §3.1 — the clean generator in this normalization, satisfying $\widetilde K^3=-\widetilde K$ on $M$-unit axes (§01.7.5). Substituting the operator entries $\partial_j$ for the axis coordinates $u_j$, the first component is
$$(\operatorname{curl}F)_1=\tfrac{1}{4}\big[(\partial_3-\partial_4)F_2+(\partial_4-\partial_2)F_3+(\partial_2-\partial_3)F_4\big],$$
and the remaining components by cyclic permutation of $(1,2,3,4)$. The constant $\tfrac14$ is fixed by the conjugacy of Theorem 5.3 (Appendix A, $\kappa=\tfrac14$); we write $K(\nabla)$ as a normalization-free shorthand for "$\widetilde K$ (or $K$) on the gradient axis," with the exact constant always as verified here.

**Proposition 5.2 (Closure on $H$).** *For any vector field $F$, $\operatorname{curl}F$ takes values in $H$: $\sum_i(\operatorname{curl}F)_i=0$.*

*Proof.* Each row of $K(\nabla)$ has coefficient sum zero — the same vanishing row-sum property of $K$ used in Cor. 01.3.7 — so $\sum_i(\operatorname{curl}F)_i=\sum_j\big(\sum_i K(\nabla)_{ij}\big)F_j=0$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.3 (Conjugacy with the Cartesian curl; differential Proposition 01.3.3).** *Under the synthesis isometry, the simplicial curl is the ordinary curl: with $\tilde F=\widehat S_H F\circ \widehat S_H^{-1}$ the Cartesian field,*
$$\widehat S_H\,\operatorname{curl}F=\nabla_x\times\tilde F .$$

*Proof.* Proposition 01.3.3, in the $M$-normalization of §01.7.5, gives $\widehat S\,\widetilde K(u)\,\widehat S_H^{-1}=[\widehat S u]_\times$ for constant axes $u\in H$. The entries of $\widetilde K$ are linear in $u$, so this is an identity of linear-in-$u$ matrices; substituting the gradient operator for $u$ and using $\widehat S\,\operatorname{grad}=\nabla_x$ (Proposition 3.3, applied componentwise to $\tilde F$) yields $\widehat S\,\widetilde K(\operatorname{grad})\,\widehat S_H^{-1}=[\nabla_x]_\times$, whose action is $\tilde F\mapsto\nabla_x\times\tilde F$. The exact-arithmetic check (Appendix A) confirms the constant: $\widehat S\,\big(\tfrac14\widetilde K(\nabla)F\big)=\nabla_x\times(\widehat S_H F)$ identically on general polynomial fields. $\square$

Theorem 5.3 is the precise sense in which "curl $=K(\nabla)$" is not a definition of convenience but the genuine curl written in overcomplete coordinates: it is conjugate, by a fixed isometry, to the operator every reader already knows.

## 6. The Second-Order Identities

The three second-order identities of vector calculus are the operator promotions of Paper 01's algebra. Each requires the algebraic identity of Paper 01 **plus** equality of mixed partials.

**Theorem 6.1 (Curl of a gradient vanishes).** *For every scalar field $f$, $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}f=0$.*

*Proof.* Write $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}f=\tfrac14\widetilde K(d)\operatorname{grad}f$ with $\operatorname{grad}f=\tfrac14\,df$, so the first component is the nonzero constant $\tfrac1{16}$ times
$$(\partial_3-\partial_4)\partial_2 f+(\partial_4-\partial_2)\partial_3 f+(\partial_2-\partial_3)\partial_4 f
=(\partial_3\partial_2-\partial_2\partial_3)f+(\partial_4\partial_3-\partial_3\partial_4)f+(\partial_2\partial_4-\partial_4\partial_2)f,$$
which vanishes term-by-term by $\partial_i\partial_j=\partial_j\partial_i$. This is structurally identical to the cancellation that proves $K(u)u=0$ in Appendix 01.B (Step 1): there the terms cancel because the scalars $u_iu_j$ commute; here they cancel because the operators $\partial_i\partial_j$ commute. The other components follow cyclically. $\square$

**Theorem 6.2 (Divergence of a curl vanishes).** *For every vector field $F$, $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}F=0$.*

*Proof.* $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}F=\sum_i\partial_i(K(\nabla)F)_i=\sum_{i,j}\partial_i\,K(\nabla)_{ij}\,F_j$. For fixed $j$ the second-order operator $\sum_i\partial_i K(\nabla)_{ij}$ is a sum of terms $\partial_i(\partial_k-\partial_l)$ in which the elementwise skew-symmetry $K(\nabla)_{ij}=-K(\nabla)_{ji}$ pairs each $\partial_i\partial_k$ against $-\partial_k\partial_i$; commutativity of partials cancels them. This is the operator form of the column-sum/skew identity behind $a\cdot(a\times b)=0$. $\square$

**Theorem 6.3 (Curl–curl identity; promotion of Corollary 01.3.6).** *For every vector field $F$,*
$$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}F=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}F-\Delta F .$$

*Proof.* Corollary 01.3.6 states $K(u)^2=P_\parallel-I$ on $H$, equivalently the un-normalized Cartesian identity $[a]_\times^2=aa^\top-|a|^2 I$. Both sides are quadratic in the axis, so the identity holds as an identity of quadratic-in-axis operators. Substituting $a\mapsto\nabla$ and reading $aa^\top\mapsto \nabla\nabla^\top=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\,)=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}$ and $|a|^2\mapsto\nabla\cdot\nabla=\Delta$ (each requiring $\partial_i\partial_j=\partial_j\partial_i$ for the symmetry $\nabla\nabla^\top$),
$$K(\nabla)^2=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\Delta .$$
Acting on $F$ and using $\operatorname{curl}=K(\nabla)$ gives the claim. $\square$

**Remark 6.4 (The cubic identity is the same object at every order).** The unit-axis cubic $K^3=-K$ of Theorem 01.3.5 powered the Rodrigues exponential $R=\exp(\theta K)=I+\sin\theta\,K+(1-\cos\theta)K^2$ in Paper 01. Its operator promotion $K(\nabla)^3=-\Delta\,K(\nabla)$ (apply $K(\nabla)$ to Theorem 6.3 and use Theorem 6.2) is the third-order curl identity $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}F=-\Delta\operatorname{curl}F$. Thus the single cyclic-difference object $K$ supplies, in one stroke, the Lie algebra of rotations (Paper 01 §3), the finite rotation group (Paper 01 §5), the curl, and — via §8 below — the wave operator. The differentiating ingredient between these faces is only what the axis is: a constant vector, or $\nabla$.

## 7. The Gauge–Hodge Decomposition

In the algebra of Paper 01 the gauge direction $\mathbf 1$ is inert: $K(u)\mathbf 1=0$ and $R(u,\theta)\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$. The differential layer is where $\mathbf 1$ acquires a role, as the distinguished class of a Hodge/Helmholtz decomposition.

**Theorem 7.1 (Gauge–Hodge / Helmholtz decomposition).** *On a domain $\Omega\subseteq H$ with trivial second de Rham cohomology and suitable decay/boundary conditions, every smooth vector field $F:\Omega\to H$ decomposes uniquely as*
$$F=\operatorname{grad}f+\operatorname{curl}A+h,$$
*where $f$ is a scalar field, $A$ an $H$-valued field, and $h$ a harmonic field ($\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}h=\Delta h$, $\operatorname{curl}h=0$, $\operatorname{div}h=0$). The three summands are mutually $L^2(H)$-orthogonal.*

*Proof sketch.* This is the Hodge decomposition of $1$-forms on $(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_s)$ transported to vector fields by the metric, using $\Delta=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}$ (Theorem 6.3) as the Hodge Laplacian. Orthogonality of the gradient and curl sectors is integration by parts together with Theorems 6.1–6.2. $\square$

**Proposition 7.2 (The gauge direction is the harmonic parity).** *The constant field $F\equiv\mathbf 1$ is the prototypical harmonic field, and the recovery of the fourth coordinate from the first three by the zero-sum parity $c_4=-(c_1+c_2+c_3)$ is one-erasure decoding of the regular-simplex ETF (Proposition 01.2.0). The differential operators of §§3–6 descend to ETF-coded fields without prior decoding: $\operatorname{grad}$, $\operatorname{div}$, $\operatorname{curl}$, $\Delta$ all preserve the zero-sum constraint, exactly as the rotation $R$ does in Proposition 01.6.1(7).*

This is the differential face of the erasure-robustness remark in §01.8.3: the gauge sector is not noise to be quotiented away but a coding redundancy that the entire differential complex respects, and on multiply-connected domains it is where the genuine cohomology lives.

### 7.3 The Helmholtz split is the curvature/current decomposition of the field Jacobian

The Helmholtz decomposition of §7.1 acquires an operator-level reading that connects it to the symmetric/antisymmetric ("$S+A$") decomposition used across statistical-mechanics and learning-theoretic treatments of reversibility. Given a vector field $F$, its *field Jacobian* $DF$ (the $H$-endomorphism $c\mapsto\partial_c F$, with entries $(DF)_{ij}=\partial_j F_i$) carries both first-order invariants at once. Because the simplicial metric $M$ restricts to a scalar on $H$, the metric adjoint there is the ordinary transpose, so $DF=S+A$ with $S=\tfrac12(DF+DF^\top)$ symmetric and $A=\tfrac12(DF-DF^\top)$ antisymmetric.

**Proposition 7.3 (Curvature/current split).** *For every vector field $F$ on $H$:*

1. *(Symmetric part carries the divergence.) $\operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{tr}DF=\operatorname{div}F$. The symmetric part is the strain/curvature sector; its trace is the source density.*
2. *(Antisymmetric part is the curl.) $A=\tfrac12\,\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$ exactly — the antisymmetric part is the vorticity, and the isomorphism carrying it to the curl vector is the very same cyclic-difference map $\widetilde K$ that Theorem 10.1 identifies with the coboundary on $K_4$.*
3. *(Helmholtz diagonalizes the split.) For $F=\operatorname{grad}f$, $A=0$ (the Hessian is symmetric, $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$): the gradient sector is pure curvature. For $F=\operatorname{curl}B$, $\operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}B=0$: the solenoidal sector is trace-free, pure current. The harmonic sector $h$ is annihilated by both and, on a domain with loops, has dimension $b_1$ (the first Betti number).*

*Proof.* (1) is $\operatorname{tr}DF=\sum_i\partial_iF_i=\operatorname{div}F$ (Proposition 4.2). (2) is the simplicial form of the classical fact $\tfrac12(\nabla F-\nabla F^\top)=\tfrac12[\operatorname{curl}F]_\times$; the constant $\kappa=\tfrac12$ and the exact matrix identity $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$ are certified in Appendix A (`cert_curvature_current.py`). (3) follows from Theorems 6.1–6.2. $\square$

The reading is structural. The reversible/irreversible dichotomy that elsewhere is imposed by hand — split a transfer or response operator $T=S+A$ into its symmetric part (detailed-balance / reversible "curvature") and its antisymmetric part (the probability current, the arrow) — is, for a field's own Jacobian, exactly the Helmholtz split: $\operatorname{grad}$ (curvature, $A=0$) versus $\operatorname{curl}$ (current, $\operatorname{tr}S=0$), with the harmonic sector as the topologically protected current of dimension $b_1$. A model that instruments only a self-adjoint object — the $A=0$ corner — sees the gradient/curvature sector and is blind to the curl/current sector; recovering the antisymmetric part requires a genuinely non-self-adjoint (predictive, two-sided) probe. The cross-product generator $\widetilde K$ is the exact dictionary between the antisymmetric operator and the curl vector, so Paper 01's algebra, the discrete coboundary (Theorem 10.1), and the reversibility split are three uses of one object.

## 8. Applications: Five Doors the Calculus Opens

A vector calculus is only as useful as the equations it can carry. This section walks through five canonical problems — electromagnetism, heat, fluid vorticity, potential theory, and rotational dynamics — each posed and solved *intrinsically in the simplicial frame*, with the fields realized as zero-sum tetrahedral $4$-tuples and no Cartesian chart. The point is not new physics: each is a known equation, and the discrete/mesh versions are well developed in DEC and FEEC [Hirani, Desbrun–Hirani–Leok–Marsden, Arnold–Falk–Winther, Mohamed–Hirani–Samtaney, Elcott–Tong–Kanso–Schröder–Desbrun, Crane–Weischedel–Wardetzky]. The point is that the *continuum* frame-native operators of §§3–6 reproduce them cleanly, and that one simplicial identity recurs as the load-bearing step. All five are exact-certified in Appendix A (`cert_doors.py`). Throughout, fields are gauge-invariant (functions of the physical point $x=\widehat S c$); a field written directly in raw coordinates $c$ that is *not* gauge-invariant has no well-defined simplicial curl, a constraint the certificate enforces.

### 8.1 Electromagnetism: the wave equation

Let $E,B:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_t\to H$ be zero-sum tetrahedral fields. In a source-free linear medium ($\varepsilon,\mu$) Maxwell's equations are
$$\operatorname{div}E=0,\quad \operatorname{div}B=0,\quad \operatorname{curl}E=-\partial_t B,\quad \operatorname{curl}B=\mu\varepsilon\,\partial_t E,$$
each meaningful in $H$ since $\operatorname{curl}$ closes on $H$ (Proposition 5.2). Applying $\operatorname{curl}$ to Faraday's law and using the curl–curl identity (Theorem 6.3) with $\operatorname{div}E=0$,
$$\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=-\mu\varepsilon\,\partial_t^2E,\qquad \operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}E-\Delta E=-\Delta E,$$
gives the vector wave equation $\Delta E=\mu\varepsilon\,\partial_t^2E$ (and identically for $B$), with speed $c=1/\sqrt{\mu\varepsilon}$. **The single load-bearing step is Theorem 6.3 — the operator promotion of Corollary 01.3.6 ($K^2=P_\parallel-I$).** Electromagnetic waves, in this presentation, descend from the same cubic algebra ($K^2,\,K^3=-K$) that exponentiated rotations in Paper 01: one identity yields both the rotation group and the wave operator. The time-harmonic reduction $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}E=\omega^2\mu\varepsilon\,E$ is the curl–curl eigenproblem, the simplicial-frame form of the FEEC cavity/edge-element problem [Bossavit, Arnold–Falk–Winther].

*Remarks.* The four components are amplitudes along the tetrahedral rays that (by §01.8.3) are the Bloch vectors of a single-qubit SIC-POVM; whether this aids frame-robust discretization is open, not claimed. The frame gauge $\mathbf 1$ and the electromagnetic potential gauge $(\phi,A)$ are independent gauge freedoms in different places; we keep them separate and assert no relation.

### 8.2 Heat and diffusion

The heat equation is $\partial_t u=\Delta u$ with $\Delta=\tfrac14\sum_i\partial_i^2$ (Definition 4.3). A simplicial plane wave $u=e^{-\omega t}\cos\langle k,c\rangle$ with frequency $k\in H$ (so $\langle k,c\rangle$ is gauge-invariant) solves it precisely when
$$\omega=\tfrac14\textstyle\sum_i k_i^2=\tfrac14\|k\|^2,$$
the simplicial dispersion relation (certified). The evolution is the heat semigroup $e^{t\Delta}$, whose discrete companion on the frame's $1$-skeleton is the $K_N$ graph-Laplacian heat trace $\operatorname{Tr}e^{-tM}=1+(N-1)e^{-Nt}$ (here $1+3e^{-4t}$) — the single gauge mode at $0$ plus the $N-1$ physical modes at the universal eigenvalue $N$ (cf. the spectral structure of the companion structural paper). The same operator underlies the DEC heat method for geodesic distance [Crane–Weischedel–Wardetzky].

### 8.3 Incompressible fluids and vorticity

For a velocity field $u$ on $H$, incompressibility is $\operatorname{div}u=0$; any solenoidal field is a curl, $u=\operatorname{curl}\psi$ (certified, via $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$, Theorem 6.2). The **vorticity** is $\omega=\operatorname{curl}u$, and Proposition 7.3 gives its mechanical meaning directly: the velocity gradient splits as $Du=S+A$ into strain $S$ (symmetric) and spin $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\omega)$ (antisymmetric). Vorticity *is* the antisymmetric/current half of the flow's own Jacobian — the curvature/current split of §7.3 made physical. Frame-native conservation of circulation and vorticity is the continuum shadow of the structure-preserving DEC fluid schemes of [Elcott–Tong–Kanso–Schröder–Desbrun, Mohamed–Hirani–Samtaney], which conserve discrete vorticity to machine precision.

### 8.4 Poisson and potential theory

The Poisson equation is $\Delta\varphi=\rho$. Its solvability is governed exactly by the gauge structure. On the frame's $1$-skeleton the Laplacian is the graph Laplacian $M=NI-J$ with $\ker M=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$ (Theorem 10.1), so $M\varphi=\rho$ is solvable **iff** $\rho$ is orthogonal to the gauge line — iff $\sum_i\rho_i=0$ (zero mean) — and the solution is unique modulo $\mathbf 1$ (certified, $\varphi=M^{+}\rho$). The gauge sector is precisely the Fredholm obstruction; in the continuum the same statement reads $\Delta\varphi=\rho$ solvable iff $\rho\perp\ker\Delta=\{\text{constants}\}$, with $\varphi$ the minimizer of the Dirichlet energy $\int(\tfrac12\|\operatorname{grad}\varphi\|^2-\rho\varphi)$. Potential theory on the simplicial frame is thus governed by the same gauge line that organizes the whole construction.

### 8.5 Rotational dynamics: closing the loop to Paper 01

A rigid rotation with angular velocity $\Omega\in H$ has velocity field $v(c)=\widetilde K(\Omega)\,c$. Two facts certify (and recover the classical ones): its vorticity is **constant**, $\operatorname{curl}v=2\,\Omega$ (uniform vorticity equal to twice the angular velocity — the textbook result), and it is incompressible, $\operatorname{div}v=0$. Moreover Paper 01's finite rotation is exactly the flow of this field:
$$R(\Omega,\theta)=\exp\!\big(\theta\,\widetilde K(\Omega)\big),\qquad \frac{dR}{d\theta}=\widetilde K(\Omega)\,R,$$
so the rotation operator of Paper 01 (algebra) is the time-$\theta$ map of the rotational velocity field of this paper (calculus). The cross-product generator $\widetilde K$ is once more the hinge: cross product, curl, and the generator of rigid-body flow are one object viewed at constant axis, gradient axis, and angular-velocity axis respectively.

All five doors are exact-certified for $N=4$ (`empirical_01b/cert_doors.py`, `sympy` only, deterministic local gate, $12/12$): the heat dispersion $\omega=\tfrac14\|k\|^2$ and discrete trace; $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}\psi=0$ and $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\omega)$; the Poisson solvability dichotomy ($\rho$ zero-mean solvable, nonzero-mean obstructed); and the rigid-rotation identities $\operatorname{curl}v=2\Omega$, $\operatorname{div}v=0$, $dR/d\theta=\widetilde KR$. These join the core certificate (Theorems 5.3, 6.1–6.3) and the discrete-bridge and curvature/current certificates of Appendix A.

## 10. The Discrete Bridge and Relation to Existing Frameworks

### 10.1 $\widetilde K$ is a Hodge-dual coboundary on the tetrahedron (resolving Paper 01 §1.3)

DEC [Hirani, Desbrun–Hirani–Leok–Marsden] and FEEC [Arnold–Falk–Winther] define $d$, $\star$, $\wedge$ on simplicial *complexes*, with degrees of freedom on edges, faces, and cells and a circumcentric (or barycentric) dual. The present construction is on a *single* simplex's tangent hyperplane; the two are complementary, and they meet exactly on the tetrahedron's $1$-skeleton, the complete graph $K_4$. Paper 01 §1.3 asked whether $\widetilde K(u)$ is "a discrete exterior derivative applied to $u$ viewed as a $0$-cochain on the tetrahedron's $1$-skeleton, possibly combined with an edge-to-vertex contraction." The answer is affirmative and exact.

Identify the cochain spaces of $K_4$: $C^0=\mathbb{R}^4$ (a value per vertex — the simplicial frame amplitudes themselves), $C^1=\mathbb{R}^6$ (a value per edge), $C^2=\mathbb{R}^4$ (per face). Let $d_0:C^0\to C^1$ be the coboundary $(d_0u)([ij])=u_j-u_i$ and $d_1:C^1\to C^2$ the next coboundary. A skew-symmetric $4\times4$ matrix is exactly a $1$-cochain: its six upper entries are edge values, and skew-symmetry encodes edge orientation. Under this identification, write $\operatorname{coch}\widetilde K(u)\in C^1$ for the $1$-cochain with $\operatorname{coch}\widetilde K(u)([ij])=\widetilde K(u)_{ij}$.

**Theorem 10.1 (Coboundary form of the cross-product generator).** *On the tetrahedron $K_4$:*

1. *(Metric = graph Laplacian.) The integer simplicial metric $M=4I-J$ of §01.7.5 is the graph Laplacian $L_0=d_0^\top d_0$ of $K_4$.*
2. *(Gauge = zeroth cohomology.) $\ker d_0=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}=H^0(K_4)$: the gauge direction is the space of constant $0$-cochains, the kernel of the graph Laplacian.*
3. *(Coboundary form.) $\operatorname{coch}\widetilde K(u)=\star_E\,(d_0 u)$, where $\star_E:C^1\to C^1$ is the signed opposite-edge involution — the map sending the value on each edge to (a sign times) the value on the unique opposite edge. Equivalently $\widetilde K(u)$ is the Hodge dual of the discrete gradient $d_0u$.*

*Proof.* All three are finite identities verified in exact arithmetic (`empirical_01b/cert_dec_bridge.py`). (1) $L_0=d_0^\top d_0$ has diagonal $\deg=3$ and off-diagonal $-1$, i.e. $3I-(J-I)=4I-J=M$. (2) A connected graph has $\ker d_0$ the constants, of dimension $b_0=1$. (3) With opposite pairs $\{[12],[34]\},\{[13],[24]\},\{[14],[23]\}$ and signs $(-1,+1,-1,-1,+1,-1)$ on the ordered edges, the cochain $\star_E d_0u$ matches $\operatorname{coch}\widetilde K(u)$ identically; the signs are the Levi-Civita signs $\varepsilon_{ij\,kl}$ of §01.3.1, so $\star_E$ is the combinatorial Hodge star induced by the canonical self-duality of the $3$-simplex (which, on edges, is the opposite-edge involution). $\square$

Theorem 10.1(3) is the discrete shadow of Paper 01's continuum definition $K(u)v=\star(u\wedge v)$: the continuum Hodge star of $u\wedge\,\cdot$ and the combinatorial Hodge star $\star_E$ of the discrete gradient $d_0u$ are the *same construction at two levels*. The "edge-to-vertex contraction" anticipated in §01.1.3 is identified precisely as the opposite-edge Hodge involution $\star_E$. Two consequences are worth recording. First, the curl $\operatorname{curl}=\widetilde K(\operatorname{grad})$ inherits $d^2=0$ from the genuine coboundary identity $d_1d_0=0$ (verified in the certificate), giving an independent, purely topological proof of $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$ (Theorem 6.1). Second, the cohomological reading of part (2) sharpens §7: the harmonic/gauge sector $\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$ is literally $H^0$ of the tetrahedron, and the de Rham map (integration of continuum forms over the simplices of a tetrahedral mesh) intertwines the continuum operators of §§3–6 with the DEC coboundaries $d_0,d_1,d_2$ in the standard FEEC commuting diagram [Arnold–Falk–Winther, Hirani]. The single-cell continuum algebra developed here is therefore the local fibre of which DEC is the global assembly; on one cell, $\operatorname{grad}\leftrightarrow d_0$, $\operatorname{curl}\leftrightarrow d_1$ (via $\star_E$), $\operatorname{div}\leftrightarrow d_2$.

### 10.2 Geometric calculus and information geometry

**Geometric calculus.** That all first-order operators come from a single vector derivative $\nabla$ acting by inner/outer product is the organizing identity of geometric calculus [Hestenes, Macdonald]; $\operatorname{curl}F=K(\nabla)F$ is its simplicial-coordinate, matrix-valued instance. The matrix-representation viewpoint in arbitrary dimension is [Hayes]; the new element here is the overcomplete frame and the gauge bookkeeping.

**Information geometry.** The hyperplane $H$ is the tangent space of the categorical simplex at the uniform point $p_*=\tfrac14\mathbf 1$, and Corollary 01.3.9 already realizes the Amari–Chentsov cubic at $p_*$ from the cross-product squares $I+K(\tau_i)^2$. The differential operators of this paper are the natural setting for the *moving* version: the $\alpha$-connections $\nabla^{(\alpha)}$ [Amari, Chentsov, Nielsen] are first-order differential data on the simplex, the Amari–Chentsov tensor is the derivative of the Fisher metric, and dual-flatness is the vanishing of the curvature of $\nabla^{(\pm1)}$. Writing these in simplicial-frame coordinates — and asking whether the cubic-as-derivative-of-metric inherits the cross-product-square structure of Corollary 01.3.9 pointwise across the simplex — is the substantive information-geometric door (§11, C3). This is the floor under the program's "mechanics on the categorical simplex" line.

## 11. Conclusion and Open Problems

The differential calculus that Paper 01 deferred is, in its second-order skeleton, already present in Paper 01's algebra: the curl is $K(\nabla)$, and the identities $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{grad}=0$, $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{curl}=0$, $\operatorname{curl}\operatorname{curl}=\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}-\Delta$ are the operator promotions of $K(u)u=0$, the column-sum identity, and $K(u)^2=P_\parallel-I$, with $d^2=0$ the single new differential input. The gauge direction, inert in the algebra, becomes the distinguished class of a Hodge/Helmholtz decomposition. The electromagnetic wave equation falls out of the same cubic identity that exponentiated rotations.

We close with the open problems this floor exposes, ordered from most tractable to highest-ceiling. These are the candidate next directions.

- **C1 (DEC identification) — RESOLVED, §10.1.** $\widetilde K(u)=\star_E(d_0u)$: the cross-product generator is the opposite-edge Hodge dual of the discrete gradient on $K_4$; the metric $M$ is the $K_4$ graph Laplacian; the gauge direction is $H^0$. Theorem 10.1, exact-certified. The remaining sub-question — a quantitative FEEC convergence statement for the de Rham map on a refining tetrahedral mesh — is standard FEEC and left as engineering.

- **C2 (Exact-arithmetic discrete calculus).** In the $M=3G$ normalization (§01.7.5), $\operatorname{grad}$, $\operatorname{div}$, $\operatorname{curl}$ on polynomial fields are rational. Does this give a fully exact (square-root-free) discrete vector calculus on rational tetrahedral data, in the spirit of Wildberger's rational trigonometry? *Tractable; concrete.*

- **C3 (Moving information geometry).** Write the $\alpha$-connections and the Amari–Chentsov tensor in simplicial-frame coordinates across the simplex (not only at $p_*$). Does the cubic retain the cross-product-square form of Cor. 01.3.9 pointwise, and does dual-flatness appear as a clean operator identity? *Medium; high program value — this is the floor under the mechanics papers.*

- **C4 (Curvature/current split at the operator level) — RESOLVED, §7.3.** The field Jacobian splits $DF=S+A$ with $\operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{div}F$ (curvature/source) and $A=\tfrac12\widetilde K(\operatorname{curl}F)$ (current/vorticity); the Helmholtz sectors diagonalize it (grad $\Rightarrow A=0$, curl $\Rightarrow\operatorname{tr}S=0$, harmonic $\Rightarrow$ dim $b_1$). Proposition 7.3, exact-certified. This is the operator-level form of the reversibility $T=S+A$ split, with $\widetilde K$ as the antisymmetric$\leftrightarrow$curl dictionary.

- **C5 (Gauge curvature of varying frames).** Construct $D=\nabla+A$ over a family of simplices with per-cell orientation classes and compute the curvature $\mathcal F=dA$. Is it ever nontrivial? A nonzero $\mathcal F$ would turn §01.2.2.1's analogy into a genuine gauge theory of simplicial frames. *Harder; genuinely new if it lands.*

- **C6 (Simplicial Laplacian spectrum and the apex operator).** Study the spectrum and heat kernel $e^{-t\Delta}$ of the simplicial Laplacian, and ask whether the program's apex/archimedean reflection operator admits a differential realization on this complex. *Speculative; highest ceiling — flagged forward-only.*
