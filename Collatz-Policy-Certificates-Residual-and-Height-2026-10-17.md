# Two policy certificates: residual transport and integer height

17 October 2026. Analytic results, exact finite certificates, and failed compact constructions.

**What this pass establishes.** We constructed and exactly verified residual-loss certificates at levels 2–7. We also constructed integer-height certificates on 25 numerically selected policy graphs, including a separate level-10 test. These heights give parameter control for each certified graph. The finite results do not prove either an all-level residual bound or coverage of every minimizing policy. Consequently they do not establish convergence of the coverage exponents or the Collatz conjecture.

The main manuscript was not modified.

## 1. Certify the loss directly

At level k and parameter gamma=1, write Phi for the minimum operator, mu for the Syracuse probability vector, and

\[
d=\mu-\Phi\mu\ge0.
\]

For a minimizing policy M with spectral radius r=r_k(1) and positive left Perron vector u, its residual-weighted concentration is

\[
\mathcal E(M)=\frac{u\cdot d}{u\cdot\mu}.
\]

We know 1-r<=E(M). Bounding E(M) directly can be much less demanding than controlling max u/(u dot mu) or the full relative entropy of those weights.

**Residual certificate theorem.** Suppose b is a proved upper bound for r, h>=0, and epsilon>=0 satisfy

\[
\boxed{\Phi h-bh\ge d-\epsilon\mu.} \tag{1}
\]

Then every minimizing policy obeys

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E(M)\le\epsilon,\qquad r_k(1)\ge1-\epsilon.} \tag{2}
\]

**Proof.** Every policy obeys Mh>=Phi h. Pair (1) with the left Perron vector of a minimizing policy:

\[
u\cdot d\le\epsilon u\cdot\mu+(r-b)u\cdot h
\le\epsilon u\cdot\mu.
\]

Also M mu>=Phi mu=mu-d, so (1-r)u dot mu<=u dot d. This proves (2). The same concentration bound applies to any policy with spectral radius at most b, not only exact minimizers. Square.

Thus h transports the local residual through the allowed policy moves. The spectral cutoff b is important: a bound over policies with arbitrary spectral radius is obstructed by the previously identified self-loop examples.

For a positive test vector x, an upper bound is

\[
b\ge\max_s\frac{(\Phi x)_s}{x_s}.
\]

Choose a policy attaining Phi x. Its matrix maps x below b x, hence its spectral radius, and therefore the minimum spectral radius, is at most b. Similarly min_s (Phi x)_s/x_s is a lower bound for r. At gamma=1 all coefficients are rational, so these checks need no transcendental arithmetic.

The saved floating-point h and x values were then treated as exact dyadic rationals. ## 3. Sibling and ladder constructions tested

We tested small linear spaces generated by the ordinary law and the approximate nonlinear eigenvector, augmented with sibling shifts mu(A^j r), doubling shifts mu(2^j r), or both. Values on multiples of three are extended by zero. The selected sibling shifts were j=+-1,+-2,+-3,+-4,+-9, and doubling shifts were j=+-1,...,+-6.

A second family lifts the previous level's computed barrier in two ways: directly, and as its density h_(k-1)/mu_(k-1) multiplied by mu_k. It also adds two independent zero-sum top-digit contrasts to the latter lift. This explicitly tests a simple form of transport up the Helmert ladder.

At level 7, with discovery cap h/mu<=10:

| Trial space | Verified-after-discovery floating residual bound |
|---|---:|
| Sibling shifts plus mu and x | 0.280869 |
| Doubling shifts plus mu and x | 0.234365 |
| Combined shifts plus mu and x | 0.234202 |
| Previous-level lift with two top-digit contrasts, plus mu and x | 0.202341 |
| Ladder terms plus combined shifts | 0.162829 |
| Unrestricted residue variables | 0.0667382 |

The compact results in this table are exploratory floating-point values, not the exact certificates of Section 2. Enlarging the cap to 100 gives approximately 0.145341 for the ladder-plus-shifts family, with its actual maximum h/mu about 17.38. The unrestricted bound becomes about 0.0656972. Thus the compact family's shortfall at that level is not merely an artifact of the cap of ten.

For comparison, allowing all policies with spectral radius at most one (b=1), rather than using the sharper minimizing-policy cutoff, gives about 0.322726 in the unrestricted level-7 program. The sharper spectral information materially improves this certificate.

These experiments do not disprove that a richer sibling or ladder formula could work. They show that these particular small spaces do not reproduce the unrestricted finite certificates. The improvement from the previous-level barrier suggests a specific next task: control the new detail needed to transport h from one level to the next, rather than fit every residue independently.

## 4. Integer heights give parameter control

Fix one policy graph, with a doubling edge labelled o=0 and an active odd edge labelled o=1. Parallel labelled edges are treated separately. Suppose an integer function V on its vertices satisfies

\[
\boxed{2o_e+V(t(e))-V(s(e))\le1\quad\text{for every edge}.} \tag{3}
\]

Thus an odd edge lowers V by at least one, and a doubling edge raises it by at most one. Summing around a cycle proves that at most half its edges are odd. Conversely, the absence of a cycle with more odd than doubling edges is the feasibility condition for these integer difference inequalities.

**Parameter theorem for a fixed graph.** Put

\[
c=\log2-\tfrac12\log3=\tfrac12\log(4/3)>0.
\]

The graph's weighted policy matrix satisfies, for every gamma and every delta>=0,

\[
\boxed{\rho(M_{\gamma+\delta})\le e^{-c\delta}\rho(M_\gamma).} \tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** An edge has weight exp(gamma*(-log2+o_e log3)). From (3),

\[
-\log2+o_e\log3\le-c+\tfrac12\log3\,(V(s)-V(t)).
\]

For D=diag(3^(delta V/2)), this gives the entrywise matrix inequality

\[
M_{\gamma+\delta}\le e^{-c\delta}D M_\gamma D^{-1}.
\]

Monotonicity of spectral radius for nonnegative matrices and invariance under similarity prove (4). Square.

Equivalently, d log rho/d gamma<=-c. This supplies parameter control without computing a uniform lower bound for the Markov entropy. It also gives the finite-path inequality

\[
\#\{\text{odd edges on a path of length }L\}
\le\frac L2+\frac{V(\text{start})-V(\text{end})}{2}.
\]

No bound on the range of V is needed for the spectral statement (4). The path statement, however, concerns this particular policy graph, not all Collatz trajectories.

## 5. Exact graph checks and their limitation

We selected policy graphs numerically at gamma=0.8,0.9,1 for levels 2–9. All 24 admitted (3). A separate level-10 endpoint graph also admitted it. An independent integer-only checker verified graph admissibility and all **131,208 edge inequalities across 25 graphs**.

The check certifies each stored graph and therefore its fixed-graph parameter bound for all gamma. **It does not certify that the selected graph is an exact minimizer, or that these graphs cover all minimizing policies as gamma varies.** It also does not prove the one-half bound persists at higher levels.

## 6. What would complete this route to exponent convergence

Here is a precise sufficient statement combining the two certificates:

1. At every sufficiently large level, construct (1) with a certified b>=r_k(1) and epsilon_k->0.
2. For almost every gamma in [gamma_k,1], at least one active minimizing policy admits (3).

The first gives r_k(1)>=1-epsilon_k. At parameters where the lower envelope log r_k is differentiable, the second gives its derivative at most -c. Integrating from its unique critical root gamma_k to one yields

\[
-\log r_k(1)\ge c(1-\gamma_k),
\qquad
\boxed{\gamma_k\ge1+\frac{\log(1-\epsilon_k)}c\longrightarrow1.} \tag{5}
\]

This uses the established local Lipschitz and finite-policy lower-envelope properties; it does not assume a single policy stays minimizing over the interval. A uniform cycle bound theta_0<log2/log3 would also suffice, replacing c by log2-theta_0 log3. One half is a concrete stronger target suggested by the tested graphs, not a proved general fact.

**Neither all-level hypothesis has been established.** Finite linear programs give witnesses, not a proof of their uniform extension. The main new open construction is a level-to-level residual correction with vanishing epsilon; the main parameter question is whether minimization forces the cycle balance (3), or a sufficiently strong replacement.

Even proving (5) establishes convergence of the coverage exponents, not the full Collatz conjecture. An argument ruling out exceptional integer orbits is still necessary.
