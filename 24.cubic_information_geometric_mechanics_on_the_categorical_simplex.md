## Abstract

We formulate the cubic stage of information-geometric mechanics on the
categorical probability simplex. The phase space is the strictly positive
simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ\) associated with the minimal mixed arithmetic skeleton
\[
R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
The Fisher volume form supplies the native Nambu structure, while the first
nonlinear momentum gap is the raised Amari-Chentsov cubic
\[
K^{CH}(v,v).
\]
The resulting mechanics has a sharp obstruction-completion pattern. On the
real simplex, positivity, Fisher-volume preservation, and CRT compatibility
force the continuous symmetry algebra to the Cartan sector
\(\mathfrak u(1)^2\). The cubic Noether correspondence then exhausts the
admissible classical cubic charges. The next cumulant, represented by
\(\nabla^{(e)}T\), points toward higher momentum but does not create a
classical completion, because incorporating it requires nonabelian packet
mixing forbidden by the same real-cone and CRT constraints.

The missing nonabelian directions become legitimate after lifting from real
probabilities to the Bures-Helstrom geometry of density matrices on
\(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3\). On the diagonal commuting submanifold, the
Bures metric restricts exactly to the Fisher metric. Off the diagonal, local
unitary conjugation supplies the effective gauge algebra
\[
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]
Thus the cubic real-simplex theory closes only in an abelian Cartan sector,
while its nonabelian completion is forced by the density-matrix lift. A finite
trajectory-level audit confirms that embedding the squarefree \(R=6\) support
into \(R=12\) and \(R=18\) does not produce cubic-force leakage into
prime-power depth above numerical floor.

## 1. Introduction

This paper studies cubic mechanics in information geometry. The word "cubic"
is literal: the first nonlinear force term is governed by the third cumulant,
equivalently by the Amari-Chentsov tensor. The relevant phase object is not a
configuration space of point masses, but a categorical probability simplex
with Fisher geometry and conductor-resolved arithmetic coordinates.

The central result is an obstruction-completion theorem for the row
\(R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\). The real simplex admits a natural Nambu-Fisher
mechanics, but its continuous symmetries are forced to be abelian once one
requires:

1. preservation of the real positive cone;
2. preservation of the Fisher volume form;
3. compatibility with the CRT factorization
   \[
   \mathbb C[R]\cong \mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3.
   \]

The obstruction is not a missing higher-order term. The fourth-cumulant
derivative \(\nabla^{(e)}T\) identifies where higher momentum would have to
enter, but the nonabelian generators needed to represent that momentum are not
admissible on the real positive simplex. The completion therefore occurs by
changing the phase object: real probability vectors are lifted to Hermitian
density matrices, and Fisher geometry is lifted to Bures-Helstrom geometry.

The result is a clean pattern:

\[
\text{real simplex}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak u(1)^2
\qquad
\text{but}
\qquad
\text{Bures density matrices}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]

This is the mathematical contribution of the cubic row.

## 2. The Categorical Simplex and Its Fisher Volume

Let \(R\) be a finite quotient ring and let
\[
\Delta_R^\circ
=
\left\{
p:R\to \mathbb R_{>0}\;:\;\sum_{a\in R}p_a=1
\right\}
\]
be the strictly positive categorical simplex. The Fisher metric is
\[
g_p(u,w)=\sum_{a\in R}{u_a w_a\over p_a},
\qquad
\sum_a u_a=\sum_a w_a=0.
\]
Its Riemannian volume form is denoted \(\Omega_R^F\).

For \(R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), the Chinese remainder factorization gives
\[
R\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times \mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
This is the minimal mixed row with two independent prime axes. In the group
algebra it corresponds to the tensor product
\[
\mathbb C[R]\cong \mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3.
\]
The conductor decomposition of \(R\) supplies the packet coordinates in which
off-centroid Fisher responses are organized.

## 3. Nambu-Fisher Mechanics

On the \((|R|-1)\)-dimensional simplex, a tuple of \(|R|-2\) smooth
macro-Hamiltonians
\[
\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{|R|-2})
\]
defines a Nambu-Fisher vector field \(X_{\mathbf C}\) by
\[
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{|R|-2}.
\]
Equivalently, for any smooth observable \(f\),
\[
\{f,C_1,\ldots,C_{|R|-2}\}_F\,\Omega_R^F
=
df\wedge dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{|R|-2}.
\]

The Nambu formalism is the correct volume-form analogue of Hamiltonian
mechanics for this simplex. It preserves the Fisher volume and the
macro-Hamiltonians by construction.

## 4. The Cubic Force Law

Let \(T\) denote the Amari-Chentsov cubic tensor of the categorical simplex.
Raising an index with the Fisher metric gives the cubic momentum-gap map
\[
K^{CH}(v,v).
\]
In \(e\)-affine coordinates, the cubic acceleration law is
\[
\ddot\eta = K^{CH}(v,v).
\]

This law is the first nonlinear correction to Fisher-geodesic motion. It is
not inserted externally: it is the raised third cumulant of the categorical
log-partition geometry.

The cubic action density used in the companion Noether theorem is
\[
\ell_3(\eta,v)
=
{1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)
+
{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v),
\]
where \(h=\eta-\eta_*\) is displacement from the categorical centroid. The
corresponding autonomous energy density is
\[
\mathcal E_3
=
{1\over2}g_\eta(v,v)
+
{1\over3}T_\eta(h,v,v).
\]
The first variation of the cubic action recovers the force
\(K^{CH}(v,v)\), while the next \(e\)-covariant derivative
\(\nabla^{(e)}T\) marks the first fourth-cumulant correction.

## 5. The Classical Cartan Obstruction

The real-simplex symmetry problem asks for continuous vector fields preserving:

1. the Fisher volume form \(\Omega_R^F\);
2. the positive cone \(p_a>0\);
3. the CRT tensor structure
   \[
   \mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3.
   \]

For \(R=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z\), these conditions force the admissible
continuous symmetry algebra to the Cartan subalgebra
\[
\mathfrak u(1)^2.
\]

The nonabelian root directions of
\[
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)
\]
mix conductor packets in a way that cannot be represented by real
positive-cone-preserving simplex flows. Thus the real-simplex theory is
classically abelian even though the CRT tensor product already points toward a
larger local algebra.

This is the Cartan obstruction.

## 6. Cubic Noether Closure

The cubic Noether correspondence attaches a conserved cubic charge to each
admissible symmetry vector field \(X\). Let
\[
P_{\mathbf C}
=
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{|R|-2}
\]
be the Fisher-dual momentum form. The associated cubic charge is
\[
Q_X=\iota_XP_{\mathbf C}.
\]

Since the leading off-centroid term in \(P_{\mathbf C}\) contains the
Amari-Chentsov tensor \(T\), the charge \(Q_X\) is cubic in the simplex jet.
But the Cartan obstruction restricts \(X\) to the two abelian generators.
Therefore the real-simplex cubic charges are exhausted by the Cartan sector.

The fourth-cumulant derivative \(\nabla^{(e)}T\) points to higher momentum,
but it does not create a classical completion. Any attempt to include it as an
additional conserved macrovariable requires the forbidden nonabelian mixing
directions. Hence the real-simplex cubic theory is variationally complete only
inside its abelian truncation.

## 7. Bures Completion

Let
\[
\mathcal H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3
\]
and let \(\mathcal D(\mathcal H)\) be the manifold of strictly positive
trace-one Hermitian operators on \(\mathcal H\). The Bures-Helstrom metric is
defined by
\[
g^B_\rho(X,Y)
=
2\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(XL_\rho^{-1}(Y)\bigr),
\qquad
L_\rho(Z)=\rho Z+Z\rho.
\]
For diagonal states \(\rho=\operatorname{diag}(p)\) and diagonal tangent
vectors \(X=\operatorname{diag}(x)\), \(Y=\operatorname{diag}(y)\),
\[
g^B_\rho(X,Y)
=
\sum_a{x_a y_a\over p_a}.
\]
Thus the Bures metric restricts exactly to the Fisher metric on the diagonal
simplex, and the Bures volume form restricts to \(\Omega_R^F\).

Off the diagonal, the local unitary action
\[
(U_2,U_3)\cdot\rho
=
(U_2\otimes U_3)\rho(U_2\otimes U_3)^\dagger
\]
preserves trace, positivity, and the Bures metric. Its effective infinitesimal
algebra, modulo scalar phases that act trivially by commutator, is
\[
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]

The nonabelian directions that were forbidden on the real simplex are
therefore legitimate unitary coherences on the density-matrix manifold. The
completion is not a new classical force term; it is the Bures lift of the phase
space.

## 8. Conservative Dynamics Upstairs

For a traceless Hermitian observable \(H\), define
\[
Y_H(\rho)=-i[H,\rho].
\]
Because the Bures geometry is unitarily invariant,
\[
\mathcal L_{Y_H}\Omega^B=0.
\]
On a regular tube, choose transverse functions
\[
C_1(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho H),\qquad C_2,\ldots,C_{34}
\]
so that
\[
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{34}
=
\iota_{Y_H}\Omega^B.
\]
Then the Nambu-Bures vector field is exactly
\[
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho].
\]

Thus conservative density-matrix dynamics is ordinary unitary von Neumann
flow. The projected classical cumulant-defect obstruction appears only after a
nonabelian density-matrix trajectory is measured back into the diagonal
commuting sector.

## 9. Finite Constraint 3 Audit

The finite audit tests whether prime-power depth creates a new independent
cubic axis. The diagnostic follows a volume-dual Nambu-Fisher vector field
\(X_C\) and records the normalized cubic contraction
\[
{T_p(h,X_C,X_C)\over \|h\|_{F,p}\|X_C\|_{F,p}^2}.
\]
Closure is measured by the Fisher-norm fraction of
\[
K^{CH}_p(X_C,X_C)
\]
outside the squarefree support \(\{2,3,6\}\).

With 50 seeds, amplitude \(0.04\), initial Fisher speed \(0.02\), horizon
\(1.0\), and 240 RK4 steps, the audit gives:

| row | support | outside depth | mean normalized cubic charge | mean leakage | max leakage | assessment |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 6 | \([2,3,6]\) | \([]\) | 0.457667 | \(3.13\times10^{-32}\) | \(2.25\times10^{-31}\) | known-closed floor |

The result supports the squarefree two-prime cubic row: embedding into
\(R=12\) or \(R=18\) does not force cubic leakage into prime-power depth above
the \(R=6\) numerical floor.

## 10. Scope

This paper establishes the cubic information-geometric obstruction-completion
pattern on the categorical simplex:

1. the cubic force is the raised Amari-Chentsov tensor \(K^{CH}(v,v)\);
2. the real-simplex symmetry algebra is forced to the Cartan sector;
3. the cubic Noether charges are exhausted by the admissible Cartan
   generators;
4. the fourth-cumulant correction does not open a classical completion;
5. the Bures density-matrix lift restores the nonabelian local algebra
   \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)\);
6. finite trajectory evidence supports the squarefree \(R=6\) cubic support.

It does not derive spacetime gravitation, a complete quantum field theory,
chirality, generations, Yukawa couplings, anomaly cancellation, or
renormalization-group flow. The transformer residual audits in the companion
density-matrix paper remain exploratory and are not proof inputs here.

## Conclusion

Cubic information-geometric mechanics on the categorical simplex has a precise
shape. The real \(R=6\) simplex carries a Fisher-volume Nambu mechanics with a
natural cubic force, but its admissible continuous symmetries and cubic Noether
charges close only in the abelian Cartan sector. The next cumulant identifies
the obstruction rather than removing it.

The nonabelian completion appears only after lifting to Bures density-matrix
geometry on \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^3\). There the diagonal submanifold
recovers the Fisher simplex exactly, while off-diagonal coherences realize the
effective local algebra
\[
\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3).
\]
The contribution is therefore a no-go plus completion theorem for cubic
information geometry: the real classical shadow is Cartan, and the full local
nonabelian structure is a property of the Bures lift.
