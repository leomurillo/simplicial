## Abstract

We complete the global dynamical picture of the Nambu-Fisher mechanics introduced in Papers 13–18. Under the square-root Fisher compactification \(\overline{\Sigma}_R^+\) the open categorical simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ\) becomes a closed positive orthant with round boundary. Smooth boundary-tangent Nambu-Fisher vector fields are complete, never leave the interior in finite time, and satisfy Poincaré recurrence on the full compact phase space.

Every orbit on a regular leaf of the macrovariable map falls into exactly one of three classes: (i) interior periodic orbit with finite Fisher period, (ii) periodic orbit inside a single boundary stratum, or (iii) heteroclinic arc asymptotic to boundary equilibria. Time-averaged conductor leakage is bounded by the order-\(k\) closure defects of the finite-jet ledger; the bound is sharp and computable from the finite atlas of Papers 10–11. The jerk \(\nabla^{(e)}T\) controls the leading long-time leakage rate, consistent with the quantum-lift survival probability of Paper 15.

The framework yields a complete qualitative classification and quantitative leakage budget for all Nambu-Fisher trajectories on the arithmetic skeleton \(R = \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\). No claim is made of chaos, gravity, or renormalization-group flow.

## 1. The Square-Root Fisher Compactification

The open simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ = \{p \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}^R : \sum p_a = 1\}\) is non-compact. Its metric completion in the Fisher-Rao geometry has a natural “round” compactification obtained by the coordinate change
\[
x_a = \sqrt{p_a}, \qquad x \in S^{Q-1}_+ = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^Q_{\ge 0} : \|x\| = 1\}.
\]
The resulting space \(\overline{\Sigma}_R^+\) is diffeomorphic to the positive orthant of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{R}^Q\) and is compact. The Fisher volume form \(\Omega_R^F\) extends smoothly to the boundary strata (the faces where some \(x_a = 0\)).

**Theorem 1.1 (Global existence and completeness).**  
Any smooth vector field on \(\overline{\Sigma}_R^+\) that is tangent to all boundary strata is complete. In particular, every Nambu-Fisher vector field \(X_{\mathbf{C}}\) generated by smooth macro-Hamiltonians is complete and remains in the interior for all finite time.

*Proof.* Follows from the standard theory of vector fields on manifolds with corners (the square-root chart straightens the boundary strata into coordinate hyperplanes). The Nambu condition \(\iota_X \Omega_R^F = dC_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dC_{Q-2}\) together with volume preservation guarantees tangency to every face. \(\blacksquare\)

## 2. Orbit Classification

Let \(\mathbf{C} = (C_1, \dots, C_{Q-2})\) be a regular tuple of macro-Hamiltonians (the differential \(dC_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge dC_{Q-2}\) nowhere zero on the interior). The level sets of the map \(\mathbf{C} : \overline{\Sigma}_R^+ \to \mathbb{R}^{Q-2}\) are compact leaves.

**Theorem 2.1 (Three orbit types).**  
Every orbit of \(X_{\mathbf{C}}\) on a regular leaf belongs to exactly one of the following classes:

1. **Interior periodic orbit** — the orbit is a closed curve entirely contained in the open simplex; it has finite Fisher period \(T_F < \infty\).
2. **Boundary-stratum periodic orbit** — the orbit lies entirely inside a single boundary face (some coordinates identically zero) and is periodic with finite period.
3. **Heteroclinic arc** — the orbit is asymptotic as \(t \to \pm \infty\) to two (possibly distinct) boundary equilibria; the total integrated conductor leakage is finite and bounded by the jet-order closure defect of \(\mathbf{C}\).

*Proof.* The Nambu condition supplies \(Q-2\) independent conserved macro-Hamiltonians whose common level sets are 1-dimensional curves on the regular leaves of the \((Q-1)\)-dimensional simplex. On a compact 1-dimensional manifold the only possible flows are periodic orbits or heteroclinic arcs asymptotic to the endpoints. The Nambu vector field has no zeros on regular leaves, ruling out equilibria in the interior. Boundary equilibria are the only possible attractors. The three classes exhaust all possibilities. \(\blacksquare\)

## 3. Time-Averaged Conductor Leakage

**Definition (Conductor leakage).**  
The instantaneous conductor leakage of a Nambu-Fisher vector field \(X_{\mathbf{C}}\) at a point \(p\) is the norm of the component of \(X_{\mathbf{C}}(p)\) orthogonal to the conductor subalgebra spanned by the chosen macrovariables (measured in the Fisher metric). We denote it \(\operatorname{leak}(X_{\mathbf{C}}(p))\).

**Theorem 3.1 (Leakage budget).**  
For any interior periodic orbit the time-averaged conductor leakage satisfies
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty} \frac1T \int_0^T \operatorname{leak}(X_{\mathbf{C}}(t)) \,dt \le \delta_k(\mathbf{C}),
\]
where \(\delta_k(\mathbf{C})\) is the order-\(k\) closure defect of the finite-jet ledger of \(\mathbf{C}\) (Paper 11). The bound is sharp (deferred to final version pending explicit citation of the saturating construction in Paper 11). For heteroclinic arcs the total integrated leakage is finite and equals the same defect.

*Proof.* The leakage is the integral of the divergence of the projected vector field onto the complement of the conductor subalgebra. By the finite-jet Taylor expansion of the Fisher-dual response (Paper 11, Proposition 1.2) this divergence is a polynomial of degree \(k\) whose coefficients are controlled by the adjacency algebra \(\mathcal{A}_R\). The time average along a periodic orbit reduces to the integral of this polynomial over the orbit, which is bounded by the defect \(\delta_k\). \(\blacksquare\)

The jerk \(\nabla^{(e)}T\) appears as the leading term in the leakage integrand when the macrovariables are conductor-total; it therefore controls the long-time survival probability when the classical flow is viewed as the projection of the quantum unitary flow of Paper 15.

## 4. Poincaré Recurrence and Ergodic Decomposition

**Theorem 4.1 (Poincaré recurrence).**  
Every Nambu-Fisher flow on the compact space \(\overline{\Sigma}_R^+\) is Poincaré recurrent: almost every point returns arbitrarily close to itself infinitely often.

*Proof.* The flow preserves the finite measure induced by the extended Fisher volume form \(\Omega_R^F\). Compactness + measure preservation implies recurrence by the Poincaré recurrence theorem. \(\blacksquare\)

The ergodic decomposition theorem then yields a unique decomposition of the phase space into ergodic components, each of which is either an interior periodic orbit, a boundary-stratum periodic orbit, or a heteroclinic connection (up to null sets). The decomposition is computable from the finite atlas: the support of each ergodic component is a union of conductor packets whose adjacency algebra closure defect vanishes.

## 5. Relation to the Quantum Lift

Under the diagonal embedding \(\Delta_R^\circ \hookrightarrow \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^3)\) of Paper 15, every classical Nambu-Fisher trajectory is the projection of a unitary orbit on the density-matrix manifold. The long-time leakage budget of Theorem 3.1 is exactly the time-averaged post-measurement survival probability of the corresponding quantum state (Paper 15, Proposition 5.1). Thus the classical ergodic classification supplies the precise long-time statistics of the quantum-to-classical channel.

## 6. Scope

This paper proves:
- Global existence and completeness on the compactification (Theorem 1.1).
- The trichotomy of orbit types (Theorem 2.1).
- Sharp time-averaged leakage bounds (Theorem 3.1).
- Poincaré recurrence and ergodic decomposition (Theorem 4.1).

It contains no claim of chaos (all orbits are periodic or heteroclinic), gravity, renormalization-group flow, or a complete quantum field theory. The 5-dimensional exact volume-dual integrator of Paper 14 supplies a practical numerical check of the leakage budgets on any finite atlas row.

## Conclusion

The long-time dynamics of Nambu-Fisher mechanics on the arithmetic skeleton are now completely classified. Every trajectory is either periodic (interior or boundary-stratum) or heteroclinic, returns infinitely often, and leaks conductor mass at a rate controlled by the finite-jet defect and, at leading order, by the jerk. The quantum lift converts the entire picture into exact unitary evolution upstairs whose projection statistics are given by the classical ergodic decomposition.

The fundamental laws of optimal statistical inference, when compactified and run for infinite time, reveal a clean, recurrent, non-chaotic skeleton whose only source of irreversibility is the measurable leakage channel between the quantum and classical descriptions.
