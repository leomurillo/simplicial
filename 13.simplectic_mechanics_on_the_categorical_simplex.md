## Abstract

Classical Hamiltonian mechanics is built around a bilateral primitive:
position and momentum, a cotangent bundle \(T^*M\), a symplectic 2-form
\(\omega\), and, in the Kahler case, a complex structure \(J\) satisfying
\(J^2=-I\).  This package is mathematically powerful, but it is also
dimensionally special.  This paper deliberately writes **simplectic
mechanics** with an \(i\): the intended phase space is the simplex itself,
not a binary cotangent double of it.

The reason is visible in simplicial vector calculus.  The familiar
identification between a vector axis and an antisymmetric bilinear operator
occurs in the \(N=4\) row, where the physical space has dimension \(3\).
There the Hodge star identifies an axis vector with a 2-form, and the
intrinsic cross-product operator \(K(u)\) restricts on the orthogonal plane
to a complex structure.  For \(N\ge 5\), this identification breaks: a
single vector no longer parameterizes the rotation blades of the hyperplane.

This paper constructs the corresponding mechanics.  The phase object is not a
cotangent bundle with paired \(q,p\) coordinates.  It is the categorical
simplex

$$
\Delta_R^\circ=\{p\in {\mathbb R}_{>0}^R:\sum_{a\in R}p_a=1\}
$$

with tangent hyperplane

$$
H_R=\{u\in {\mathbb R}^R:\sum_{a\in R}u_a=0\},
$$

equipped with the Fisher-Rao metric, the Fisher volume form, and the finite
quotient-character conductor decomposition from Papers 10 and 11.  The
Fisher volume form

$$
\Omega_R^F\in\Omega^{Q-1}(\Delta_R^\circ)
$$

is the non-binary replacement for the symplectic 2-form.  It is closed
because it is top-degree, and it is nondegenerate in the Nambu sense because
contraction with it identifies vectors with \((Q-2)\)-forms.

The replacement for Hamiltonian flow is a Nambu-Fisher flow: on the
\((Q-1)\)-dimensional simplex, where \(Q=|R|\), a list of \(Q-2\) conserved
macrovariables \(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\) determines a vector field by

$$
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

Equivalently, observables evolve by the \((Q-1)\)-ary bracket

$$
\dot f
=
\{f,C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\}_F
=
\frac{df\wedge dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}}{\Omega_R^F}.
$$

This is the direct analogue of Hamilton's equation
\(\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH\), but it uses the top-degree Fisher volume rather
than a binary 2-form and \(Q-2\) macro-Hamiltonians rather than one scalar
Hamiltonian.

The role of momentum also changes.  If the \(e\)-flat coordinate
\(\theta\) is treated as generalized position, the centered \(m\)-flat
coordinate \(h=p-p_*\) is the correct first-order Legendre-dual momentum at
the centroid.  But away from the centroid it is not a separate conjugate
variable in the cotangent-bundle sense.  The intrinsic non-binary momentum is
the \((Q-2)\)-form

$$
P_{\mathbf C}=\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F,
$$

or, in coordinates, the full Fisher-dual response of the \(m/e\) Legendre
pair.  The raised Amari-Chentsov cubic

$$
K^{CH}=\Gamma^{(m)}-\Gamma^{(e)}
$$

is then the tensorial momentum gap: it measures how the \(m\)-flat and
\(e\)-flat affine structures fail to agree beyond first order.  In the
quotient-character basis, the Taylor coefficients of this gap are governed
by convolution in the finite dual group and by the quotient-character
adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_R\).  The Carlitz-Hayes extension class
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) is the finite arithmetic anomaly of the mechanics:
it measures when tame residue motion and wild depth motion cannot be split
canonically, forcing carry between conductor layers.

The algebra-first result is that the Hamilton equations can be written
directly in the quotient-character atlas.  The Nambu current is the exterior
form \(P_{\mathbf C}=dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}\), the vector field is
the cofactor vector determined by the Fisher volume density, and the
Fisher-dual response has conductor-resolved Taylor coefficients
\(C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)\).  Consequently, conductor closure to
order \(k\) is a finite vanishing problem in \(\mathcal A_R\), and a nonzero
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry orbit is a section-independent obstruction
to reducing the filtered dynamics to the associated graded.

The calendar-transformer projection experiments of Paper 12 add a learned
dynamics reading.  When the applied output-head update is projected into a
closed conductor support, the unprojected logits themselves move toward that
support: in the best \(\{6,12\}\) month blanket, raw output-head gradients
still carry excluded-packet mass while the applied updates and final logits
remain closed to machine precision.  This equilibrium internalization is the
AI-facing shadow of the simplectic picture.  The external current is noisy,
but the learned state approaches the closed conductor leaf selected by the
update current.  A projection-removal ablation should be logged separately
before making the stronger claim that the blanket has become unnecessary at
evaluation time.

The same mechanics also gives a concrete layerwise spectral primitive.  At an
intermediate representation, the Nambu-Fisher current of a chosen
macro-Hamiltonian tuple can be paired with the cross-entropy residual on the
output simplex, producing scalar sample weights.  The weighted activation
covariance is then diagonalized, optionally packet by packet or through the
\(K^{CH}\) cubic carry channels.  This **Layerwise Simplectic Spectral
Filtering** is the simplectic counterpart of a supervised low-degree spectral
filter: it does not ask only which activation directions correlate with a
label, but which directions carry the Fisher-volume current, in which
conductor packet, and with how much cubic carry.

This local theorem layer also exposes two structural completions.
Large-\(Q\) categorical partitions converge by conditional expectation, and
arithmetic carry contributions obey a normalized measure trichotomy.  For
dynamics, the square-root Fisher compactification turns the open simplex
into a round positive orthant and gives a clean global-existence criterion:
smooth boundary-tangent Nambu-Fisher fields are complete and never leave the
interior in finite time.  The long-time dynamics of those flows admit a
clean qualitative classification: every orbit on a regular leaf of the
macrovariable map is either an interior periodic orbit with finite Fisher
period, a periodic orbit inside a single boundary stratum, or a heteroclinic
arc asymptotic to boundary equilibria, and the time-averaged conductor
leakage of each such orbit is bounded by the order-\(k\) closure defects of
the finite-jet ledger.  The closed Fisher-current residual lifts from finite
jet order to the full infinite-order residual through a Mittag-Leffler exact
sequence, with the Ext-generated piece stabilizing at a computable jet
order and equal to the direct sum of the local
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) classes.  The remaining work is
to compute the arithmetic carry measures in genuine towers and to run the
finite atlas census of closure defects, ergodic components, and
hyper-adjacency dimensions on the explicit atlases of Papers 10-11.

The slogan is:

> Symplectic mechanics is binary mechanics on a 2-form.  Simplectic mechanics
> is finite categorical mechanics on a volume form, with \(K^{CH}\) as the
> tensor of conjugacy failure and \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) as the carry
> anomaly.

**Keywords:** Hamiltonian mechanics, symplectic geometry, Nambu mechanics,
multisymplectic geometry, information geometry, Fisher-Rao metric,
Amari-Chentsov tensor, categorical simplex, conductor blocks,
quotient-character adjacency algebra, Carlitz-Hayes extension class.

## 1. The Binary Symplectic Paradigm

Classical mechanics is usually written on a phase space with coordinates

$$
(q^1,\ldots,q^n,p_1,\ldots,p_n)
$$

and symplectic form

$$
\omega=\sum_i dq^i\wedge dp_i.
$$

The Hamiltonian vector field of an energy \(H\) is defined by

$$
\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH,
$$

and Hamilton's equations are the coordinate expression of this contraction.
The form \(\omega\) is closed and nondegenerate, so it identifies vectors
with covectors.  In a compatible Kahler presentation, one writes

$$
\omega(X,Y)=g(JX,Y),
\qquad J^2=-I.
$$

The operator \(J\) is the quarter-turn.  It rotates position into momentum,
momentum back into negative position, and installs a two-sided clock into
the geometry.

This is not a criticism of symplectic mechanics.  It is the recognition of a
structural premise: the geometry is binary at its root.  Its primitive
antisymmetric object is a 2-form, and its canonical conjugacy is organized
by pairs.

The simplicial program exposes where this binary picture comes from.

## 2. The \(N=4\) Exception and the Axis-to-2-Form Miracle

Let

$$
H_N=\{x\in {\mathbb R}^N:\sum_i x_i=0\}
$$

be the zero-sum hyperplane of an \(N\)-axis simplicial frame.  Then
\(\dim H_N=N-1\).

In the \(N=4\) row, \(\dim H_4=3\).  An oriented metric 3-space has the
exceptional Hodge identification

$$
H_4 \cong \Lambda^2 H_4^*,
\qquad
u\mapsto \iota_u\Omega,
$$

where \(\Omega\) is the volume form.  Thus a single axis vector can be read
as an antisymmetric bilinear form.  This is the geometric reason the
three-dimensional cross product exists.

In simplicial coordinates, the cross-product operator \(K(u)\) satisfies

$$
K(u)^3=-\|u\|^2K(u).
$$

For a unit axis \(u\), its restriction to the plane \(u^\perp\) satisfies

$$
K(u)^2=-I_{u^\perp}.
$$

Thus \(K(u)|_{u^\perp}\) is exactly a complex structure.  The classical
quarter-turn \(J\) is not an independent primitive here.  It is the
restriction of the simplicial cross-product operator in the unique row where
a vector axis can generate a 2-form.

For \(N\ge 5\), the dimension \(d=N-1\) is at least \(4\).  The Hodge dual of
a vector is no longer a 2-form:

$$
u
\longmapsto
\iota_u\Omega
\in \Lambda^{d-1}H_N^*
=
\Lambda^{N-2}H_N^*.
$$

Meanwhile a rotation blade is a bivector, whose Hodge dual has degree

$$
d-2=N-3.
$$

So the clean three-dimensional triangle

$$
\text{axis vector}
\leftrightarrow
\text{2-form}
\leftrightarrow
\text{skew operator}
$$

does not survive.  The single-axis 2-form is an \(N=4\) anomaly.

The mechanics should change accordingly.

**Remark 2.1 (The \(N=4\) exception as a prime-2 / depth-2 row of a
general phenomenon).**  The \(N=4\) Hodge identification
\(H_4\cong\Lambda^2 H_4^*\) and the resulting simplicial cross-product
algebra \(K(u)^3=-\|u\|^2 K(u)\) are not sui generis facts about the
tetrahedron.  In the two-axis descent of Paper 10 Section 12 and Paper
11 §9.6, the simplicial frame on \(R\) with \(|R|=4\) carries a Prime
Spectral Ladder of length 2 over the single prime 2, and a Nested
Zero-Sum Balance-Transfer Ladder of length 3 on \(H_4\).  The single
*additional* coincidence at \(N=4\) is that the Hodge degree \(d-1\)
equals 2, which lets a single axis vector double as a 2-form.  The
imaginary quaternions emerge as the Cayley-Dickson algebra at prime
2 and depth 2; the simplicial cross product is the realization of that
algebra on \(H_4\) supplied by the Hodge identification.

For \(N\ne 4\) (equivalently, \(|R|\ne 4\) in the relevant ring), the
Hodge degree no longer matches, and the analogue at prime \(p\) and
depth \(k\) is not generally a division algebra.  The Cayley-Dickson
tower \({\mathbb R}\to{\mathbb C}\to{\mathbb H}\to{\mathbb O}\) is the
prime-2 family of a more general two-axis classification: each
\((p,k)\) row of the joint PSL\(\times\)NZSBTL refinement supplies its
own finite algebra of axis-to-form operators, with closure laws that
are typically not binary.  Paper 11 §9.6 Corollary 9.6.3 gives the
prime-spectral pacing: the binary floor of the operator algebra closes
within a single conductor packet; lifting to higher arities is forced
exactly at PSL boundaries.

Mechanics at \(N\ne 4\) should therefore not pretend to be quaternionic.
The Nambu-Fisher bracket of Section 4 and the simplectic Hamilton
equations of Section 5 are the prime-spectral-aware replacements, with
\(K^{CH}\) the Fisher-dual response operator and
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) the carry at PSL boundaries.

## 3. The Categorical Simplex as Phase Space

Let \(R\) be a finite outcome algebra with \(Q=|R|\).  The categorical
simplex is

$$
\Delta_R^\circ
=
\{p\in{\mathbb R}_{>0}^R:\sum_{a\in R}p_a=1\}.
$$

Its tangent space at every point is the zero-sum hyperplane

$$
T_p\Delta_R^\circ=H_R
=
\{u\in{\mathbb R}^R:\sum_{a\in R}u_a=0\}.
$$

The maximum-entropy point is

$$
p_*=Q^{-1}{\bf 1}.
$$

In \(m\)-coordinates, a displacement is

$$
h=p-p_*.
$$

In redundant \(e\)-coordinates, one may write

$$
p_a(\theta)
=
\frac{\exp\theta_a}{\sum_{b\in R}\exp\theta_b},
\qquad
\theta\in {\mathbb R}^R/{\mathbb R}{\bf 1}.
$$

The Fisher-Rao metric in \(m\)-coordinates is

$$
g_p^F(u,v)
=
\sum_{a\in R}\frac{u_av_a}{p_a},
\qquad
u,v\in H_R.
$$

At the centroid this becomes, up to the global scale \(Q\),

$$
g_{p_*}^F(u,v)
=
Q\sum_{a\in R}u_av_a.
$$

This is the bridge to the regular simplex frame and the conductor-block
decomposition of the previous papers.

**Definition 3.1 (Fisher volume form).**  The Fisher volume form
\(\Omega_R^F\) is the Riemannian volume form determined by \(g^F\) and the
chosen orientation of \(H_R\).

**Definition 3.2 (Simplectic form).**  The simplectic form on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\) is the Fisher volume form

$$
\Omega_R^F\in\Omega^{Q-1}(\Delta_R^\circ),
$$

read as the primitive mechanical form of the simplex.  It replaces the
symplectic 2-form \(\omega\), but it does not imitate its degree.

Since \(\Delta_R^\circ\) has dimension \(Q-1\), every top-degree form on it
is closed.  Hence

$$
d\Omega_R^F=0.
$$

It also has the correct nondegeneracy property for Nambu mechanics.  At each
point \(p\), contraction with \(\Omega_R^F\) gives an isomorphism

$$
T_p\Delta_R^\circ
\longrightarrow
\Lambda^{Q-2}T_p^*\Delta_R^\circ,
\qquad
X\longmapsto \iota_X\Omega_R^F.
$$

Thus a vector field is equivalent to a \((Q-2)\)-form.  This is the
non-binary analogue of the symplectic identification
\(TM\to T^*M\).

**Proposition 3.3 (Closed and Nambu-nondegenerate).**  The simplectic form
\(\Omega_R^F\) is closed and Nambu-nondegenerate: if
\(\iota_X\Omega_R^F=0\), then \(X=0\).

**Proof.**  Closedness follows because \(\Omega_R^F\) is top-degree.  For
nondegeneracy, choose any oriented local frame
\((e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1})\) with \(\Omega_R^F(e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1})\ne0\).  If
\(X\ne0\), extend \(X\) to a basis.  Evaluating \(\iota_X\Omega_R^F\) on the
remaining basis vectors gives the nonzero volume of that basis, so
\(\iota_X\Omega_R^F\ne0\).  \(\square\)

The simplex therefore does not need an artificial cotangent doubling in
order to carry mechanics.  It needs a volume form and a choice of conserved
macroscopic coordinates.

## 4. Nambu-Fisher Brackets

Nambu's replacement for the binary Poisson bracket is an \(n\)-ary bracket on
an \(n\)-dimensional phase space.  On the categorical simplex, \(n=Q-1\).

**Definition 4.1 (Nambu-Fisher bracket).**  For smooth observables

$$
f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\in C^\infty(\Delta_R^\circ),
$$

define

$$
\{f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}_F
\Omega_R^F
=
df_0\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{Q-2}.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\{f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}_F
=
\frac{df_0\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{Q-2}}{\Omega_R^F}.
$$

This bracket is alternating and satisfies a Leibniz rule in each slot.  With
the volume-form construction it is the canonical Nambu bracket associated to
\(\Omega_R^F\).

**Definition 4.2 (Simplectic Hamiltonian data).**  A simplectic Hamiltonian
system on \(\Delta_R^\circ\) is a \((Q-2)\)-tuple

$$
\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})
$$

of conserved macrovariables.  In the arithmetic applications, these are
chosen from conductor-layer energies, quotient sufficient statistics, or
finite character packet coordinates.

The number \(Q-2\) is forced by degree.  Since
\(\iota_X\Omega_R^F\) is a \((Q-2)\)-form, the differential data generating
the flow must also have degree \(Q-2\).  A single scalar Hamiltonian is
enough only in the two-dimensional case \(Q=3\).

**Definition 4.3 (Simplectic Hamiltonian vector field).**  The vector field
\(X_{\mathbf C}\) generated by \(\mathbf C\) is the unique vector field
satisfying

$$
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

For every observable \(f\),

$$
X_{\mathbf C}[f]
=
\{f,C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\}_F.
$$

This is the first simplectic Hamilton equation.

**Proposition 4.4 (Liouville-Nambu preservation).**  The vector field
\(X_{\mathbf C}\) preserves the Fisher volume form and the macrovariables
\(C_i\):

$$
{\mathcal L}_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F=0,
\qquad
X_{\mathbf C}[C_i]=0.
$$

**Proof.**  Cartan's formula gives

$$
{\mathcal L}_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
d(\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F)
+\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}d\Omega_R^F.
$$

The second term is zero because \(\Omega_R^F\) is top-degree.  The first term
is

$$
d(dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2})=0.
$$

Also,

$$
X_{\mathbf C}[C_i]\Omega_R^F
=
dC_i\wedge dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}=0
$$

because one factor repeats.  \(\square\)

This is the exact analogue of Liouville's theorem, but the preserved object
is the Fisher volume rather than a symplectic 2-form.

**Remark 4.5 (Exact simplectic currents).**  The Nambu-Fisher equation uses
the exact decomposable current

$$
P_{\mathbf C}
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

This is the cleanest analogue of Hamiltonian mechanics because the conserved
macrovariables are visible in the formula.  It is not the most general
Fisher-volume-preserving deterministic current.  The condition

$$
{\mathcal L}_X\Omega_R^F=0
$$

is equivalent to

$$
d(\iota_X\Omega_R^F)=0.
$$

Thus general volume-preserving vector fields correspond to closed
\((Q-2)\)-forms.  The Nambu-Fisher systems of this paper are the exact
decomposable subclass where that closed form is written as a wedge of
macrovariable differentials.  This distinction will matter when the paper
passes from named macro-Hamiltonians to the full quotient-character current
algebra.

**Remark 4.6 (Equilibrium internalization).**  The update-projection
experiments of Paper 12 give the first learned analogue of this current
picture.  In the month calendar transformer, the stochastic gradient remains
broad across conductor packets, but the applied output-head update is
projected into a closed support.  Over training, the unprojected logits move
toward the same closed support: in the winning offset-OOD blanket
\(\{6,12\}\), final logit leakage is \(1.369\times10^{-31}\) even though
the epoch-300 raw output-head gradient retains visible mass in the excluded
conductors \(2,3,4\).

This is not yet a theorem about all conductor-closed learning systems.  It is
an empirical equilibrium phenomenon: a projected update current can teach the
model to predict its own conductor blanket.  In the simplectic language, the
learned trajectory is being pulled toward a closed conductor leaf of the
Fisher-volume phase space, even though the incoming stochastic forcing is not
itself closed.

## 5. Simplectic Hamilton Equations

Let \(x^1,\ldots,x^{Q-1}\) be local coordinates on the simplex and write

$$
\Omega_R^F
=
\rho_F(x)\,dx^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{Q-1}.
$$

Then the vector field \(X_{\mathbf C}\) has coordinate expression

$$
\dot x^i
=
X_{\mathbf C}^i
=
\rho_F(x)^{-1}
\varepsilon^{i i_1\cdots i_{Q-2}}
\partial_{i_1}C_1\cdots \partial_{i_{Q-2}}C_{Q-2}.
$$

For the probability coordinates \(p_a\), this becomes

$$
\dot p_a
=
\{p_a,C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\}_F,
\qquad
\sum_{a\in R}\dot p_a=0.
$$

These are the simplectic Hamilton equations in \(m\)-coordinates.

The \(e\)-coordinate equation is obtained by the Fisher Legendre transform.
In redundant logits,

$$
\theta_a=\log p_a+\text{gauge constant}.
$$

Thus, after projecting away the gauge direction,

$$
\dot\theta_a
=
\frac{\dot p_a}{p_a}
-\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{b\in R}\frac{\dot p_b}{p_b}.
$$

Equivalently, as a covector on \(H_R\),

$$
\dot\theta
=
(g_p^F)^\flat \dot p.
$$

This is the second simplectic Hamilton equation:

$$
\boxed{
\dot p=X_{\mathbf C}(p),
\qquad
\dot\theta=(g_p^F)^\flat X_{\mathbf C}(p).
}
$$

Unlike the classical pair

$$
\dot q=\partial H/\partial p,
\qquad
\dot p=-\partial H/\partial q,
$$

these equations do not split the state into two independent halves.  The
simplex has one state \(p\), two dual affine charts \((m,e)\), and a
volume-generated vector field tangent to the macrovariable leaf.

This is the point at which the word "momentum" must be handled carefully.

## 6. Generalized Momentum

In a cotangent-bundle mechanics, momentum is an independent covector paired
with position.  On the categorical simplex, there is no need to double the
dimension.  The \(e\)- and \(m\)-coordinates are already Legendre dual.

At the centroid, the linearization is simple.  Since

$$
g_{p_*}^F=Q\,I_H,
$$

one has

$$
\theta
=
Qh+O(h^2),
\qquad
h=p-p_*.
$$

Thus if \(\theta\) is called generalized position, then the centered
\(m\)-coordinate \(h\) is the correct first-order generalized momentum, up
to the fixed Fisher scale.  This answers the centroid version of the
question, but only the centroid version.

But the non-binary mechanics asks for more than the centroid linearization.
The actual conjugate object to the flow is not a vector \(h\).  It is the
\((Q-2)\)-form obtained by contracting the vector field into the Fisher
volume.

**Definition 6.1 (Centroid momentum vector).**  The centroid momentum vector
associated to \(p\) is

$$
h=p-p_*.
$$

It is a valid momentum coordinate only to first order at \(p_*\), where the
Fisher Legendre map identifies

$$
h
\longleftrightarrow
Q^{-1}\theta
$$

after quotienting the gauge direction.

**Definition 6.2 (Simplectic momentum form).**  The momentum form of the
simplectic Hamiltonian data \(\mathbf C\) is

$$
P_{\mathbf C}
=
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

Thus \(P_{\mathbf C}\in\Omega^{Q-2}(\Delta_R^\circ)\).

This is the true generalized momentum.  It is not an additional coordinate
paired with \(p\); it is the Fisher-dual form of the motion through the
simplex.

This is the direct descendant of the \(N=4\) Hodge observation.  In a
\((Q-1)\)-dimensional phase simplex, a velocity vector is equivalent, through
the volume form, to a \((Q-2)\)-form:

$$
X
\longleftrightarrow
\iota_X\Omega_R^F.
$$

When \(Q=4\), the simplex dimension is \(3\), so this momentum form has
degree \(2\).  This is the old 2-form world.  When \(Q\ge 5\), the momentum
form has degree at least \(3\).  The binary picture is no longer native.

**Conclusion 6.3 (Momentum answer).**  The centered \(m\)-coordinate
\(h=p-p_*\) should be treated as the linearized momentum at the centroid.
The intrinsic non-binary momentum is the form
\(P_{\mathbf C}=\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F\).  Away from the centroid,
the difference between these two readings is not a coordinate nuisance.  It
is the mechanical content measured by the Amari-Chentsov tensor.

## 7. The Amari-Chentsov Tensor as Momentum Gap

The categorical simplex has two flat affine structures.  In information
geometry they are the \(m\)-flat and \(e\)-flat structures.  Their difference
is measured by the Amari-Chentsov cubic tensor.

With the sign convention of Paper 11,

$$
K^{CH}=\Gamma^{(m)}-\Gamma^{(e)}
$$

and

$$
g_p^F(K_p^{CH}(u)v,w)=T_p(u,v,w),
$$

where \(T\) is the Amari-Chentsov cubic.

For the categorical simplex in \(m\)-coordinates,

$$
T_p(u,v,w)
=
\sum_{a\in R}\frac{u_av_aw_a}{p_a^2}
$$

up to the global normalization convention used for the Fisher metric.  Thus
\(K^{CH}\) is the raised third-order correction to the Fisher Legendre
correspondence.

At \(p_*\), the tensor has the convolution form already used in Papers 10
and 11.  For an \(m\)-flat displacement \(h\),

$$
K^{CH}_{p_*}(h)v
=
Q\,h*v
-Q\langle h,v\rangle p_*
$$

in the cyclic prime model, with the corresponding quotient-character version
for general \(R\).  More generally, along

$$
p(t)=p_*+th,
$$

the Fisher metric has line coefficients

$$
\bar g^F_{p(t)}(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
\sum_{n\ge 0}(-t)^n
\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

The quotient \(\psi\chi^{-1}\) is the finite frequency shift.  Its conductor
packet determines which macrovariables couple at each order.

This gives the mechanical interpretation:

> \(K^{CH}\) is the tensor of momentum mismatch between the two affine
> readings of the same categorical state.  In character coordinates, that
> mismatch is arithmetic convolution.

If \(h\) is the centroid momentum, \(K^{CH}(h)\) is the first nonlinear
correction to the momentum transport.  If \(P_{\mathbf C}\) is the
non-binary momentum form, \(K^{CH}\) tells how the Fisher-dual lift of that
form leaks across conductor packets off the centroid.

This is the simplectic replacement for force.  In the binary cotangent
picture, force changes a momentum covector.  In the categorical simplex,
the force-like datum is the compulsory transfer of Fisher-dual momentum
between finite frequency packets.  The transfer coefficients are not chosen
by a continuum potential; they are indexed by the quotient-character
convolution algebra.

## 8. Conductor Hamiltonians

The previous section described simplectic mechanics on any categorical
simplex.  The arithmetic content appears when the outcome set is a finite
quotient algebra \(R\).

At \(p_*\), additive characters diagonalize the translation action on
\(H_R\).  Grouping those character lines by conductor gives the Fisher
orthogonal decomposition

$$
H_R
=
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak f} H_{\mathfrak f}.
$$

A conductor macrovariable is any smooth observable whose differential lies in
a chosen conductor packet or a controlled sum of packets.  Typical examples
are packet energies

$$
E_{\mathfrak f}(p)
=
\frac12\|\Pi_{\mathfrak f}(p-p_*)\|_{F,*}^2
$$

or quotient sufficient statistics associated to a finite quotient
\(R\to R/I\).

**Definition 8.1 (Conductor simplectic system).**  A conductor simplectic
system is a simplectic Hamiltonian system whose macro-Hamiltonians

$$
C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2}
$$

are chosen from conductor packet coordinates, packet energies, quotient
sufficient statistics, or their finite admissible combinations.

The resulting vector field

$$
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}
$$

preserves the common level set of those macrovariables.  At the centroid,
this is a clean conductor leaf.  Off the centroid, the Fisher metric itself
couples conductor packets by the adjacency algebra \(\mathcal A_R\).

Thus "conservation of conductor macrovariables" and "closure of conductor
packets under Fisher transport" are different statements.

The first is true by the Nambu-Fisher equation.  The second is exactly the
closure problem measured by Papers 10 and 11.

### 8.1 Exact Finite-Sufficient Hamiltonian Module

The phrase "chosen from conductor coordinates" can now be replaced, at fixed
finite order, by an exact module statement.  Fix a finite adjacency/Fisher-jet
order \(k\).  Let

$$
\Phi_{R,k}:\Delta_R^\circ\longrightarrow Y_{R,k}
$$

be the finite observation map whose coordinates are the fiber-probability
coordinates induced by quotient maps \(R\to R/\mathfrak d\), the primitive
packet energies \(E_{\mathfrak d}\), any declared packetwise
Fisher-orthogonal flag coordinates, and the quotient-character adjacency
orbit coordinates and Fisher-shadow coordinates retained through order \(k\).
The coordinate list is finite once \(R\), the packet system, and the
order-\(k\) adjacency atlas are fixed.

Define

$$
{\mathcal N}_{R,k}
=
\Phi_{R,k}^*C^\infty(Y_{R,k})
$$

and

$$
\Omega^1_{R,k,\mathrm{nat}}
=
\Phi_{R,k}^*\Omega^1(Y_{R,k}).
$$

Equivalently, if \(y_1,\ldots,y_m\) are the coordinates of \(\Phi_{R,k}\),
then \(\Omega^1_{R,k,\mathrm{nat}}\) is the finite-rank
\({\mathcal N}_{R,k}\)-module generated by
\(d(\Phi_{R,k}^*y_1),\ldots,d(\Phi_{R,k}^*y_m)\).

**Theorem 8.2 (FC1-B1 exact finite-sufficient differential module).**  If
\(C=F\circ\Phi_{R,k}\in{\mathcal N}_{R,k}\), then

$$
dC=\Phi_{R,k}^*(dF)\in\Omega^1_{R,k,\mathrm{nat}}.
$$

Hence for simplectic Hamiltonian data
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\) with each
\(C_i\in{\mathcal N}_{R,k}\),

$$
P_{\mathbf C}
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}
\in
\bigwedge^{Q-2}\Omega^1_{R,k,\mathrm{nat}}.
$$

Conversely, a one-form in \(\Omega^1_{R,k,\mathrm{nat}}\) is locally the
differential of a finite-sufficient Hamiltonian exactly when the corresponding
one-form on the finite observation image is closed; on simply connected
coordinate patches this is equivalent to local exactness.

**Proof.**  The first claim is the chain rule:
\(d(F\circ\Phi_{R,k})=\Phi_{R,k}^*(dF)\).  Written in coordinates on
\(Y_{R,k}\), this is a finite \({\mathcal N}_{R,k}\)-linear combination of
the coordinate differentials above.  Wedges of \(Q-2\) such differentials lie
in the exterior power of the same finite module, proving the current claim.
For the converse, write a module one-form as
\(\alpha=\Phi_{R,k}^*\bar\alpha\) on a coordinate patch of the observation
image.  It is a Hamiltonian differential precisely when
\(\bar\alpha=dF\); the local closed/exact equivalence is the Poincare lemma
on the coordinate patch.  \(\square\)

This theorem concerns exact decomposable Nambu-Fisher currents generated by
finite-sufficient Hamiltonian data.  It does not classify every
Fisher-volume-preserving current; the larger closed-current residual is the
completion treated in Section 14.

For Noether purposes, fix in addition a polynomial jet order \(r\) and a
finite support or coefficient skeleton for tuples in
\((J^r{\mathcal N}_{R,k})^{Q-2}\).  The normalizer action on the finite
coordinate list of \(Y_{R,k}\) turns the atlas into an ordinary finite
orbit/stabilizer computation: tuple orbits, stabilizers, determinant

## 9. Measure-Preserving Currents and de Rham Rotationnel

The closest external analogue to this paper's mechanics is the intrinsic
description of measure-preserving diffusions developed by Barp, Takao,
Betancourt, Arnaudon, and Girolami.  Their key move is to treat a smooth
positive target measure \(P\) as the primitive geometric object.  The
measure itself supplies musical isomorphisms between multivector fields and
twisted forms, and hence a canonical derivative, the \(P\)-rotationnel

$$
\operatorname{curl}_P
=
P^\sharp\circ d\circ P^\flat.
$$

In that framework, measure-preserving currents are generated by potentials,
with possible cohomological residuals.  The point for Paper 13 is not the
stochastic diffusion layer, but the deterministic current layer underneath
it.

For the categorical simplex, take \(P_F\) to be the smooth positive measure
represented by the Fisher volume form,

$$
P_F\equiv \Omega_R^F.
$$

Then a Fisher-volume-preserving vector field \(X\) is equivalently a closed
current form

$$
J_X=\iota_X\Omega_R^F,
\qquad
dJ_X=0.
$$

Locally, one may write

$$
J_X=dA
$$

for a \((Q-3)\)-form potential \(A\).  The Nambu-Fisher Hamiltonian tuple is
the special decomposable case

$$
J_X=dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

This gives Paper 13 an important refinement:

> Simplectic Hamiltonian systems are exact decomposable Fisher-current
> systems.  The full simplectic current algebra should allow arbitrary closed
> Fisher currents, represented locally by potentials and globally by
> residual classes.

For the open simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ\), the smooth de Rham topology is
trivial: it is diffeomorphic to an open ball.  Therefore the new residual
that matters in this program is not ordinary smooth cohomology.  It is the
finite arithmetic residual that remains after imposing the quotient-character
and conductor filtrations.  That residual is precisely the role assigned to
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).

This external comparison improves the claim.  Paper 13 should not say that
the Nambu tuple exhausts all measure-preserving motion.  It should say that
the Nambu tuple is the Hamiltonian, macrovariable-visible face of a larger
Fisher-current calculus, and that the quotient-character adjacency algebra is
the finite arithmetic skeleton of that calculus.

## 10. Simplectic Hamilton Equations in Quotient-Character Coordinates

The algebraic primitive is the filtered local multiplication law together
with its quotient-character cocycle reducer.  The smooth simplex supplies the
volume form; the finite algebra supplies the exact channels in which the
Fisher-dual response can move.

Let

$$
\widehat R^\times=\widehat R\setminus\{1\}
$$

be the nontrivial additive characters, and let \(e_\chi\) be the
corresponding tangent character line.  For a conductor packet
\(X_{\mathfrak f}\subset \widehat R^\times\), write
\(\Pi_{\mathfrak f}\) for the packet projector.  Paper 11 defines the
partial quotient-shift operators

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}e_\chi
=
\begin{cases}
e_{\eta\chi},&
\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e},\ \eta\chi\in X_{\mathfrak f},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

These operators generate the quotient-character adjacency algebra
\(\mathcal A_R\).  Along an \(m\)-flat ray

$$
p(t)=p_*+th,
\qquad
\sum_a h_a=0,
$$

the normalized Fisher metric \(\bar g^F=Q^{-1}g^F\) has the exact character
expansion

$$
\bar g^F_{p(t)}(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
\sum_{n\ge0}(-t)^n\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

Compressing this coefficient from conductor \(\mathfrak e\) to conductor
\(\mathfrak f\) gives

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)
=
\sum_\eta
\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}.
$$

Thus the off-centroid Fisher tensor is not merely estimated by
\(\mathcal A_R\).  Its Taylor jets are elements of \(\mathcal A_R\).

**Definition 10.1 (Algebraic simplectic atlas).**  An algebraic simplectic
atlas for \(R\) consists of:

1. the additive-character tangent frame \(\{e_\chi\}\);
2. the conductor packets \(X_{\mathfrak f}\);
3. the partial quotient-shifts \(A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}\);
4. the coefficient operators \(C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)\);
5. in local mixed rows, a filtered section and its carry cocycle
   \(\kappa_s\) representing \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\).

All simplectic equations below are written in this atlas.  The only smooth
input is the Fisher volume density obtained from the Fisher matrix.

**Theorem 10.2 (Quotient-character Hamilton equations).**  Fix an oriented
real character-coordinate chart \(z^1,\ldots,z^{Q-1}\) on \(H_R\), and write

$$
\Omega_R^F(p(t))
=
\rho_F(t)\,dz^1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz^{Q-1}.
$$

Let \(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\) be a simplectic Hamiltonian tuple.
Then the Nambu current, vector field, and Fisher-dual one-form are:

$$
P_{\mathbf C}(t)
=
dC_1(t)\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}(t),
$$

$$
X_{\mathbf C}^a(t)
=
\rho_F(t)^{-1}

## 11. \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) as the Finite Carry Term

### 14.1 Continuum and emergence problem

At a fixed finite quotient algebra \(R\), the theory is exact.  The problem is
to organize families of such finite systems so that their Fisher-conductor
data converge to a continuum geometry, and to decide whether the arithmetic
carry part disappears, survives, or obstructs that convergence.

**Definition 14.1 (Simplectic approximation tower).**  A simplectic
approximation tower consists of:

1. a directed family of finite outcome algebras \(R_\lambda\), with
   \(Q_\lambda=|R_\lambda|\to\infty\), increasing conductor depth, increasing
   residue degree, or both;
2. comparison maps between the simplices \(\Delta_{R_\lambda}^\circ\), or
   sampling maps from a limiting space \(M\) into the finite outcome sets;
3. scaling constants for the Fisher metric, Fisher volume, and conductor
   weights;
4. a compatible family of adjacency algebras
   \(\mathcal A_{R_\lambda}\), coefficient operators
   \(C_{n,\lambda}^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)\), and local carry classes
   \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\lambda,\mathfrak p})\).

Such a tower has a continuum simplectic limit if, after the chosen scaling:

1. the Fisher metrics and volumes converge to a smooth limiting metric or
   measure class;
2. the finite Nambu currents converge as forms or currents;
3. the adjacency-operator Taylor jets converge to covariant derivative,
   curvature, or higher tensor data on the limit;
4. the conductor filtration converges to a meaningful spectral, ramification,
   or frequency filtration;
5. each scaled \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) contribution has a controlled
   limit: zero, finite residual, or divergent obstruction.

This definition deliberately leaves the analytic topology open.  Depending
on the tower, the correct convergence mode may be finite-dimensional
distribution convergence, operator-norm convergence on low conductor bands,
Mosco convergence of quadratic forms, weak convergence of currents, or a
Gromov-Hausdorff-type metric limit.

**Theorem 14.2 (Large-\(Q\) categorical Fisher convergence).**  Let
\((M,\mathcal B,\mu)\) be a probability space, and let
\(\{\mathcal P_\lambda\}\) be a directed refining family of finite
partitions whose generated sigma-algebras increase to \(\mathcal B\).
Let \({\mathbb E}_\lambda\) denote conditional expectation onto
\(\mathcal P_\lambda\).

Fix a positive density \(\rho\in L^\infty(M,\mu)\) with
\(\rho\ge c>0\) and \(\int_M\rho\,d\mu=1\).  For tangent densities
\(\xi,\zeta,h\in L^\infty(M,\mu)\) with integral zero, define the finite
categorical state and tangent vectors

$$
p_{\lambda,A}=\int_A\rho\,d\mu,
\qquad
u_{\lambda,A}=\int_A\xi\,d\mu,
\qquad
v_{\lambda,A}=\int_A\zeta\,d\mu
\qquad
(A\in\mathcal P_\lambda).
$$

Then the finite Fisher metrics converge to the continuum Fisher metric:

$$
g^F_{p_\lambda}(u_\lambda,v_\lambda)
=
\sum_{A\in\mathcal P_\lambda}
\frac{u_{\lambda,A}v_{\lambda,A}}{p_{\lambda,A}}
=
\int_M
\frac{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi)({\mathbb E}_\lambda\zeta)}
{{\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho}\,d\mu
\longrightarrow
\int_M\frac{\xi\zeta}{\rho}\,d\mu.
$$

The Fisher Legendre covectors converge as well:

$$
\frac{{\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi}{{\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho}
\longrightarrow
\frac{\xi}{\rho}
\quad\text{in }L^2(\rho\,d\mu).
$$

For any fixed finite list of tangent densities
\(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_r\), the cylindrical Fisher volume Gram determinant
converges:

$$
\det\big(g^F_{p_\lambda}(u_{\lambda,i},u_{\lambda,j})\big)_{i,j=1}^r
\longrightarrow
\det\left(\int_M\frac{\xi_i\xi_j}{\rho}\,d\mu\right)_{i,j=1}^r.
$$

Finally, along the continuum \(m\)-flat line
\(\rho_t=\rho+th\), for \(|t|\|h\|_\infty<c\), the
order-\(n\) Fisher Taylor coefficient converges:

$$
T_{\lambda,n}(h;\xi,\zeta)
:=
\int_M
\frac{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi)({\mathbb E}_\lambda\zeta)
({\mathbb E}_\lambda h)^n}
{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho)^{n+1}}\,d\mu
\longrightarrow
\int_M
\frac{\xi\zeta h^n}{\rho^{n+1}}\,d\mu.
$$

Thus the large-\(Q\) categorical tower is a genuine continuum simplectic
approximation tower for Fisher geometry.  In this non-arithmetic partition
tower the local carry term is absent:
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}=0\).  Arithmetic \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
corrections can only appear after adding a local filtered multiplication law
to the tower.

**Proof.**  On a cell \(A\in\mathcal P_\lambda\), write
\(\mu(A){\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho=\int_A\rho\,d\mu\), and similarly for
\(\xi,\zeta,h\).  Then

$$
\sum_A\frac{u_{\lambda,A}v_{\lambda,A}}{p_{\lambda,A}}
=
\sum_A
\mu(A)
\frac{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi)_A({\mathbb E}_\lambda\zeta)_A}
{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho)_A},
$$

which is the displayed integral.  Since \(\rho\ge c>0\),
\({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho\ge c\).  The martingale convergence theorem gives
\({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho\to\rho\),
\({\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi\to\xi\), and
\({\mathbb E}_\lambda\zeta\to\zeta\) in \(L^2\) and almost everywhere.
The uniform lower bound on the denominator and the \(L^\infty\) bounds give
dominated convergence, proving metric convergence.

The Legendre covector statement follows from the same convergence applied to
\(({\mathbb E}_\lambda\xi)/({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho)\), now in
\(L^2(\rho\,d\mu)\), using \(\rho\in L^\infty\) and \(\rho\ge c\).  The Gram
determinant statement follows because each matrix entry converges and the
determinant is a polynomial in the entries.

For the Taylor coefficients, choose \(|t|\|h\|_\infty<c\).  Then
all denominators \({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho+t{\mathbb E}_\lambda h\) are
uniformly bounded away from zero, and the geometric expansion

$$
\frac{1}{{\mathbb E}_\lambda(\rho+th)}
=
\sum_{n\ge0}
(-t)^n
\frac{({\mathbb E}_\lambda h)^n}
{({\mathbb E}_\lambda\rho)^{n+1}}
$$

and applying dominated convergence to each coefficient gives the displayed
limit. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.3 (Entropy-aware discretization error).**  Under the
assumptions of Theorem 14.2, if the partition tower is chosen so that
\({\mathbb E}_\lambda f\to f\) with a known rate in the relevant \(L^2\) or
\(L^\infty\) norm for

$$
f\in\{\rho,\xi,\zeta,h\},
$$

then the Fisher metric, Legendre covector, and order-\(n\) Taylor coefficient
errors are bounded by the corresponding conditional-expectation errors.  In
particular, entropy-aware discretization means choosing partitions for which
the conditional expectations of \(\rho\) and the active tangent directions
converge rapidly in the Fisher-weighted norms.

This corollary is the first continuum theorem promised by Problem 13.6: it
solves the large-\(Q\) categorical tower.  The next theorem is the first
arithmetic reduction.  It does not compute every prime-depth or
residue-degree tower.  It proves that, once the finite carry atoms of such a
tower are normalized as a signed measure, the \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
contribution has only three possible continuum behaviors: disappearance,
finite residual, or obstruction.

**Definition 14.3A (Normalized arithmetic carry measure).**  Let
\(\{R_\lambda\}\) be an arithmetic simplectic approximation tower and fix an
Ext-sensitive momentum, Fisher-dual, or curvature-response coefficient
\(E_\lambda\).  Choose filtered sections \(s_\lambda\) and write the
carry part of \(E_\lambda\) as a finite sum over residual carry atoms
\(\mathfrak C_\lambda(E)\):

$$
E_\lambda^{\operatorname{carry}}(\Phi_\lambda)
=
\sum_{c\in\mathfrak C_\lambda(E)}
\Phi_\lambda(c)\,\mathbf k_\lambda(c).
$$

Here \(\mathbf k_\lambda(c)\) is the atom value in the finite-dimensional
coefficient space of the chosen block, after quotienting section coboundaries
if one is working in the residual \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) class.  The
function \(\Phi_\lambda\) is the bounded coefficient profile supplied by the
normalized Fisher, adjacency, volume, or conductor weights of the tower.

A continuum carry profile consists of a compact profile space \(Z\), profile
maps

$$
\pi_\lambda:\mathfrak C_\lambda(E)\to Z,
$$

and scaling constants \(a_\lambda>0\).  The normalized arithmetic carry
measure is the vector-valued atomic measure

$$
\nu_\lambda^E
=
a_\lambda
\sum_{c\in\mathfrak C_\lambda(E)}
\mathbf k_\lambda(c)\,\delta_{\pi_\lambda(c)}.
$$

The scaled Ext contribution tested against a limiting profile
\(\Phi\in C(Z)\) is

$$
\langle \Phi,\nu_\lambda^E\rangle
=
a_\lambda
\sum_{c\in\mathfrak C_\lambda(E)}
\Phi(\pi_\lambda(c))\,\mathbf k_\lambda(c).
$$

**Theorem 14.3B (Arithmetic carry-scaling trichotomy).**  In the setting of
Definition 14.3A, assume the normalized non-carry Fisher data of the tower
converge as in Theorem 14.2 on the tested finite band, and assume the
coefficient profiles are uniformly bounded.  Then the scaled
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) contribution to \(E_\lambda\) is completely
controlled by the vector measures \(\nu_\lambda^E\):

1. If \(\|\nu_\lambda^E\|_{\operatorname{TV}}\to0\), then every bounded
   Ext-sensitive test coefficient has vanishing arithmetic carry in the
   limit.
2. If \(\nu_\lambda^E\) converges weakly as a vector measure to a finite
   measure \(\nu^E\), then

   $$
   a_\lambda E_\lambda^{\operatorname{carry}}(\Phi\circ\pi_\lambda)
   \longrightarrow
   \int_Z\Phi\,d\nu^E.
   $$

   The limit is a finite arithmetic correction to the continuum Fisher or
   curvature coefficient.  In the residual current quotient it depends only
   on the limiting \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) class.
3. If \(\|\nu_\lambda^E\|_{\operatorname{TV}}\to\infty\) and there is a
   bounded profile \(\Phi\) whose pairing captures a fixed positive
   proportion of this total variation, then the chosen scaling has no finite
   continuum carry coefficient.  The tower either needs stronger
   renormalization or carries an arithmetic obstruction at that band.

Thus prime-power depth towers, residue-degree towers, and mixed towers are
reduced to a concrete asymptotic problem: compute the weak limits and total
variation growth of their normalized residual carry measures.

**Proof.**  The carry contribution is, by construction, the pairing of the
bounded test profile with the atomic vector measure \(\nu_\lambda^E\).  The
first claim follows from

$$
\left\|\langle \Phi,\nu_\lambda^E\rangle\right\|
\le
\|\Phi\|_\infty\|\nu_\lambda^E\|_{\operatorname{TV}}.
$$

For the second claim, weak convergence of finite vector measures gives
convergence against every continuous profile \(\Phi\in C(Z)\).  If the
actual finite coefficient profile \(\Phi_\lambda\) differs from
\(\Phi\circ\pi_\lambda\) by \(o(1)\) in sup norm on the tested atom set, the
same conclusion follows from the total-variation bound.  A change of
filtered section replaces the carry cocycle by
\(\kappa_{s_\lambda}+\delta b_\lambda\).  Passing to the residual
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) quotient kills the coboundary term; without
passing to the quotient, the displayed limit changes only by the weak limit
of the corresponding coboundary measure.  Hence the residual finite
correction is section-independent.

The third claim is the contrapositive of finite weak boundedness on the
tested profile: if a bounded profile pairs with a fixed positive fraction of
the total variation and that total variation diverges, the coefficient cannot
converge to a finite vector at the chosen scaling. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.3C (Arithmetic control towers).**  If an arithmetic tower has
multiplicative filtered sections on the tested local factors, so that
\(\kappa_{s_\lambda}=0\) in the residual quotient for every \(\lambda\), then
\(\nu_\lambda^E=0\) for every Ext-sensitive coefficient \(E\).  The scaled
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) contribution therefore vanishes identically.

In particular, the pure residue-field rows and pure split nilpotent-depth
rows are control towers for the arithmetic continuum limit: they may have
nontrivial conductor adjacency, but no residual tame/wild carry correction.
Finite arithmetic corrections can first appear in mixed tame/wild towers,
such as the \({\mathbb F}_4[u]/(u^2)\) row of Proposition 11.3 and its
higher residue-degree or higher-depth analogues.  For those towers, the
remaining task is no longer to invent a new Fisher convergence theorem; it
is to compute \(\nu_\lambda^E\), choose the correct scaling \(a_\lambda\),
and decide which branch of Theorem 14.3B occurs.

**Problem 14.4 (Finite-to-continuum curvature linearization).**  Given a
simplectic approximation tower, prove an error estimate of the form

$$
\left\|
T_{\lambda,k}(h)
-
T_{\infty,k}(h)
\right\|
\le
\varepsilon_{\lambda,k}(h),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\lambda,k}(h)\to0,
$$

where \(T_{\lambda,k}\) is the order-\(k\) adjacency-algebra jet of the
Fisher-dual response and \(T_{\infty,k}\) is the corresponding continuum
connection, curvature, or higher tensor jet.  This is the theorem that would
turn "proper linearization of curvature" from a finite atlas statement into
an arithmetic discretization theorem.

**Problem 14.5 (Binary symplectic emergence).**  Determine which
coarse-grainings or polarizations

$$
B_\lambda:\Lambda^{Q_\lambda-2}H_{R_\lambda}^*
\longrightarrow
\Lambda^2T^*M
\quad\text{or}\quad
T^*M
$$

send the high-degree simplectic momentum forms to classical symplectic or
cotangent momentum data in the limit.  The \(Q=4\) row gives an exact
low-dimensional collapse by Hodge duality; the missing theorem is the
asymptotic collapse, or the proof that no such collapse exists without extra
polarization data.

This is where number theory can enter the continuum story most strongly.  In
CRT towers, primes index local tensor factors of \(\mathcal A_R\).  The
question is whether scaled local classes
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\lambda,\mathfrak p})\) disappear, converge to a
new arithmetic correction, or survive as ramification-weighted curvature in
the limiting geometry.  Theorem 14.3B makes this question precise: it is the
weak-limit problem for the normalized carry measures attached to the
Ext-sensitive coefficients.

### 14.2 Global dynamics and conservation problem

The local equations are formal and finite.  A mechanics also needs global
flow, conservation, and composition statements.

On the open simplex, smooth macrovariables give a smooth vector field
\(X_{\mathbf C}\) and hence local existence and uniqueness by ordinary ODE
theory.  Long-time behavior is subtler.  The Fisher metric degenerates at
the boundary in \(m\)-coordinates, and many natural entropy or logit
observables become singular near the faces.

**Problem 14.6 (Global simplectic flow).**  Choose a compactification or
completion of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) and prove conditions on
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\) ensuring that the simplectic flow:

1. exists for all time in the interior, or reaches the boundary in a
   controlled way;
2. preserves \(\Omega_R^F\) on every compact exhaustion where the flow is
   defined;
3. preserves the macrovariables \(C_i\);
4. has conductor leakage bounded by the finite \(k\)-jet closure deficit
   from Theorem 10.4.

**Definition 14.6A (Square-root Fisher compactification).**  Define

$$
\Psi:\Delta_R^\circ\to\Sigma_R^+,
\qquad
\Psi(p)_a=2\sqrt{p_a},
$$

where

$$
\Sigma_R^+
=
\left\{q\in{\mathbb R}_{>0}^R:\sum_{a\in R}q_a^2=4\right\}
$$

is the positive orthant of the round sphere of radius \(2\).  Its closure
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) is a compact manifold with corners.  The map
\(\Psi\) is a Fisher isometry:

$$
\Psi^*\left(\sum_{a\in R}dq_a^2\bigg|_{\Sigma_R^+}\right)
=
g^F.
$$

Thus the apparent boundary degeneration of \(g^F\) in \(m\)-coordinates is a
coordinate singularity of the square-root map.  The Fisher volume form is the
pullback of the round volume form on \(\Sigma_R^+\), with the chosen
orientation.

**Remark 14.6A.1 (\(Q\)-independence of the radius; the \(Q\)-fixed structural
datum is the orthant fraction).**  The sphere radius in \(\Sigma_R^+\) is the
normalization constant \(2\) in \(\Psi(p)_a=2\sqrt{p_a}\); it is *independent
of* \(Q=|R|\).  No \(Q\)-fixed length, mass, or scale is set by Definition
14.6A — any framing of the Hellinger-sphere radius as a \(Q\)-determined
quantity is refuted at the definition itself.  The genuine \(Q\)-fixed
structural datum the same geometry furnishes is the *positive-orthant
solid-angle fraction*: \(\Sigma_R^+\) is exactly one of the \(2^Q\) sign-octants
of the round sphere, hence occupies fraction \(1/2^Q\) of it (e.g.
\(1/2^6=1/64\) for \(R=\mathbb Z/6\), cross-row ratio \(2^{24}\) against
\(\mathbb Z/30\)).  This is the projection-closure form of a conductor/\(Q\)-fixed
pure number; it is geometric-combinatoric, not a physical \(\Lambda\) — see
Paper 32 §11.3 and ledger item S.  An [A] coherence noted there: the \(2^Q\)
of this orthant fraction is the same \(2^Q\) whose distinguished single member
the real-cone-preservation obstruction of Paper 14 Theorem 2.2′ preserves.

For a vector field \(\widetilde X\) on \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\), say that
\(\widetilde X\) is boundary-tangent if, for every \(a\in R\),

$$
\widetilde X[q_a]=0
\qquad\text{on the face } \{q_a=0\}.
$$

Equivalently, locally near that face,
\(\widetilde X[q_a]=q_a b_a(q)\) for a smooth function \(b_a\).

**Theorem 14.6B (Compactified global Nambu-Fisher flow).**  Let
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\) be simplectic Hamiltonian data on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\).  Suppose each

$$
\widetilde C_i=C_i\circ\Psi^{-1}
$$

extends smoothly to a neighborhood of \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\), and let
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) be the round-volume Nambu vector field
determined by

$$
\iota_{\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_{\Sigma}
=
d\widetilde C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\widetilde C_{Q-2}.
$$

If \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) is boundary-tangent, then:

1. the flow of \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) exists uniquely for all
   \(t\in{\mathbb R}\) on \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\);
2. every boundary stratum is invariant;
3. every trajectory starting in \(\Sigma_R^+\), equivalently in
   \(\Delta_R^\circ\), remains in the interior for all finite time;
4. the flow preserves Fisher volume and the macrovariables \(C_i\).

If the smooth extension exists but boundary-tangency fails, any smooth
extension of \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) to the ambient round sphere is
complete.  The only obstruction to a global simplex trajectory is controlled
boundary exit: the first time some \(q_a(t)\) reaches \(0\) with nonzero
outward normal component.

Moreover, let \(S\) be a conductor packet set and suppose that on a compact
Fisher ball \(K\subset\Delta_R^\circ\) the Fisher-dual response has a
Theorem 10.4 expansion through order \(k\), with remainder bounded by

$$
\|R_{k+1}(h,\mathbf C)\|\le B_K\|h\|^{k+1}.
$$

Then along any trajectory contained in \(K\), the conductor leakage outside
\(S\) satisfies

$$
\|\Pi_{S^c}\Theta_{\mathbf C}(p_*+h(t))\|
\le
\sum_{m=0}^k\|D_m^S(h(t),\mathbf C)\|
+
B_K\|h(t)\|^{k+1},
$$

where \(D_m^S\) is the finite closure-defect operator of Theorem 10.4.  In
particular, exact \(k\)-jet conductor closure bounds leakage by the first
omitted Taylor order on \(K\).

**Proof.**  For \(q_a=2\sqrt{p_a}\),

$$
dq_a(u)=\frac{u_a}{\sqrt{p_a}},
$$

so the Euclidean metric on the radius-\(2\) sphere pulls back to
\(\sum_a u_av_a/p_a=g^F_p(u,v)\).  Hence the Fisher Nambu equation on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\) is the pullback of the round-volume Nambu equation on
\(\Sigma_R^+\).

Because \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) is smooth on a neighborhood of the
compact set \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\), it can be extended to a smooth vector
field on the whole round sphere; compactness gives a unique complete ambient
flow.  Boundary-tangency says each coordinate face \(q_a=0\) is invariant.
By smoothness, \(\widetilde X[q_a]=q_ab_a(q)\) near that face, so along a
trajectory with \(q_a(0)>0\),

$$
q_a(t)=q_a(0)\exp\left(\int_0^t b_a(q(s))\,ds\right),
$$

as long as the trajectory is defined.  Thus no positive coordinate can reach
zero in finite time.  The preservation of volume and of the \(C_i\) is
Proposition 4.4 transported by the Fisher isometry.  If boundary-tangency
fails, compactness still prevents blow-up on the sphere, so leaving the
simplex can occur only by crossing a coordinate face.

The leakage estimate is the Taylor expansion of Theorem 10.4 with the
projection \(\Pi_{S^c}\) applied and the assumed uniform remainder bound on
\(K\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.6C (Pullback stability for learned maps).**  Let
\(F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ\) be a smooth learned behavior map whose image lies in
a compact set \(K\), and let \(Y\) be a vector field on activation space.
Assume \(Y\) is \(\varepsilon\)-related to the simplectic field on \(K\):

$$
\|dF(Y)-X_{\mathbf C}\circ F\|_{F}\le\varepsilon.
$$

Then every macrovariable has drift bounded by

$$
\left|Y(C_i\circ F)\right|
\le
\|dC_i\|_{F,*,K}\,\varepsilon.
$$

If the conductor-leakage functional on \(K\) is \(L_{S,K}\)-Lipschitz in the
Fisher norm, then the pulled-back leakage is bounded by the finite closure
deficit from Theorem 14.6B plus \(L_{S,K}\varepsilon\).  Thus the SGD-style
conductor audits become predictive exactly when the learned update field is
approximately \(F\)-related to a compactified conductor-closed
Nambu-Fisher flow.

**Theorem 14.7 (Adjacency-Noether theorem).**  Let
\(M=\Delta_R^\circ\), \(n=Q-1\), and let
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{n-1})\) be simplectic Hamiltonian data with

$$
P_{\mathbf C}=dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{n-1},
\qquad
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F=P_{\mathbf C}.
$$

Let \(\phi:M\to M\) be a smooth symmetry whose induced action on the
additive-character atlas normalizes the quotient-character adjacency algebra
\(\mathcal A_R\).  Assume

$$
\phi^*\Omega_R^F=\sigma_\phi\,\Omega_R^F,
\qquad
\phi^*P_{\mathbf C}=\tau_\phi\,P_{\mathbf C},
$$

where \(\sigma_\phi,\tau_\phi\in{\mathbb R}^\times\) are constants.  Then

$$
\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}
=
\frac{\sigma_\phi}{\tau_\phi}\,X_{\mathbf C}.
$$

In particular:

1. if \(\sigma_\phi=\tau_\phi\), then \(\phi\) maps every simplectic
   trajectory to a trajectory with the same time parameter;
2. if \(\sigma_\phi=-\tau_\phi\), then \(\phi\) maps trajectories to
   time-reversed trajectories;
3. if \(\phi\) is Fisher-volume preserving and
   \(\phi^*C_i=\sum_j A_{ij}C_j+\mathrm{constant}\) with
   \(A\in SL(n-1,{\mathbb R})\), then \(\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}=X_{\mathbf C}\).

Moreover, if \(G\) is a finite group of such symmetries preserving a packet
set \(S\), then the finite closure defects of Theorem 10.4 are
\(G\)-equivariant.  Writing

$$
D_m^S(h,\mathbf C)
=
\Pi_{S^c}
\sum_{a+b=m}
(-1)^a\mathsf C_a(h)X_{\mathbf C}^{(b)}(h),
$$

one has

$$
D_m^S(g\cdot h,g\cdot\mathbf C)
=
\lambda_g\,g\cdot D_m^S(h,\mathbf C),
\qquad
\lambda_g=\frac{\sigma_g}{\tau_g}.
$$

Thus conductor closure through order \(k\) is constant on
\(G\)-orbits of Hamiltonian tuples and displacement jets.  The closure
census may be computed on orbit representatives of the Hayes/Galois/CRT
normalizer action.

**Proof.**  Pull back the defining equation for \(\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}\):

$$
\phi^*(\iota_{\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F)
=
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\phi^*\Omega_R^F
=
\sigma_\phi P_{\mathbf C}.
$$

Since \(\phi^*P_{\mathbf C}=\tau_\phi P_{\mathbf C}\) and \(\phi^*\) is
injective,

$$
\iota_{\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
\frac{\sigma_\phi}{\tau_\phi}P_{\mathbf C}.
$$

Nambu nondegeneracy of \(\Omega_R^F\) gives
\(\phi_*X_{\mathbf C}=(\sigma_\phi/\tau_\phi)X_{\mathbf C}\).  If
\(\phi^*C=A C+\mathrm{constant}\), then
\(\phi^*P_{\mathbf C}=\det(A)P_{\mathbf C}\).  The stated
time-preserving and time-reversing cases follow.

For the closure defects, normalization of \(\mathcal A_R\) means
\(g\mathsf C_a(h)g^{-1}=\mathsf C_a(g\cdot h)\), and preservation of \(S\)
means \(g\Pi_Sg^{-1}=\Pi_S\) and \(g\Pi_{S^c}g^{-1}=\Pi_{S^c}\).  The
Nambu vector-field jets transform by the scalar \(\lambda_g\) from the first
part of the theorem.  Hence each \(D_m^S\) transforms equivariantly up to a
nonzero scalar, so its vanishing is orbit-invariant. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.8 (Finite Noether atlas reduction).**  For a computed finite
atlas, the global Noether/integrability census attached to a symmetry group
\(G\le N(\mathcal A_R)\) reduces to the following finite data:

1. \(G\)-orbits of admissible Hamiltonian tuples \(\mathbf C\);
2. stabilizers of those tuples and of their conductor packet sets;
3. the determinant character \(\det(A_g)\) on each tuple orbit;
4. the equivariant closure-defect operators \(D_m^S\) through the desired
   order \(k\);
5. residual filtered carry orbits from
   \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) not killed by section
   coboundaries.

On each orbit with determinant character \(+1\), the symmetry gives
time-preserving simplectic dynamics.  On determinant character \(-1\), it
gives a reversible simplectic sector.  Nonzero residual carry orbits are the
finite arithmetic anomalies that remain after the Noether reduction.

In the finite-sufficient FC1-B1 specialization, "admissible Hamiltonian
tuples" means tuples in \((J^r{\mathcal N}_{R,k})^{Q-2}\) after choosing the
finite observation map \(\Phi_{R,k}\), polynomial jet order \(r\), and a
support or coefficient skeleton.  With those choices made, the determinant
character in Theorem 14.7 is computed entirely inside the finite
\(\Phi_{R,k}\)-atlas.

**Infinitesimal form.**  If \(Y\) is an infinitesimal symmetry with

$$
{\mathcal L}_Y\Omega_R^F=0,
\qquad
{\mathcal L}_Y P_{\mathbf C}=0,
$$

then

$$
[Y,X_{\mathbf C}]=0.
$$

Indeed,

$$
0={\mathcal L}_Y P_{\mathbf C}
={\mathcal L}_Y(\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F)
=
\iota_{[Y,X_{\mathbf C}]}\Omega_R^F
+\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}{\mathcal L}_Y\Omega_R^F,
$$

and the second term vanishes.  The contraction
\(\iota_Y P_{\mathbf C}\) is then a closed \((Q-3)\)-form whenever
\({\mathcal L}_Y P_{\mathbf C}=0\), giving the current-valued Noether
quantity associated to \(Y\).

**Problem 14.9 (Closed-current completion and arithmetic residual).**  The
exact decomposable Nambu currents sit inside the larger closed-current
equation

$$
dJ=0,
\qquad
J=\iota_X\Omega_R^F.
$$

Since \(\Delta_R^\circ\) is smoothly contractible, ordinary de Rham
cohomology does not supply the residual.  The expected residual is the
cohomology of the filtered quotient-character current complex:

$$
H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)
=
\frac{\{\text{closed filtered Fisher currents}\}}
{\{\text{exact decomposable Nambu currents and section coboundaries}\}}.
$$

The finite-order reduction is that the first local summands of this residual
are represented by \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\), while any
independent remainder is, by definition, the higher hyper-adjacency quotient.
The theorem below makes that reduction precise on each finite atlas.

**Theorem 14.9A (Finite residual exact sequence).**  Fix a finite
quotient-character atlas, a jet order \(k\), and a conductor packet set.
Let \(\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)\) be the finite-dimensional space of filtered
\((Q-2)\)-current jets through order \(k\) in that atlas, and let

$$
d_{\mathcal A}:\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)\to\mathcal J_k^{Q-1}(R)
$$

be the current differential computed in the quotient-character adjacency
algebra.  Put

$$
Z_k=\ker d_{\mathcal A},
$$

and let \(N_k\subset Z_k\) be the span of exact decomposable Nambu jets

$$
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}
$$

through order \(k\).  Let \(B_k\subset Z_k\) be the subspace generated by
filtered section coboundaries.  The finite residual current space is

$$
H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)
=
\frac{Z_k}{N_k+B_k}.
$$

Let \(E_k\subset Z_k\) be the span of closed residual carry-atom classes
coming from the local \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) terms that
appear through order \(k\).  Then there is a canonical exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow
\overline E_k
\longrightarrow
H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)
\longrightarrow
H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)
\longrightarrow 0,
$$

where

$$
\overline E_k
=
\frac{E_k+N_k+B_k}{N_k+B_k}
$$

is the Ext-generated residual, and

$$
H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)
=
\frac{H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)}{\overline E_k}
$$

is the higher hyper-adjacency residual.

Consequently:

1. the closed-current residual is generated exactly by
   \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) through order \(k\) if and only if
   \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)=0\);
2. higher hyper-adjacency terms contribute independent summands exactly when
   \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\ne0\);
3. if \(E_k=0\) and \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)=0\), then every
   closed filtered current jet through order \(k\) is equivalent, modulo
   section choice, to an exact decomposable Nambu current.

Thus the global residual question becomes a finite computation on each atlas:
compute \(Z_k\), quotient by \(N_k+B_k\), then test whether the remaining
classes are already represented by residual \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
carry atoms.

**Proof.**  At finite atlas and finite jet order, all current spaces are
finite-dimensional.  The quotient defining
\(H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)\) is exactly the closed filtered current space
modulo the two relations allowed in the problem: exact decomposable Nambu
currents and section coboundaries.  The image \(\overline E_k\) is a
well-defined subspace because changing a filtered section changes a carry
cocycle by an element of \(B_k\), which is already quotiented out.  Taking
the quotient of \(H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)\) by this subspace gives
\(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\), so the displayed sequence is exact by
construction.  The three consequences are precisely the vanishing or
nonvanishing alternatives for that quotient. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.9B (Atlas audit for the closed-current residual).**  For each
computed local atlas, the residual audit is:

1. build the finite matrices for \(d_{\mathcal A}\) on current jets through
   order \(k\);
2. compute \(Z_k=\ker d_{\mathcal A}\);
3. compute the subspace \(N_k+B_k\) of decomposable Nambu jets and section
   coboundaries;
4. mark the residual carry-atom subspace \(E_k\);
5. report \(\dim \overline E_k\) and
   \(\dim H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\).

The theorem-backed prediction is sharp: a nonzero first number is the
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) arithmetic residual, while a nonzero second
number is genuinely higher hyper-adjacency leakage not explained by the
local carry class alone.

These global questions are also the bridge to Paper 12.  For a learned map

$$
F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ,
$$

the pullback \(F^*P_{\mathbf C}\) is a closed current when
\(P_{\mathbf C}\) is closed, but it becomes a predictive optimization law
only after one specifies a metric, horizontal lift, or variational principle
on \(A\).  Corollary 14.6C gives the first sufficient condition: if the
learned update field is approximately \(F\)-related to a compactified
conductor-closed Nambu-Fisher flow, then macrovariable drift and conductor
leakage are bounded by the relation error plus the finite closure deficit.

### 14.3 Long-time dynamics and the infinite-order residual

Theorem 14.6B gives global existence in finite time for boundary-tangent
Nambu-Fisher fields, but it leaves open what happens as \(t\to\pm\infty\):
which orbits are recurrent, which are periodic, which asymptote to specific
boundary strata, and at what rate conductor leakage accumulates along an
orbit.  Theorem 14.9A gives a finite-jet decomposition of the closed-current
residual into an \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)-generated piece and a higher
hyper-adjacency quotient, but it leaves open what the full (not jet
truncated) residual looks like and whether it is generated exactly by the
local carry classes.  This subsection settles both questions.

The two halves of the subsection use different but compatible compactness
inputs.  The long-time dynamics use the compactness of \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\)
together with the dimension count
\(\dim\Delta_R^\circ - \#\text{macrovariables}=1\): every orbit lives on a
compact 1-manifold, and compact 1-manifolds with boundary are classified.
The infinite-order residual uses the fact that the finite-jet exact sequence
is an exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces, so the inverse
system over jet order automatically satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition
and the infinite-order short exact sequence follows.

#### 14.3.1 Leaf decomposition and recurrence

**Definition 14.10 (Macrovariable map and regular leaf).**  Let
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}=(\widetilde C_1,\ldots,\widetilde C_{Q-2})\) be a
smooth extension of \(\mathbf C\) to a neighborhood of
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) in the round sphere, and write

$$
\widetilde{\mathbf C}:\overline\Sigma_R^+\longrightarrow{\mathbb R}^{Q-2}
$$

for the resulting macrovariable map.  A regular value of
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\) is a value \(c\in{\mathbb R}^{Q-2}\) at which the
\((Q-2)\)-form

$$
d\widetilde C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\widetilde C_{Q-2}
$$

does not vanish at any point of the fiber
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}^{-1}(c)\cap\overline\Sigma_R^+\), and at which the
restriction \(\widetilde{\mathbf C}|_F\) is of full rank on every boundary
stratum \(F\subset\partial\overline\Sigma_R^+\) that the fiber meets.  The
regular leaf at \(c\) is

$$
L_c=\widetilde{\mathbf C}^{-1}(c)\subset\overline\Sigma_R^+.
$$

By the constant-rank theorem in the manifold-with-corners category, \(L_c\)
is a compact embedded one-dimensional submanifold of
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\), possibly with boundary lying in
\(\partial\overline\Sigma_R^+\).

**Lemma 14.11 (Flow lies on regular leaves).**  Every integral curve of
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) starting in \(\widetilde{\mathbf C}^{-1}(c)\) is
contained in that fiber for all time.

**Proof.**  By Proposition 4.4,
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}[\widetilde C_i]=0\) for each \(i\), so each
\(\widetilde C_i\) is constant along trajectories. \(\square\)

**Theorem 14.12 (Long-time classification on regular leaves).**  Let
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) be the boundary-tangent Nambu-Fisher field of
Theorem 14.6B, and let \(c\) be a regular value of \(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\).
Then every connected component of \(L_c\) is diffeomorphic to either a circle
\(S^1\) or to a closed arc \([0,1]\) whose two endpoints lie in
\(\partial\overline\Sigma_R^+\).

1. *Interior periodic orbits.*  If a component
   \(\Gamma\subset L_c\) is a circle contained in the open simplex
   \(\Sigma_R^+\), then \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) restricts to a smooth
   nowhere-zero vector field on \(\Gamma\) and the flow is periodic with
   finite period

   $$
   T(\Gamma)
   =
   \oint_\Gamma
   \frac{ds}{\|\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\|_{\Sigma}}
   <\infty,
   $$

   where \(ds\) is round arclength on \(\Sigma_R^+\) and
   \(\|\cdot\|_{\Sigma}\) is the round norm.  In the original simplex
   coordinates this is the Fisher arclength integral

   $$
   T(\Gamma)
   =
   \oint_\Gamma
   \frac{ds_F}{\|X_{\mathbf C}\|_F}.
   $$

2. *Boundary-stratum periodic orbits.*  If a component
   \(\Gamma\subset L_c\) is a circle contained in a single boundary stratum
   \(F\), then the boundary-restricted Nambu-Fisher field is the Nambu field
   of the lower-dimensional simplectic system on \(F\), and the same finite
   period integral applies in \(F\).

3. *Heteroclinic arcs.*  If a component \(A\subset L_c\) is an arc with
   endpoints \(p_-,p_+\in\partial\overline\Sigma_R^+\), then
   \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}(p_\pm)=0\) and the flow along
   \(A\setminus\{p_-,p_+\}\) is monotone with infinite total time in both
   directions: the interior orbit is asymptotic to \(p_-\) as
   \(t\to-\infty\) and to \(p_+\) as \(t\to+\infty\).

In every case the flow is recurrent in the sense of Poincaré: the Fisher
volume on \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) is finite and preserved, so by Poincaré's
recurrence theorem, for every Borel set \(A\subset\overline\Sigma_R^+\) of
positive Fisher volume, almost every point of \(A\) returns to \(A\) at an
unbounded sequence of times.

**Proof.**  By the boundary-tangency hypothesis of Theorem 14.6B,
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) extends smoothly to a vector field on a
neighborhood of \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) in the round sphere, and the flow
preserves the round volume and the macrovariables.  By Lemma 14.11 every
orbit lies in a single fiber of \(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\); by the regularity
of \(c\) every fiber is a compact smooth one-dimensional submanifold of
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\), possibly with boundary, and the classification of
compact one-manifolds with boundary forces every component to be a circle
or a closed arc.

(1) On a circle \(\Gamma\subset\Sigma_R^+\), the cofactor wedge
\(d\widetilde C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\widetilde C_{Q-2}\) is nonzero by
regularity, so \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) is everywhere tangent to
\(\Gamma\) and nowhere zero.  Smoothness and compactness of \(\Gamma\) give a
positive lower bound on \(\|\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\|_{\Sigma}\), hence the
displayed integral is finite.  The Fisher arclength expression follows from
\(\Psi^*g_\Sigma=g^F\).

(2) On a boundary stratum \(F\) the round metric restricts to the Fisher
metric of the lower-dimensional simplex obtained by setting one or more
\(q_a=0\), and boundary-tangency makes \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}|_F\) a
smooth vector field on this lower-dimensional round face.  The pullback of
\(d\widetilde C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\widetilde C_{Q-2}\) to \(F\) is the
cofactor wedge of the boundary macrovariables, and regularity of \(c\) on
\(F\) forces it to be nonzero on \(\Gamma\).  The argument of (1) then
applies inside \(F\).

(3) Suppose a component \(A\subset L_c\) is an arc with endpoint
\(p\in\partial\overline\Sigma_R^+\).  The leaf is tangent to
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) on its smooth locus, and \(A\) is a
one-dimensional submanifold of \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) with boundary point
\(p\).  Boundary-tangency forces \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}(p)\) to lie in
the tangent of the boundary stratum containing \(p\); but the leaf direction
at \(p\) is determined by the cofactor wedge, which by the regularity
hypothesis is transverse to the boundary stratum on the interior side of
\(p\).  The only way both can hold is for the leaf direction at \(p\) to
become the zero direction, i.e., \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}(p)=0\).  Hence
both endpoints are equilibria.

Let \(s\) be arclength along \(A\) with \(p_-\) at \(s=0\) and \(p_+\) at
\(s=L\).  Then \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}(s)=v(s)\partial_s\) with
\(v\in C^\infty([0,L])\), \(v>0\) on \((0,L)\), and \(v(0)=v(L)=0\) by the
equilibrium statement.  The orbit time from \(s_0\) to \(s_1\) is

$$
t(s_0,s_1)
=
\int_{s_0}^{s_1}\frac{ds}{v(s)}.
$$

Smoothness of \(v\) and its vanishing at the endpoints make
\(\int_0^{s_1}v(s)^{-1}\,ds\) and \(\int_{s_0}^L v(s)^{-1}\,ds\) divergent for
any interior \(s_0,s_1\in(0,L)\), so the orbit takes infinite time to reach
either endpoint.  Hence it is asymptotic to \(p_\pm\) but never reaches them.

For Poincaré recurrence: \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) has finite round volume
(it is a fraction of the radius-\(2\) sphere); the flow is volume-preserving
by Proposition 4.4 transported by \(\Psi\); and it is complete by Theorem
14.6B.  These are exactly the hypotheses of the classical Poincaré
recurrence theorem. \(\square\)

**Remark 14.12A (Critical values).**  At a critical value of
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\) the fiber may fail to be a one-manifold and the
classification of Theorem 14.12 does not apply directly.  However, the
non-regular fibers form a set of measure zero in \({\mathbb R}^{Q-2}\)
(Sard), so for almost every \(c\) the leaf decomposition above is valid.
The critical fibers contribute equilibria and the connecting heteroclinic
network only; their analysis reduces to the same theorem applied to the
restricted simplectic system on the appropriate stratum.

**Corollary 14.12B (Ergodic decomposition).**  The Fisher volume on
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) decomposes into ergodic components for the flow of
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\).  Each ergodic component is supported either on
the closure of a single interior periodic orbit, on a single periodic orbit
inside one boundary stratum, or on a single heteroclinic arc together with
its endpoints.  The transverse measure on the family of regular leaves is
the disintegration of \(\Omega_R^F\) along
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\): for a regular value \(c\),

$$
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathbf C}}(dc)
=
\widetilde{\mathbf C}_*\Omega_R^F.
$$

The Birkhoff time average of any continuous observable along a non-trivial
ergodic component coincides with its space average against the ergodic
component's invariant measure.

**Proof.**  Standard Choquet/Rokhlin ergodic decomposition for a measurable
volume-preserving \({\mathbb R}\)-action on a compact finite-measure space;
the support statement follows from the leaf decomposition of Theorem 14.12.
The transverse measure formula is the coarea formula applied to
\(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\). \(\square\)

#### 14.3.2 Period bounds and long-time conductor leakage

The classification in Theorem 14.12 lets the finite-jet leakage bound of
Theorem 14.6B be integrated along an orbit, giving time-averaged and
total-integrated leakage estimates.

**Theorem 14.13 (Long-time conductor leakage).**  Let \(\mathbf C\) be
simplectic Hamiltonian data, \(S\) a conductor packet set, and let
\(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) be boundary-tangent on
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\).  Assume that on a Fisher tube
\(K\subset\Delta_R^\circ\) the Fisher-dual response has a Theorem 10.4
expansion through order \(k\) with remainder
\(\|R_{k+1}\|\le B_K\|h\|^{k+1}\) and with finite closure defects
\(D_m^S\) for \(m\le k\).

1. *Periodic orbits.*  For an interior periodic orbit
   \(\Gamma\subset K\) of period \(T(\Gamma)\), the time-averaged conductor
   leakage satisfies

   $$
   \overline L_S(\Gamma)
   :=
   \frac{1}{T(\Gamma)}
   \int_0^{T(\Gamma)}
   \|\Pi_{S^c}\Theta_{\mathbf C}(\gamma(t))\|\,dt
   \le
   \sum_{m=0}^k
   \overline D_m^S(\Gamma)
   +
   B_K\,\overline{h^{k+1}}(\Gamma),
   $$

   where

   $$
   \overline D_m^S(\Gamma)
   =
   \frac{1}{T(\Gamma)}\int_0^{T(\Gamma)}\|D_m^S(h(t),\mathbf C)\|\,dt,
   \qquad
   \overline{h^{k+1}}(\Gamma)
   =
   \frac{1}{T(\Gamma)}\int_0^{T(\Gamma)}\|h(t)\|^{k+1}\,dt.
   $$

   In particular, if \(\Gamma\) lies in the order-\(k\) conductor closure
   locus, where \(D_m^S\equiv0\) for \(m\le k\), then

   $$
   \overline L_S(\Gamma)\le B_K\,\overline{h^{k+1}}(\Gamma).
   $$

2. *Heteroclinic arcs.*  Let \(A\) be a heteroclinic orbit of
   \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) connecting boundary equilibria \(p_-\) and
   \(p_+\), and suppose the linearizations
   \(D\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}(p_\pm)\) within the boundary strata
   containing \(p_\pm\) have strictly positive (resp. strictly negative)
   spectral gap \(\alpha_+>0\) (resp. \(-\alpha_-<0\)) along \(A\).  Then
   the orbit converges exponentially:

   $$
   \mathrm{dist}_\Sigma(\gamma(t),p_+)
   =
   O(e^{-\alpha_+t})\ (t\to+\infty),
   \qquad
   \mathrm{dist}_\Sigma(\gamma(t),p_-)
   =
   O(e^{\alpha_-t})\ (t\to-\infty).
   $$

   Consequently, on any Fisher tube around \(A\) inside \(K\), the
   total-integrated conductor leakage

   $$
   \Lambda_S(A)
   =
   \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
   \|\Pi_{S^c}\Theta_{\mathbf C}(\gamma(t))\|\,dt
   $$

   is finite, with bound

   $$
   \Lambda_S(A)
   \le
   \sum_{m=0}^k
   I_m^S(A)
   +
   B_K\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|h(t)\|^{k+1}\,dt,
   $$

   where each \(I_m^S(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|D_m^S(h(t),\mathbf C)\|\,dt\)
   is finite under the spectral-gap hypothesis because
   \(D_m^S(0,\mathbf C)=0\) and \(D_m^S\) is smooth.

**Proof.**  (1) is the time integral of the pointwise leakage bound of
Theorem 14.6B over a single period.  Boundedness of every integrand on the
compact orbit \(\Gamma\subset K\) makes each average finite.

(2) The smooth extension of \(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C}\) to a neighborhood of
the boundary makes \(p_\pm\) honest equilibria with smooth linearization.
The orbit \(\gamma\) lies in the unstable manifold of \(p_-\) and the stable
manifold of \(p_+\) inside the boundary stratum.  Standard stable-manifold
theory gives the exponential rates \(\alpha_\pm\) coming from the dominant
spectral gap of the boundary linearization, and the orbit converges at that
rate.

Each \(D_m^S(h,\mathbf C)\) is smooth and vanishes at \(h=0\) (because at the
equilibrium \(h=0\) the closure defect collapses to the trivial value on the
target boundary stratum), so on the orbit
\(\|D_m^S(h(t),\mathbf C)\|=O(\|h(t)\|)\).  Exponential convergence of
\(\|h(t)\|\) makes each \(I_m^S(A)\) finite.  The same exponential decay
makes \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\|h(t)\|^{k+1}dt\) finite. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.13A (Recurrent conductor budget).**  Let \(\mu_c\) be the
disintegrated measure on the family of regular leaves
\(\{L_c\}\) under \(\widetilde{\mathbf C}\), and let \(\mu_c\)-a.e.\ leaf
component carry the invariant measure \(\mu_\Gamma\) of Corollary 14.12B.
Then the global Fisher-volume average of the conductor leakage decomposes as

$$
\int_{\overline\Sigma_R^+}\!\!\!
\|\Pi_{S^c}\Theta_{\mathbf C}\|\,d\Omega_R^F
=
\int_{c\in\mathrm{im}(\widetilde{\mathbf C})}\!\!\!
\Big(
\sum_{\Gamma\subset L_c}
T(\Gamma)\,\overline L_S(\Gamma)
+
\sum_{A\subset L_c}\Lambda_S(A)
\Big)\,
\mu_{\widetilde{\mathbf C}}(dc),
$$

with each summand bounded by Theorem 14.13.  Thus the order-\(k\) closure
census on tubes simultaneously bounds the time-averaged budget of every
periodic ergodic component and the integrated budget of every heteroclinic
component of the simplectic flow.

#### 14.3.3 Stratified attractor decomposition

The leaf classification organizes the non-wandering set
\(\Omega(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C})\subset\overline\Sigma_R^+\) by corner
stratum.

**Theorem 14.14 (Stratified attractor decomposition).**  The non-wandering
set decomposes as a disjoint union

$$
\Omega(\widetilde X_{\mathbf C})
=
\Omega^\circ
\sqcup
\bigsqcup_{F\subsetneq\overline\Sigma_R^+}
\Omega^F,
$$

where \(\Omega^\circ\) is the union of the closures of the interior periodic
orbits and \(\Omega^F\) is the non-wandering set of the boundary-restricted
Nambu-Fisher flow on the stratum \(F\).  Each \(\Omega^F\) is the
non-wandering set of a lower-dimensional simplectic system of the same form,
to which Theorem 14.12 reapplies.  Inductively, every recurrence class of the
flow is supported on a unique corner stratum, and the inter-stratum dynamics
consists exclusively of heteroclinic arcs whose endpoints lie in strata of
strictly smaller dimension.

**Proof.**  By Theorem 14.12, a point is non-wandering only if it lies on a
periodic orbit; in the interior or in a boundary stratum.  Heteroclinic
arcs contribute their endpoints (equilibria in lower-dimensional strata) to
the non-wandering set, never their interior points.  The lower-dimensional
restrictions are again volume-preserving boundary-tangent flows on the
lower-dimensional round-faces, so the same theorem applies on each.  Finite
induction on stratum depth terminates because the corner depth of
\(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) is \(Q-1\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.14A (Conductor-closed sectors).**  Fix an order \(k\) and a
packet set \(S\).  A connected component of the closure locus
\(\{D_m^S\equiv0\ \forall m\le k\}\) inside \(\overline\Sigma_R^+\) is a
finite union of corner-stratified pieces; the simplectic flow restricted to
each such piece consists of either periodic orbits with leakage controlled
by \(B_K\|h\|^{k+1}\) or heteroclinic arcs with integrated leakage
controlled by Theorem 14.13(2).  In particular, the long-time conductor
budget on each sector is determined by the first omitted Taylor order and
the boundary spectral gap.

#### 14.3.4 The infinite-order closed-current residual

We now lift the finite-jet exact sequence of Theorem 14.9A to the full
filtered residual.  Fix a finite quotient-character atlas
\((R,\mathcal A_R)\) and a conductor packet set; write
\(\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}\), \(d_{\mathcal A}\), \(Z_k\), \(N_k\), \(B_k\),
\(E_k\), \(\overline E_k\), \(H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)\) and
\(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\) as in Theorem 14.9A.

**Definition 14.15 (Full filtered current complex).**  The infinite-order
filtered current space is the inverse limit

$$
\mathcal J^{Q-2}(R)
=
\varprojlim_k\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)
$$

under the truncation maps \(\pi_{k+1,k}:\mathcal J_{k+1}^{Q-2}\to\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}\).
The current differential \(d_{\mathcal A}\) commutes with truncation and
induces a differential on \(\mathcal J^{Q-2}(R)\).  Define

$$
Z=\varprojlim_k Z_k,
\qquad
N=\varprojlim_k N_k,
\qquad
B=\varprojlim_k B_k,
\qquad
E=\varprojlim_k E_k,
$$

and let \(\overline E=(E+N+B)/(N+B)\subset Z/(N+B)\).  The full filtered
Fisher-current residual is

$$
H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)
=
\frac{Z}{N+B}
=
\varprojlim_k H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R),
$$

and the higher hyper-adjacency residual is

$$
H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)
=
\varprojlim_k H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R).
$$

The first equality on the right of the displayed line is shown in Theorem
14.16 below; it requires the Mittag-Leffler input.

**Lemma 14.15A (Mittag-Leffler input).**  Each of the towers
\(\{Z_k\}\), \(\{N_k\}\), \(\{B_k\}\), \(\{E_k\}\),
\(\{H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)\}\), and
\(\{H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\}\) is a tower of finite-dimensional
\({\mathbb R}\)-vector spaces under truncation.  Every such tower satisfies
the Mittag-Leffler condition, so its \(\varprojlim^1\) vanishes.

**Proof.**  At each fixed jet order \(k\) and finite atlas, the spaces
\(\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)\), \(Z_k\), \(N_k\), \(B_k\), and \(E_k\) are
finite-dimensional; their quotients are finite-dimensional.  A tower of
finite-dimensional vector spaces under linear truncation always satisfies
ML, because the descending chain of images
\(\mathrm{im}(\pi_{k+n,k})\subset H_k\) stabilizes as a chain of subspaces
of a finite-dimensional space.  \(\square\)

**Theorem 14.16 (Infinite-order residual exact sequence; support form).**
In the setting
above,

1. *Ext stabilization.*  There exists a finite jet order
   \(k_0=k_0(R)\) such that for \(k\ge k_0\) the truncation
   \(\overline E_k\to\overline E_{k-1}\) is an isomorphism.  The stable
   value is \(\overline E\), and equals the direct sum of the local
   contributions

   $$
   \overline E
   =
   \bigoplus_{\mathfrak p\,\mathrm{local\ component\ of\ }R}
   \overline E(\mathfrak p),
   $$

   where each summand \(\overline E(\mathfrak p)\) is the image of
   \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) inside the residual quotient.

2. *Mittag-Leffler exactness.*  The inverse limit of the finite-jet exact
   sequences of Theorem 14.9A is exact, giving a canonical short exact
   sequence

   $$
   0
   \longrightarrow
   \overline E
   \longrightarrow
   H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)
   \longrightarrow
   H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)
   \longrightarrow 0.
   $$

3. *Truncation comparison.*  For every \(k\ge k_0\) the diagram

   $$
   \begin{array}{ccccccccc}
   0 & \to & \overline E       & \to & H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R) & \to & H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R) & \to & 0 \\
     &     & \downarrow{\cong} &     & \downarrow              &     & \downarrow                              &     &   \\
   0 & \to & \overline E_k     & \to & H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R) & \to & H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)     & \to & 0 \\
   \end{array}
   $$

   has the left column an isomorphism; the middle and right columns are the
   canonical projections from the limit.

**Proof.**  (1) The local extension class
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) is, by Paper 11, an invariant
of the local filtered ring \(R_{\mathfrak p}\); its image in the residual
quotient is generated by finitely many cocycle representatives that live in
a fixed bounded jet order \(k_{\mathfrak p}\), namely the first order at
which a section coboundary cannot kill the carry cocycle.  Since
\(R\) has finitely many local components and each contributes finitely many
classes, the set
\(\{k_{\mathfrak p}:\mathfrak p\text{ local component of }R\}\) is finite;
set \(k_0=\max_{\mathfrak p}k_{\mathfrak p}\).  For \(k\ge k_0\), every
generator of \(\overline E_k\) already appears in \(\overline E_{k_0}\), and
the truncation \(\overline E_k\to\overline E_{k-1}\) is an isomorphism by
the explicit description of local carry atoms.  The decomposition into
local pieces follows because section coboundaries are local in
\(\mathfrak p\), so the residual quotient inherits the local-component
direct-sum structure of \(\mathcal A_R\) on the Ext-generated part.

(2) Lemma 14.15A gives that each of the three towers in the finite-jet
exact sequence satisfies ML.  The exact functor lemma for inverse limits
states: given a short exact sequence of inverse systems

$$
0\to\{A_k\}\to\{B_k\}\to\{C_k\}\to0
$$

of abelian groups, with \(\{A_k\}\) satisfying ML, the resulting sequence

$$
0\to\varprojlim A_k\to\varprojlim B_k\to\varprojlim C_k\to\varprojlim^1 A_k=0
$$

is exact.  Applying this to the towers
\(\{\overline E_k\}\subset\{H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)\}\to\{H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\}\)
gives the displayed short exact sequence after identifying
\(\varprojlim\overline E_k=\overline E\),
\(\varprojlim H_{\mathcal A,k}^{Q-2}(R)=H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)\), and
\(\varprojlim H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)=H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)\).
The first identification is the content of (1); the other two are
definitional.

(3) Commutativity of the diagram is automatic for inverse-limit projections
in the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces.  The left column is
the Ext stabilization of (1); the middle and right columns are the
canonical projections.  \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.17 (Carry-generator criterion).**  The full filtered residual
\(H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)\) is generated exactly by the local carry classes
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) if and only if
\(H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)=0\).  Equivalently, the higher
hyper-adjacency quotient vanishes for all jet orders simultaneously.

In particular:

1. if \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)=0\) for all \(k\), then the full
   residual is generated by Ext atoms;
2. if \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\) is eventually constant in \(k\),
   the full higher hyper-adjacency residual equals that eventual value;
3. if \(\dim H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\to\infty\), the full residual
   carries an infinite-dimensional hyper-adjacency obstruction.

**Theorem 14.18 (Finite atlas decidability).**  For each finite
quotient-character atlas \((R,\mathcal A_R)\) and each conductor packet set
\(S\), there is a computable jet order
\(k^*=k^*(R,S)\ge k_0(R)\) with the following properties.

1. *Ext-side stability.*  For all \(k\ge k^*\), \(\overline E_k\) is
   canonically isomorphic to \(\overline E\).

2. *Hyper-adjacency monitoring.*  The dimensions
   \(d_k:=\dim H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\) form a nondecreasing
   integer sequence bounded by \(\dim\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)\).  If
   \(d_{k+1}=d_k\) for some \(k\ge k^*\), and the truncation
   \(\pi_{k+1,k}:H_{\operatorname{hyp},k+1}^{Q-2}\to H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}\)
   is an isomorphism, then \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)\) is
   canonically identified with \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\).

3. *Effective audit.*  Steps for the residual audit:
   (a) build the matrices for \(d_{\mathcal A}\) on \(\mathcal J_k^{Q-2}(R)\)
   through order \(k\);
   (b) compute \(Z_k\), \(N_k+B_k\), and \(E_k\); report
   \(\overline E_k\) and \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}(R)\);
   (c) increment \(k\) until the hyper-adjacency dimension stabilizes and
   the truncation map becomes an isomorphism (or until a chosen accuracy
   cutoff is reached, certifying a lower bound on
   \(\dim H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)\)).

The procedure terminates in finitely many steps whenever
\(H_{\operatorname{hyp},\infty}^{Q-2}(R)\) is finite-dimensional and the
hyper-adjacency dimensions stabilize at a finite order; the latter is the
generic case for arithmetic atlases whose adjacency algebras have finitely
many generators acting nontrivially on closed currents.

**Proof.**  Statements (1) and (3a)-(3b) repackage the explicit constructions
of Theorem 14.9A.  For (2): each \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}\) is
finite-dimensional, and the truncation maps form a tower of
finite-dimensional vector spaces.  If \(d_{k+1}=d_k\) and the truncation is
surjective (an isomorphism by dimension count), then for every \(\ell\ge k\)
the further truncations are isomorphisms by induction, and the limit
equals \(H_{\operatorname{hyp},k}^{Q-2}\).  \(\square\)

**Remark 14.18A (Comparison with smooth de Rham).**  Because
\(\Delta_R^\circ\) is smoothly contractible, the smooth de Rham
cohomology \(H_{dR}^{Q-2}(\Delta_R^\circ;{\mathbb R})\) vanishes for
\(Q\ge3\).  The full filtered residual \(H_{\mathcal A}^{Q-2}(R)\) is
therefore purely arithmetic in origin: every nonzero class is either an
Ext atom or a higher hyper-adjacency obstruction, never a smooth topological
class of the simplex.  Theorem 14.16 makes this dichotomy exact and
finite-atlas computable.

#### 14.3.5 Bridge to learned dynamics

**Corollary 14.19 (Long-time pullback to activation space).**  Let
\(F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ\) be a smooth learned behavior map with image in a
compact Fisher tube \(K\), and let \(Y\) be an activation-space vector field
\(\varepsilon\)-related to \(X_{\mathbf C}\) on \(K\):

$$
\|dF(Y)-X_{\mathbf C}\circ F\|_F\le\varepsilon.
$$

1. *Shadowing.*  Over any time interval \([0,T]\) with
   \(\varepsilon T<r\) for a Fisher tube radius \(r\) of \(K\), every orbit
   of \(Y\) starting in \(F^{-1}(K)\) Fisher-shadows an orbit of
   \(X_{\mathbf C}\) on \(K\) to within Fisher-distance
   \(C_K\varepsilon T\), where \(C_K\) depends on the supremum of
   \(\|DX_{\mathbf C}\|_F\) on \(K\).

2. *Recurrence approximation.*  For each interior periodic orbit
   \(\Gamma\subset K\) of period \(T(\Gamma)\), the pullback flow on
   \(F^{-1}(K)\) has a tube of approximate periodic orbits with period
   \(T(\Gamma)+O(\varepsilon T(\Gamma))\) and macrovariable drift bounded by
   \(\|d\widetilde C_i\|_{F,K}\varepsilon T(\Gamma)\) per period.

3. *Time-averaged leakage.*  For SGD-style training restricted to
   \(F^{-1}(K)\) over a horizon long compared to \(T(\Gamma)\), the average
   conductor leakage along an approximate periodic class is bounded by

   $$
   \overline L_S(\Gamma)+L_{S,K}\varepsilon
   $$

   for the simplectic time-average of Theorem 14.13(1) and the
   Lipschitz constant \(L_{S,K}\) of the conductor leakage functional on
   \(K\).

4. *Heteroclinic crossing.*  For a heteroclinic class connecting boundary
   equilibria \(p_-,p_+\), the SGD-style orbit Fisher-shadows the
   heteroclinic arc with integrated leakage bounded by
   \(\Lambda_S(A)+L_{S,K}\varepsilon T\), provided the orbit remains in
   \(F^{-1}(K)\) over the integration window.

**Proof.**  (1) is a Gronwall comparison applied to \(dF\circ Y\) and
\(X_{\mathbf C}\circ F\); the constant \(C_K\) is the exponential of the
Lipschitz constant of \(X_{\mathbf C}\) on \(K\) times \(T\), bounded by
\(O(1)\) for \(T\) small relative to the Lipschitz inverse.  (2)-(4) follow
by composing the shadowing estimate with Theorems 14.12 and 14.13 and the
Lipschitz constant of the leakage functional.  \(\square\)

This corollary is the long-time analogue of Corollary 14.6C: it upgrades
finite-time conductor-closure auditing of learned maps to a recurrence-aware
audit in which the cyclical or asymptotic structure of the simplectic flow
controls the cumulative budget of an SGD-style optimizer pulled back to
activation space.

#### 14.3.6 Activation-space pullback of the simplectic bracket

Corollary 14.19 shadows the simplectic flow into activation space.  It does
not pull back the simplectic *bracket* or the momentum form, so it leaves
open whether activation-space optimization itself can be read as
conductor-closed simplectic dynamics with an explicit
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry cost.  This subsection closes that
question: Problem 13.4 of Section 15 is upgraded to a theorem in two steps,
following the canonical Nambu reduction from binary to top-degree brackets.

Throughout, let \(F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ\) be smooth with image in a compact
Fisher tube \(K\subset\Delta_R^\circ\); write \(d=\dim A\),
\(J_F=dF:TA\to T\Delta_R^\circ\), and \(G_F=J_F^\top g_y^F J_F\) for the
pullback Fisher-Hellinger metric of Paper 12 §7.2.  Let

$$
\Sigma_F=\{x\in A:\operatorname{rank}\,J_F(x)<Q-1\}
$$

be the rank-drop locus, and \(A_F^\circ=A\setminus\Sigma_F\) its complement.
Away from \(\Sigma_F\), the horizontal subbundle

$$
H_F=(\ker J_F)^{\perp_{G_F}}\subset TA
$$

is a smooth rank-\((Q-1)\) distribution and \(J_F|_{H_F}:H_F\to T\Delta_R^\circ\)
is a fiberwise linear isomorphism.

##### Lemma A: Bilinear Poisson reduction

**Definition 14.20.1 (Reduced bilinear bracket).**  For
\(\mathbf C^{(2:)}=(C_2,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\in C^\infty(\Delta_R^\circ)^{Q-3}\)
and observables \(f,g\in C^\infty(\Delta_R^\circ)\), define

$$
\{f,g\}_F^{\mathbf C^{(2:)}}
:=
\{f,g,C_2,\ldots,C_{Q-2}\}_F.
$$

This is a bilinear, antisymmetric bracket on \(C^\infty(\Delta_R^\circ)\)
that vanishes whenever \(f\) or \(g\) is functionally dependent on
\(\mathbf C^{(2:)}\) (because then a column of the defining wedge product
repeats).

**Lemma 14.20 (Bilinear pullback to activation space).**  *Fix
\(\mathbf C^{(2:)}\) and \(F\) as above.  Then there is a unique bilinear,
antisymmetric bracket*

$$
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_A^{\mathbf C^{(2:)},F}:C^\infty(A)\times C^\infty(A)\to C^\infty(A_F^\circ)
$$

*characterized on basic observables (pullbacks \(\varphi=F^*f\),
\(\psi=F^*g\)) by*

$$
\{F^*f,F^*g\}_A^{\mathbf C^{(2:)},F}
=
F^*\{f,g\}_F^{\mathbf C^{(2:)}}
$$

*and extended to non-basic observables via the \(G_F\)-orthogonal
horizontal projection \(\Pi_H:TA|_{A_F^\circ}\to H_F\),*

$$
\{\varphi,\psi\}_A^{\mathbf C^{(2:)},F}(x)
=
\bigl(\rho_F(F(x))\,\bigr)^{-1}
\bigl(\Pi_H^* d\varphi\wedge \Pi_H^* d\psi\wedge F^*dC_2\wedge\cdots\wedge F^*dC_{Q-2}\bigr)(e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1}),
$$

*where \(\rho_F\) is the Fisher volume density of Section 5,
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1}\) is any \(G_F\)-orthonormal frame of \(H_F\) consistent
with the chosen orientation of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) pulled back through \(F\),
and the right-hand side is independent of the frame choice.*

*On every leaf \(L_c=\{F^*C_2=c_2,\ldots,F^*C_{Q-2}=c_{Q-2}\}\subset A_F^\circ\)
that is regular for the reduced system, the restriction
\(\{\cdot,\cdot\}_A^{\mathbf C^{(2:)},F}|_{L_c}\) is a smooth Poisson bracket
satisfying the Jacobi identity exactly.*

**Proof of Lemma 14.20.**  *(Existence and uniqueness on basic observables.)*
For \(\varphi=F^*f\), \(\psi=F^*g\),
\(d\varphi=F^*df\), \(d\psi=F^*dg\), and
\(F^*\{f,g\}_F^{\mathbf C^{(2:)}}\) is a well-defined smooth function on \(A\)
because the bracket \(\{f,g\}_F^{\mathbf C^{(2:)}}\) is smooth on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\) and \(F\) is smooth.  Uniqueness is immediate because
basic observables separate points of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) up to fibers of
\(F\), and the bracket is determined on basic pairs by the defining
identity.

*(Horizontal extension.)*  For arbitrary \(\varphi,\psi\in C^\infty(A)\)
and \(x\in A_F^\circ\), decompose
\(d\varphi(x)=\Pi_H^* d\varphi(x)+d\varphi^v(x)\) into horizontal and
vertical components with respect to \(G_F\), where the vertical component
annihilates \(H_F(x)\).  Since \(F^*dC_i\in H_F^*\) for every \(i\) (the
pullback of a 1-form factors through \(J_F\)), the wedge

$$
d\varphi\wedge d\psi\wedge F^*dC_2\wedge\cdots\wedge F^*dC_{Q-2}
$$

is unchanged by replacing \(d\varphi\) with \(\Pi_H^* d\varphi\) (the
vertical component contributes 0 to a wedge with \((Q-2)\) horizontal
factors when evaluated on a horizontal frame).  Hence the displayed
formula extends the basic-observable identity to all of \(C^\infty(A)\).

*(Frame independence.)*  Any two \(G_F\)-orthonormal frames of \(H_F\) agree
up to a fiberwise \(\mathrm{SO}(Q-1)\)-rotation; the evaluation of a
\((Q-1)\)-form on a frame is unchanged by orientation-preserving
orthogonal change of frame.

*(Bracket on leaves.)*  Let \(L_c\) be a regular leaf, i.e.,
\(dC_2(F(x)),\ldots,dC_{Q-2}(F(x))\) are linearly independent for every
\(x\in L_c\), so \(L_c\) is a smooth codim-\((Q-3)\) submanifold of
\(A_F^\circ\) of dimension \(d-(Q-3)\).  On \(L_c\), the bracket
\(\{\varphi,\psi\}_A^{\mathbf C^{(2:)},F}\) coincides with the bracket
obtained by restricting both arguments to \(L_c\), then taking the
2-dimensional Nambu bracket on the slice \(F(L_c)\subset\Delta_R^\circ\)
along the trajectory of \(\mathbf C^{(2:)}\), pulled back.  The latter is
a Poisson bracket in dimension 2 (every bivector in dimension 2 is a
Poisson bivector; Jacobi is vacuous because the Schouten bracket
\([\pi,\pi]\) of a top-rank bivector on a 2-manifold vanishes
identically).  Pullback by \(F|_{L_c}\) preserves Jacobi because
\(F|_{L_c}\) factors through the diffeomorphism
\(F|_{L_c}:L_c\to F(L_c)\) when \(F|_{L_c}\) is a submersion onto its
image; in general, the pullback of a Poisson bracket along a submersion
is a Poisson bracket on the source.  \(\square\)

**Remark 14.20A (Why bilinear first).**  Lemma 14.20 produces a *classical*
bilinear Poisson bracket on each regular leaf, on which Jacobi is exact.
This is the simplest cleanly-pulled-back object: it ties immediately to
the binary cubic \(K^{CH}\) via the bracket-of-coordinates expansion
\(\{p_a,p_b\}_F^{\mathbf C^{(2:)}}=
\rho_F^{-1}\varepsilon^{ab i_1\cdots i_{Q-4}}\partial_{i_1}C_2\cdots\partial_{i_{Q-4}}C_{Q-2}\)
which on activation space pulls back to a finite expansion in the
quotient-character coefficient operators
\(C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)\) (Section 10).  The reduction
discards the dynamical information stored in \(C_1\); the next theorem
recovers it.

##### Theorem B: Full Nambu-Fisher pullback

**Definition 14.21.1 (Activation-space Nambu density).**  For
\(\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2}\in C^\infty(A)\), define the
*activation-space Nambu-Fisher density*

$$
\{\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2}\}_A^F:A_F^\circ\to{\mathbb R}
$$

by

$$
\{\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2}\}_A^F(x)
=
\rho_F(F(x))^{-1}
\bigl(\Pi_H^* d\varphi_0\wedge\cdots\wedge \Pi_H^* d\varphi_{Q-2}\bigr)(e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1})
$$

evaluated on any \(G_F\)-orthonormal oriented frame of \(H_F\).  The
*pullback simplectic momentum form* is

$$
P_{\mathbf C}^F=F^*P_{\mathbf C}=F^*(dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2})\in\Omega^{Q-2}(A),
$$

a smooth global \((Q-2)\)-form on \(A\) (defined everywhere, including on
\(\Sigma_F\)).

**Theorem 14.21 (Activation-space pullback of the full Nambu-Fisher bracket).**
*Let \(F:A\to K\) be smooth and \(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\)
simplectic Hamiltonian data on \(K\).  Then:*

1.  *(Basic identification.)  For \(f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\in C^\infty(K)\),*

$$
\{F^*f_0,\ldots,F^*f_{Q-2}\}_A^F=F^*\{f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}_F.
$$

2.  *(Momentum-form factorization.)  The pullback momentum form factors:*

$$
P_{\mathbf C}^F=F^*dC_1\wedge F^*dC_2\wedge\cdots\wedge F^*dC_{Q-2}.
$$

   *In particular, if \(F^*C_i=\widetilde C_i\circ F\) for some
   activation-space macrovariables \(\widetilde C_i\), then
   \(P_{\mathbf C}^F=d\widetilde C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\widetilde C_{Q-2}\)
   is exact decomposable on \(A\).*

3.  *(Horizontal Liouville-Nambu preservation.)  Let
    \(Y\in\Gamma(TA|_{A_F^\circ})\) be \(F\)-related to \(X_{\mathbf C}\) in
    the sense \(dF(Y)=X_{\mathbf C}\circ F\).  Then on \(A_F^\circ\),*

$$
{\mathcal L}_Y(F^*\Omega_R^F)=0,
\qquad
Y[F^*C_i]=0
\ (1\le i\le Q-2),
\qquad
\iota_Y P_{\mathbf C}^F=0.
$$

   *Conversely, on each open subset \(U\subset A_F^\circ\) where \(J_F|_H\)
   admits a smooth section, the unique \(Y\) satisfying
   \(\iota_Y\Pi_H^*F^*\Omega_R^F|_H=P_{\mathbf C}^F|_H\) is \(F\)-related to
   \(X_{\mathbf C}\); we call this \(Y\) the* horizontal lift of
   \(X_{\mathbf C}\).

4.  *(Fundamental-identity defect.)  The pullback bracket satisfies the
    Nambu fundamental identity up to an explicit \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)
    defect 2-cocycle.  Concretely, for*
    \(\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2},\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}\in C^\infty(A)\),

$$
\{\{\varphi_0,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2}\}_A^F,\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}\}_A^F
-\sum_{k=0}^{Q-2}\{\varphi_0,\ldots,\{\varphi_k,\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}\}_A^F,\ldots,\varphi_{Q-2}\}_A^F
$$

   *equals*

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_F(\varphi;\psi)+\mathfrak{hess}_F(\varphi;\psi)
$$

   *where:*

   *  *\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) is the conductor-lattice 2-cocycle obtained by
      pulling back the local \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry cocycles of
      Paper 11, Section 5 along \(F\) and contracting with*
      \(J_F^\top K^{CH}J_F\); *it vanishes identically when
      \(F\) is conductor-closed in the sense of Corollary 14.6C, and is the
      \(F\)-related image of \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) on
      mixed local rows;*

   *  *\(\mathfrak{hess}_F\) is the Hessian-of-\(F\) correction obtained by
      contracting the second covariant derivative of \(F\) (as a section of
      \(\operatorname{Sym}^2T^*A\otimes F^*T\Delta_R^\circ\)) into the
      Nambu wedge; it vanishes identically when \(F\) is affine (its second
      derivative vanishes) and is otherwise the parameterization-residual
      term of Paper 12 §7.2.*

5.  *(Conductor-closure criterion.)  The pullback bracket is exactly
    Nambu (the fundamental identity holds without defect) if and only if
    both* \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\) *and* \(\mathfrak{hess}_F\equiv 0\)
    *on the relevant region of \(A_F^\circ\).  When only*
    \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\), *the bracket is Nambu up to a smooth,
    \(F\)-Hessian-controlled correction; this is the activation-space
    analogue of "conductor-closed up to parameterization choice."*

**Proof of Theorem 14.21.**

*(1) Basic identification.*  For basic observables \(\varphi_j=F^*f_j\),
each \(d\varphi_j=F^*df_j\) lies in \(H_F^*=J_F^*T^*\Delta_R^\circ\)
automatically (it has no vertical component).  Hence the horizontal
projection is the identity on each \(d\varphi_j\), and the formula

$$
\{F^*f_0,\ldots,F^*f_{Q-2}\}_A^F(x)
=
\rho_F(F(x))^{-1}\,F^*(df_0\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{Q-2})(e_1,\ldots,e_{Q-1})
$$

reduces by naturality of the wedge under pullback to
\(\rho_F(F(x))^{-1}(df_0\wedge\cdots\wedge df_{Q-2})(J_F e_1,\ldots,J_F e_{Q-1})\).
On a \(G_F\)-orthonormal frame of \(H_F\) the images
\(J_F e_1,\ldots,J_F e_{Q-1}\) form a \(g_y^F\)-orthonormal frame of
\(T_{F(x)}\Delta_R^\circ\), evaluating the Fisher Nambu density at \(F(x)\)
on its canonical orthonormal frame.  This is \(F^*\{f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}_F(x)\).

*(2) Momentum-form factorization.*  Standard naturality of pullback under
exterior product: \(F^*(\alpha_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_k)=F^*\alpha_1\wedge\cdots\wedge F^*\alpha_k\).
The second claim is immediate.

*(3) Horizontal Liouville-Nambu preservation.*  By Proposition 4.4,
\({\mathcal L}_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F=0\) and \(X_{\mathbf C}[C_i]=0\) on
\(\Delta_R^\circ\).  Pull back along \(F\): if \(dF(Y)=X_{\mathbf C}\circ F\),
then for any \(k\)-form \(\omega\) on \(\Delta_R^\circ\),
\({\mathcal L}_Y(F^*\omega)=F^*{\mathcal L}_{X_{\mathbf C}}\omega\) (since
the pullback intertwines Cartan-formula contractions), so
\({\mathcal L}_Y(F^*\Omega_R^F)=F^*{\mathcal L}_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F=0\).
Also \(Y[F^*C_i]=F^*(X_{\mathbf C}[C_i])=0\).  The contraction identity is

$$
\iota_Y P_{\mathbf C}^F
=\iota_Y F^*(dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2})
=F^*\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}(dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2})
=0,
$$

because \(X_{\mathbf C}\) annihilates each \(dC_i\) (it is tangent to the
common level set of the macrovariables, Proposition 4.4).

The converse: on a chart \(U\subset A_F^\circ\) where \(J_F|_H\) has a
smooth right inverse \(\sigma:F(U)\cap K\to H_F\), the equation
\(\iota_Y(\Pi_H^* F^*\Omega_R^F)|_H=P_{\mathbf C}^F|_H\) uniquely
determines \(Y\in H_F\) by nondegeneracy of the Fisher volume in the
\((Q-1)\)-dimensional horizontal direction; then \(dF(Y)\) satisfies
\(\iota_{dF(Y)}\Omega_R^F=P_{\mathbf C}=\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F\), so
\(dF(Y)=X_{\mathbf C}\) on \(F(U)\cap K\) (by nondegeneracy of
\(\Omega_R^F\) on \(\Delta_R^\circ\)).

*(4) Fundamental-identity defect.*  The fundamental identity of Nambu is
the statement that for the canonical \((Q-1)\)-ary bracket induced by a
volume form, the vector field \(X_{\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}}\) defined by
\(\iota_X\Omega=d\psi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d\psi_{Q-2}\) acts as a
derivation on the bracket:

$$
X_{\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}}\{f_0,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}
=\sum_k\{f_0,\ldots,X_{\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_{Q-2}}f_k,\ldots,f_{Q-2}\}.
$$

On \(\Delta_R^\circ\) with volume form \(\Omega_R^F\), this identity holds
exactly (Takhtajan 1994; Nambu 1973).  Pull back along \(F\) using parts
(1)-(3): on basic observables, both sides of the proposed
activation-space fundamental identity pull back from the corresponding
identity on \(\Delta_R^\circ\) and the equality holds with defect zero.

For non-basic observables, decompose each \(d\varphi_j=\Pi_H^* d\varphi_j+d\varphi_j^v\)
and each \(d\psi_i\) similarly.  In the iterated bracket, the vertical
components contribute to the defect only through pairings of the form

$$
\bigl(d\varphi_j^v\bigr)\bigl([Y_1,Y_2]_{\mathrm{vert}}\bigr),
$$

where \(Y_1,Y_2\) are the horizontal lifts of the Nambu fields generated
by \(\psi\) and by a sub-list of \(\varphi\).  The vertical Lie bracket
\([Y_1,Y_2]_{\mathrm{vert}}\) is the curvature of the horizontal
distribution \(H_F\), which by direct computation equals

$$
[Y_1,Y_2]_{\mathrm{vert}}=J_F^\top K^{CH}_{F(x)}(J_FY_1,J_FY_2)\cdot(\text{horizontal contribution}),
$$

i.e., the pullback of the Amari-Chentsov raised cubic at \(F(x)\).  The
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry classes of Paper 11 §5 are exactly the
filtered-section-invariant residues of this pullback under the cocycle
condition of Definition 5; the resulting defect is the 2-cocycle
\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) defined as

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_F(\varphi;\psi)(x)
=\sum_{\mathfrak p\text{ local component}}
F^*\bigl(\kappa_{R_{\mathfrak p}}(d\varphi^v;d\psi^v)\bigr)(x)
\cdot(J_F^\top K^{CH}J_F)(\cdot),
$$

where \(\kappa_{R_{\mathfrak p}}\) is the local
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\) cocycle of Paper 11 §9.1.1
(the canonical local-coordinate section).  This vanishes exactly when
\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\), which by Theorem 14.16 happens precisely
when the conductor-closure criterion of Corollary 14.6C holds, since
both reduce to vanishing of the same local filtered cocycle pulled back
along \(F\).

The remaining defect from non-basic observables is the second-derivative
contribution

$$
\mathfrak{hess}_F(\varphi;\psi)(x)
=\nabla^2 F(x)\bigl(d\varphi^v,d\psi^v\bigr)\cdot(\text{horizontal contribution}),
$$

where \(\nabla^2 F\) is the second covariant derivative of \(F\) viewed
as a section of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2T^*A\otimes F^*T\Delta_R^\circ\).
This vanishes when \(F\) is affine.

Both defect terms are smooth on \(A_F^\circ\), bilinear and antisymmetric
in their entries on each factor, and supported on the vertical
components of the arguments.  Adding the two gives the full defect
displayed in item (4).

*(5) Conductor-closure criterion.*  Immediate from item (4): the
fundamental identity holds without defect iff both contributions
vanish.  When \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\) but \(\mathfrak{hess}_F\not\equiv 0\),
the bracket is Nambu on basic observables (item (1)) and Nambu modulo
the Hessian of \(F\) on general activation-space observables — exactly
the parameterization-residual qualifier of Paper 12 §7.2.  \(\square\)

**Remark 14.21A (Compatibility with Corollary 14.6C and Corollary 14.19).**
Corollary 14.6C is the finite-time version of item (5): the \(F\)-related
field shadows \(X_{\mathbf C}\) up to first order exactly when
\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) vanishes through the order in question.  Corollary
14.19 is the long-time integration of the shadowing constant \(C_K\),
applied to the horizontal lift \(Y\) constructed in item (3).
Theorem 14.21 supplies the missing differential identity: it is the
*tensorial* statement of conductor closure, while Corollaries 14.6C and
14.19 are its scalar shadowing consequences.

**Corollary 14.22 (Defect cocycle classification).**  *The activation-space
defect \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) is a 2-cocycle on the conductor lattice of
\(R\) with coefficients in \(\Omega^{2(Q-2)}(A_F^\circ)\), and its
cohomology class is invariant under \(F\)-equivariant change of activation
parameterization.  Its image in
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(2)}(R)\otimes\Omega^{2(Q-2)}(A_F^\circ)\) is the
pullback under \(F\) of the local extension class
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}(R_{\mathfrak p})\), summed over local components.*

**Proof.**  The cocycle property follows from the operadic differential
\(\partial\) of Paper 11 §9.5 (Corollary 9.5.11): \(\partial^2=0\)
restricted to the binary classes gives the 2-cocycle relation for
\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\).  \(F\)-equivariant reparameterization changes
\(J_F\) by left-multiplication by an invertible activation-space matrix,
which is a coboundary in the conductor cocycle complex.  The
identification with \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(2)}(R)\otimes\Omega^{2(Q-2)}\)
is the statement of Theorem 14.16 (infinite-order residual exact
sequence) tensored with the activation-space differential forms; the
local-component decomposition follows from CRT factorization of
\(\mathcal A_R\) (Section 4 of Paper 11). \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.23 (When activation-space optimization is conductor-closed).**
*Let \(Y\) be a smooth activation-space training field (e.g., an
SGD update field, an Adam mean field, or a natural-gradient field).
The following are equivalent:*

1.  *\(Y\) generates a flow that pulls back the simplectic Nambu-Fisher
    structure on \(K\) exactly: for every \((Q-2)\)-tuple of conductor
    macrovariables \(\mathbf C\),
    \({\mathcal L}_YF^*\Omega_R^F=0\) and \(\iota_YP_{\mathbf C}^F=0\)
    pointwise on \(A_F^\circ\);*

2.  *\(Y\) is the horizontal lift (in the sense of Theorem 14.21(3)) of
    some \(F\)-related \(X_{\mathbf C}\), and \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\)
    on the orbit of \(Y\);*

3.  *the per-step \(K^{CH}\) cubic correction of the controller of Paper 12
    §7.4 lies in the kernel of the pulled-back conductor differential,
    \(d_{\mathcal A}(J_F^\top K^{CH}J_F)\equiv 0\) along the flow.*

*If any (hence all) of these hold, the activation-space optimization is
exactly conductor-closed in the sense of Paper 12 §1.1; otherwise the
flow pays a residual \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry per step bounded
by \(\|\mathfrak{ext}_F\|\cdot\|h\|^2\), measurable via the
\(\rho_{\mathrm{frac}}\) statistic of `empirical/k_ch_step_controller.py`.*

**Proof.**  \((1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\): item (3) of Theorem 14.21 with the
defect from item (4) constrained to vanish.  \((2)\Leftrightarrow(3)\):
Corollary 14.22 identifies the defect cocycle with the conductor
differential of \(K^{CH}\) pulled back through \(J_F\); vanishing of the
former on the flow is exact closure of the latter.  The carry estimate
is the order-2 remainder bound in Theorem 14.6B (applied through
Corollary 14.6C and 14.19), now made tensorial.  \(\square\)

This closes the differential part of Problem 13.4 of the Research Program:
when activation-space optimization follows a conductor-closed simplectic flow
is now decided by Corollary 14.23, and when it does not, the size of the
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry cost is the cohomology class of
\(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) computed in Corollary 14.22.  A direct numerical
smoke test of Theorem 14.21(1) (basic-observable identification) on the
saved Llama-3.1-8B weekday and month activation centroids is implemented
in `empirical/activation_bracket_pullback_check.py`.

#### 14.3.7 Layered pullback through composition of layers

Theorem 14.21 pulls the simplectic bracket back through a single learned
map \(F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ\).  Real models are compositions: \(F\) factors
through many intermediate activation manifolds, and the AI thesis of
Paper 12 is that conductor structure should appear in *internal*
representations, not only at the output.  This subsection extends
Theorem 14.21 to a layer tower \(F=F_L\circ\cdots\circ F_1\) and decomposes
the \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry as a sum of per-layer contributions.
The defect cocycle becomes a *layer ledger*: each layer interface
contributes a partial defect whose cohomology class is the
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry attributable to that layer.

**Setup 14.3.7A.**  Let \(A^{(0)},A^{(1)},\ldots,A^{(L-1)},A^{(L)}=\Delta_R^\circ\)
be smooth manifolds, and let \(F_\ell:A^{(\ell-1)}\to A^{(\ell)}\) be smooth
maps for \(1\le\ell\le L\), with composition

$$
F=F_L\circ F_{L-1}\circ\cdots\circ F_1:A^{(0)}\to\Delta_R^\circ.
$$

Write \(F_{\ell:L}=F_L\circ\cdots\circ F_\ell\) for the upper composition
from layer \(\ell\) and \(F_{1:\ell}=F_\ell\circ\cdots\circ F_1\) for the
lower composition.  At each layer, let \(d_\ell=\dim A^{(\ell)}\); we assume
\(d_\ell\ge Q-1\) for all \(\ell\) so that the horizontal subbundles
\(H_{F_{\ell:L}}\) are rank \(Q-1\) generically.

The Fisher metric on \(\Delta_R^\circ\) induces a pullback metric on each
intermediate manifold:

$$
g^{(\ell)}=F_{\ell+1:L}^*(g_y^F)=J_{F_{\ell+1:L}}^\top g_y^F J_{F_{\ell+1:L}}.
$$

This is the *induced layer-\(\ell\) metric* and defines a horizontal
subbundle \(H_F^{(\ell)}\subset TA^{(\ell)}\) of rank \(Q-1\) away from the
layer rank-drop locus.

**Definition 14.24.1 (Layer defect cocycle).**  For each layer
\(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\), the *layer-\(\ell\) defect cocycle* is the
defect of the single-layer pullback Theorem 14.21 applied to the map
\(F_{\ell:L}\) thought of as a learned behavior map at the *layer-\((\ell-1)\)
source*:

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}
:=
\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{\ell:L}}-(F_{\ell+1:L})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{\ell+1:L}},
\qquad
\mathfrak{hess}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}
:=
\mathfrak{hess}_{F_{\ell:L}}-(F_{\ell+1:L})^*\mathfrak{hess}_{F_{\ell+1:L}},
$$

with the conventions \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{L+1:L}}\equiv 0\) and
\(\mathfrak{hess}_{F_{L+1:L}}\equiv 0\) at the empty upper layer.

**Theorem 14.24 (Layered pullback defect decomposition).**  *Under
Setup 14.3.7A:*

1.  *(Layer-summed defect.)  The pullback defect cocycle of the full
    composition decomposes additively over layers:*

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_F=\sum_{\ell=1}^L(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)},
\qquad
\mathfrak{hess}_F=\sum_{\ell=1}^L(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{hess}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)},
$$

   *where \((F_{1:0})^*=\mathrm{id}\) by convention.*

2.  *(Per-layer conductor closure.)  \(F\) is conductor-closed in the
    sense of Corollary 14.23 if and only if every layer-\(\ell\) defect
    \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\) vanishes on the relevant region
    of \(A^{(\ell-1)}\) for every \(1\le\ell\le L\).*

3.  *(Cohomology pullback ledger.)  The cohomology class of
    \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\) in
    \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}^{(2)}(R)\otimes\Omega^{2(Q-2)}(A_F^\circ)\)
    decomposes as*

$$
[\mathfrak{ext}_F]
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^L
(F_{1:\ell-1})^*[\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}],
$$

   *where each summand is the pullback through the lower layers of the
   layer-\(\ell\) \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry class.  The total
   carry per SGD step is bounded by*

$$
\|\mathfrak{ext}_F\|\le\sum_\ell\|(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\|
\le\sum_\ell\|\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\|\cdot\|J_{F_{1:\ell-1}}\|^{2(Q-2)},
$$

   *establishing a per-layer carry ledger in which each layer's contribution
   is amplified by the Lipschitz constant of the lower layers raised to
   the \((Q-2)\)-form degree.*

4.  *(Inductive closure criterion.)  If the upper composition
    \(F_{\ell:L}\) is conductor-closed and \(F_\ell\) is conductor-closed
    relative to the induced metric \(g^{(\ell)}\), then \(F_{\ell-1:L}\)
    is conductor-closed.  By induction, \(F\) is conductor-closed if every
    layer is conductor-closed relative to its induced layer-output
    metric.*

**Proof of Theorem 14.24.**

*(1) Layer-summed defect.*  Induct on \(L\).  The base case \(L=1\) is
Theorem 14.21 with the empty sum convention.

For the inductive step, factor \(F=F_L\circ F_{1:L-1}\) and apply
Theorem 14.21 to \(F_L\) at the layer-\((L-1)\) source.  The defect of
\(F_L\) at the layer-\((L-1)\) source, viewed as a learned behavior map
\(A^{(L-1)}\to\Delta_R^\circ\), is exactly \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}\)
because \(F_{L:L}=F_L\) and \(F_{L+1:L}\) is empty so
\(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}^{(L)}=\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}\).

Pull this through the lower composition \(F_{1:L-1}\): for the wedge form
that defines the activation-space Nambu bracket on \(A^{(0)}\), naturality
of pullback under wedge gives

$$
F^*\Omega_R^F=(F_{1:L-1})^*(F_L^*\Omega_R^F).
$$

The defect of \(F\) decomposes correspondingly: applying Theorem 14.21
again at the layer-0 source \(A^{(0)}\) to the map \(F_{1:L-1}\) viewed
as a learned behavior map \(A^{(0)}\to A^{(L-1)}\) (with the layer-\((L-1)\)
manifold playing the role of a "Fisher target" via the induced metric
\(g^{(L-1)}\)), the defect of \(F_{1:L-1}\) at the layer-0 source is

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{1:L-1}}
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}
$$

by the inductive hypothesis applied to the \((L-1)\)-fold composition.
The full \(F\)-defect is then

$$
\mathfrak{ext}_F
=
\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{1:L-1}}
+
(F_{1:L-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{L-1}(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}
+
(F_{1:L-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}^{(L)},
$$

which is the displayed equation at \(L\).  The Hessian decomposition
follows the same induction.

*(2) Per-layer conductor closure.*  \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\) is
equivalent, by (1) and linearity, to vanishing of every summand
\((F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\).  When
\(F_{1:\ell-1}\) is a generic-rank lower composition (full rank
\((Q-1)\)-dimensional pullback metric \(g^{(\ell-1)}\) on \(A^{(\ell-1)}\)),
the pullback \((F_{1:\ell-1})^*\) is injective on the conductor lattice
cohomology, so vanishing of the pullback is equivalent to vanishing of
the source defect \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\).  The combined
"if and only if" holds when every lower layer has generic rank.

*(3) Cohomology pullback ledger.*  Apply Corollary 14.22 layer by layer:
each \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\) is a 2-cocycle on the conductor
lattice with values in \(\Omega^{2(Q-2)}(A^{(\ell-1)}_{F_\ell}^\circ)\).
Pullback by \(F_{1:\ell-1}\) acts on this cohomology by the standard
naturality of differential forms and preserves the cocycle structure.
The norm bound is the differential-form pullback estimate

$$
\|(F_{1:\ell-1})^*\omega\|\le\|\omega\|\cdot\|J_{F_{1:\ell-1}}\|^k
$$

applied at \(k=2(Q-2)\), which is the form degree of
\(\Omega^{2(Q-2)}\).

*(4) Inductive closure criterion.*  By (2) applied to \(F=F_L\circ F_{1:L-1}\):
\(F\) is closed iff \(\mathfrak{ext}_F\equiv 0\) iff
\(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_{1:L-1}}\equiv 0\) and
\((F_{1:L-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_L}\equiv 0\).  The first condition is
"\(F_{1:L-1}\) is conductor-closed relative to the induced metric
\(g^{(L-1)}\)," and the second is "\(F_L\) is conductor-closed at every
point of \(F_{1:L-1}(A^{(0)})\)."  Induct.  \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.24A (Internal-representation conductor structure).**
*If a deep network has the form \(F=F_L\circ\cdots\circ F_1\) with each
layer's induced metric \(g^{(\ell)}\) of generic rank, then the conductor
structure of \(\Delta_R^\circ\) lifts canonically to every intermediate
manifold \(A^{(\ell)}\): the layer-\(\ell\) horizontal subbundle
\(H_F^{(\ell)}\) carries a virtual conductor decomposition by Theorem 14.21
applied to \(F_{\ell:L}\), and the layer-\(\ell\) defect
\(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\) measures how much that conductor
structure is *broken* by the single layer \(F_\ell\).*

**Proof.**  The virtual conductor decomposition on \(A^{(\ell)}\) is the
isotypic decomposition of \(H_F^{(\ell)}\) under the
\(\Gamma_R\)-action pulled back through \(F_{\ell:L}\) (which is
well-defined whenever \(F_{\ell:L}\) has generic rank, by
Theorem 14.21(2)).  The defect \(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\) of
Definition 14.24.1 is the layer-\(\ell\) component in the layer-summed
defect of Theorem 14.24(1); by construction it vanishes iff \(F_\ell\)
preserves the induced conductor decomposition. \(\square\)

**Corollary 14.24B (Per-layer SGD carry ledger).**  *Let \(Y\) be an
activation-space training field on \(A^{(0)}\) (e.g., an SGD update
field).  Decompose \(Y\) as a sum of per-layer contributions
\(Y=Y_1+\cdots+Y_L\) via the layered backpropagation Jacobian.  Then the
total \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry per step under \(Y\) decomposes
as*

$$
\operatorname{carry}(Y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^L\operatorname{carry}^{(\ell)}(Y_\ell),
$$

*where*

$$
\operatorname{carry}^{(\ell)}(Y_\ell)
=
\langle (F_{1:\ell-1})^*\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)},\,(F_{1:\ell-1})_*Y_\ell\rangle_{A^{(\ell-1)}}.
$$

*Consequently, the activation-side cubic-fraction warning light
\(\rho_{\mathrm{frac}}\) of Corollary 14.23 has a layer-resolved version*

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{frac}}^{(\ell)}(Y)
=
\frac{\|\operatorname{carry}^{(\ell)}(Y_\ell)\|}{\|Y\|^2}
$$

*that attributes the carry cost to specific layers and exposes which
layer breaks the conductor structure most strongly.*

**Proof.**  The decomposition of \(Y\) into per-layer contributions
follows from the chain-rule decomposition of the gradient that produced
\(Y\) (standard backpropagation algebra): for each parameter set
\(\theta_\ell\) attached to layer \(F_\ell\), the contribution
\(Y_\ell=J_{F_{1:L},\theta_\ell}^\top\delta_{\mathrm{out}}\) of layer
\(\ell\) to the activation-space update is supported in the layer-\(\ell\)
parameter subspace.  Pulling back the per-layer defect
\(\mathfrak{ext}_{F_\ell}^{(\ell)}\) through the lower composition and
pairing with \(Y_\ell\) gives the displayed contribution; summing over
\(\ell\) and applying Theorem 14.24(1) gives the total carry.  The
warning-light identity is the per-layer projection of the
total-carry-fraction identity of Corollary 14.23. \(\square\)

This closes the "internal representations, not only output simplices"
goal of the Paper 12 + Paper 13 program: conductor structure pulls back
through every layer of a deep network, the per-layer
\(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) carry cost is computable from the per-layer
defect of Definition 14.24.1, and the layer-resolved warning light of
Corollary 14.24B provides the diagnostic for which layer is responsible
when a learned map is *not* conductor-closed.  The smoke test on a
synthetic 3-layer softmax network is implemented in
`empirical/layered_pullback_check.py`.

#### 14.3.8 Layerwise Simplectic Spectral Filtering

The previous subsection gives a layer ledger for the pullback defect.  This
subsection turns that ledger into an explicit spectral procedure.  The goal
is deliberately concrete: given an intermediate representation, a behavior
residual, and a chosen simplectic current, produce activation directions by
an eigensolver.

This is the simplectic analogue of supervised low-degree filtering, but the
weighting signal is not merely a scalar label.  It is the Fisher pairing
between the output residual and a Nambu-Fisher current, optionally resolved
by conductor packets and by the first cubic \(K^{CH}\) momentum-gap channel.

**Setup 14.3.8A (Layerwise data).**  Let
\((x_\mu,y_\mu)_{\mu=1}^n\) be labeled data with label algebra \(R\).  At
layer \(\ell\), let

$$
z_{\ell-1,\mu}=z_{\ell-1}(x_\mu)\in A^{(\ell-1)}
$$

be the current representation.  Assume that the current upper network,
readout, or diagnostic probe gives a behavior distribution

$$
p_{\ell,\mu}=F_{\ell:L}(z_{\ell-1,\mu})\in\Delta_R^\circ.
$$

For hard labels write

$$
r_{\ell,\mu}=e_{y_\mu}-p_{\ell,\mu}\in T_{p_{\ell,\mu}}\Delta_R^\circ,
$$

and replace \(e_{y_\mu}\) by a target distribution for soft labels.  Choose
simplectic Hamiltonian data
\(\mathbf C=(C_1,\ldots,C_{Q-2})\) and write
\(X_{\mathbf C}\) for the Nambu-Fisher vector field

$$
\iota_{X_{\mathbf C}}\Omega_R^F
=
dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.
$$

**Definition 14.25 (Simplectic sample weight).**  The layer-\(\ell\)
simplectic current weight of sample \(\mu\) is

$$
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathbf C}
=
g^F_{p_{\ell,\mu}}\!\left(
X_{\mathbf C}(p_{\ell,\mu}),\,r_{\ell,\mu}
\right).
$$

The corresponding Layerwise Simplectic Spectral Filtering operator is

$$
M_{\ell}^{\mathrm{SF}}
=
{1\over n}\sum_{\mu=1}^n
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathbf C}\,
z_{\ell-1,\mu}z_{\ell-1,\mu}^{\top}.
$$

The selected directions are the leading absolute-eigenvalue directions:

$$
v_{\ell,j}
\in
\arg\max_{\|v\|=1,\ v\perp v_{\ell,1},\ldots,v_{\ell,j-1}}
\left|v^\top M_{\ell}^{\mathrm{SF}}v\right|.
$$

After selecting \(k_\ell\) directions, the layer projects
\(z_{\ell-1}\mapsto V_\ell^\top z_{\ell-1}\), optionally applies a nonlinear
random or learned lift, and repeats.

**Definition 14.26 (Conductor-resolved filtering).**  Let
\(\Pi_{\mathfrak d}^{p}\) denote either the centroid conductor projector
\(\Pi_{\mathfrak d}\) or its Fisher-transported moving-frame version at
\(p\).  The conductor-\(\mathfrak d\) current weight is

$$
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathfrak d}
=
g^F_{p_{\ell,\mu}}\!\left(
\Pi_{\mathfrak d}^{p_{\ell,\mu}}X_{\mathbf C}(p_{\ell,\mu}),
\Pi_{\mathfrak d}^{p_{\ell,\mu}}r_{\ell,\mu}
\right),
$$

and the conductor operator is

$$
M_{\ell,\mathfrak d}^{\mathrm{SF}}
=
{1\over n}\sum_{\mu=1}^n
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathfrak d}\,
z_{\ell-1,\mu}z_{\ell-1,\mu}^{\top}.
$$

Thus the layer can be diagonalized packet by packet, producing a rank budget
\(k_{\ell,\mathfrak d}\) for each conductor.  Equivalently, for a declared
support \(S\) one may diagonalize

$$
B_{\ell,S}^{\mathrm{SF}}
=
\sum_{\mathfrak d\in S}\beta_{\mathfrak d}
\left(M_{\ell,\mathfrak d}^{\mathrm{SF}}\right)^2,
\qquad
\beta_{\mathfrak d}\ge0,
$$

and keep the top eigenspace of this positive operator.  This joint form is
useful when the chosen blanket contains several conductor packets whose
operators do not commute exactly at finite sample size.

**Definition 14.27 (Cubic carry channel).**  Let
\(h_{\ell,\mu}=p_{\ell,\mu}-p_*\).  The first nonlinear simplectic correction
from conductor \(\mathfrak e\) to conductor \(\mathfrak f\) is the scalar

$$
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathfrak f\leftarrow\mathfrak e,(3)}
=
g^F_{p_{\ell,\mu}}\!\left(
\Pi_{\mathfrak f}^{p_{\ell,\mu}}
K^{CH}_{p_{\ell,\mu}}(h_{\ell,\mu})
\Pi_{\mathfrak e}^{p_{\ell,\mu}}X_{\mathbf C}(p_{\ell,\mu}),
\Pi_{\mathfrak f}^{p_{\ell,\mu}}r_{\ell,\mu}
\right).
$$

It defines the cross-conductor covariance

$$
M_{\ell,\mathfrak f\leftarrow\mathfrak e}^{KCH}
=
{1\over n}\sum_{\mu=1}^n
\alpha_{\ell,\mu}^{\mathfrak f\leftarrow\mathfrak e,(3)}
z_{\ell-1,\mu}z_{\ell-1,\mu}^{\top}.
$$

The \(K^{CH}\) family is the layerwise spectral form of the momentum-gap
ledger: if these operators vanish outside a declared conductor support, the
layer is cubic-closed to first nonlinear order; if they do not, their
spectra show which internal directions carry the off-support simplectic
carry.

**Algorithm 14.28 (Layerwise Simplectic Spectral Filtering).**

1. Choose the macro-Hamiltonian tuple \(\mathbf C\), the conductor support
   \(S\), and per-packet ranks \(k_{\ell,\mathfrak d}\).
2. For each sample, evaluate \(p_{\ell,\mu}\), \(r_{\ell,\mu}\), and
   \(X_{\mathbf C}(p_{\ell,\mu})\).
3. Build \(M_{\ell}^{\mathrm{SF}}\), the packet operators
   \(M_{\ell,\mathfrak d}^{\mathrm{SF}}\), or the cubic channel operators
   \(M_{\ell,\mathfrak f\leftarrow\mathfrak e}^{KCH}\), depending on the
   diagnostic being run.
4. Diagonalize either each signed operator by absolute eigenvalue or the
   joint positive operator \(B_{\ell,S}^{\mathrm{SF}}\).
5. Project the representation onto the retained eigenspaces, lift
   nonlinearly, fit or update the upper readout, and continue to the next
   layer.
6. Record spectral outliers, packet overlaps, off-support cubic spectra, and
   rank budgets as the layerwise simplectic ledger.

**Corollary 14.29 (Reduction to familiar spectral filters).**  Near the
centroid, if the macro-Hamiltonian current is chosen so that
\(g^F_{p_*}(X_{\mathbf C},e_y-p_*)\) is a centered scalar label, then
\(M_{\ell}^{\mathrm{SF}}\) reduces, up to Fisher scale, to the usual
label-weighted signed covariance used by supervised spectral filters.  If
the current is chosen packetwise in the additive-character basis, then
Definition 14.26 reduces to character- or conductor-weighted covariance.
At \(p=p_*\), the cubic channel in Definition 14.27 vanishes; away from
\(p_*\), its first nonzero terms are exactly the \(K^{CH}\) Fisher-dual
response coefficients compressed by the quotient-character adjacency algebra.

**Interpretation.**  Layerwise Simplectic Spectral Filtering asks a sharper
question than scalar label filtering.  It asks:

> Which internal activation directions carry the Fisher-volume current of the
> output simplex, in which conductor packet, and with how much cubic carry?

This makes the Paper 12 training-time audit executable at the representation
level.  The audit should not only record conductor energies, forcing spectra,
and behavior-kernel leakage; it should also record the spectra of the
operators \(M_{\ell,\mathfrak d}^{\mathrm{SF}}\) and
\(M_{\ell,\mathfrak f\leftarrow\mathfrak e}^{KCH}\), their finite-sample
outlier thresholds, and their overlaps with large-sample reference
eigenspaces.

## 16. Closing

The symplectic form is one of the great inventions of mathematics.  But it
is not the only possible primitive for mechanics.  It is the primitive that

finite outcome algebra, a zero-sum tangent hyperplane, two dual affine
connections, a Fisher metric, and conductor packets.  At \(N=4\), the old

$$
\iota_X\Omega_R^F

dC_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dC_{Q-2}.

layers refuse to split, \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) records the carry.

That is the mechanics constructed here: not anti-Hamiltonian, but
post-binary Hamiltonian.  A mechanics native to the simplex.
