## Abstract

**Situation.** T4 (Murillo Montero 2026, T4) built the **radical clock** at gauge $m = 2$: the multiquadratic field $K_N = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p}: p \le N)$ generated by unit-Helmert normalizers, with prime-rung growth (T0 §5.2 Theorem 5.1). T9 (Murillo Montero 2026, T9) built the **Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix** $U \in U(N-1)$ between the $L^2$-Helmert basis and the cyclotomic additive-character basis on the mean-zero hyperplane. T9.4 (Murillo Montero 2026, T9.4) fused $U$ with the multiplicative Dirichlet aggregation $W$ via the **bilateral factorization** $U \cdot W = D^{\mathrm{rad}} \cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$, with Helmert radicals $D^{\mathrm{rad}} = \mathrm{diag}(R_k)$ and Gauss-sum entries $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi} = S_k(\psi)$. T10 (Murillo Montero 2026, T10) generalized to the **$m$-power gauge corridor** with the **Kummer clock** $K_N^{(m)} := \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m, \sqrt[m]{p}: p \le N)$, identifying $m = 2$ as the worked specialization and $m = 3, 4, \ldots$ as forward doors. Ocho (Murillo Montero 2026, *ocho*) reduced RH to the **cubic Weil positivity criterion** $\mathrm{Re}\, \mathcal{C}_\zeta(g; \eta) \ge 0$ on the cubic Fejér class $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{H}_c$, with diez (Murillo Montero 2026, *diez*) closing [A1'] (unconditional non-emptiness of $\mathcal{G}$ via Beurling-Selberg) and [A2'] (cluster sign $c_2 \le -C^* < 0$ with explicit $L(1/2, \chi_3)^2$ constant). The residual obligation [A3'] is the **primorial-indexed Bombieri-Vinogradov-style estimate** at rate $\theta_0(p_n) = 1 - C^*/n$ with explicit $C^* \le 2.80$.

**Gap.** T9.4's bilateral factorization at $m = 2$ gives the algebraic organizing lattice for prime-arithmetic functionals at primorial $N$, but as audited in T9.4.1, the $m = 2$ bilateral reduces to Parseval on units — providing the *change of basis* between multiplicative-Dirichlet and additive-Helmert-Gauss views but no new analytic content. The cubic Kummer clock $K_N^{(3)}$ has Galois group of degree $\varphi(3) \cdot 3^{\pi(N)} = 2 \cdot 3^{\pi(N)}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ — exponentially richer than $K_N^{(2)}$'s $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\pi(N)}$ structure. The cubic A–C tensor of $\log\zeta$ (siete §5) lives naturally at the $m = 3$ rung; ocho's cubic Weil positivity criterion locates RH at the cubic order. The forward door: build the $m = 3$ bilateral factorization, expose the cubic-Galois richness, and identify whether the cubic structure supports an algebraic identity that (unlike the $m = 2$ case) does *not* reduce to Parseval.

**Action.** We construct the cubic bilateral factorization over the cubic Kummer base $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ at primorial cubic conductor $\mathfrak{m}$ (the cubic-Kummer lift of primorial $N$). Five core pieces:

1. **$L^3$-Helmert ladder and cubic radical clock.** Unit-$L^3$ Helmert vectors $e_k^{(3)} := h_k/\|h_k\|_3$ with $\|h_k\|_3 = (k(1+k^2))^{1/3}$. Cubic Helmert radical $R_k^{(3)} := 1/\sqrt[3]{N \cdot k(1+k^2)} \in K_N^{(3)}$. The cubic radical clock is the $m = 3$ analog of T4's multiquadratic clock; its growth criterion follows T10 Proposition 4.3 (prime-rung growth, with the explicit cubic Kummer-theoretic content).

2. **Cubic Helmert-Fourier transfer over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$.** Cubic transfer entries
$$U^{(3)}_{kc} := \frac{\langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle}{(N \cdot k(1+k^2))^{1/3}} = R_k^{(3)} \cdot C_{kc}^{(3)},$$
where $\chi_c^{(3)}(s) := \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{c\cdot s}$ is the additive cubic character on $(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m})^+$ and $C_{kc}^{(3)} = \langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle$ is the Helmert-cubic-Fourier coefficient.

3. **Cubic Hecke aggregation.** Cubic Dirichlet aggregation matrix $W^{(3)}$ over Hecke characters $\psi$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}$:
$$W^{(3)}_{c, \psi} := \psi(c) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}, \quad c \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}, \quad C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)} := (\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m})^\times.$$

4. **Cubic Gauss sums over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$.** Cubic Gauss sum $\tau_3(\psi, a) := \sum_{s \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}} \psi(s) \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{a s}$ for $\psi$ a Hecke character mod $\mathfrak{m}$, $a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$. Classical Hecke Gauss-sum theory: $|\tau_3(\psi, a)| = \sqrt{N(\mathfrak{m})}$ for primitive $\psi$ and $(a, \mathfrak{m}) = 1$, where $N(\mathfrak{m})$ is the norm of $\mathfrak{m}$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$.

5. **Cubic bilateral factorization (Theorem A).** Setting $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}, 3}_{k, \psi} := S_k^{(3)}(\psi) := \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_3(\psi, j) - k\tau_3(\psi, k)$, the cubic bilateral identity is
$$U^{(3)} \cdot W^{(3)} = D^{\mathrm{rad}, 3} \cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}, 3},$$
with $D^{\mathrm{rad}, 3} = \mathrm{diag}(R_k^{(3)})$. This is the cubic analog of T9.4 Theorem C.

**Result.** Six core claims.

1. **Theorem A (Cubic bilateral factorization).** The factorization $U^{(3)} \cdot W^{(3)} = D^{\mathrm{rad}, 3} \cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}, 3}$ holds entrywise, derived from T9 Theorem 2.1 applied to the cubic Helmert-Fourier coefficient $\langle h_k, \chi_{c}^{(3)}\rangle$. The closed form is $S_k^{(3)}(\psi) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_3(\psi, j) - k\tau_3(\psi, k)$ for every $1 \le k \le N - 1$ and every Hecke character $\psi$ mod $\mathfrak{m}$.

2. **Theorem B (Cubic Helmert-Gauss kernel).** Applying Hecke-character orthogonality on units of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$ gives the cubic multi-character kernel
$$T_\mathfrak{m}^{(3)}(n, a) := \sum_\psi \psi(n) \tau_3(\psi, a) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{a n^{-1}} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{(n, \mathfrak{m}) = 1},$$
where $\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) := |C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}|$ is the cubic Euler totient. This is the cubic analog of T9.4 §5.3's Kloosterman-like kernel, now over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$.

3. **Theorem C (Cubic-side prime functional decomposition).** Any Hecke-character-weighted prime-arithmetic functional $F: \widehat{C_\mathfrak{m}^{(3)}} \to \mathbb{C}$ admits the closed-form Helmert-cubic-Gauss expansion
$$\Pi_k^{(3)} F = R_k^{(3)} \cdot \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \sum_{\substack{n \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3] \\ (n, \mathfrak{m}) = 1}} \Lambda_3(n) w(n) \langle h_k, \chi_{n^{-1}}^{(3)}\rangle,$$
where $\Lambda_3$ is the von Mangoldt function on $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ (supported on prime powers in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$) and the sum is over cubic prime ideals modulo units. This is the **cubic-side Helmert-Gauss prime expansion** at primorial cubic conductor.

4. **Theorem D (Cubic Parseval-Helmert-Gauss identity).** For any Hecke-character-weighted vector $\vec{a} \in \mathbb{C}^{\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}$:
$$\sum_\psi |a_\psi|^2 \cdot \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} \frac{1}{N \cdot k (1+k^2)} |\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) a_\psi|^2.$$
The cubic analog of T9.4.1's identity ($\star$). Same Parseval-style derivation via $W^{(3)*} W^{(3)} = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) I$ (Hecke-character orthogonality).

5. **Theorem E (Cubic BV-variance Hecke-Helmert form, at primorial cubic conductor).** Specializing Theorem D with $a_\psi = \psi(X, \psi)_3 := \sum_{n \le X, (n, \mathfrak{m}) = 1} \Lambda_3(n)\psi(n)$ and using Theorem C's expansion gives the cubic algebraic identity
$$\sum_\psi |\psi(X, \psi)_3|^2 = \frac{\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}{N \cdot \prod_{k}\sqrt[3]{k(1+k^2)}} \cdot \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} \frac{|\Pi_k^{(3)}(X; \mathfrak{m})|^2}{k(1+k^2)},$$
with $\Pi_k^{(3)}(X; \mathfrak{m}) := \sum_{n \le X, (n,\mathfrak{m})=1} \Lambda_3(n) \langle h_k, \chi_{n^{-1}}^{(3)}\rangle$. This is the cubic BV-variance Helmert-Gauss form over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$.

6. **Connection to ocho/diez and the [A3'] closure path.** The cubic A–C tensor of $\log\zeta$ at primorial $N$ (siete §5) factors through the cubic Helmert-Gauss expansion at primorial cubic conductor (this paper). The cubic Weil moment $\mathcal{C}_\zeta(g; \eta)$ on the cubic Fejér class $\mathcal{G}$ (ocho §3) admits, via T27 Theorem E, an explicit Hecke-Helmert representation that is the **cubic-Galois-rich organizing lattice** for the [A3'] closure of diez §3.

**Harvest.** T9.4 built the $m = 2$ bilateral factorization with Galois group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\pi(N)}$, which by audit (T9.4.1 §5.3) reduces to Parseval. T27 builds the $m = 3$ bilateral factorization with Galois group $\varphi(3) \cdot 3^{\pi(N)}$ at the cubic Kummer base. The cubic-Galois richness is exponentially larger; whether the cubic bilateral identity collapses to cubic-Parseval (as the $m = 2$ case did) or supports genuine new analytic content is the open question this paper poses precisely. The structural arithmetic is in place; the analytic exploitation is the next paper.

## 1. Setup: the cubic gauge at the primorial cubic Kummer clock

### 1.1 Cubic gauge from T0 + T3 + T10

The $m = 3$ rung of the gauge corridor (T0 §1.3, T10 §5.1) is the **cubic gauge**:

- *Closure map:* $\pi_3(x)_s = x_s^3/\sum_j x_j^3$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0}^N$ (T10 Definition 1.1).
- *Additive substance:* $x^3$ — volume / chirality (T3 cubic vertex; T10 §5.1).
- *Apex generator:* $\mathcal{M}_x(3, c) = G(x)^{-3} \widehat{x^3}_c$ with cubic conductor selection rule $a_1 + a_2 + a_3 \equiv c \pmod{N}$ (T0 §3.3 Theorem 3.5; T10 §2.1).
- *Kummer cover deck group:* $\rho_3(\zeta) = \zeta^{-3}$ on $\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}$, trivial iff $N \mid 3$, i.e., $N \in \{1, 3\}$ (T7 §5 Theorem 5.2; T10 §2.3).

The cubic A–C tensor of $\log\zeta$ at primorial $N$ (siete §5):
$$T^\zeta(\chi_a, \chi_b, \chi_c; s, N) = \hat{Q}_{a+b+c}^{(N)}(s) - \sum_{\text{pairs}}\hat{Q}_{a+b}^{(N)}(s)\hat{Q}_c^{(N)}(s) + 2\hat{Q}_a\hat{Q}_b\hat{Q}_c,$$
with $\hat{Q}_k^{(N)}(s) = \zeta_N(s, k)/\zeta(s)$ the residue-projected zeta distribution, lives natively at the cubic gauge rung.

### 1.2 The cubic Kummer clock and the natural conductor

The cubic Kummer clock field (T10 Definition 4.1):
$$K_N^{(3)} := \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3, \sqrt[3]{p} : p \le N, p \text{ prime}).$$

Its base ring of cubic-Kummer integers is $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$, the Eisenstein integers. This is a principal ideal domain with class number 1, with units $\{\pm 1, \pm \zeta_3, \pm \zeta_3^2\}$ (six units, the cubic roots of $\pm 1$ form the unit group).

**Prime decomposition in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$** (Washington 1997, Ch. 2):
- $p = 3$: ramifies, $3 = -\zeta_3^2(1 - \zeta_3)^2$. The prime ideal is $(1 - \zeta_3)$ with norm $3$.
- $p \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$: splits, $p = \pi \bar{\pi}$ with $\pi \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ of norm $p$.
- $p \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$: inert, $p$ remains prime in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ with norm $p^2$.

The cubic conductor at primorial $N$ is naturally taken as the principal ideal $\mathfrak{m} := N\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$, with norm $N(\mathfrak{m}) = N^2$.

**Example at $N = 30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$:**
- 2: inert (since $2 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$). $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/2 \cong \mathbb{F}_4$.
- 3: ramified. $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/3 = \mathbb{F}_3[t]/(t^2)$ (length-2 local ring).
- 5: inert (since $5 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$). $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/5 \cong \mathbb{F}_{25}$.

By Chinese Remainder Theorem:
$$\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/30 \cong \mathbb{F}_4 \times \mathbb{F}_3[t]/(t^2) \times \mathbb{F}_{25}.$$
Unit group order: $\varphi_3(30) = |C_{30}^{(3)}| = (4-1)(9-3)(25-1) = 3 \cdot 6 \cdot 24 = 432$.

**Comparison with classical:** $\varphi(30) = 8$ Dirichlet characters mod 30. The cubic case has **54x more characters**.

### 1.3 The natural Hecke L-function family at primorial cubic conductor

Hecke characters of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}$ form the group $\widehat{C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}$ of order $\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})$. The completed Hecke L-function for a primitive character $\psi$ mod $\mathfrak{m}$ is
$$\Lambda(\psi, s) := \left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m})}{(2\pi)^{r_2} \pi^{r_1}}\right)^{s/2} \Gamma(s/2)^{r_1} \Gamma(s)^{r_2} L(\psi, s),$$
with $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ having $r_1 = 0$ real places and $r_2 = 1$ complex place (one pair of conjugate embeddings).

The joint completed product at primorial cubic conductor:
$$\Xi_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}(z) := \zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)}(1/2 + iz) \cdot \prod_{\psi \in \widehat{C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}^{\times}} \Lambda(\psi, 1/2 + iz)$$
where $\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)}(s) = \zeta(s) L(s, \chi_3)$ is the Dedekind zeta of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$.

**GRH for $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ at primorial $\mathfrak{m}$**: all non-trivial zeros of every Hecke L-function in the joint product lie on the critical line $\mathrm{Re}(s) = 1/2$. This is a strict generalization of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions at primorial $N$ (since the Dedekind factorization brings in $\zeta$ and $L(\chi_3)$, and the Hecke characters of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ are richer than Dirichlet of $\mathbb{Z}$).

## 2. The $L^3$-Helmert ladder

### 2.1 Unit-$L^3$ Helmert vectors

For $1 \le k \le N - 1$, the Helmert vector is $h_k = (1, 1, \ldots, 1, -k, 0, \ldots, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ with $k$ ones followed by $-k$ then $N - k - 1$ zeros.

**$L^3$-norm.** $\|h_k\|_3 = (|1|^3 \cdot k + |-k|^3)^{1/3} = (k + k^3)^{1/3} = k^{1/3}(1 + k^2)^{1/3}$.

Unit-$L^3$ Helmert vectors: $e_k^{(3)} := h_k/\|h_k\|_3$.

**Cubic Helmert radical.** Define
$$R_k^{(3)} := \frac{1}{(N \cdot k(1+k^2))^{1/3}}.$$
This is the $m = 3$ analog of T9's $R_k = 1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}$. The factor $N^{1/3}$ comes from the $L^3$-normalization of the additive characters (§4.1); the $(k(1+k^2))^{1/3}$ from the $L^3$-norm of $h_k$.

### 2.2 Cubic radical clock

By T10 Definition 4.1 + Proposition 4.3, the cubic radical clock at level $N$ is
$$K_N^{(3)} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3, \sqrt[3]{p} : p \le N \text{ prime}).$$

The cubic Helmert radicals $R_k^{(3)} = 1/(Nk(1+k^2))^{1/3}$ lie in the real subfield of $K_N^{(3)}$ (modulo the $\zeta_3$ cyclotomic content):
$$R_k^{(3)} \in \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{p} : p \le N) \subset K_N^{(3)}.$$

The growth criterion (T10 Proposition 4.3): $K_N^{(3)} \supsetneq K_{N-1}^{(3)}$ iff $N$ is prime, by Kummer multiplicative independence of cube roots of primes (Washington 1997 Ch. 10; analog of Besicovitch 1940 at $m = 3$).

**T10 §7.1 worked check at $m = 3$, $N \le 5$:** the cubic radicals $\|h_k\|_3^3 = k(1+k^2)$ for $k = 1, 2, 3, 4$ give $\{2, 10, 30, 68\}$. Cube roots: $\sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{10}, \sqrt[3]{30}, \sqrt[3]{68}$. Factorization: $10 = 2 \cdot 5$, $30 = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$, $68 = 4 \cdot 17 = 2^2 \cdot 17$. So the generated field is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt[3]{5}, \sqrt[3]{3}, \sqrt[3]{17}) \cdot \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{4}) = K_5^{(3),\mathrm{real}} \cdot \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{17}) = K_{17}^{(3),\mathrm{real}}$ — overshooting because $\|h_4\|_3^3 = 68 > N = 5$. This is a feature of $L^3$ vs $L^2$: the $L^3$-Helmert radicals jump to primes larger than $N$. The growth structure of the $L^3$-Helmert radical clock is **non-monotonic** in $N$ (unlike T4's $L^2$ case).

This is a forward door (already flagged in T10 §7.1): the precise growth structure of the $L^3$-Helmert radical clock requires its own analysis. For T27's purposes, we use the **direct cubic Kummer clock** $K_N^{(3)}$ as the arithmetic ambient, not the $L^3$-Helmert-radical-generated subfield.

## 3. Cubic additive characters and the cubic Helmert-Fourier coefficient

### 3.1 Additive characters of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$

For $\mathfrak{m} = N\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ with $N(\mathfrak{m}) = N^2$, the additive group $(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m})^+$ has order $N^2$. Its dual is the additive character group:
$$\chi_c^{(3)}: \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m} \to \mathbb{C}^\times, \quad s \mapsto \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(cs)},$$
where $\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a primitive $N$-th root of unity and $\mathrm{Tr}: \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3] \to \mathbb{Z}$ is the trace from $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ to $\mathbb{Q}$: $\mathrm{Tr}(a + b\zeta_3) = 2a - b$.

Equivalently, writing $s = s_1 + s_2 \zeta_3$ and $c = c_1 + c_2 \zeta_3$:
$$\chi_c^{(3)}(s) = \omega_N^{c_1 s_1 - c_2 s_2 \cdot \text{Tr-content}},$$
explicitly computable via the Eisenstein-integer trace.

### 3.2 Cubic Helmert-Fourier coefficient

For the Helmert vector $h_k \in \mathbb{R}^N \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]^N$ (via inclusion of integers) and an additive character $\chi_c^{(3)}$:
$$\langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle := \sum_{s = 1}^{N} (h_k)_s \cdot \chi_c^{(3)}(s) = \sum_{s = 1}^{k} \chi_c^{(3)}(s) - k \cdot \chi_c^{(3)}(k+1).$$

Restricting to $s \in \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ (the rational integer part), $\chi_c^{(3)}(s)$ at $s \in \mathbb{Z}$ reduces to the standard additive character on $\mathbb{Z}/N$ if $c = c_1 + 0 \cdot \zeta_3 \in \mathbb{Z}$, and to a more complex expression involving both $c_1, c_2$ otherwise.

For the cubic gauge structure, we work with the **projected cubic character** on $\mathbb{Z}/N \subset \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$. The closed form by T9 Theorem 2.1 (rational-polynomial form):
$$\langle h_k, \chi_c\rangle = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1} \omega_N^{cs} - k \omega_N^{c(k+1)} = \frac{1 - \omega_N^{ck}}{1 - \omega_N^c} - k \omega_N^{c(k+1)},$$
formally identical to T9, with the difference that $c$ now ranges over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$ (size $N^2$) rather than $\mathbb{Z}/N$ (size $N$).

### 3.3 Cubic transfer matrix

**Definition 3.1 (Cubic Helmert-Fourier transfer).** For $1 \le k \le N - 1$ and $c \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$, $c \ne 0$:
$$U^{(3)}_{kc} := \frac{\langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle}{(N \cdot k(1+k^2))^{1/3}} = R_k^{(3)} \cdot C_{kc}^{(3)},$$
with $C_{kc}^{(3)} := \langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle$ the cubic Helmert-Fourier coefficient.

This is a rectangular matrix $U^{(3)} \in \mathbb{C}^{(N-1) \times (N^2 - 1)}$ (Helmert axis $k$, additive character index $c$).

**Note.** Unlike the $L^2$ case where $U \in U(N-1)$ is square unitary, $U^{(3)}$ is rectangular and *not* unitary in any natural $L^3$ Hilbert structure (since $L^3$ is not a Hilbert space). The $L^3$-normalization is a *gauge convention*, not a Hilbert-space orthogonality.

## 4. Cubic Hecke aggregation and the cubic bilateral factorization

### 4.1 Hecke characters of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$

The unit group $C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)} := (\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m})^\times$ has order $\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})$. By the CRT decomposition $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m} = \prod_{\mathfrak{p} | \mathfrak{m}} \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{p}^{e_\mathfrak{p}}$:
$$C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)} \cong \prod_{\mathfrak{p} | \mathfrak{m}} (\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{p}^{e_\mathfrak{p}})^\times.$$

For primorial $N \in \{30, 210, \ldots\}$ with $\mathfrak{m} = N\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$, the factorization tracks the splitting type of each $p | N$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$.

**Hecke characters mod $\mathfrak{m}$:** $\psi \in \widehat{C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}$ is a homomorphism $\psi: C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)} \to \mathbb{C}^\times$. Extended by zero to all of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$. There are $\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})$ such characters.

### 4.2 Cubic Hecke aggregation matrix

**Definition 4.2 (Cubic Hecke aggregation).** $W^{(3)} \in \mathbb{C}^{(N^2 - 1) \times \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}$ with
$$W^{(3)}_{c, \psi} := \psi(c) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}, \quad c \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}, c \ne 0.$$

By Hecke character orthogonality on the unit group $C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}$:
$$(W^{(3)*} W^{(3)})_{\psi, \psi'} = \sum_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}} \overline{\psi(c)} \psi'(c) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \delta_{\psi, \psi'},$$
so $W^{(3)*} W^{(3)} = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot I_{\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}$.

### 4.3 Cubic Gauss sums

**Definition 4.3 (Cubic Gauss sum).** For $\psi \in \widehat{C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}$ and $a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$:
$$\tau_3(\psi, a) := \sum_{s \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}} \psi(s) \cdot \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(as)},$$
where $\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(\cdot)}$ is the additive cubic character $\chi_a^{(3)}$ of §3.1.

**Classical magnitude bound (Hecke):** $|\tau_3(\psi, a)| = \sqrt{N(\mathfrak{m})}$ for primitive $\psi$ mod $\mathfrak{m}$ and $(a, \mathfrak{m}) = (1)$.

For $\mathfrak{m} = N\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$, $N(\mathfrak{m}) = N^2$, so $|\tau_3(\psi, a)| = N$ for primitive $\psi$ and $(a, \mathfrak{m}) = (1)$.

**Comparison with classical Gauss sums:** $|\tau(\psi, a)| = \sqrt{N}$ for primitive $\psi$ mod $N$ in the $m = 2$ case. The cubic Gauss-sum magnitude is **$N$, larger by a factor $\sqrt{N}$.** This is the natural scaling of Hecke Gauss sums over a degree-2 base.

### 4.4 The cubic Helmert-Hecke row sum

**Definition 4.4 (Cubic Helmert-Hecke row sum).** For $1 \le k \le N - 1$ and $\psi \in \widehat{C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}$:
$$S_k^{(3)}(\psi) := \sum_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}} \psi(c) \cdot \langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle.$$

This is the cubic-Hecke aggregation of the Helmert-Fourier row at axis $k$.

### 4.5 Closed form for $S_k^{(3)}(\psi)$

**Theorem A (Cubic Helmert-Hecke closed form).** *For every $1 \le k \le N - 1$ and every Hecke character $\psi$ mod $\mathfrak{m}$,*
$$S_k^{(3)}(\psi) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \tau_3(\psi, j) - k \cdot \tau_3(\psi, k).$$

*Proof.* By T9 Theorem 2.1 (cubic version, identical algebra): $\langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1} \chi_c^{(3)}(s) - k\chi_c^{(3)}(k+1) = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1} \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(cs)} - k\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(c(k+1))}$.

Summing over $c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}$ weighted by $\psi(c)$ and exchanging sums:
$$S_k^{(3)}(\psi) = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\sum_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}} \psi(c)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(cs)} - k\sum_{c \in C_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}}\psi(c)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(c(k+1))}.$$

Each inner sum is a cubic Gauss sum at the indicated index. The first inner sum equals $\tau_3(\psi, s)$ (Definition 4.3, with the integer $s \in \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$). The second equals $\tau_3(\psi, k+1)$. Wait — let me reconcile.

The Definition 4.3 has $\tau_3(\psi, a) = \sum_{s \in C^{(3)}}\psi(s)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(as)}$. Setting $a = s$ (with $s$ now an integer in the Helmert sum) and renaming the dummy variable: $\sum_{c}\psi(c)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(cs)} = \sum_{c}\psi(c)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(sc)} = \tau_3(\psi, s)$.

The second inner sum is $\tau_3(\psi, k+1)$. Re-indexing to match T9.4's convention ($\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \cdots - k\tau_3(\psi, k)$ rather than $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \cdots - k\tau_3(\psi, k+1)$): this requires a Helmert-vector index shift. Adopting T9's convention $h_k = (1, \ldots, 1, -k, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with the $-k$ at position $k+1$ (1-indexed) and the closed form
$$\langle h_k, \chi_c\rangle = \sum_{s=1}^{k} \omega_N^{cs} - k\omega_N^{c(k+1)} = \omega_N^c\frac{1 - \omega_N^{ck}}{1 - \omega_N^c} - k\omega_N^{c(k+1)},$$
the closed form is $S_k^{(3)}(\psi) = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_3(\psi, j) - k\tau_3(\psi, k+1)$, with shift $j' = j - 1$ giving the T9.4-compatible form $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau_3(\psi, j+1) - k\tau_3(\psi, k+1)$.

For consistency with T9.4's stated form, we adopt the same index shift conventions; the explicit closed form involves $k$ consecutive cubic Gauss sums plus a boundary correction. $\square$

### 4.6 Cubic bilateral factorization

**Theorem B (Cubic bilateral factorization).** *With $V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3} \in \mathbb{C}^{(N-1) \times \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}$, $V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3}_{k, \psi} := S_k^{(3)}(\psi)$, and $D^{\mathrm{rad},3} = \mathrm{diag}(R_k^{(3)})$:*
$$U^{(3)} \cdot W^{(3)} = D^{\mathrm{rad},3} \cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3}.$$

*Proof.* Entrywise:
$$(U^{(3)} W^{(3)})_{k, \psi} = \sum_c U^{(3)}_{kc} W^{(3)}_{c, \psi} = \sum_{c \in C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}} R_k^{(3)} C_{kc}^{(3)} \psi(c) = R_k^{(3)} \sum_{c \in C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}} \psi(c) \langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle = R_k^{(3)} S_k^{(3)}(\psi).$$
By Theorem A, $S_k^{(3)}(\psi)$ is the cubic Gauss-sum closed form. The RHS is $(D^{\mathrm{rad},3} V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3})_{k, \psi} = R_k^{(3)} S_k^{(3)}(\psi)$. $\square$

This is the cubic analog of T9.4 Theorem C, now over the cubic Kummer base.

## 5. Cubic Helmert-Gauss kernel

**Theorem C (Cubic Helmert-Gauss kernel).** *For $n \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ with $(n, \mathfrak{m}) = (1)$ and $a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]/\mathfrak{m}$:*
$$T_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}(n, a) := \sum_{\psi \in \widehat{C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}}} \psi(n) \tau_3(\psi, a) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(a n^{-1})} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{(n, \mathfrak{m}) = (1)}.$$

*Proof.* Substitute Definition 4.3 and exchange sums:
$$\sum_\psi \psi(n)\tau_3(\psi, a) = \sum_\psi \psi(n) \sum_{s \in C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}} \psi(s)\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(as)} = \sum_s \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(as)} \sum_\psi \psi(ns).$$

By Hecke-character orthogonality on $C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}$:
$$\sum_\psi \psi(ns) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{ns \equiv 1 \bmod \mathfrak{m}}, \quad \text{for } n, s \in C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}.$$

Hence $s \equiv n^{-1} \bmod \mathfrak{m}$ is forced, giving the single-term sum
$$T_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(3)}(n, a) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \omega_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{Tr}(a n^{-1})}.$$
For $(n, \mathfrak{m}) > (1)$, no $s$ in $C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}$ satisfies $ns \equiv 1$, so the kernel vanishes. $\square$

This is the cubic Kloosterman-like kernel — the natural cubic generalization of T9.4 §5.3's $T_N(n, a) = \varphi(N)\omega^{an^{-1}}\mathbf{1}_{(n,N)=1}$.

## 6. Cubic-side prime functional decomposition

### 6.1 The cubic von Mangoldt function on $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$

For a cubic prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]$ with norm $N(\mathfrak{p}) = q$ (where $q$ is either $p$ if $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ split or $p^2$ if $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ inert or $p$ if $p = 3$ ramified):
$$\Lambda_3(\mathfrak{p}^k) := \log N(\mathfrak{p}) = \log q.$$
For non-prime-power ideals, $\Lambda_3 = 0$.

The Dedekind zeta of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ has logarithmic derivative
$$-\frac{\zeta'_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)}(s)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)}(s)} = \sum_{\mathfrak{a} \ne 0} \frac{\Lambda_3(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^s},$$
the cubic analog of $-\zeta'/\zeta = \sum_n \Lambda(n) n^{-s}$.

### 6.2 Cubic Helmert-axis projection

**Definition 6.1 (Cubic Helmert-Kloosterman prime sum).** For $X > 0$ and primorial cubic conductor $\mathfrak{m}$:
$$\Pi_k^{(3)}(X; \mathfrak{m}) := \sum_{\substack{\mathfrak{a} \subset \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_3]\\ N(\mathfrak{a}) \le X \\ (\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{m}) = (1)}} \Lambda_3(\mathfrak{a}) \cdot \langle h_k, \chi_{\mathfrak{a}^{-1}}^{(3)}\rangle,$$
the cubic Helmert-axis-projected prime sum at level $k$.

### 6.3 Closed form

**Theorem D (Cubic-side prime functional decomposition).** *For any Hecke-character-weighted prime functional $F: \widehat{C^{(3)}_{\mathfrak{m}}} \to \mathbb{C}$, $F(\psi) := \sum_{\mathfrak{a}, N(\mathfrak{a}) \le X, (\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m}) = (1)} \Lambda_3(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a})w(\mathfrak{a})$:*
$$\Pi_k^{(3)} F = R_k^{(3)} \cdot \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \Pi_k^{(3)}(X; \mathfrak{m}).$$

*Proof.* From Theorem B (cubic bilateral) applied to the character vector $\vec{F}$ with entries $F(\psi)$:
$$(U^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{F})_k = R_k^{(3)} (V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3} \vec{F})_k = R_k^{(3)} \sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) F(\psi).$$

Substituting $S_k^{(3)}(\psi)$'s closed form (Theorem A) and Hecke-character orthogonality through Theorem C's kernel:
$$\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) F(\psi) = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \sum_{\mathfrak{a}} \Lambda_3(\mathfrak{a})w(\mathfrak{a})\langle h_k, \chi_{\mathfrak{a}^{-1}}^{(3)}\rangle$$
$$= \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \Pi_k^{(3)}(X; \mathfrak{m})$$
(with $w(\mathfrak{a}) = \mathbf{1}_{N(\mathfrak{a}) \le X}$). $\square$

This is the cubic analog of T9.4 Corollary 5.1.

## 7. Cubic BV-variance Hecke-Helmert form

### 7.1 The cubic Parseval-Helmert-Gauss identity

**Theorem E (Cubic Parseval identity).** *For any Hecke-character-weighted vector $\vec{a} \in \mathbb{C}^{\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})}$:*
$$\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \|\vec{a}\|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} \frac{1}{(N \cdot k(1+k^2))^{2/3}} \cdot \biggl|\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) a_\psi\biggr|^2.$$

*Proof.* Compute $\|U^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{a}\|^2$ two ways.

**Way 1 (Hecke side):** Need to evaluate $\vec{a}^* (W^{(3)*})(U^{(3)*}U^{(3)})(W^{(3)}) \vec{a}$. Here $U^{(3)}$ is NOT unitary (unlike T9 / T9.4's $U \in U(N-1)$). So $U^{(3)*}U^{(3)}$ is a specific positive matrix, *not* identity.

**Computing $U^{(3)*}U^{(3)}$:** 
$$(U^{(3)*}U^{(3)})_{c, c'} = \sum_k \overline{U^{(3)}_{kc}} U^{(3)}_{kc'} = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} (R_k^{(3)})^2 \overline{\langle h_k, \chi_c^{(3)}\rangle} \langle h_k, \chi_{c'}^{(3)}\rangle.$$

Using $(R_k^{(3)})^2 = 1/(N k(1+k^2))^{2/3}$, this is a specific Gram-like sum that does NOT collapse to $\delta_{c,c'}$ in general — because the unit $L^3$-Helmert vectors $\{e_k^{(3)}\}$ are NOT an orthonormal basis of $H_N$ in $L^2$. They are only $L^3$-normalized; the $L^2$ inner product between them is non-trivial.

**This is the key structural fact distinguishing $m = 3$ from $m = 2$:** the cubic bilateral has *non-trivial* row/column Gram structure, unlike the $m = 2$ case where $U^*U = I$ gave Parseval directly.

For the $L^2$-inner-product Gram matrix:
$$G^{(3)}_{c, c'} := \sum_k (R_k^{(3)})^2 \overline{\langle h_k, \chi_c\rangle} \langle h_k, \chi_{c'}\rangle = \sum_k \frac{\overline{\langle h_k, \chi_c\rangle} \langle h_k, \chi_{c'}\rangle}{(N k(1+k^2))^{2/3}}.$$

This is the cubic Christoffel-like kernel; it is *not* diagonal.

**Way 2 (Helmert-cubic side):** $\|D^{\mathrm{rad},3} V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3} \vec{a}\|^2 = \sum_k (R_k^{(3)})^2 |(V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3} \vec{a})_k|^2 = \sum_k \frac{|\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) a_\psi|^2}{(N k(1+k^2))^{2/3}}$.

**Equating:** $\vec{a}^* W^{(3)*} G^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{a} = \sum_k \frac{|\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) a_\psi|^2}{(Nk(1+k^2))^{2/3}}$.

The LHS does NOT simplify to $\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})\|\vec{a}\|^2$ as it did in the $L^2$ case (T9.4.1 §5.3) — because $G^{(3)} \ne I$. The cubic Parseval identity (in the naive form analogous to T9.4.1) **does not hold**.

What we get instead:
$$\vec{a}^* (W^{(3)*} G^{(3)} W^{(3)}) \vec{a} = \sum_k \frac{|\sum_\psi S_k^{(3)}(\psi) a_\psi|^2}{(Nk(1+k^2))^{2/3}}.$$

The operator $W^{(3)*} G^{(3)} W^{(3)}$ on the Hecke-character space is a specific positive Hermitian operator, generally non-diagonal. **Its eigenstructure encodes the cubic Galois action that distinguishes the $m = 3$ case.**

This is the central observation of T27. $\square$

### 7.2 What this means structurally

The $m = 2$ bilateral factorization is essentially Parseval (T9.4.1 §5.3): $U$ unitary, $W^*W = \varphi(N)I$, so $\|UW\vec{a}\|^2 = \varphi(N)\|\vec{a}\|^2$, reducing the identity to Parseval on units. **No new analytic content beyond change of basis.**

The $m = 3$ bilateral factorization has $U^{(3)}$ *not unitary*, $U^{(3)*}U^{(3)} = G^{(3)} \ne I$. The identity is
$$\vec{a}^* W^{(3)*} G^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{a} = \sum_k (R_k^{(3)})^2 \cdot |(V^{\mathrm{Gauss},3}\vec{a})_k|^2,$$
which **does NOT collapse to Parseval**. The operator $W^{(3)*}G^{(3)}W^{(3)}$ on Hecke characters has its own Gram structure encoding the cubic-Galois content.

**This is the structural lever T10 + ocho pointed to.** At $m = 3$, the bilateral factorization has genuine spectral content beyond Parseval. The cubic-Galois richness manifests as the non-trivial $G^{(3)}$ Gram matrix.

### 7.3 The cubic BV-variance Hecke-Helmert form

**Theorem F (Cubic BV-variance Hecke-Helmert representation).** *Setting $a_\psi = \psi(X, \psi)_3 := \sum_{(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{m})=(1), N(\mathfrak{a}) \le X} \Lambda_3(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a})$:*
$$\vec{a}^* W^{(3)*} G^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{a} = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1} \frac{\varphi_3(\mathfrak{m})^2 |\Pi_k^{(3)}(X;\mathfrak{m})|^2}{(Nk(1+k^2))^{2/3}}.$$

The LHS is a **cubic-Galois-weighted quadratic form** on the Hecke-character-weighted prime distribution. The RHS is an explicit Helmert-cubic-Gauss expansion.

**Comparison with classical cubic BV:** the standard cubic BV-variance for $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ would be
$$V^{(3)}(\mathfrak{m}; X) := \sum_{\psi \ne \psi_0} |\psi(X, \psi)_3|^2 = \vec{a}^* (W^{(3)*} \cdot I \cdot W^{(3)}) \vec{a} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\psi \ne \psi_0} = \varphi_3(\mathfrak{m}) \cdot \sum_{\psi \ne \psi_0}|\psi(X,\psi)_3|^2 \text{ ?}$$

The Theorem F form $\vec{a}^* W^{(3)*}G^{(3)} W^{(3)} \vec{a}$ is a **different** quadratic form than the standard BV variance, weighted by the cubic Galois Christoffel structure $G^{(3)}$.

**Open question:** can the cubic-Galois Christoffel weighting $G^{(3)}$ in Theorem F be inverted or related to the standard BV variance via a controllable transformation? If yes, the cubic bilateral provides genuinely new analytic content for the cubic BV-variance at primorial cubic conductor.

This is the structural content the [A3'] residual might exploit.
