## Abstract

Papers 8 and 9 can be read as the arithmetic spectral resolution of the
categorical Fisher geometry at the uniform distribution.  Let \(R\) be a finite
outcome set of size \(Q\), usually a finite quotient ring, and let

$$
p_* = Q^{-1}{\bf 1}
$$

be the centroid of the probability simplex \(\Delta_R^\circ\).  In the
overparameterized categorical exponential family, the coordinate score at
\(p_*\) is

$$
S_a(x)=1_{x=a}-Q^{-1},
$$

which is exactly the centered simplex vector

$$
v_a=e_a-Q^{-1}{\bf 1}.
$$

After the harmless global Fisher rescaling already used in Paper 2, the Fisher
Gram matrix of these score functions is

$$
\langle v_a,v_b\rangle_{F,*}=\delta_{ab}-Q^{-1}.
$$

Thus the regular simplex frame of Papers 8 and 9 is the Fisher score frame of
the categorical family at maximum entropy.

When \(R\) is a finite quotient ring, the additive translation action fixes
\(p_*\), so it acts unitarily on the Fisher tangent space

$$
T_{p_*}\Delta_R^\circ=H_R=\{x\in{\mathbb C}^R:\sum_a x_a=0\}.
$$

The additive-character decomposition of this tangent representation, grouped
by conductor, is precisely Paper 9's conductor-block decomposition.  At this
same point the \(e\)-coordinate \(\theta\) and centered \(m\)-coordinate
\(p-p_*\) have the same tangent splitting, because the Legendre map has scalar
linearization at \(\theta=0\).  Thus the conductor decomposition is not only
Fisher-orthogonal at the centroid; it is simultaneously compatible with the
\(e\)-flat and \(m\)-flat affine structures there.  The Ramanujan and Hayes
sums appearing in Papers 8 and 9 are matrix elements of the Fisher metric
between conductor-projected score functions.  The block-sign group \(B_R\) is
the Boolean sign skeleton of the \(R\)-equivariant Fisher-orthogonal isometry
group of this tangent space.  In this reading, Papers 8 and 9 are not only
algebraic-combinatorial spectroscopy: they are the \(R\)-equivariant,
dually-flat Fisher decomposition theorem for the categorical exponential
family at the centroid.

The bridge is exact at \(p_*\).  Away from \(p_*\), the Fisher metric weights
tangent vectors by \(1/p_a\), the translation action no longer fixes the
basepoint, and conductor orthogonality is generically destroyed.  The best
off-centroid continuation is therefore not a literal fixed orthogonal
decomposition, but a choice among three related structures: transported
Fisher-orthogonal conductor frames along the sphere pullback, \(e\)-flat and
\(m\)-flat conductor foliations in the dually flat structure, and a possible
Carlitz-Hayes equivariant refinement of the off-centroid normalizer problem.
The exact orthogonal theorem is a centroid theorem; the promising research
surface is the arithmetic tensor that measures how it breaks.  At first
order, if \(p=p_*+h\), the Fisher coupling between character lines
\({\mathbb C}\chi\) and \({\mathbb C}\psi\) is controlled by
\(\widehat h(\psi\chi^{-1})\), giving an immediate conductor selection rule.
At second order, pointwise multiplication becomes convolution in the
character group, so \(\widehat{h^2}=Q^{-1}(\widehat h*\widehat h)\) in the
unnormalized Fourier convention.  Along conductor-flat \(m\)- and
\(e\)-geodesics this becomes an all-order support theorem: the \(n\)-th
Taylor coefficient couples two character lines only when their quotient lies
in the \(n\)-fold product support of the displacement in the dual character
group.  The split \(Q_f=4\) atlas gives the first complete worked case.

One further bridge vocabulary will be useful later.  The derivatives of the
log-partition \(\psi\) form a categorical cumulant tower.  The first cumulant
is the moment point \(p=\nabla\psi\), hence the centroid/position at
\(\theta=0\); the second is the Fisher information \(D^2\psi\), the metric or
inertia tensor of the local simplex geometry; the third is the
Amari-Chentsov cubic, or after raising an index \(K^{CH}\), the first
force/source term measuring off-centroid coupling.  Fourth and higher
cumulants do not replace the conductor expansion.  They record projection
defects and controlled residuals when the full categorical family is
compressed to conductor macrovariables, learned pullbacks, or finite atlases.
In later physics language this tower is the candidate categorical effective
action; in this paper the native object remains the Fisher-conductor Taylor
expansion.

**Keywords:** information geometry, Fisher metric, categorical exponential
family, regular simplex, conductor blocks, Ramanujan sums, Hayes sums,
dually flat geometry, uniform distribution, block-sign isometries.

## 2. The Score Frame at the Uniform

Let \(R\) be a finite outcome set with \(Q=|R|\).  The categorical exponential
family in the redundant logit chart is

$$
p_\theta(a)=\frac{\exp\theta_a}{\sum_{c\in R}\exp\theta_c},
\qquad
\theta\in{\mathbb R}^R/{\mathbb R}{\bf 1}.
$$

The uniform distribution corresponds to \(\theta=0\):

$$
p_*(a)=Q^{-1}.
$$

For the coordinate logit \(\theta_a\), the score function at \(\theta=0\) is

$$
S_a(x)
=
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_a}\log p_\theta(x)\right|_{\theta=0}
=
1_{x=a}-Q^{-1}.
$$

As a function of \(x\in R\), this is the centered simplex vector

$$
v_a=e_a-Q^{-1}{\bf 1}.
$$

The usual Fisher score covariance is

$$
I_*(S_a,S_b)
=
{\mathbb E}_{p_*}[S_aS_b]
=
Q^{-1}\left(\delta_{ab}-Q^{-1}\right).
$$

Papers 8 and 9 use the Euclidean Gram on \(H_R\),

$$
\langle x,y\rangle_0=\sum_{c\in R}x_c\overline{y_c}.
$$

This is \(Q\) times the Fisher score covariance at \(p_*\):

$$
\langle S_a,S_b\rangle_0
=
Q\,I_*(S_a,S_b)
=
\delta_{ab}-Q^{-1}.
$$

Equivalently, in moment coordinates the Fisher--Rao metric is

$$
g^F_{p_*}(u,w)=Q\sum_{c\in R}u_cw_c,
\qquad u,w\in H_R,
$$

so the same Gram appears after a reciprocal global rescaling.  Since the
Fisher metric is unique only up to an overall positive scale in the
Chentsov/Amari normalization convention, the Papers 8 and 9 inner product is
the Fisher inner product at \(p_*\) in the simplicial scale.

This is the first bridge:

**Proposition 2.1 (Simplex frame = Fisher score frame).**  The centered
simplex vectors

$$
v_a=e_a-Q^{-1}{\bf 1}
$$

are the coordinate score functions of the categorical exponential family at
the uniform distribution.  In the simplicial Fisher scale,

$$
\langle v_a,v_b\rangle_{F,*}
=
\delta_{ab}-Q^{-1}.
$$

Thus the regular simplex frame \(V_R=\{v_a:a\in R\}\) is the Fisher score
frame at maximum entropy.

The point is not that the regular simplex happens to resemble the categorical
score frame.  It is the score frame.

## 3. Translation Symmetry and the Fisher Tangent Representation

Now assume \(R=A/I\) is a finite quotient ring, as in Paper 9.  The additive
group of \(R\) acts on outcomes by translation:

$$
\tau_b(a)=a+b.
$$

It acts on functions by

$$
(\tau_b f)(a)=f(a-b),
$$

and therefore on the coordinate vectors by \(\tau_b e_a=e_{a+b}\).  Since the
uniform distribution is fixed by every permutation of outcomes, this action
fixes \(p_*\) and acts on the tangent space

$$
T_{p_*}\Delta_R^\circ
=
H_R
=
\left\{x\in{\mathbb C}^R:\sum_{a\in R}x_a=0\right\}.
$$

Inside \(\Delta_R^\circ\), \(p_*\) is the unique fixed point of the regular
translation action: if \(p_a=p_{a-b}\) for every \(a,b\in R\), then all
coordinates are equal, hence \(p=p_*\).

With respect to the Fisher inner product at \(p_*\), the translation action is
unitary.  It is the regular representation of the additive group with the
constant line removed.

Over \({\mathbb C}\), the irreducible representations of the finite abelian
additive group are the additive character lines

$$
{\mathbb C}\chi,\qquad \chi\in\widehat R.
$$

The trivial character spans the constant line.  Removing it gives

$$
H_R
=
\bigoplus_{\chi\ne1}{\mathbb C}\chi.
$$

Paper 9 groups these irreducible character lines by conductor.  For a
nontrivial conductor \(\mathfrak d\mid I\),

$$
H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}
=
\operatorname{span}\{\chi:\operatorname{cond}(\chi)=\mathfrak d\}.
$$

Thus Paper 9, Theorem 2.1,

$$
H_R
=
\bigoplus_{\substack{\mathfrak d\mid I\\\mathfrak d\ne1}}
H_R^{[\mathfrak d]},
$$

is the conductor-coarsened character decomposition of the Fisher tangent
representation at \(p_*\).  More precisely, the character lines are the
irreducible pieces; the conductor blocks are the primitive-conductor packets.
They are Fisher-orthogonal because the character basis is unitary for the
uniform measure.

This is the second bridge:

**Proposition 3.1 (Conductor blocks = Fisher tangent spectral blocks).**  At
the uniform categorical distribution on \(R\), the additive translation action
of \(R\) on the Fisher tangent space \(H_R\) decomposes into additive
characters.  Grouping those character lines by conductor gives exactly the
conductor-block decomposition of Paper 9, Theorem 2.1.  The orthogonality in
that theorem is Fisher orthogonality at \(p_*\), in the simplicial scale.

For \(R={\mathbb Z}/N{\mathbb Z}\), this says that the cyclotomic decomposition
of Paper 8 is the cyclic spectral decomposition of the Fisher tangent
representation at the uniform categorical distribution on \(N\) outcomes.

### 3.1 The Doubly Flat Centroid

The previous proposition can be strengthened.  At \(p_*\), the conductor
decomposition is not only Fisher-orthogonal; it is compatible with both flat
affine coordinate systems of the categorical family.

Let

$$
\mu(\theta)=p_\theta-p_*
$$

be the centered moment coordinate.  The Legendre map from natural to moment
coordinates is

$$
\theta\longmapsto p_\theta=\nabla\psi(\theta),
\qquad
\psi(\theta)=\log\sum_{a\in R}e^{\theta_a}.
$$

Its derivative is the Fisher information matrix

$$
D\mu_\theta=F(\theta)=\operatorname{diag}(p_\theta)-p_\theta p_\theta^T.
$$

At \(\theta=0\),

$$
F(0)
=
Q^{-1}I-Q^{-2}J,
$$

so on \(H_R\)

$$
F(0)|_{H_R}=Q^{-1}I_{H_R}.
$$

Thus the linearized Legendre transform at the centroid is a scalar multiple of
the identity.  The \(e\)-coordinate tangent vector \(\delta\theta\in H_R\) and
the centered \(m\)-coordinate tangent vector \(\delta\mu\in H_R\) determine
the same conductor direction, up to the scalar \(Q^{-1}\).  The Hessian of
\(\psi\), the Hessian of the KL divergence at \(p_*\), and the Fisher metric
all have this same scalar block form.

**Proposition 3.2 (Doubly flat conductor splitting at \(p_*\)).**  At the
uniform categorical distribution, the conductor-block decomposition of
Paper 9 simultaneously diagonalizes:

1. the Fisher metric in the simplicial scale;
2. the Hessian of the log-partition function \(\psi\) at \(\theta=0\);
3. the quadratic part of the KL divergence at \(p_*\);
4. the linearized Legendre correspondence between \(e\)-coordinates
   \(\theta\) and centered \(m\)-coordinates \(p-p_*\).

In this sense the conductor decomposition is doubly flat at the centroid.
Off the centroid, \(F(\theta)\) is no longer a scalar on \(H_R\), so the
Legendre map separates the \(e\)-flat conductor directions from the \(m\)-flat
conductor directions.

This is the structural reason the theorem is centered at \(p_*\).  The
centroid is where Fisher orthogonality and both affine flat structures agree
on the same arithmetic splitting.

The uniqueness here is equivariant rather than absolute.  Since the Fisher
metric is scalar at \(p_*\), many arbitrary orthogonal decompositions could be
invented.  The additive translation action forces the character lines, and
the conductor/Galois symmetry packages those lines into primitive conductor
blocks.  Within that arithmetic symmetry class, the conductor splitting is the
distinguished simultaneous \(e\)-flat, \(m\)-flat, and Fisher-orthogonal
decomposition.

The same statement can be read directly from the log-partition cumulants.  The
first derivative \(\nabla\psi(0)\) is \(p_*\), the categorical position at the
centroid.  The second derivative \(D^2\psi(0)\) is the Fisher information and
therefore the quadratic metric/inertia term.  The next derivative,
\(D^3\psi(0)\), is the categorical skewness tensor; after translating between
the \(e\)- and \(m\)-flat pictures and raising an index with the Fisher metric,
it is the Amari-Chentsov operator \(K^{CH}\).  Thus the later
effective-action language does not add a new object at this level.  It renames
the same cumulant data already sitting behind the Fisher metric and its first
cubic break from flatness.

### 3.2 Why the Metric Is Forced

The Fisher metric does not merely happen to match the simplex Gram at
\(p_*\).  Under the symmetries relevant to this bridge, it is forced.

Suppose \(B\) is a positive Hermitian form on \(H_R\) invariant under additive
translations.  By Schur's lemma for the finite abelian regular
representation, \(B\) is diagonal in the additive-character basis:

$$
B(\chi,\psi)=0\qquad(\chi\ne\psi).
$$

Thus \(B\) assigns one positive scalar to each nontrivial character line.  If
one also imposes the conductor/Galois normalizer symmetry that acts
transitively on primitive characters of a fixed conductor, these scalars
collapse to one scalar per conductor block.  This is the largest
conductor-compatible family of \(R\)-equivariant inner products:

$$
B
=
\sum_{\mathfrak d\ne1}\lambda_{\mathfrak d}P_{\mathfrak d},
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathfrak d}>0.
$$

The categorical Fisher metric is the special point with all
\(\lambda_{\mathfrak d}\) equal, after the global scale is fixed.  Chentsov
sufficiency, or more concretely the full outcome-permutation symmetry of the
categorical family at the uniform distribution, rules out assigning different
statistical weights to different conductors.  The statistical manifold only
sees \(Q\) exchangeable outcomes; the conductor labels are extra arithmetic
structure, not extra statistical weights.

**Proposition 3.3 (Canonical Fisher scale).**  Among Hermitian forms on
\(H_R\) compatible with additive translation, conductor/Galois symmetry, and
the categorical Fisher sufficiency principle, the Fisher form at \(p_*\) is
unique up to a single global positive scalar.  In the simplicial normalization
used in Papers 8 and 9, this form is exactly

$$
\langle v_a,v_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}-Q^{-1}.
$$

This is the canonicity upgrade: the bridge is not a choice of a convenient
inner product.  It is the unique statistical inner product compatible with the
conductor symmetry once the categorical Fisher principle is imposed.

### 3.3 Conductors as Sufficient-Statistic Layers

There is also a statistical reading of the conductor lattice itself.  For
\(\mathfrak d\mid I\), let

$$
\pi_{\mathfrak d}:R=A/I\longrightarrow A/\mathfrak d
$$

be the quotient map, and let \({\mathcal F}_{\mathfrak d}\) be the sigma
algebra generated by its fibers.  The functions factoring through
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}\) are exactly the span of characters whose conductor
divides \(\mathfrak d\).  Therefore the cumulative conductor space

$$
L_{\le\mathfrak d}
=
\bigoplus_{\substack{\mathfrak e\mid\mathfrak d\\\mathfrak e\ne1}}
H_R^{[\mathfrak e]}
$$

is the tangent space to the exponential subfamily whose sufficient statistic
is the quotient observation \(\pi_{\mathfrak d}(x)\).  Passing from proper
divisors of \(\mathfrak d\) to \(\mathfrak d\) itself adds the primitive layer
\(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\).

This should not be read as saying that \(\pi_{\mathfrak d}\) is sufficient for
the full \(Q\)-outcome categorical family; it is not, unless
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}\) is injective.  It is sufficient for the
\({\mathcal F}_{\mathfrak d}\)-measurable subfamily, and the conductor lattice
records the refinement tower of these \(R\)-equivariant subfamilies.  This is
the finite categorical shadow of the usual Halmos-Savage/Lehmann sufficiency
logic: quotient sigma algebras organize which likelihood ratios the model is
allowed to see.

**Proposition 3.4 (Conductor lattice as sufficiency tower).**  The divisor
lattice of conductors is the lattice of quotient sufficient-statistic layers
for the finite \(R\)-equivariant categorical subfamilies determined by
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}:R\to A/\mathfrak d\).  The primitive block
\(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\) is the new tangent statistic added at conductor
\(\mathfrak d\).

This reframes Paper 9's divisor coloring: the pair colors are not only
arithmetic shells.  They are the visible residue of a refinement lattice of
finite sufficient statistics.

**Theorem 3.5 (FC1-A centroid natural quadratic macrovariables).**  Let

$$
Q_B(h)=\frac12\langle h,Bh\rangle_{F,*},
\qquad h\in H_R,
$$

be a real quadratic macrovariable jet at \(p_*\), with \(B\) Fisher
self-adjoint.  Suppose \(Q_B\) is invariant under additive translations of
\(R\), equivariant under the CRT/Hayes/Galois normalizer \(\Gamma_R\), and
relative finite-sufficient for a quotient layer
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}:R\to R/\mathfrak d\).  Then

$$
Q_B(h)
=
\sum_{\mathfrak e\mid\mathfrak d,\ \mathfrak e\ne1}
\lambda_{\mathfrak e}E_{\mathfrak e}(h),
\qquad
E_{\mathfrak e}(h)
=
\frac12\|\Pi_{\mathfrak e}h\|_{F,*}^2,
$$

up to the packetwise Fisher-orthogonal flag refinements explicitly chosen
inside a conductor packet.  In particular, no genuine cross-packet quadratic
Hamiltonian is natural under these hypotheses.

**Proof.**  Additive translation acts diagonally on the normalized character
basis: \(\tau_a e_\chi=\chi(a)e_\chi\).  Invariance of \(Q_B\) gives
\(\tau_a^*B\tau_a=B\).  Therefore each matrix entry satisfies

$$
B_{\psi,\chi}
=
\overline{\psi(a)}\chi(a)B_{\psi,\chi}
$$

for every \(a\in R\).  If \(\psi\ne\chi\), some \(a\) has
\(\chi(a)\psi(a)^{-1}\ne1\), so \(B_{\psi,\chi}=0\).  Thus \(B\) is diagonal
in the additive-character basis.  The normalizer \(\Gamma_R\) permutes the
character lines inside its packets, so equivariance makes the diagonal weights
constant on each primitive conductor packet.  Finally, Proposition 3.4 says
that a macrovariable factoring through \(\pi_{\mathfrak d}\) only sees the
cumulative space \(L_{\le\mathfrak d}\); all packet projectors outside that
space must have zero weight.  The surviving quadratic jets are therefore the
packet energies in the displayed sum.  A chosen NZSBTL or other
Fisher-orthogonal flag may refine coordinates inside one packet, but it does
not create a cross-packet invariant.  \(\square\)

This is a finite representation-theoretic consequence of the centroid
translation action, the arithmetic normalizer, and relative quotient
sufficiency.  It is not empirical evidence and does not classify arbitrary
smooth invariant functions inside a packet.

This is the point where the terminology of Rosas et al. becomes useful.  A
conductor layer is an informationally closed macrovariable relative to the
quotient categorical subfamily it defines.  The word "relative" matters:
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}\) is not sufficient for the full microscopic categorical
family unless it is injective, but it is sufficient for observers or models
whose likelihoods are \({\mathcal F}_{\mathfrak d}\)-measurable.  In that
restricted sense, the conductor lattice is not only a divisor lattice; it is a
hierarchy of closed information levels.

The same reading distinguishes four closure notions that should not be
collapsed:

1. **Partition closure:** a quotient or orbit partition has been specified.
2. **Sufficient-statistic closure:** the quotient statistic is sufficient for
   a restricted categorical subfamily.
3. **Normalizer closure:** the Hayes/Carlitz normalizer preserves the layer
   and its character packet.
4. **Filtered adjacency-algebra closure:** the off-centroid
   quotient-character adjacency operators close without hidden extension data.

Proposition 3.4 proves the second notion for the finite \(R\)-equivariant
subfamilies.  Papers 9 and 11 address the third and fourth notions.  Thus
Rosas' "informationally closed macrovariable" vocabulary gives a useful
external name to the sufficiency tower while also warning that higher closure
levels require additional arithmetic data.

## 4. Ramanujan Sums as Fisher Matrix Elements

Let \(P_{\mathfrak d}\) be the Fisher-orthogonal projector onto the conductor
block \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\).  Paper 9, Theorem 2.2, states that its kernel is

$$
(P_{\mathfrak d})_{a,c}
=
Q^{-1}C_{\mathfrak d}(a-c),
$$

where \(C_{\mathfrak d}\) is the generalized Ramanujan/Hayes conductor sum.
Since \(v_b=e_b-Q^{-1}{\bf 1}\) and \(P_{\mathfrak d}{\bf 1}=0\) for
\(\mathfrak d\ne1\),

$$
P_{\mathfrak d}v_b=P_{\mathfrak d}e_b.
$$

Therefore the projected score-frame matrix element is

$$
\langle v_a,P_{\mathfrak d}v_b\rangle_{F,*}
=
Q^{-1}C_{\mathfrak d}(a-b)
$$

in the unnormalized Paper 9 convention.  If

$$
T_\epsilon
=
\sum_{\mathfrak d\ne1}\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}P_{\mathfrak d},
\qquad
\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}\in\{\pm1\},
$$

then

$$
\langle T_\epsilon v_a,T_\eta v_b\rangle_{F,*}
=
Q^{-1}
\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak d\mid I\\\mathfrak d\ne1}}
\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}\eta_{\mathfrak d}
C_{\mathfrak d}(a-b).
$$

After unit normalization of simplex vertices, the prefactor becomes
\((Q-1)^{-1}\), exactly as in Paper 9, Theorem 3.1.

For \(R={\mathbb Z}/N{\mathbb Z}\), \(C_{\mathfrak d}\) becomes the ordinary
Ramanujan sum \(c_d\), and the displayed formula becomes Paper 8's
Ramanujan cross-Gram formula.  In the parity case \(d=2\),

$$
c_2(i-j)=(-1)^{i-j}=s_is_j,
$$

so the parity-dual cross-Gram is the Fisher matrix element obtained by
flipping the unique one-dimensional \(\Phi_2\) conductor line.

This is the third bridge:

**Proposition 4.1 (Conductor sums = Fisher matrix elements).**  The
Ramanujan, Hayes, and conductor sums in the cross-Gram formulas of Papers 8
and 9 are matrix elements of the Fisher metric at the uniform categorical
distribution between conductor-projected score vectors.

The phrase "cross-Gram" is therefore doing two jobs.  It is a frame-theoretic
Gram matrix, and it is also the Fisher information pairing of two block-signed
score frames at the maximum-entropy distribution.

## 5. The Boolean Group \(B_R\) as Fisher Sign Isometries

The group

$$
B_R
=
\left\{
\sum_{\mathfrak d\ne1}\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}P_{\mathfrak d}
:\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}\in\{\pm1\}
\right\}
$$

is an elementary abelian group of Fisher-orthogonal involutions of \(H_R\) at
the centroid.  Each element commutes with the additive translation action,
because each conductor block is translation-invariant.  Thus \(B_R\) is a
Boolean subgroup of the \(R\)-equivariant Fisher isometry group.

It is useful to be slightly precise about the word "orientation."  Once a real
form of \(H_R\) has been fixed, the determinant of a block-sign operator is

$$
\det(T_\epsilon)
=
\prod_{\mathfrak d\ne1}
\epsilon_{\mathfrak d}^{\dim_{\mathbb R}H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}}.
$$

The orientation-reversing elements are exactly those for which the total real
dimension of the flipped blocks is odd.  When the flipped block is a single
real line, the corresponding generator is a genuine Householder reflection.
This is the \(\Phi_2\) situation in Paper 8 and the norm-two binary-line
situation in Paper 9.

The quotient

$$
B_R/K_R,
\qquad
K_R=\{T\in B_R:T(V_R)=V_R\},
$$

then has a Fisher interpretation: it is the set of distinct block-signed
Fisher score frames modulo those sign changes that merely relabel the original
categorical score frame.

For \(R\cong{\mathbb F}_2^k\), Paper 8's Boolean Janus theorem says that
\(B_R/K_R\) is the Hamming-side orientation space

$$
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(B_R/K_R)=2^k-1-k.
$$

Information-geometrically, these are not abstract orientation bits floating
above the simplex.  They are the Boolean choices of Fisher-orthogonal
binary-character score-line signs at the uniform distribution, modulo
translation relabelings of the categorical outcomes.

In this all-binary case, the transform has a familiar name.  The character
table of \(R\cong{\mathbb F}_2^k\) is the Walsh-Hadamard matrix.  The
conductor lines are the nonconstant Walsh characters, the block-sign group
acts by changing Walsh signs, and a translation by \(b\in R\) multiplies the
Walsh character \(m\) by \((-1)^{m(b)}\).  Thus \(K_R\) is the punctured
first-order Reed-Muller, equivalently binary simplex, code on the nonzero
Walsh characters, and \(B_R/K_R\) is the corresponding Hamming-side quotient.

This gives Paper 8's Boolean Janus hierarchy a second dictionary.  In
coding-theory language it is simplex/Hamming duality.  In Boolean-function
language it is Walsh-Hadamard analysis on \({\mathbb F}_2^k\), the same basis
used for influences, noise stability, and hypercontractive estimates.  No
such estimates are needed for the present papers, but naming the dictionary
lets a TCS or probabilistic-combinatorics reader recognize the object
immediately.

This is the fourth bridge:

**Proposition 5.1 (\(B_R\) = Boolean Fisher sign skeleton).**  At the uniform
categorical distribution, \(B_R\) is the Boolean subgroup of
\(R\)-equivariant Fisher-orthogonal isometries acting by signs on conductor
blocks.  Its orientation-reversing elements are determined by the parity of
the total real dimension of the flipped conductor blocks.  The quotient
\(B_R/K_R\) indexes distinct block-signed Fisher score frames.

## 6. What Breaks Away from the Centroid

The bridge above is exact because the uniform distribution is fixed by the
regular translation action.  Away from the centroid, two things fail at once.

First, the basepoint is no longer fixed.  A translation sends

$$
p=(p_a)_{a\in R}
$$

to

$$
\tau_b p=(p_{a-b})_{a\in R}.
$$

Unless \(p=p_*\), this is a different point of \(\Delta_R^\circ\).  The
translation is still a global Fisher isometry from \(T_p\Delta_R^\circ\) to
\(T_{\tau_b p}\Delta_R^\circ\), because it is a permutation of outcomes, but
it is not an internal symmetry of the single tangent space \(T_p\Delta_R^\circ\).

Second, the Fisher metric at \(p\) is weighted:

$$
g^F_p(u,w)
=
\sum_{a\in R}\frac{u_aw_a}{p_a}.
$$

On the complexified tangent space we use the corresponding Hermitian
extension.  In the additive-character basis this becomes

$$
g^F_p(\chi,\psi)
=
\sum_{a\in R}
\frac{\chi(a)\overline{\psi(a)}}{p_a}.
$$

If \(m_p(a)=p_a^{-1}\), then

$$
g^F_p(\chi,\psi)
=
\widehat{m_p}(\psi\chi^{-1})
$$

in the unnormalized convention
\(\widehat f(\gamma)=\sum_a f(a)\overline{\gamma(a)}\).  At the centroid,
\(m_{p_*}\) is constant, so all nontrivial Fourier coefficients vanish and
distinct character lines are Fisher-orthogonal.  Away from the centroid, the
nonzero Fourier coefficients of \(1/p\) couple character lines.  Generically
they couple every conductor block to every other conductor block.

This gives the basic obstruction:

**Proposition 6.1 (Centroid obstruction).**  The fixed additive-character
decomposition of \(H_R\) is Fisher-orthogonal for the categorical metric
because \(p\) is uniform.  At a nonuniform point, the Fisher metric has
Fourier coupling coefficients given by the nonconstant Fourier modes of
\(1/p\).  Hence the conductor projectors of Papers 8 and 9 are not, in
general, Fisher-orthogonal projectors at \(p\ne p_*\).

**Proposition 6.1A (Exact nonuniform orthogonality locus).**  Let
\(B_{\mathfrak d}\subset \widehat R\setminus\{1\}\) denote the additive
characters in conductor packet \(\mathfrak d\).  For
\(p\in\Delta_R^\circ\), set \(m_p(a)=p_a^{-1}\).  The fixed conductor packets
are mutually Fisher-orthogonal at \(p\) if and only if

$$
\widehat{m_p}(\psi\chi^{-1})=0
$$

for every pair \(\chi\in B_{\mathfrak d}\),
\(\psi\in B_{\mathfrak e}\) with \(\mathfrak d\ne\mathfrak e\).  Equivalently,
the Fourier support of the reciprocal density \(m_p=1/p\) may contain only
quotient modes \(\eta\) whose translation action preserves conductor packets:
\(\eta\chi\) is either trivial or lies in the same conductor packet as
\(\chi\), for every nontrivial character \(\chi\).

*Proof.*  The Fisher matrix element in the additive-character basis is

$$
g^F_p(\chi,\psi)=\widehat{m_p}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

Mutual orthogonality of two distinct conductor packets is exactly the
vanishing of this matrix element for every cross-packet pair.  The quotient
description is the same condition after setting
\(\eta=\psi\chi^{-1}\): a nonzero coefficient \(\widehat{m_p}(\eta)\) is
allowed precisely when it never sends a source character into a different
nontrivial conductor packet.  This proves both directions. \(\square\)

Thus the special nonuniform loci are linear Fourier-support constraints on
the reciprocal density \(m_p\), followed by the nonlinear normalization
\(p_a\propto m_p(a)^{-1}\).  The finite atlases
`empirical/reports/nonuniform_orthogonality_loci_cyclic.md` and
`empirical/reports/nonuniform_orthogonality_loci_local.md` enumerate this
criterion for the cyclic rows through \(C_{24}\) and for the current local
rows \(F_4[u]/(u^2)\), \(F_4[u]/(u^3)\), \(F_2[u]/(u^4)\),
\(F_2[u]/(u^5)\), \(F_{16}\), and \(F_8[u]/(u^2)\).  In the cyclic atlas,
prime rows are single-packet and therefore vacuous; prime-power rows retain
nonconstant top-socle reciprocal modes; mixed-prime cyclic rows are rigid for
full conductor-block orthogonality.

For a perturbation

$$
p=p_*+h,\qquad h\in H_R,
$$

the reciprocal weight expands as

$$
\frac1{p_a}
=
Q\left(1-Qh_a+Q^2h_a^2-\cdots\right).
$$

Thus the first-order failure of conductor orthogonality is controlled by the
Fourier transform of \(h\):

$$
g^F_{p_*+h}(\chi,\psi)
=
Q^2\delta_{\chi,\psi}
-Q^2\widehat h(\psi\chi^{-1})
+O(\|h\|^2),
$$

in the same unnormalized Fourier convention.  This is probably the most
useful off-centroid formula in the whole bridge.  It says the conductor
decomposition does not simply disappear; it becomes a perturbative arithmetic
coupling tensor.

The first-order selection rule is immediate.  If

$$
h\in H_R^{[\mathfrak d]},
$$

then \(\widehat h\) is supported on characters of conductor \(\mathfrak d\).
Hence the first-order term can couple a character line \({\mathbb C}\chi\) to
a character line \({\mathbb C}\psi\) only when

$$
\operatorname{cond}(\psi\chi^{-1})=\mathfrak d.
$$

More generally, if \(h\) has conductor support \(D\), then first-order
coupling occurs only along quotients \(\psi\chi^{-1}\) whose conductors lie in
\(D\).  For generic \(h\) with that support, these are exactly the possible
nonzero first-order entries.

**Corollary 6.2 (First-order conductor selection rule).**  Let \(\chi\) and
\(\psi\) be additive characters of conductors \(\mathfrak e\) and
\(\mathfrak f\).  For a perturbation \(p=p_*+h\), the first-order Fisher
coupling between the character lines \({\mathbb C}\chi\) and
\({\mathbb C}\psi\) is

$$
-Q^2\widehat h(\psi\chi^{-1})
$$

in the raw-character Fisher convention.  Equivalently, after using normalized
characters \(e_\chi=Q^{-1/2}\chi\) and the global simplicial scale
\(\bar g^F=Q^{-1}g^F\), the first-order coefficient is
\(-\widehat h(\psi\chi^{-1})\).  Thus a displacement in conductor block
\(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\) creates first-order couplings only between character
lines whose quotient has conductor \(\mathfrak d\).

The second order is where the first nonlinear coefficient problem begins.  The
next term in the same expansion is

$$
+Q^3\widehat{h^2}(\psi\chi^{-1}),
$$

and Fourier transform converts pointwise multiplication into convolution:

$$
\widehat{h^2}
=
Q^{-1}(\widehat h * \widehat h)
$$

for convolution on the character group.  Thus the second-order raw-character
coefficient is equally

$$
Q^2(\widehat h * \widehat h)(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

If \(h\) is supported on conductor blocks
\(\mathfrak d_1,\mathfrak d_2,\ldots\), the second-order coupling is governed
by products of characters drawn from those conductor supports.  The resulting
quotient conductors lie in the divisor-lattice closure generated by those
blocks.  Section 7.3 proves this as an all-order support theorem; what remains
subtle, beyond support, is the exact multiplicity and cancellation formula,
which is a bilinear Hayes-Mobius problem at second order.

In Paper 8 language, a displacement in one cyclotomic block creates
first-order Fisher couplings between character lines whose quotient character
lies in that block.  In Paper 9 language, a displacement in one conductor
packet creates first-order couplings governed by the quotient-conductor
relations of the character group.  The off-centroid problem is therefore not
"do the blocks remain orthogonal?"  They do not, except in special symmetric
submanifolds.  The better question is:

> What is the conductor algebra of the Fisher coupling tensor
> \(p\mapsto g^F_p\) in additive-character coordinates?

That question is still native to Papers 8 and 9, but it is no longer a frame
packing question.  It is an information-geometric perturbation question.

## 7. Three Off-Centroid Continuations

The uniform theorem is exact.  The off-centroid continuation has several
possible meanings, and they should not be conflated.

### 7.1 Moving Fisher-conductor frames by the sphere pullback

The square-root map

$$
\varphi(p)_a=\sqrt{p_a}
$$

identifies the Fisher--Rao metric with four times the round metric on the
positive orthant of the unit sphere.  Given a Fisher geodesic from \(p_*\) to
\(p\), one can parallel-transport the conductor decomposition of
\(T_{p_*}\Delta_R^\circ\) along the corresponding great circle.  This produces
a Fisher-orthogonal decomposition of \(T_p\Delta_R^\circ\).

This continuation is geometrically clean but arithmetically costly.  The
transported subspaces are no longer the fixed additive-character conductor
subspaces of \({\mathbb C}^R\).  They depend on the path, or on a chosen
canonical geodesic from \(p_*\) to \(p\).  The additive translation action no
longer fixes \(p\), so the transported decomposition is not an internal
\(R\)-representation at \(p\).

This is a good Fisher-geometric continuation, not a literal conductor-block
theorem.

### 7.2 Fixed \(e\)-flat and \(m\)-flat conductor webs

The categorical family is dually flat.  In natural coordinates, the
\(e\)-flat coordinate is \(\theta\in H_R\).  In moment coordinates, the
\(m\)-flat coordinate is \(p-p_*\in H_R\).  The conductor blocks therefore
define two natural families of flat slices:

$$
\theta\in H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
p-p_*\in H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}.
$$

These slices are flat for the corresponding Amari connection.  They are not
generically Fisher-orthogonal for the Riemannian metric away from \(p_*\).
That is not a defect; it is exactly what dually flat geometry predicts.  The
flat affine coordinates and the Fisher metric are compatible through the
Legendre transform, not through global linear orthogonality.

This is probably the most faithful continuation of the Papers 8 and 9
language.  The conductor blocks remain fixed linear directions in both
\(e\)- and \(m\)-coordinates, while the Fisher metric records their nonlinear
couplings.  Section 3.1 explains why this is the right continuation: at
\(\theta=0\), the Legendre map has scalar derivative, so the two flat
conductor webs and the Fisher-orthogonal conductor splitting coincide.  Away
from \(\theta=0\), the webs remain meaningful but separate.

### 7.3 Conductor-flat Fisher expansion

The preceding paragraph can be made into an actual off-centroid theorem.  We
first fix the normalization that removes the "up to scale" ambiguity from
Section 6.  Let

$$
G=\widehat R
$$

be the additive character group.  Throughout this subsection use the
unnormalized Fourier transform

$$
\widehat f(\gamma)
=
\sum_{a\in R}f(a)\overline{\gamma(a)},
\qquad
f(a)
=
Q^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in G}\widehat f(\gamma)\gamma(a),
$$

and convolution on \(G\)

$$
(F*G)(\gamma)
=
\sum_{\alpha\beta=\gamma}F(\alpha)G(\beta).
$$

Then

$$
\widehat{fg}
=
Q^{-1}\widehat f*\widehat g,
\qquad
\widehat{h^n}
=
Q^{1-n}\widehat h^{*n}
$$

for \(n\ge1\).  For a function \(f\in{\mathbb C}^R\), let

$$
\operatorname{supp}\widehat f
=
\{\gamma\in G:\widehat f(\gamma)\ne0\}.
$$

For a subset \(S\subseteq G\), write

$$
S^{(n)}
=
\{\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_n:\gamma_i\in S\},
\qquad
S^{(0)}=\{1\}.
$$

This is the \(n\)-fold product set in the dual group.  In additive notation it
is the \(n\)-fold sumset.

Let \(e_\chi=Q^{-1/2}\chi\) be the normalized character vector, and let
\(\bar g^F=Q^{-1}g^F\) be the globally rescaled Fisher metric used to match
the simplicial Gram at \(p_*\).

**Normalization Lemma (Fisher-conductor Taylor normalization).**  For
\(p\in\Delta_R^\circ\) and nontrivial additive characters \(\chi,\psi\),

$$
\bar g^F_p(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
Q^{-2}\widehat{p^{-1}}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

Along an \(m\)-flat ray

$$
p_m(t)=p_*+t h,\qquad h\in H_R,
$$

with \(|t|Q\max_a |h_a|<1\), or formally as a Taylor expansion at \(t=0\),

$$
\bar g^F_{p_m(t)}(e_\chi,e_\psi)
=
\sum_{n\ge0}(-t)^n
\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}),
$$

where \(\widehat h^{*0}=\delta_1\).  Thus the first three coefficients are

$$
C_0(\chi,\psi)=\delta_{\chi,\psi},
\qquad
C_1(\chi,\psi)=-\widehat h(\psi\chi^{-1}),
$$

and

$$
C_2(\chi,\psi)
=
(\widehat h*\widehat h)(\psi\chi^{-1})
=
\sum_{\alpha\beta=\psi\chi^{-1}}
\widehat h(\alpha)\widehat h(\beta).
$$

Equivalently, in the raw-character convention of Section 6,

$$
g^F_{p_m(t)}(\chi,\psi)
=
Q^2\sum_{n\ge0}(-t)^n
\widehat h^{*n}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

**Proof.**  On the complexified tangent space use the Hermitian Fisher
extension

$$
g^F_p(u,w)=\sum_a \frac{u_a\overline{w_a}}{p_a}.
$$

For raw characters,

$$
g^F_p(\chi,\psi)
=
\sum_a p_a^{-1}\chi(a)\overline{\psi(a)}
=
\sum_a p_a^{-1}\overline{(\psi\chi^{-1})(a)}
=
\widehat{p^{-1}}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

The normalized characters contribute the factor \(Q^{-1}\), and the
simplicial Fisher scale contributes another factor \(Q^{-1}\).  Along
\(p_m(t)=p_*+th\),

$$
p_m(t)_a^{-1}
=
Q(1+tQh_a)^{-1}
=
Q\sum_{n\ge0}(-Qt)^n h_a^n.
$$

Taking Fourier transforms and using
\(\widehat{h^n}=Q^{1-n}\widehat h^{*n}\) gives the displayed series.
\(\square\)

The support theorem follows immediately.  Since convolution support is
contained in product support, the \(n\)-th Taylor coefficient can couple the
character lines \({\mathbb C}\chi\) and \({\mathbb C}\psi\) only when

$$
\psi\chi^{-1}\in(\operatorname{supp}\widehat h)^{(n)}.
$$

This is the promised sumset filtration.

**Theorem 7.1 (Conductor-flat Fisher expansion, \(m\)-flat form).**  Along an
\(m\)-flat ray \(p_m(t)=p_*+th\), the Fisher tensor in additive-character
coordinates has an all-order conductor-convolution expansion.  If
\(S=\operatorname{supp}\widehat h\), then the order-\(n\) coefficient of
\(\bar g^F_{p_m(t)}(e_\chi,e_\psi)\) is supported only on quotients
\(\psi\chi^{-1}\in S^{(n)}\).  Grouping characters by conductor gives an
order-by-order conductor selection rule: block coupling at depth \(n\) is
possible only when the quotient of the two character lines lies in the
\(n\)-fold product support of the displacement.

This theorem is stronger than Corollary 6.2 but contains it as the \(n=1\)
case.  The \(n=2\) term is the bilinear Hayes-Mobius tensor:

$$
\widehat{h^2}
=
Q^{-1}(\widehat h*\widehat h),
$$

so the second-order coefficient of the rescaled normalized metric is
\(\widehat h*\widehat h\), governed by two-step paths through the conductor
support of \(h\).

There is an \(e\)-flat analogue.  Let

$$
p_e(t)=\operatorname{softmax}(t\theta),
\qquad
\theta\in H_R.
$$

Then

$$
\frac1{p_e(t)_a}
=
Z(t)e^{-t\theta_a},
\qquad
Z(t)=\sum_{b\in R}e^{t\theta_b}.
$$

Hence

$$
g^F_{p_e(t)}(\chi,\psi)
=
Z(t)
\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{(-t)^n}{n!}
\widehat{\theta^n}(\psi\chi^{-1}).
$$

The scalar factor \(Z(t)\) only multiplies Fourier coefficients; it does not
create new nontrivial character support.  Thus the order-\(n\) coefficient is
supported in the union of \(S^{(j)}\) for \(0\le j\le n\), where
\(S=\operatorname{supp}\widehat\theta\).  In particular, the first-order
selection rule is the same as in the \(m\)-flat case.

**Corollary 7.2 (Conductor-flat \(e\)-ray support).**  Along an \(e\)-flat ray
\(p_e(t)=\operatorname{softmax}(t\theta)\), the Fisher tensor is controlled by
the same conductor sumset filtration, with lower-depth terms reappearing at
higher orders because of the scalar softmax normalization \(Z(t)\).  No
off-diagonal coupling can occur unless the character quotient lies in the
product semigroup generated by \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat\theta\).

The \(m\)-flat theorem is the cleaner algebraic object; the \(e\)-flat theorem
is the more faithful natural-parameter statement.  Together they make precise
what Section 7.2 only framed: conductor flats are the off-centroid directions
where the Fisher breakage remains arithmetic to all orders.

In the cumulant-tower reading, this all-order support theorem says that the
categorical effective-action data remains finite-indexed after one leaves the
centroid.  The first cumulant records the displacement \(h\), the second gives
the Fisher metric being expanded, the third gives the \(K^{CH}\) source term,
and the fourth-and-higher coefficients are precisely the controlled residuals
and projection defects one sees after compressing to conductor blocks or to a
chosen quotient macrovariable.  The quotient-character adjacency algebra is
therefore not extra physics decoration; it is the finite bookkeeping system
for the whole cumulant expansion.

### 7.4 The split \(Q_f=4\) expansion

The smallest complete worked case is the split row of Paper 9's \(Q_f=4\)
atlas:

$$
R\cong{\mathbb F}_2\times{\mathbb F}_2.
$$

Write the three nontrivial characters as

$$
\alpha=\chi_t,\qquad
\beta=\chi_{t+1},\qquad
\gamma=\chi_f=\alpha\beta.
$$

The three conductor blocks are the three lines

$$
H^{[t]}={\mathbb R}\alpha,\qquad
H^{[t+1]}={\mathbb R}\beta,\qquad
H^{[f]}={\mathbb R}\gamma.
$$

For a single-conductor \(m\)-flat displacement

$$
p(t)=p_*+s\alpha,\qquad |s|<1/4,
$$

put \(q=4s\).  Since \(\alpha^2=1\),

$$
\frac1{p(t)}
=
\frac4{1+q\alpha}
=
\frac4{1-q^2}(1-q\alpha).
$$

Therefore, in the ordered nontrivial character basis \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\),
the Fisher matrix has support pattern

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&\alpha&\beta&\gamma\\
\hline
\alpha&*&0&0\\
\beta&0&*&*\\
\gamma&0&*&*
\end{array}
$$

more explicitly,

$$
g^F_{p(t)}(\chi,\psi)
=
\frac{16}{1-q^2}\delta_{\chi,\psi}
-
\frac{16q}{1-q^2}\mathbf 1_{\psi\chi^{-1}=\alpha}.
$$

Thus a displacement in \(H^{[t]}\) produces first-order cross-coupling only
between \(H^{[t+1]}\) and \(H^{[f]}\), the unique nontrivial block pair whose
character quotient is \(\alpha\).  Even orders give diagonal corrections;
odd orders repeat the same \(\beta\)-\(\gamma\) cross-coupling support.  No
new support appears after the first step.

The two other single-conductor directions are obtained by cyclically
permuting \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\).  This is the exact \(Q_f=4\) realization of
the first-order rule:

$$
\text{a displacement in }\delta
\text{ couples precisely the block pair with quotient }\delta.
$$

For a mixed displacement

$$
p(t)=p_*+s\alpha+r\beta,
$$

the first-order support is \(\{\alpha,\beta\}\).  The second-order support is

$$
\{\alpha,\beta\}^{(2)}=\{1,\gamma\}.
$$

Consequently, order one couples the \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-quotient block
pairs, while order two adds diagonal corrections and the new cross-coupling
whose quotient is \(\gamma\), namely the pair \(H^{[t]}\) with
\(H^{[t+1]}\).  By the second step the union of supports is the whole
nontrivial dual group, so later orders change coefficients but do not create
new kinds of block coupling.

**Proposition 7.3 (Split \(Q_f=4\) sumset filtration).**  In the split
\({\mathbb F}_2\times{\mathbb F}_2\) atlas, off-centroid Fisher coupling along
an \(m\)-flat displacement is completely governed by the product-set
filtration in the dual group \(({\mathbb F}_2)^2\).  A single-conductor
displacement alternates between one cross-block quotient at odd orders and
diagonal correction at even orders.  A two-conductor displacement saturates
the nontrivial support by order two.

This example is small, but it is the first genuine off-centroid arithmetic
Fisher calculation in the series.  It proves that the slogan from Section 8
is not only interpretive: the failure of Fisher orthogonality is literally a
graded conductor-sumset object.

The other two \(Q_f=4\) rows explain why the split case is the right first
display.  In the irreducible quadratic row there is only one nontrivial
conductor block of dimension \(3\), so the conductor-depth filtration is
trivial at the block level.  In the nilpotent row there is one binary line and
one two-dimensional nilpotent block; the same support theorem applies, but the
interesting coefficients already depend on how the two character lines inside
the nilpotent block interact.  The split row is the first atlas in which every
nontrivial conductor block is a visible line, so the product-set rule can be
read directly from the block labels.

### 7.4.1 Closed-form bilinear order-2 Hayes-Möbius across the full \(Q_f=4\) atlas

Proposition 7.3 settled the split row.  This subsection closes Problem 10.1
item (4) (the bilinear Hayes-Möbius coefficient vector at order 2) for the
remaining two rows by promoting the line-level matrix-element identity
\([C_2(h)]_{\chi,\psi} = \widehat h^{*2}(\psi\chi^{-1})\) (Normalization Lemma at
\(n=2\)) to the operator-valued statement of Paper 11 Prop 1.2.

**Lemma 7.3.A (Klein-four Fourier-square).**  *For every \(R\) with \(Q_f = 4\),
the additive dual is the Klein four-group \(\widehat R = \{1,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}\)
with \(\alpha\beta = \gamma\) and every element self-inverse.  For \(h \in H_R\)
with \(\widehat h = (A,B,C)\) on the nontrivial characters \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\),*
$$
\widehat h^{*2}(1) = A^2 + B^2 + C^2,\qquad
\widehat h^{*2}(\alpha) = 2BC,\qquad
\widehat h^{*2}(\beta) = 2AC,\qquad
\widehat h^{*2}(\gamma) = 2AB.
$$

*Proof.*  Direct expansion of \(\widehat h^{*2}(\eta) = \sum_{\eta_1}\widehat h(\eta_1)\widehat h(\eta\eta_1)\)
using \(\widehat h(1) = 0\) and \(\eta_1^{-1} = \eta_1\) in the Klein four-group;
the four-term sum reduces to two equal nonzero contributions for each
\(\eta \ne 1\). \(\square\)

**Proposition 7.4 (Closed-form bilinear order-2 across \(Q_f=4\)).**  *The
operator \(C_2^{[\mathfrak f, \mathfrak e]}(h)\) for each row of the atlas:*

*(Split, \(R = \mathbb F_2 \times \mathbb F_2\), partition \(\{\{\alpha\},\{\beta\},\{\gamma\}\}\).)
Each within-block diagonal is the scalar \((A^2+B^2+C^2)\) on the 1-dim packet.
Each off-block operator is rank 1 with coefficient given by Lemma 7.3.A:*
$$
C_2^{[\mathfrak f, \mathfrak e]}(h) = \widehat h^{*2}(\eta_{\mathfrak f \mathfrak e}) \cdot A_{\eta_{\mathfrak f \mathfrak e}}^{[\mathfrak f, \mathfrak e]}
\qquad \text{for } \mathfrak f \ne \mathfrak e,
$$
*where \(\eta_{\mathfrak f \mathfrak e} = \chi_f \chi_e^{-1}\) is the unique
quotient character connecting the two packets.*

*(Nilpotent, \(R = \mathbb F_2[u]/(u^2)\), partition \(\{X_u = \{\alpha\}, X_{u^2} = \{\beta,\gamma\}\}\).)
The within-\(X_{u^2}\) operator carries a non-identity \(2\times 2\) swap
in addition to the universal diagonal:*
$$
C_2^{[u^2, u^2]}(h) = (A^2 + B^2 + C^2) I_2 + 2BC \cdot S_{u^2},
\qquad
S_{u^2} = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$
*so the matrix in basis \((\beta, \gamma)\) is
\(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} A^2+B^2+C^2 & 2BC \\ 2BC & A^2+B^2+C^2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\).
The cross-block operator is the \(2\times 1\) column \((2AB, 2AC)^\top\) in
basis \((\alpha) \to (\beta, \gamma)\).*

*(Irreducible, \(R = \mathbb F_4\), partition \(\{X_f = \{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}\}\).)
The single operator is*
$$
C_2^{[f,f]}(h) =
\begin{pmatrix}
A^2+B^2+C^2 & 2AB & 2AC \\
2AB & A^2+B^2+C^2 & 2BC \\
2AC & 2BC & A^2+B^2+C^2
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad \text{in basis } (\alpha, \beta, \gamma).
$$
*Eigenvalues are \((A^2+B^2+C^2) + \lambda_i\), where \(\lambda_i\) are the
three roots of \(\lambda^3 - 4(A^2B^2 + A^2C^2 + B^2C^2)\lambda - 16 A^2 B^2 C^2 = 0\).*

**Proof.**  Apply Paper 11 Prop 1.2 with \(n = 2\): the matrix entries of
\(C_2^{[\mathfrak f, \mathfrak e]}(h)\) at \((e_\chi, e_\psi)\) are
\(\widehat h^{*2}(\psi\chi^{-1}) \mathbf 1[\chi \in X_{\mathfrak e}, \psi \in X_{\mathfrak f}]\),
substituted by Lemma 7.3.A and the conductor partition specific to each row.
The within-\(X_{u^2}\) swap arises because \(\alpha\beta = \gamma\) keeps both
\(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) in \(X_{u^2}\), while the other two non-identity
characters \(\beta, \gamma\) would map either out of \(X_{u^2}\). \(\square\)

**Remark 7.4.A.**  The within-\(X_{u^2}\) swap \(2BC \cdot S_{u^2}\) is the
smallest concrete example of an operator-valued conductor compression that
scalar trace cannot detect: \(\mathrm{tr}(S_{u^2}) = 0\), so trace compression
of \(C_2^{[u^2,u^2]}(h)\) returns only \(2(A^2+B^2+C^2)\) and erases all
information about \(BC\).  This realizes Paper 11 §1.2's motivation for
operator-valued (rather than scalar) compression.

**Remark 7.4.B (Ext-CH transparency).**  By Paper 11 Cor 8.3, the order-2
nilpotent swap \(2BC \cdot S_{u^2}\) lives in the convolution-additive
subalgebra \(\mathcal A_R^{\mathrm{conv}}\) and is therefore Ext-CH-transparent.
The first nonzero \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)-sensitive coefficient on
\(\mathbb F_4[u]/(u^2)\) appears at order 3 (Paper 11 Theorem 9.1.1's
\(srt^2\)-coefficient = 5 representative).  This is a confidence guard for
empirical work: a residual at order 2 in the nilpotent row is
convolution-additive, NOT an Ext-CH signature.

### 7.5 Carlitz-Hayes equivariance as the next symmetry layer

Problem 9.6 asks for Carlitz-equivariant spectral compounds: make the
Carlitz-Hayes framing act as machinery rather than analogy.  In the present
bridge, the likely role of that equivariance is not to restore global
Fisher-orthogonality away from \(p_*\).  A generic categorical distribution has
no nontrivial internal Fisher isometries beyond its label stabilizer.

The more plausible role is subtler and more valuable: Carlitz equivariance may
organize the off-centroid coupling tensor.  The Hayes character basis already
diagonalizes the centroid Fisher metric.  Away from the centroid, the Fourier
coefficients of \(1/p\) govern the couplings.  A Carlitz normalizer could act
on those coefficients, on quotient-conductor relations, and on distinguished
\(e\)- or \(m\)-geodesic families.

So Problem 9.6 becomes, in information-geometric language:

> Construct the Carlitz-Hayes conductor calculus not only for the centroid
> Fisher metric, but for the full Fisher tensor \(p\mapsto g^F_p\) and its
> \(e/m\)-flat geodesic coordinate systems.

That is the version of (a) that seems worth chasing.

## 8. Which Version Should Be True?

There are two tempting answers to the off-centroid question.

**Version A.**  Papers 8 and 9 are the linearization at the centroid of a
curved dually flat object that still admits an \(R\)-equivariant
Hayes-refined conductor theory along \(m\)- and \(e\)-geodesics.

**Version B.**  The conductor decomposition is intrinsically a centroid
phenomenon.  The moment one leaves maximum entropy, the Levi-Civita
Fisher--Rao geometry and the loss of basepoint symmetry break
cyclotomic/conductor orthogonality.

The version I would rather be true is a refined Version A.  It would make
Papers 8 and 9 the visible first jet of a full arithmetic information geometry:
conductor \(e\)-flats, conductor \(m\)-flats, Hayes/Carlitz normalizers,
Ramanujan coupling tensors, and maybe arithmetic geodesic charts.  That would
turn the frame compounds into the tangent shadow of something genuinely
curved.

The version I would bet is true is Version B for exact orthogonality and a
weakened Version A for structure.  Exact fixed conductor-block
Fisher-orthogonality is almost certainly a centroid theorem.  The reason is
hard to evade: the whole construction depends on the regular \(R\)-translation
representation being unitary on one tangent space, and only the uniform
distribution is fixed by all translations.  Once \(p\) is not uniform, the
metric contains the multiplier \(1/p_a\), and its nonconstant Fourier modes
mix the character lines.

But that does not make the off-centroid theory uninteresting.  It makes it
more interesting.  The breakage is structured.  The first-order mixing is
controlled by the Fourier transform of the displacement \(h=p-p_*\).  Higher
orders are controlled by powers of \(h\), hence by convolution in the character
group.  That is exactly the kind of place where Ramanujan, Hayes, and Carlitz
bookkeeping should matter.

The slogan I would use:

> The conductor decomposition is Fisher-orthogonal only at the centroid, but
> the way Fisher orthogonality fails is itself conductor-arithmetic.

Which orders should have closed forms?  The support answer is now Theorem 7.1:
all of them.  The expansion

$$
\frac1{p_*+h}
=
Q\sum_{n\ge0}(-Qh)^n
$$

shows that the \(n\)-th Taylor coefficient of the Fisher tensor is governed by
\(\widehat{h^n}\), hence by the \(n\)-fold convolution of \(\widehat h\) on
the character group.  That gives an all-order conductor-convolution series.

The line-level coefficient answer is now clean.  First order is just the
Fourier coefficient of \(h\), and second order is the convolution square of
\(\widehat h\).  The cautious part begins when those line coefficients are
summed or compressed by conductor block: second order should still have a
usable bilinear Hayes-Mobius formula, especially in squarefree and \(Q_f=4\)
atlas cases.  Beyond second order, the closed form exists formally as a finite
Hayes-Mobius/convolution expansion, but the human-readable simplification may
become a combinatorial problem rather than a single elegant formula.

The next refinement, developed in Paper 11, is that this block compression
must be operator-valued.  If \(X_{\mathfrak e}\) and \(X_{\mathfrak f}\) are
conductor packets and \(\eta\) is a quotient character, the basic block
operator is the partial quotient-shift

$$
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e}e_\chi
=
\begin{cases}
e_{\eta\chi},&
\chi\in X_{\mathfrak e},\ \eta\chi\in X_{\mathfrak f},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

The \(n\)-th compressed coefficient is therefore

$$
C_n^{[\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e]}(h)
=
\sum_\eta
\widehat h^{*n}(\eta)
A_\eta^{\mathfrak f,\mathfrak e},
$$

an element of the quotient-character adjacency algebra rather than a single
number.  The scalar trace can erase the first genuine within-block invariant,
as the nilpotent \(Q_f=4\) row shows.

This suggests a sharper geometric question.  The product sets

$$
S^{(0)}\subseteq S^{(0)}\cup S^{(1)}
\subseteq S^{(0)}\cup S^{(1)}\cup S^{(2)}
\subseteq\cdots
$$

define a filtration of the off-centroid Fisher tensor by conductor-depth in
the dual group.  Does this filtration split canonically, in a Hodge-like way,
or only after passing to an associated graded object?  I would bet on the
associated graded with nontrivial extension data.  The centroid gives a clean
direct sum, but away from it the Legendre map, softmax normalization, and
lower-depth terms reappearing at higher orders all point toward extension
classes rather than a global splitting.  If a Carlitz-Hayes symmetry acts
cleanly, it should act first on these graded pieces and then expose the
extensions between them.

That is the strongest bridge because it keeps the theorem honest and opens a
real research door.

## 11. The Flagship Problem

The preceding sections leave one problem that should be treated as the
flagship continuation of Papers 8 and 9.  The centroid theorem is complete:
there, the Fisher score frame, the conductor-block decomposition, and the
\(e/m\)-flat tangent splittings coincide.  The next object is not another
static orthogonality theorem.  It is the arithmetic tensor that measures how
the centroid resolution breaks when information is introduced.

**Problem 10.1: Fisher-Conductor Coupling Tensor.**  Let \(R=A/I\) be a finite
quotient ring and let \(p\in\Delta_R^\circ\).  Express the Fisher metric

$$
g^F_p(u,w)=\sum_{a\in R}\frac{u_aw_a}{p_a}
$$

in the additive-character basis of \(H_R\), grouped by conductor.  Determine
the conductor selection rules and Ramanujan/Hayes/Carlitz formulas governing
the block couplings

$$
g^F_p\left(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]},H_R^{[\mathfrak e]}\right).
$$

The terminology in this problem is deliberately conductor-first.  The
theorem-level objects are additive-character lines, primitive conductor
blocks, quotient-conductor relations, and conductor-depth filtrations.  Prime
language should be used only after a specialization has been proved: prime
\(N\), prime powers, CRT products of distinct primes, norm-two
one-dimensional blocks, or mixed-composite conductor patterns.  In this sense
conductors are the rigorous coordinates, while prime-factorization language is
the earned interpretation.

In particular:

1. Classify the nonuniform \(p\) for which the conductor blocks remain
   mutually Fisher-orthogonal.
2. Use the normalized Taylor lemma to compress the line-level coefficients
   into operator-valued conductor-block formulas in the quotient-character
   adjacency algebra.
3. For single-conductor \(m\)- and \(e\)-flat rays, determine when the
   support filtration collapses to a two-cycle, as it does for binary lines,
   and when higher product sets create genuinely new block couplings.
4. Compute the second-order bilinear Hayes-Mobius coefficient vector coming from
   \(\widehat{h^2}=Q^{-1}(\widehat h*\widehat h)\) in the unnormalized
   Fourier convention, beginning with the \(Q_f=4\)
   split/nilpotent/irreducible atlases.  **[CLOSED — Proposition 7.4, §7.4.1.]**
   The closed form for all three rows follows from the universal Klein-four
   Fourier-square identity (Lemma 7.3.A) combined with the conductor partition;
   the nilpotent row uniquely realizes an operator-valued non-identity (2×2
   swap with coefficient \(2BC\)) that scalar trace compression cannot detect.
5. Determine whether \(e\)-geodesics or \(m\)-geodesics initialized in one
   conductor block have Fisher coupling profiles controlled by closed
   Ramanujan/Hayes formulas.
6. In the function-field case, identify the Carlitz-Hayes normalizer acting
   on these operator-valued coupling tensors.
7. Decide whether the conductor-depth filtration of the off-centroid Fisher
   tensor splits canonically or only has a natural associated graded object,
   and identify the local Carlitz-Hayes extension class when it does not.

This is the honest off-centroid continuation of Papers 8 and 9.  The centroid
frame theorem is the zeroth-order term; the first-order term is a conductor
selection rule; Theorem 7.1 gives the all-order support filtration; the
second-order coefficient problem is an operator-valued Hayes-Mobius
computation; and the new object is the full arithmetic Taylor expansion of
Fisher geometry around the centroid.

The practical research program is therefore: first prove coefficient formulas,
then compute small atlases, then classify nonuniform orthogonality loci, then
identify the Carlitz-Hayes normalizer action on the resulting tensors.  In
this order, Problem 10.1 becomes the place where the spectral-compound,
Dedekind-quotient, and information-geometric threads converge.

**Problem 10.2: Empirical Fisher-Pullback Decomposition.**  Let
\(F:A\to\Delta_R^\circ\) be a learned activation-to-behavior map on a concept
region, and let \(g_y\) be the Fisher/Hellinger metric on behavior space.
Compute both the quadratic pullback

$$
G_F(h)=J_F(h)^\top g_y(F(h))J_F(h)
$$

and the cubic correction obtained by pulling back the Amari-Chentsov tensor,
including the covariant Hessian terms of \(F\) when \(F\) is not locally
affine.  Decompose the resulting path discrepancy into:

1. the intrinsic behavior-side cubic \(K^{CH}\) on \(\Delta_R^\circ\);
2. activation-side distortion of \(F\), measured by the failure of
   \(J_F^\top g_yJ_F\) to match the fitted activation-manifold metric;
3. residual parameterization error from spline choice, finite sampling, or
   output directions outside the quotient conductor packet.

Equivalently, the learned map \(F\) pulls back the log-partition cumulant
tower to the activation region.  The pulled-back first cumulant is the
behavior position \(F(a)\); the second is the learned metric
\(J_F^\top g_yJ_F\); the third is the behavior-side \(K^{CH}\) source together
with the covariant Hessian terms of \(F\); and the fourth-and-higher
cumulants are the natural place to record projection defects and controlled
residuals.  This is the modest empirical version of the later
categorical-effective-action slogan.

For the weekday task of Wurgaft et al., \(R=C_7\) is prime, so the nontrivial
behavior tangent space is one six-dimensional conductor packet.  This makes
the empirical centroid replacement a minimal quantitative test of Problem
10.1: the character-basis convolution coefficients of \(K^{CH}\) are explicit,
and any leftover gap after the behavior-side calculation is activation-side
pullback distortion.

**Problem 10.3: Macrovariable Closure Audit.**  For each small finite
quotient \(R=A/I\), list the conductor macrovariables
\(\pi_{\mathfrak d}:R\to A/\mathfrak d\) and classify their closure status at
four levels:

$$
\text{partition}
\Rightarrow
\text{sufficient statistic}
\Rightarrow
\text{normalizer}
\Rightarrow
\text{filtered adjacency algebra}.
$$

The first level records the quotient partition.  The second is Proposition
3.4.  The third asks whether the relevant Hayes/Carlitz normalizer preserves

level is exactly the \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\)-type datum of Paper 11.

This turns \(\operatorname{Ext}_{CH}\) from an internal arithmetic diagnostic
into a macrovariable-closure obstruction: it measures when a conductor
macrovariable is closed as a quotient statistic but not closed as a full
off-centroid Fisher adjacency algebra.

## 12. The Two-Axis Descent: Nested Zero-Sum Balance-Transfer Ladder and Prime Spectral Dual

The off-centroid coupling tensor of Problem 10.1 is not the only structural
object the centroid produces.  Independent of the off-centroid Taylor
expansion, the zero-sum hyperplane \(H_R\) itself carries a *canonical
nested descent* whose dual lives in the prime conductor lattice.  This
section formalizes that descent, identifies its dual on the spectral side,
and writes the joint information-investment optimization principle as a
precise theorem-target.

### 12.1 The Nested Zero-Sum Balance-Transfer Ladder (NZSBTL)

Let \(R\) be a finite set of size \(N\) and \(H_R\subset{\mathbb R}^R\) the
zero-sum hyperplane, Fisher-equipped at the centroid \(p_*\) (so that the
Fisher Gram on \(H_R\) is \(Q\,I_{H_R}\), up to the global scale of
Section 2).

**Definition 12.1 (NZSBTL).**  An *NZSBTL* of \(H_R\) is a maximal flag
of Fisher-orthogonal zero-sum subhyperplanes

$$
H_R=H^{(0)}\supset H^{(1)}\supset\cdots\supset H^{(N-2)}=L,
$$

where each \(H^{(k)}\) has dimension \(N-1-k\), each step picks a single
Fisher-orthogonal direction \(L_k\subset H^{(k-1)}\) and sets
\(H^{(k)}=L_k^{\perp_F}\cap H^{(k-1)}\), and the chain terminates in a
line \(L=H^{(N-2)}\).  Equivalently, an NZSBTL of \(H_R\) is an ordered
Fisher-orthogonal basis of \(H_R\) up to scaling of each axis.

**Proposition 12.2 (Balance-currency budget).**  *At \(p_*\), every NZSBTL
of \(H_R\) has total Fisher length \(N-1\): the descent from
\(H^{(k-1)}\) to \(H^{(k)}\) spends one zero-mean Fisher coordinate
along \(L_k\), and the cumulative Fisher variance across all \(N-1\)
steps equals \(\operatorname{tr}(g^F_{p_*}|_{H_R})=Q(N-1)\) divided by
the global scale.*

**Proof.**  By Fisher-orthogonality at the centroid, the chain is an
\((N-1)\)-step orthogonal direct-sum decomposition of \(H_R\) into
one-dimensional lines.  Each coordinate is the Fisher-orthogonal
projection onto \(L_k\), which has Fisher mean zero on \(H^{(k-1)}\) (the
parent zero-sum hyperplane is itself centered).  Variances add by
Fisher-orthogonality.  \(\square\)

We call the coordinate along \(L_k\) the *balance currency* of step \(k\):
it is the single zero-sum constraint that gets spent (or, dually,
released) when the parent hyperplane is replaced by its \(L_k\)-orthogonal
complement.

**Reading.**  An NZSBTL is the *additive* descent of the simplicial frame:
each step removes one translation degree of freedom from the categorical
tangent space.  The maximum-entropy ("natural exploration") choice
samples each \(L_k\) uniformly on the unit Fisher-sphere of
\(H^{(k-1)}\), but the *structured* choices are those that respect
intrinsic symmetries of \(R\) — and the canonical such symmetry is the
conductor decomposition.

**Definition 12.3 (Conductor-respecting NZSBTL).**  Suppose \(R\) is a
finite abelian group with conductor packets
\(H_R=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak d}H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\) (Paper 9 Theorem 2.1).
An NZSBTL of \(H_R\) is *conductor-respecting* if every line \(L_k\)
lies inside a single conductor packet \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d_k]}\).

**Proposition 12.4 (Internal freedom of conductor-respecting NZSBTLs).**
*A conductor-respecting NZSBTL of \(H_R\) is determined by:*

  *(a) an ordering of the descent steps among the conductor packets, i.e.
  a multiset filtration*
  \(\bigl(\mathfrak d_1,\ldots,\mathfrak d_{N-1}\bigr)\)
  *with the property that the multiplicity of any conductor
  \(\mathfrak d\) equals \(\dim H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\); and*

  *(b) an arbitrary Fisher-orthogonal flag of each packet
  \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\).*

**Proof.**  At \(p_*\) the Fisher metric is diagonal on the conductor
decomposition, so an NZSBTL line is conductor-pure iff it lies in a
single packet.  A maximal conductor-respecting flag is obtained by
choosing, for each packet, an ordering of its internal Fisher-orthogonal
basis, and then interleaving those internal orderings across packets in
any way compatible with packet dimensions.  The packet-ordering data is
the multiset filtration of (a); the within-packet ordering is the free
flag of (b).  \(\square\)

### 12.2 The Prime Spectral Ladder (PSL)

The cross-packet ordering data of Proposition 12.4(a) is not arbitrary
once \(R\) is given the structure of a finite quotient ring.  It is
canonically constrained by the conductor lattice, and that constraint
admits a clean prime-spectral description.

**Definition 12.5 (PSL).**  Let \(R=A/I\) be a finite quotient ring with
CRT factorization \(R=\prod_p R_{(p)}\) into \(p\)-primary local
components.  Each local factor \(R_{(p)}\) carries the depth filtration

$$
\{0\}=R_{(p)}^{[0]}\subset R_{(p)}^{[1]}\subset\cdots\subset R_{(p)}^{[d_p]}=R_{(p)}.
$$

The **Prime Spectral Ladder** of \(R\) is the totally ordered set of
nontrivial conductor levels

$$
\mathrm{PSL}(R)
=
\bigl\{(p,k):p\mid|R|,\ 1\le k\le d_p\bigr\},
$$

equipped with the partial order
\((p,k)\le(p,k')\) iff \(k\le k'\), and with no cross-prime order
relation.  A *maximal PSL flag* is a total refinement of this partial
order.  The prime-conductor depth of \(R\) is \(d(R)=\sum_p d_p\).

**Proposition 12.6 (PSL refines NZSBTL cross-packet data).**  *For a
conductor-respecting NZSBTL of \(H_R\), the multiset filtration
\((\mathfrak d_1,\ldots,\mathfrak d_{N-1})\) of Proposition 12.4(a) is
the lift to the conductor lattice of a unique maximal PSL flag.  Distinct
maximal PSL flags correspond bijectively to distinct cross-packet orderings
of conductor-respecting NZSBTLs.*

**Proof.**  The conductor lattice of \(R\) is the lattice of subrings of
the form \(R/\mathfrak d\) for ideals \(\mathfrak d\subseteq R\); by CRT
it factors as a product over primes, and within each prime as a totally
ordered chain by depth.  A maximal PSL flag is therefore a *shuffle* of
the within-prime chains.  Each shuffle corresponds to the order in which
NZSBTL exhausts the conductor packets, with within-packet ordering free.
\(\square\)

### 12.3 The Pontryagin duality at the centroid

NZSBTL and PSL are *dual descents* on the same Fisher object at the
centroid: NZSBTL is a flag of subspaces of \(H_R\), PSL is a flag of
subgroups of the conductor lattice (equivalently, by Pontryagin duality,
a flag of quotients of \(\widehat R\)).

**Theorem 12.7 (Centroid duality).**  *Let \(R\) be a finite abelian
group.  At \(p_*\):*

  *(i)* *The conductor-respecting NZSBTLs of \(H_R\) are in canonical
        bijection with the pairs*
        \(\bigl(\mathfrak M,\{F_{\mathfrak d}\}\bigr)\)
        *consisting of a maximal PSL flag \(\mathfrak M\) of \(R\) and a
        Fisher-orthogonal flag \(F_{\mathfrak d}\) inside each conductor
        packet \(H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\).*

  *(ii)* *The balance-currency budget of a conductor-respecting NZSBTL
         splits as*
         $$
         \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\|L_k\|^2_{F,p_*}
         =\sum_{\mathfrak d\in\mathrm{Cond}(R)}\dim H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\cdot Q,
         $$
         *and the cross-packet contributions are independent currencies:
         each PSL step \((p,k)\) spends \(\dim H_R^{[\mathfrak d(p,k)]}\)
         Fisher-variance units in a way that does not interact with the
         currencies spent on other prime-depth steps.*

  *(iii)* *The Fisher-Pontryagin identification \(H_R\cong{\mathbb C}\langle
          \widehat R\setminus\{\mathbf 1\}\rangle\) carries every
          conductor-respecting NZSBTL to a subgroup flag of \(\widehat R\)
          terminating at a single nontrivial character line; the bijection
          intertwines the NZSBTL balance currency with the Plancherel norm
          on \(\widehat R\).*

**Proof.**  *(i)* combines Proposition 12.4 with Proposition 12.6:
conductor-respecting NZSBTLs are determined by their cross-packet
ordering (= PSL flag) and their within-packet flag.  *(ii)* The Fisher
metric is diagonal on the conductor decomposition with eigenvalues equal
to the global scale; the within-packet currencies sum to
\(\dim H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}\cdot Q\), and the cross-packet sum is over
PSL levels by Proposition 12.6.  *(iii)* The nontrivial characters
\(\{\chi_a\}_{a\ne 0}\) form an orthonormal basis of \(H_R\otimes{\mathbb C}\)
under the centroid Fisher metric (up to scale).  A conductor-respecting
NZSBTL is a Fisher-orthogonal flag whose levels are spanned by
characters of a single conductor packet, which is the same datum as a
subgroup flag of \(\widehat R\) refining the conductor lattice.
\(\square\)

The phrase "translation discretization harmonized with stretching
discretization" of the program-level commentary is now precise: NZSBTL is
the discretization of *translation* on \(R\) (the additive scale), PSL
is the discretization of *stretching* on \(\widehat R\) (the
multiplicative prime-power scale), and Theorem 12.7 says that at the
centroid these are conjugate descents through the same Fisher skeleton,
with independent currencies and a shared total budget.

### 12.4 The off-centroid carry obstruction

The clean Pontryagin duality is a centroid theorem.  Off the centroid,
the Fisher metric carries the multiplier \(1/p\), and the *commutativity*
of the two descents breaks in a structured way controlled by the
operator-valued carry of Paper 11.

**Theorem 12.8 (Translation-stretching carry obstruction).**  *Let
\(\mathfrak L\) be a conductor-respecting NZSBTL of \(H_R\) with PSL data
\(\mathfrak M\), and let \(h=p-p_*\) be a small displacement from the
centroid.  Along the \(m\)-flat ray \(p_*+th\), the Fisher-orthogonality
of consecutive NZSBTL levels at order \(n\) is destroyed by exactly the
quotient-character adjacency operator*

$$
\sum_{\eta\in\widehat R/H_R^{[\mathfrak d]}}
\widehat{h^n}(\eta)\,
A_\eta^{\mathfrak d',\mathfrak d}
\quad\in\mathcal A_R,
$$

*acting between the consecutive PSL boundary packets
\((\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d')\), where the sum runs over quotient
characters mediating the conductor change.  In particular, the carry
obstruction is concentrated at PSL boundaries (cross-packet descent
steps) and vanishes inside a single packet.*

**Proof.**  Within a single conductor packet, the centroid Fisher metric
is a scalar multiple of the identity; the off-centroid correction at
order \(n\) is \(\sum_\eta\widehat{h^n}(\eta)A_\eta^{\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d}\)
with \(\eta=0\) the diagonal contribution and \(\eta\ne 0\) controlling
mixing.  Conductor-respecting NZSBTL keeps all motion within one packet
along an internal flag, so the within-packet contributions are
constants times the identity — they rescale but do not mix the flag.
The cross-packet steps cross the boundary \(\mathfrak d\to\mathfrak d'\),
where \(\eta\) ranges over nontrivial quotient characters carrying the
spectral change; these are exactly the operator-valued generators of the
quotient-character adjacency algebra (Paper 11 Definition 2.1).  The
displayed expression is the order-\(n\) coefficient of the off-centroid
Fisher coupling tensor restricted to the consecutive packets
\((\mathfrak d,\mathfrak d')\), by Paper 10 Theorem 7.1 combined with
Paper 11 Theorem 4.1.  \(\square\)

Theorem 12.8 sharpens the off-centroid story.  The *only* place where
NZSBTL and PSL fail to be parallel descents is at the PSL boundaries —
the moments when descent must change conductor packet.  At those
boundaries the carry data of Paper 11 is exactly the obstruction; at all
other moments the two descents commute.

### 12.5 The joint information-investment theorem-target

**Problem 10.4 (Joint two-axis information-investment optimization).**
*Within a fixed finite categorical behavior class \(\mu\) on a finite
quotient ring \(R\) and a fixed resource proxy \(\mathrm{Res}\), define
the joint two-axis information-investment functional*

$$
\mathcal J_\infty(\mathcal D;\mu,\mathrm{Res})
=
\sum_{n\ge 0}\bigl\|\mathrm{Carry}_n^{[\mathfrak L,\mathfrak M]}(h_\mu)\bigr\|_F^2
+\lambda_{\mathrm{NZSBTL}}\cdot\mathrm{depth}_{\mathrm{NZSBTL}}(\mathcal D)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{PSL}}\cdot\mathrm{depth}_{\mathrm{PSL}}(\mathcal D)
+\lambda_{\mathrm{Res}}\cdot\mathrm{Res}(\mathcal D),
$$

*where \(\mathcal D=(\mathfrak L,\mathfrak M)\) is a pair of NZSBTL and
PSL choices and \(\mathrm{Carry}_n\) is the order-\(n\) operator of
Theorem 12.8.*

  *(i)* *Show that \(\mathcal J_\infty\) is well-defined on
        conductor-respecting NZSBTL\(\times\)PSL pairs and finite on the
        finite atlas of Paper 11.*

  *(ii)* *Show that for cyclic \(R=C_N\), \(\mathcal J_\infty\) is
         minimized by the lcm-conductor descent, and that for finite
         abelian \(R\), \(\mathcal J_\infty\) is minimized by the
         subgroup generated by the reciprocal-density support of
         \(h_\mu\).*

  *(iii)* *Identify the resource frontier
          \((\lambda_{\mathrm{NZSBTL}},\lambda_{\mathrm{PSL}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Res}})\)
          beyond which the carry-driven cross-packet steps become more
          expensive than additional within-packet descent.  This is the
          operational trust frontier: beyond it, prediction engines
          should refuse to extend PSL depth.*

  *(iv)* *Prove that the minimizer of (iii) is the unique joint
         NZSBTL\(\times\)PSL refinement compatible with the symmetry of
         \(\mu\); equivalently, that the centroid Pontryagin duality of
         Theorem 12.7 lifts to a (data-driven) off-centroid optimization
         theorem along the carry-bounded \(m\)-flat rays of Paper 11.*

Parts (i)-(ii) follow from the present paper combined with the cyclic
and finite-abelian single-axis closure theorems (cf. Paper 12 §9.2).
Part (iv) is established in Paper 12 Theorem 9.2.1, the joint
two-axis information-investment optimization theorem, via a 6-step
proof combining the centroid duality of Theorem 12.7 of the present
paper, the off-centroid carry obstruction of Paper 11 Theorem 9.6 and
Corollary 9.6.3, the cyclic and finite-abelian single-axis closures,
and the CRT decomposition of Paper 11 §4.  The conditional hypothesis
is the per-row carry-vs-depth inequality \((\dagger_p)\) of Paper 12
§9.2; cyclic and finite-abelian local rows satisfy it unconditionally,
and mixed local rows reduce it to a finite eigenvalue computation on
the operadic generators of Paper 11 Proposition 9.5.8 which has been
verified on every mixed row of the Paper 11 atlas computed to date.
Part (iii) — the resource-frontier identification — is the practical
companion to the optimization theorem: per-row finite inequalities
together with their resource-weight phase boundaries.
*Choosing the discretization is choosing an NZSBTL\(\times\)PSL pair,
and the unique such pair compatible with \(\mu\) is the one that
minimizes \(\mathcal J_\infty\) at the chosen resource weight.*

### 12.6 The \(N=4\) Cayley-Dickson coincidence as a two-axis special case

The exceptional structure at \(N=4\) (Paper 13 Section 2: simplicial
cross product, \(K(u)^3=-\|u\|^2K(u)\), single-axis 2-form via Hodge
duality) is the two-axis descent specialized at prime 2 with depth 2.
For \(|R|=4\) the PSL has length 2 over the single prime 2; for
\(\dim H_4=3\) the NZSBTL has length 3, and the Hodge identification
\(H_4\cong\Lambda^2 H_4^*\) is the *additional* coincidence that makes
the rotation-blade-and-vector identification a single antisymmetric
operator.  At higher \(N\), neither the prime tower depth nor the
Hodge degree matches; the four-dimensional quaternionic miracle is the
prime-2 / depth-2 row of a general arithmetic phenomenon, not a
universal feature of simplicial frames.  Cayley-Dickson algebras —
\({\mathbb R}\), \({\mathbb C}\), \({\mathbb H}\), \({\mathbb O}\) — are
one prime family (the 2-tower); analogous prime-\(p\) towers exist at
every prime, with their own (typically non-binary) closure laws.  See
Paper 11 §9.6 for the corresponding multiplicative side and Paper 12
§9.2 for the AI-optimization consequence.

## 13. Closing

The centroid is not merely a convenient basepoint.  It is the one point where
six structures coincide:

1. The categorical Fisher metric becomes the simplicial Gram.
2. The regular translation action becomes an internal unitary action on one
   tangent space.
3. The additive-character conductor decomposition becomes Fisher-orthogonal.
4. The \(e\)-flat and \(m\)-flat tangent splittings agree through the scalar
   linearization of the Legendre map.
5. Chentsov's statistical uniqueness collapses the conductor-compatible
   inner-product family to one global Fisher scale.
6. The conductor quotient tower is simultaneously a sufficiency tower of
   informationally closed macrovariables for quotient categorical
   subfamilies.

That coincidence is exactly what Papers 8 and 9 exploit.  The
information-geometric bridge does not dilute their arithmetic content; it
explains why their Gram identities feel so native.  They are Gram identities
because they are Fisher identities.

Off the centroid, the symmetry breaks, but it breaks in Fourier coordinates.
That is the clue.  If there is a deeper Carlitz/Hayes information geometry, it
will not preserve the centroid decomposition unchanged.  It will explain the
arithmetic pattern of its failure: first by the conductor selection rule, then
by bilinear Hayes-Mobius coupling, and ultimately by an all-order
conductor-convolution expansion.
