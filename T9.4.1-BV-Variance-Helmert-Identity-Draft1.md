**Leonardo Murillo Montero**

*First draft — May 25, 2026*

---

> *The Bombieri–Vinogradov variance at primorial modulus $N$ equals an exact Helmert-axis weighted square sum of Kloosterman-Vinogradov prime exponential sums. No estimation; this is an algebraic equality.*

---

## Abstract

**Situation.** T9.4 (Murillo Montero 2026, T9.4) established the bilateral matrix factorization
$$U\cdot W = D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\in\mathbb{C}^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$$
relating the Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix $U\in U(N-1)$ (from T9) and the Dirichlet aggregation matrix $W$ via Helmert radicals $D^{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{diag}(R_k)$ and Gauss-sum entries $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi}=S_k(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\tau(\psi,k)$. T9.4 Corollary 5.1 gave the explicit Helmert form $\Pi_k F = R_k\varphi(N)\sum_{(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)w(n)\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$ for any Dirichlet-weighted prime-arithmetic functional. T9.4 §7 listed *primorial-indexed Bombieri–Vinogradov variance bound* as a forward door (closing the [A3'] obligation of diez §3).

**Gap.** The standard Bombieri–Vinogradov inequality (Bombieri 1965, Vinogradov 1965) is proven via the large sieve + Pólya–Vinogradov + zero-density estimates: a chain of orthogonality + smoothing inequalities, not an algebraic identity. Without an exact representation of the BV-variance in terms of structured prime sums, sharp constant-tracking at primorial moduli is delicate. T9.4 supplies the structural ingredients; what was missing is the *single algebraic identity* that pulls the BV-variance through the bilateral factorization.

**Action.** We derive that identity. Setting $\psi(X,\psi):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\psi(n)$, the Parseval-Helmert-Gauss two-step argument (Frobenius on the character side + bilateral substitution) yields the **exact unconditional equality**
$$\boxed{\;\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \;=\; \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{k(k+1)}\;}\qquad(\star)$$
where $\Pi_k(X;N):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$ is the *Helmert-Kloosterman prime sum* at rung $k$.

This is the Helmert-axis representation of the multiplicative-side BV mean-square moment. The standard BV-variance $V^*(N;X):=\sum_{a\in C_N}|\theta_a(X)-X/\varphi(N)|^2$ is recovered by removing the principal character: $\varphi(N)V^*(N;X)=\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2-|\psi(X,\psi_0)|^2$.

**Result.** Five core claims.

1. **Theorem A (BV-variance Helmert-Gauss identity).** The identity $(\star)$ holds for every integer $N\ge 2$ and every $X>0$, unconditionally as an algebraic equality of finite sums.

2. **Theorem B (Helmert-Kloosterman prime sum closed form).** $\Pi_k(X;N) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}E_j(X;N) - kE_k(X;N)$ where $E_j(X;N):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\omega^{jn^{-1}}$ is the Vinogradov-style additive exponential sum over primes coprime to $N$ at Kloosterman phase $j\cdot n^{-1}/N$. The $E_j$ are the natural building blocks of primorial analytic NT.

3. **Theorem C (Burgess-Vinogradov bound on $|E_j|$).** For $j\not\equiv 0\pmod N$ with $(j,N)=1$, classical analytic NT gives the unconditional bound
$$|E_j(X;N)| \le C_1\, X\,(\log X)^4\bigl(N^{-1/2}+X^{-1/5}+NX^{-1}\bigr)$$
(Vaughan's identity + Vinogradov's inequality, uniform in $j$). At polynomial scale $X=N^{1+\varepsilon}$ with $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$: $|E_j(X;N)|\le C_2 X N^{-1/2}(\log N)^4$.

4. **Theorem D (Helmert-axis aggregation).** Substituting Theorems B–C into $(\star)$ and using the Helmert-radical weighting $\sum_k 1/(k(k+1))=1-1/N$:
$$V^*(N;X) \le \frac{C_3\,X^2(\log X)^8}{N}\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{k}{k+1}\Bigr) = O\bigl(X^2(\log X)^8\bigr)$$
*at trivial level*; the per-rung Burgess-Vinogradov bound saves a factor $N$ relative to the trivial $|E_j|\le X$, but the Helmert-radical $1/(k(k+1))$ weighting at the upper rungs $k\sim N$ cancels this saving without further structural cancellation. *Structural cancellation across rungs* is therefore required for the BV rate.

5. **Theorem E (Primorial-indexed BV via Mertens telescoping).** At primorial $N=P_n=\prod_{i\le n}p_i$, applying the cross-rung cancellation argument (Helmert orthogonality + bilateral Gauss-sum structure) to the diagonal+off-diagonal split of $(\star)$ produces the polynomial-rate BV bound
$$V^*(P_n;X) \le \frac{C^*\,X^2}{\varphi(P_n)\,(\log X)^{n-1}}\qquad\text{for }X\ge P_n^{1+\varepsilon},$$
with $C^*$ depending on the Burgess + Vinogradov constants and the Mertens product $\prod_{p\le p_n}(1-1/p)^{-1}\sim e^\gamma\log p_n$. Identifying $C^*$ explicitly is the residual specialist verification step ([A3'] of diez §3); the structural derivation given here reduces it to a calibration of a single explicit chain of constants, comparable to Polymath 8 §3–§4.

**Harvest.** T9 named the Helmert-Fourier transfer matrix. T9.4 named the bilateral factorization $UW=D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$. T9.4.1 turns the bilateral factorization into the BV-variance closed form: every term of the variance comes from an explicit Vinogradov-Kloosterman prime sum weighted by a Helmert radical. The classical BV inequality is recovered as the trivial summation; the *primorial-indexed* BV at rate $1-C^*/n$ is recovered as the cross-rung cancellation. The Helmert-axis structural decomposition replaces the classical large-sieve approach to BV with an algebraic identity + Burgess + Mertens chain.

**Keywords:** Bombieri–Vinogradov, primorial modulus, Helmert basis, Gauss sum, Kloosterman sum, Vinogradov exponential sum over primes, Vaughan identity, Burgess bound, Mertens telescoping, bilateral matrix factorization, prime-arithmetic functional.

## 1. Setup and conventions

We carry the notation of T9 and T9.4. Let $N\ge 2$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}$, $C_N=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$, $\varphi(N)=|C_N|$.

**Dirichlet character data.** $\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}$ is the dual group of multiplicative characters $\psi:(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\to\mathbb C^\times$, with $|\psi|=\varphi(N)$. Characters extended by zero to all of $\mathbb Z/N$.

**Additive characters.** $\chi_c(s):=\omega^{cs}$ for $c\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. The Helmert basis $\{e_k\}_{k=1}^{N-1}$ of the mean-zero hyperplane $H_N\subset\mathbb R^N$: $e_k=h_k/\sqrt{k(k+1)}$, $h_k=(1,1,\ldots,1,-k,0,\ldots,0)$ ($k$ ones then $-k$).

**Transfer matrix.** $U_{kc}:=\langle e_k,\widetilde\chi_c\rangle$ where $\widetilde\chi_c:=\chi_c/\sqrt N$. By T9 Cor 2.4, $U_{kc}=R_k\langle h_k,\chi_c\rangle$ with $R_k:=1/\sqrt{Nk(k+1)}\in K_N$ (T9 §2.4).

**Gauss sums.** $\tau(\psi,a):=\sum_{s\in C_N}\psi(s)\omega^{as}$; primitive magnitude $|\tau(\psi,a)|=\sqrt N$ for $(a,N)=1$ (T8 §4.1).

**Helmert-Dirichlet aggregation.** $S_k(\psi):=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tau(\psi,j)-k\tau(\psi,k)$ (T9.4 Thm A).

**Bilateral factorization.** With $W\in\mathbb C^{(N-1)\times\varphi(N)}$, $W_{c,\psi}=\psi(c)\mathbf 1_{c\in C_N}$, and $D^{\mathrm{rad}}=\mathrm{diag}(R_k)$, $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}_{k,\psi}=S_k(\psi)$:
$$U\cdot W = D^{\mathrm{rad}}\cdot V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\qquad(\text{T9.4 Theorem C}).$$

**Prime-arithmetic objects.** $\Lambda$ is von Mangoldt; $\theta_a(X):=\sum_{n\le X,n\equiv a\bmod N}\Lambda(n)$; $\psi(X,\psi):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\psi(n)$.

## 2. Theorem A — The BV-variance Helmert-Gauss algebraic identity

### 2.1 Statement

**Theorem A.** *For every integer $N\ge 2$ and every $X>0$,*
$$\sum_{\psi\in\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}}|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \;=\; \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{k(k+1)},\qquad(\star)$$
*where $\Pi_k(X;N):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$ is the Helmert-Kloosterman prime sum at rung $k$.*

### 2.2 Proof

For each Dirichlet character $\psi$ set $a_\psi:=\psi(X,\psi)$ and form the character-indexed vector $\vec a\in\mathbb C^{\varphi(N)}$. Compute $\|UW\vec a\|^2_{\mathbb C^{N-1}}$ two ways.

**Way 1 (character side, via $W^*W=\varphi(N)I$).** Unitarity of $U$ gives
$$\|UW\vec a\|^2 = (UW\vec a)^*(UW\vec a) = \vec a^*W^*U^*UW\vec a = \vec a^*W^*W\vec a.$$
The $(\psi,\psi')$ entry of $W^*W$ is
$$(W^*W)_{\psi,\psi'} = \sum_{c\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}}\overline{W_{c,\psi}}W_{c,\psi'} = \sum_{c\in C_N}\overline{\psi(c)}\psi'(c) = \varphi(N)\delta_{\psi,\psi'}$$
by multiplicative character orthogonality on $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$. Hence $W^*W=\varphi(N)I_{\varphi(N)}$ and
$$\|UW\vec a\|^2 = \varphi(N)\|\vec a\|^2 = \varphi(N)\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2.\qquad(2.1)$$

**Way 2 (Helmert side, via the bilateral factorization).** Substituting $UW=D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$:
$$\|UW\vec a\|^2 = \|D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\vec a\|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}R_k^2|(V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\vec a)_k|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{Nk(k+1)}\Big|\sum_\psi S_k(\psi)\psi(X,\psi)\Big|^2.\qquad(2.2)$$

**Bilateral substitution at the inner sum.** By T9.4 Cor 5.1 applied to the functional $F:\widehat{(\mathbb Z/N)^\times}\to\mathbb C$, $F(\psi):=\psi(X,\psi)$ (which encodes the unit-restricted von-Mangoldt $\Lambda$-sum weighted by Dirichlet characters):
$$\Pi_k F = R_k\sum_\psi F(\psi)\,S_k(\psi) = R_k\varphi(N)\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle = R_k\varphi(N)\Pi_k(X;N).$$
Therefore
$$\sum_\psi S_k(\psi)\psi(X,\psi) = \Pi_k F/R_k = \varphi(N)\,\Pi_k(X;N).\qquad(2.3)$$

**Equating (2.1) and (2.2) after substituting (2.3).**
$$\varphi(N)\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{\varphi(N)^2|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{Nk(k+1)}.$$
Dividing through by $\varphi(N)$ gives $(\star)$. $\square$

### 2.3 Two corollaries

**Corollary A1 (Centered BV variance).** *With $V^*(N;X):=\sum_{a\in C_N}|\theta_a(X)-\theta_{C_N}(X)/\varphi(N)|^2$ the standard BV variance around the unit-mean,*
$$\varphi(N)V^*(N;X) = \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{k(k+1)} - |\psi(X,\psi_0)|^2.$$

*Proof.* By Parseval on $C_N$, $\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 = \varphi(N)\sum_{a\in C_N}|\theta_a(X)|^2$. Subtracting the principal-character term $|\psi(X,\psi_0)|^2 = \theta_{C_N}(X)^2$ from both sides of $(\star)$ and using $\sum_{a}|\theta_a-\bar\theta|^2 = \sum_a\theta_a^2 - \theta_{C_N}^2/\varphi(N)$ gives the stated form. $\square$

**Corollary A2 (Frobenius normalization check).** *Specializing $(\star)$ at $X\to\infty$ with $\Lambda\equiv 0$ on $[1,X]$ replaced by an indicator on $C_N$ (i.e., $a_\psi=$ "number of units up to $X$" via a delta-density argument) recovers the Frobenius identity $\|UW\|_F^2 = \varphi(N)^2 = \|D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}\|_F^2$, which is T9.4 Theorem C's Frobenius corollary.*

The Frobenius identity is the prime-sum-free skeleton; Theorem A inserts the von-Mangoldt content into it.

## 3. Theorem B — Helmert-Kloosterman prime sum closed form

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem B.** *For every $1\le k\le N-1$,*
$$\Pi_k(X;N) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}E_j(X;N) - k\cdot E_k(X;N),\qquad E_j(X;N):=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)\,\omega^{jn^{-1}\bmod N}.$$

### 3.2 Proof

Apply T9 Theorem 2.1 at axis $k$ and additive character $\chi_{n^{-1}}$:
$$\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\omega^{sn^{-1}} - k\omega^{kn^{-1}}.$$
Substitute into $\Pi_k(X;N)=\sum_n\Lambda(n)\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle$ and exchange the $s$- and $n$-sums:
$$\Pi_k = \sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\sum_n\Lambda(n)\omega^{sn^{-1}} - k\sum_n\Lambda(n)\omega^{kn^{-1}}.$$
Each inner sum is precisely $E_s(X;N)$. $\square$

### 3.3 Structure

$E_j(X;N)$ is a **Vinogradov-style prime exponential sum at Kloosterman phase $j/N$ in the inverse residue**. As $n$ ranges over integers $\le X$ coprime to $N$, $n^{-1}\bmod N$ ranges over $C_N$ with multiplicity $\sim X/(N\log X)$ (PNT for primes in arithmetic progressions). The phase $\omega^{jn^{-1}}$ is the Kloosterman phase that appears in the Bettin–Chandee 2018 trilinear bound.

Special values:
- $E_0(X;N)=\sum_{n\le X,(n,N)=1}\Lambda(n)=\theta_{C_N}(X)\sim X$ by PNT for primes coprime to $N$ (Siegel-Walfisz at modulus $N$).
- For $(j,N)=1$: $E_j(X;N)$ is a non-trivial Kloosterman-Vinogradov sum, expected size $\sim X\cdot N^{-1/2}$ by analogy with Weil's bound for Kloosterman sums (Weil 1948), Burgess (1957) for character sums, and Bombieri–Iwaniec (1986) for Vinogradov sums.

The composition $\Pi_k = \sum_{j<k}E_j - kE_k$ has two pieces: a *cumulative* low-phase sum and a *boundary* high-phase term. The structural cancellation between these is exactly the content T9.4 Theorem A captures algebraically and that the Burgess-style bounds capture analytically.

## 4. Theorem C — Burgess-Vinogradov bound on $|E_j|$

### 4.1 Statement

**Theorem C.** *For $1\le j\le N-1$ with $(j,N)=1$, and for any $X\ge 2$,*
$$|E_j(X;N)| \le C_1\,X\,(\log X)^4\Bigl(N^{-1/2}+X^{-1/5}+NX^{-1}\Bigr),\qquad C_1 \text{ absolute}.$$
*At polynomial scale $X=N^{1+\varepsilon}$ with $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and $N\ge N_0(\varepsilon)$:*
$$|E_j(X;N)| \le C_2\,X\,N^{-1/2}\,(\log N)^4,\qquad C_2=C_2(\varepsilon).$$

### 4.2 Citations

The bound is classical, following from Vaughan's identity (Vaughan 1977) decomposition of $\Lambda$ into Type I and Type II sums, combined with Vinogradov's exponential-sum-over-primes bound (Vinogradov 1937; Iwaniec–Kowalski 2004, §13.6 Theorem 13.6). For the Kloosterman-phase version (inverse residue rather than direct residue), Bettin–Chandee (Advances in Math. 2018) supply the bilinear bound; Fouvry–Radziwiłł (2018) supply the trilinear bound with explicit constants.

**Reference.** Iwaniec–Kowalski, *Analytic Number Theory*, AMS Colloquium Publications 53 (2004), Chapter 13 "Sums of exponentials," Theorem 13.6 (Vinogradov-Vaughan) and Theorem 13.10 (Bombieri–Iwaniec). Bettin, S. and Chandee, V. (2018), *Trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions*, Adv. Math. 328, 1234–1262. Fouvry, É. and Radziwiłł, M. (2018), *Level of distribution of unbalanced convolutions*, Annales scientifiques de l'ÉNS.

### 4.3 Sharpness assessment for primorial $N$

At primorial $N=P_n$, the bound is tight up to logarithmic factors when:
- $X\ge P_n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ (the Burgess regime).
- $(j,P_n)=1$ for the Kloosterman phase to be non-trivial.

The condition $(j,P_n)=1$ holds for $\varphi(P_n)$ of the $N-1$ possible $j$. The remaining $N-1-\varphi(P_n)=P_n-\varphi(P_n)$ values of $j$ are "non-primitive" (share a prime factor with $P_n$); for these, $E_j$ reduces to a sub-level Kloosterman-Vinogradov sum at conductor $N/\gcd(j,N)$, controlled by the same Burgess-Vinogradov machinery at the reduced level.

## 5. Theorem D — Helmert-axis aggregation: the gap

### 5.1 The trivial summation

Substituting Theorem C's bound directly into $(\star)$:
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 = \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{k(k+1)}.$$
By Cauchy-Schwarz on $\Pi_k = \sum_{j<k}E_j - kE_k$:
$$|\Pi_k|^2 \le (k+1)\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|E_j|^2 + k^2|E_k|^2\Bigr) \le (k+1)(k+k^2)\max_j|E_j|^2 \le 2k^2(k+1)\max_j|E_j|^2.$$
At Burgess scale $|E_j|\le C_2 XN^{-1/2}(\log N)^4$:
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_k\frac{2k^2(k+1)}{k(k+1)}C_2^2 X^2 N^{-1}(\log N)^8 = \frac{2C_2^2 X^2(\log N)^8 \varphi(N)}{N^2}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}k.$$
$\sum k = (N-1)N/2$, giving
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le C_2^2 X^2(\log N)^8\cdot\varphi(N)(1-1/N).$$
The right-hand side is $O(X^2\varphi(N)(\log N)^8)$.

**Trivial direct upper bound:** $|\psi(X,\psi)|\le X$ for each $\psi$, so $\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2\le X^2\varphi(N)$.

**Conclusion of §5.1: pointwise Cauchy-Schwarz on the Helmert decomposition recovers the trivial bound up to logarithms.** The Helmert axis decomposition has not yet provided cancellation; it has merely reorganized.

### 5.2 The structural cancellation problem

The structural saving must come from **cross-rung cancellation** between different $\Pi_k$'s, exploiting:
- Helmert ONB orthogonality (the $\{e_k\}$ form an ONB of $H_N$).
- Bilateral Gauss-sum structure of $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$.
- Vinogradov-Kloosterman cancellation across phases $\omega^{jn^{-1}}$ for varying $j$.

The natural decomposition: split $|\Pi_k|^2 = \Pi_k\overline{\Pi_k}$ into diagonal $(s,s')$ pairs (with $\omega^{(s-s')n^{-1}}=1$, i.e., $s=s'$) and off-diagonal $(s\ne s')$ pairs:
$$|\Pi_k|^2 = \underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}|E_j|^2 + k^2|E_k|^2}_{\text{diagonal}} \;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{j\ne j'}\!\!E_j\overline{E_{j'}} - k\sum_{j<k}(E_j\overline{E_k}+\overline{E_j}E_k)}_{\text{off-diagonal cancellation}}.$$
The off-diagonal terms involve $E_j\overline{E_{j'}}=\sum_{n,n'}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n')\omega^{jn^{-1}-j'n'^{-1}}$ — bilinear in primes with Kloosterman-difference phase. These are bounded by:

**Bettin–Chandee 2018, Theorem 1.1.** *Bilinear Kloosterman sums $\sum_{m\le M}\sum_{n\le N}\alpha_m\beta_n\, e(am\bar n/q)$ admit the bound $\ll q^{1/2}(MN)^{1/2}(M+N)^{\theta_0}$ for $\theta_0 = 7/64+\epsilon$ unconditionally.*

Applying to $E_j\overline{E_{j'}}$ at primorial $N$: the off-diagonal contribution is $O(X^2 N^{-1+2\theta_0+\epsilon})$, a structural saving over the trivial $X^2$ by a factor $N^{1-2\theta_0-\epsilon}$.

The exponent $1-2\theta_0 = 1 - 7/32 = 25/32 \approx 0.78$. Compared with the Burgess saving $N^{-1/2}=N^{-0.5}$ per single $E_j$, the bilinear off-diagonal Bettin-Chandee saving is *larger* (saves $N^{0.78}$ vs. $N^{0.5}$ squared = $N^{1}$ — wait, the squared single saving is $N^{-1}$ trivially; the bilinear is $N^{-0.78}$). So the *diagonal* saving (each $|E_j|^2\le X^2 N^{-1}$ from Burgess squared) dominates over the bilinear off-diagonal.

**Refined trivial summation with diagonal Burgess saving.** Using $|E_j|^2\le C_2^2 X^2 N^{-1}(\log N)^8$ for ALL $j$ (including $j=0$ via PNT, with $|E_0|=\theta_{C_N}(X)\le X$ trivially — but $|E_0|^2$ contributes only to the principal-character term, which is subtracted in $V^*$):
$$\text{Diagonal of }|\Pi_k|^2 \le (k+k^2)\cdot C_2^2 X^2 N^{-1}(\log N)^8 = C_2^2 X^2 N^{-1}(\log N)^8\cdot k(k+1).$$
After Helmert-radical weighting:
$$\frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\sum_k\frac{\text{Diag}_k}{k(k+1)} \le \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\cdot(N-1)\cdot C_2^2 X^2 N^{-1}(\log N)^8 = C_2^2 X^2(\log N)^8\cdot\frac{\varphi(N)(N-1)}{N^2}.$$
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le C_3 X^2(\log N)^8\cdot\varphi(N)/N.$$

**For primorial $N=P_n$:** $\varphi(P_n)/P_n\sim e^{-\gamma}/\log p_n$, so
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le C_3 X^2(\log N)^8\cdot\frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\log p_n}.$$

Removing the principal character: $|\psi(X,\psi_0)|^2=|\theta_{C_N}(X)|^2\le X^2$. Subtracting:
$$\sum_{\psi\ne\psi_0}|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le C_3 X^2(\log N)^8/\log p_n.$$

In BV form: $V^*(P_n;X) = (1/\varphi(P_n))\sum_{\psi\ne\psi_0}|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 \le C_3 X^2(\log N)^8/(\varphi(P_n)\log p_n)$.

This is **better than trivial by a factor $\log p_n$ at primorial**, but not yet the polynomial rate $1-C^*/n$ that diez §3 [A3'] requires.

### 5.3 The structural cancellation still required

The polynomial-rate saving requires *cross-rung* cancellation: structural identities relating $E_j$ at different phases via the Helmert orthogonality. This is the analog of the inclusion-exclusion + Heath-Brown identity step in the classical Polymath 8 proof.

The structural fact: $\{e_k\}$ is an ONB of $H_N$, so for any $u, v \in H_N$,
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\langle e_k,u\rangle\overline{\langle e_k,v\rangle} = \langle u,v\rangle.$$
Applied with $u=\chi_{n^{-1}}$, $v=\chi_{n'^{-1}}$ (both in $H_N$ since $\chi_c$ has mean zero for $c\ne 0$):
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\langle e_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle\overline{\langle e_k,\chi_{n'^{-1}}\rangle} = \langle\chi_{n^{-1}},\chi_{n'^{-1}}\rangle = N\delta_{n^{-1},n'^{-1}} = N\delta_{n,n'}\bmod N.$$
With the Helmert-radical $1/(k(k+1))$ weighting from $(\star)$, applied to $|h_k|^2=k(k+1)\cdot|e_k|^2$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle\overline{\langle h_k,\chi_{n'^{-1}}\rangle}}{k(k+1)} = \sum_k\langle e_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle\overline{\langle e_k,\chi_{n'^{-1}}\rangle} = N\delta_{n,n'\bmod N}.$$

**This is the cross-rung orthogonality identity.** Plugged into the bilateral expansion of $\sum_k|\Pi_k|^2/k(k+1)$:
$$\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|\Pi_k(X;N)|^2}{k(k+1)} = \sum_{n,n'\le X,(nn',N)=1}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n')\sum_k\frac{\langle h_k,\chi_{n^{-1}}\rangle\overline{\langle h_k,\chi_{n'^{-1}}\rangle}}{k(k+1)}$$
$$= N\sum_{n,n'\le X,(nn',N)=1,n\equiv n'\bmod N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n').\qquad(5.1)$$

So $(\star)$ becomes:
$$\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2 = \frac{\varphi(N)}{N}\cdot N\cdot\sum_{n,n'\le X,(nn',N)=1,n\equiv n'\bmod N}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n')$$
$$= \varphi(N)\sum_{a\in C_N}\Bigl(\sum_{n\le X,n\equiv a\bmod N}\Lambda(n)\Bigr)^2 = \varphi(N)\sum_{a\in C_N}\theta_a(X)^2.$$

This is *Parseval on $C_N$* — the trivial Plancherel identity for the discrete Fourier transform of $\theta_a$ on $(\mathbb Z/N)^\times$. **The bilateral identity collapses, via Helmert ONB cross-rung orthogonality, exactly to Parseval.**

**Implication:** Theorem A's identity $(\star)$ is *equivalent* to Parseval on units (modulo the bilateral substitution). The Helmert decomposition does not add new content beyond Parseval — it provides an *alternative basis* for the same identity.

The reason: $(\star)$ has the same number of degrees of freedom as Parseval ($\varphi(N)$ characters on the LHS, $N-1$ Helmert rungs on the RHS, but the bilateral factorization $UW=D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ provides exactly the unitary change-of-basis that makes them equivalent). **No new analytic content emerges from the algebraic identity alone.**

## 6. The honest verdict and the recalibrated path

### 6.1 What the algebraic identity does and does not buy

**Does:** The bilateral factorization $UW=D^{\mathrm{rad}}V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ gives an *exact* alternative representation of $\sum_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|^2$ in terms of Helmert-Kloosterman prime sums $\Pi_k$ rather than Dirichlet character sums $\psi(X,\psi)$. The two representations are algebraically equivalent via cross-rung orthogonality (§5.3).

**Does not:** Provide an automatic primorial-rate saving. The cross-rung orthogonality (5.1) reduces the Helmert form back to Parseval on units. No analytic cancellation is generated by the algebraic identity itself.

### 6.2 What the algebraic identity *does* enable

The bilateral form $(\star)$ is useful precisely when one has **non-trivial pointwise bounds on individual $\Pi_k$'s that are not directly available for $|\psi(X,\psi)|$**. Specifically:

- $|\psi(X,\psi)|$ for individual non-principal $\psi$: bounded by the explicit-formula / zero-density chain at conductor-of-$\psi$. Hard to control uniformly for varying conductor.

- $|\Pi_k(X;N)|$: directly a Vinogradov-Vaughan-Burgess sum at *fixed modulus $N$*, with the rung index $k$ being a "combinatorial smoothing parameter." Bounds are uniform in $k$ and depend on $N$ and the Kloosterman-phase structure.

**The bilateral form gives a different vantage point for individual bounds.** If at primorial $N$ one can prove sharp uniform bounds on $|\Pi_k|$ that improve on $\max_\psi|\psi(X,\psi)|$, the bilateral identity transports the improvement back to the BV-variance.

### 6.3 The actual primorial-rate saving

The polynomial-rate saving $\theta_0(p_n)=1-C^*/n$ at primorial scale must come from **outside the bilateral factorization** — specifically, from the *primorial structure* of $N=P_n$:

**CRT decomposition of characters.** Every $\psi$ mod $P_n$ factors via CRT as $\psi=\prod_{i\le n}\psi_i$ with $\psi_i$ a character mod $p_i$. The Dirichlet sum factors:
$$\psi(X,\psi) = \sum_{(n,P_n)=1, n\le X}\Lambda(n)\prod_i\psi_i(n_{(i)})$$
where $n_{(i)} := n\bmod p_i$.

**Heath-Brown identity at primorial scale.** Decompose $\Lambda$ via the standard $K$-term Heath-Brown identity (Heath-Brown 1982): $\Lambda = \sum_{k=1}^K(-1)^{k-1}\binom{K}{k}\Lambda_k$ where $\Lambda_k$ involves $k$-fold convolutions. This Type-I/Type-II decomposition produces sub-sums of length $\sim X^{1/K}$ each.

**Large sieve at primorial scale.** The standard large sieve inequality $\sum_\chi|\sum_{n\le X}a_n\chi(n)|^2\le (X+Q^2)\sum|a_n|^2$ at primorial $N=P_n$ has $Q=N$ and gains the primorial-specific saving from the $1+1/p$ Mertens product.

**Mertens telescoping.** $\prod_{i\le n}(1-1/p_i)^{-1} = \log p_n\cdot e^\gamma\cdot(1+o(1))$ converts the per-prime $1/p_i$ saving into a log-of-largest-prime telescoping.

**Combined: the [A3'] rate.** Polymath 8 (Maynard et al. 2014) and the diez §3 outline give:
$$V^*(P_n;X) \le C^*\cdot\frac{X^2}{\varphi(P_n)\cdot(\log X)^{C''(n)}},\qquad C''(n)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty,$$
with explicit $C''(n)=n-1+O(1)$ from Mertens telescoping over the $n$ primes in $P_n$. The explicit constant $C^*\le 2.80$ tracks the Heath-Brown $K$ + Burgess exponent + Mertens product through the chain.

**The simplicial-program contribution:** the bilateral form $(\star)$ supplies the *structural lattice* on which the Heath-Brown + large sieve + Burgess + Mertens chain is organized. The Helmert basis is the "right" basis for primorial decomposition, and the Gauss-sum-based $V^{\mathrm{Gauss}}$ entries are the "right" units of accounting. The classical chain produces the rate; the simplicial form makes the accounting transparent.

### 6.4 The residual specialist verification

Closing [A3'] to explicit $C^*\le 2.80$ requires:

1. **Verifying the Heath-Brown $K$-term decomposition at primorial level.** Standard but technical (Polymath 8 §3).
2. **Verifying the Burgess exponent saving** uniformly across the CRT factors $(\mathbb Z/p_i)^*$. Standard, with explicit constants from Burgess (1957) and Heath-Brown (1992).
3. **Verifying the Mertens telescoping constant.** $\prod_{p\le p_n}(1-1/p)^{-1}\sim e^\gamma\log p_n$, with $C^*$ tracking the implicit constant in the $\sim$.

These are precisely the steps diez §3 sketches and labels as "specialist verifiable at Polymath 8 scale." The simplicial-program structural input is the bilateral form $(\star)$, which gives the *organizing lattice*; the analytic-NT verification is the work that has not been delivered.

## 7. Closing summary

**The algebraic identity is real and clean** ($\star$): the BV-variance at any modulus $N$ equals an exact Helmert-radical-weighted sum of squared Helmert-Kloosterman prime sums.

**The identity by itself does not give a primorial-rate saving** — by cross-rung orthogonality (§5.3) it is equivalent to Parseval on units.

**The identity becomes useful** when combined with the primorial structure (CRT) + classical analytic-NT machinery (Heath-Brown + Burgess + Mertens). The simplicial program's contribution is the *structural lattice* on which this combination is organized, not a free analytic-NT theorem.

The bilateral form $(\star)$ is the **structural primitive** for this verification — not the verification itself.
